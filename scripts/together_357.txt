PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 26
"Wake Up and Put On Christ"
Romans for Marriage — Day 26


Hello and welcome to your couples devotional. Whether you are sitting side by side on the couch or listening together at the end of a long day, God is here with you both. He meets married couples in a special way when they turn their hearts to Him together.

Let me read our passage for this evening. It comes from Romans 13:11-14:

"And that, knowing the time, that now it is high time to awake out of sleep: for now is our salvation nearer than when we believed. The night is far spent, the day is at hand: let us therefore cast off the works of darkness, and let us put on the armour of light. Let us walk honestly, as in the day; not in rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness, not in strife and envying. But put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh, to fulfil the lusts thereof."

That is Romans 13:11-14. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Thomas Watson was a faithful pastor and theologian whose words still carry weight centuries later. Listen to what the Puritans had to say about the truths in tonight's passage.

Paul sounds an alarm: 'It is high time to awake out of sleep!' The urgency is driven by eschatological reality — Christ is coming, the day is at hand. But the practical application is immediate: stop sleepwalking through your spiritual life. Wake up. The time for spiritual drowsiness is over.

Thomas Watson, known for his piercing applications, used this passage to confront the dangerous complacency that can settle over long marriages. Watson observed that many couples begin their marriages with spiritual fervor — they pray together, study Scripture together, serve the church together. But over time, a spiritual sleep descends. The routines of daily life lull them into drowsiness. Prayer becomes perfunctory. Scripture reading lapses. Spiritual conversation evaporates. They are still married, still attending church, still going through the motions — but they are spiritually asleep.

Watson called couples to awake. 'Cast off the works of darkness' — put away the things that belong to the night. In the context of marriage, this includes the secret sins, the hidden resentments, the unconfessed failures that fester in the dark. 'Put on the armour of light' — clothe yourselves in transparency, holiness, and the practical pursuit of godliness.

The climactic command is verse 14: 'Put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ.' To put on Christ is to make Him the defining reality of your life. It means approaching every situation as Christ would approach it. It means speaking to your spouse as Christ would speak. It means loving your spouse as Christ would love. Christ becomes your identity, your standard, and your power.

Watson also emphasized the negative counterpart: 'Make not provision for the flesh, to fulfil the lusts thereof.' Do not set up conditions where sin can thrive. Do not leave open doors for temptation. Do not create environments where your worst instincts are fed. Instead, create a home environment that nourishes the spirit and starves the flesh.


Has a spiritual drowsiness settled over your marriage? Have you been going through the motions without genuine spiritual vitality? If so, tonight is your wake-up call.

Paul says the night is far spent and the day is at hand. Time is short. Do not waste another week sleepwalking through your marriage. Wake up. Put on Christ. Cast off whatever belongs to darkness — hidden sins, unresolved conflicts, spiritual laziness — and step into the light.

Together tonight, identify one area where you have been 'asleep' spiritually as a couple. Then take one concrete step to wake up: restart your daily prayer time, begin reading Scripture together, confess a hidden sin, or plan a spiritual goal for the coming month.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Let us reflect together. These questions are designed to help you go deeper, both with God and with each other.

Question 1: Would you describe the current spiritual state of your marriage as awake or asleep? Why?

Question 2: What 'works of darkness' need to be cast off in your marriage? What 'armor of light' needs to be put on?

Question 3: What does it mean practically for your marriage to 'put on the Lord Jesus Christ' today?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


As we near the end of tonight's devotional, let us bring our hearts before God in prayer. Pray for each other. Pray for your marriage. Pray for your home.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to wake your marriage up spiritually. Pray for fresh urgency, fresh devotion, and fresh commitment to putting on Christ in every area of your shared life. The day is at hand — live like it.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May you awake together to the urgency and beauty of the time you have been given. May you put on Christ daily and walk in the light, leaving behind everything that belongs to the night.

The Lord bless you and keep you. The Lord make His face shine upon you and be gracious to you. The Lord lift up His countenance upon you both, and give you peace. Amen.
