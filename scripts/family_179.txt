PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 18
"The Secret Place of Prayer"
Matthew Henry — Family Worship and Bible Study


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Matthew Henry leads us into the heart of family prayer — not prayer as a formality but prayer as the vital breath of the Christian home. Let us come into God's presence together.

Let us pray. Father, teach us to pray. We confess that we often pray poorly, briefly, and without faith. Renew our love for this sacred privilege and help us to draw near to Your throne with boldness and expectation. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Matthew 6:6-8 and James 5:16. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly. But when ye pray, use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking. Be not ye therefore like unto them: for your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much."

That is Matthew 6:6-8 and James 5:16. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Matthew Henry was a man of prayer. Those who knew him testified that his prayers — both in public worship and in private devotion — were marked by warmth, simplicity, scriptural richness, and deep reverence. His commentary is saturated with prayer, and his treatise on family worship gives prayer a central place. For Henry, a household without prayer was a household without God — or at least, a household that was living as though God were absent.

Henry distinguished three kinds of prayer that should characterize the Christian home: private prayer, family prayer, and public prayer. All three are essential, and none can substitute for the others. Private prayer is the soul's secret communion with God — the prayer offered in the closet, behind a shut door, where only God is the audience. Family prayer is the household's corporate communion with God — the prayer offered together around the table or at the family altar. Public prayer is the congregation's united cry to God in the assembly of believers.

Tonight we focus on family prayer — the kind that is uniquely suited to the household and that brings its own distinct blessings.

Henry identifies several characteristics of effective family prayer. The first is sincerity. Jesus warns against "vain repetitions" — the empty, mechanical recitation of words without engagement of the heart. Family prayer should be real. It should express the actual needs, concerns, joys, and struggles of the household. When a child is sick, pray for healing. When a financial burden weighs heavy, pray for provision. When a family member has sinned, pray for forgiveness. When God has provided, pray with thanksgiving. The more specific and sincere our prayers, the more they engage the hearts of every family member and the more they honor the God who invites us to bring our real needs to His throne.

The second characteristic is brevity. Henry counsels against long, elaborate family prayers that exhaust the listeners and particularly the children. A prayer that is short, heartfelt, and specific is far more effective than one that is long, wandering, and general. Henry recommends that family prayers last no more than a few minutes, especially when young children are present. The goal is not to impress anyone with the length or eloquence of our prayers but to bring our needs honestly before God and to teach our children that prayer is a natural, essential, and joyful part of daily life.

The third characteristic is inclusiveness. Henry urges family leaders to include every member in the prayer life of the household. Children should be encouraged to pray aloud from an early age — even if their prayers are short and simple. A child who learns to pray at the family altar will be a man or woman of prayer for the rest of their life. Family members should also contribute prayer requests, so that the whole family knows what is being prayed for and can join in intercession together.

The fourth characteristic is faith. James says that "the effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much." Henry emphasizes the word "fervent." Family prayer should be offered with genuine expectation that God hears and answers. We are not sending messages into a void. We are speaking to a living, loving, all-powerful Father who has promised to hear His children when they call upon Him. Henry writes that one of the great purposes of family prayer is to teach children, by daily experience, that God is real, that He listens, and that He acts.

Henry often told his congregation about the remarkable answers to prayer he had witnessed in his own household and in the homes of his people. A child healed after the family prayed together. Provision that arrived on the very day it was needed. Guidance that came clearly after a season of family intercession. These testimonies, shared at the family altar, built the faith of the entire household and created a living tradition of prayer that passed from generation to generation.

Let us take Henry's counsel to heart. Pray together tonight — sincerely, briefly, inclusively, and with faith. And watch what God does in your family.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Jesus says our Father knows what we need before we ask Him. If that is true, why does He still want us to pray?

Question 2: Henry says children should be included in family prayer from an early age. What is one way we could involve every member of our family more fully in our prayer times?

Question 3: Can anyone in our family share a time when God clearly answered a prayer? How did that experience affect your faith?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much." -- James 5:16

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord God, we come to You as a family, bringing our real needs, our real struggles, and our real thanksgivings. Hear us, Father. Answer us according to Your great mercy. Teach our children to pray. Strengthen our faith. And let this household be known in heaven as a house of prayer. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
