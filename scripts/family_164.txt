PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 3
"The Wife's Noble Calling"
William Gouge — Domestical Duties

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we continue with William Gouge and explore his teaching on the wife's calling within the Christian household. Let us come before the Lord with open hearts.

Let us pray. Gracious God, we come before You tonight as a family seeking wisdom. We ask You to help us receive Your Word with humility and gladness. Teach us to see the beauty and dignity in every calling You have ordained. May Your Spirit guide our conversation and unite our hearts. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Proverbs 31:10-12, 25-28. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies. The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her, so that he shall have no need of spoil. She will do him good and not evil all the days of her life. Strength and honour are her clothing; and she shall rejoice in time to come. She openeth her mouth with wisdom; and in her tongue is the law of kindness. She looketh well to the ways of her household, and eateth not the bread of idleness. Her children arise up, and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praiseth her."

That is Proverbs 31:10-12, 25-28. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Having addressed the duties of husbands, William Gouge turns to the calling of wives with equal care and pastoral sensitivity. And here we must set aside modern caricatures of what the Puritans believed about women. Far from viewing women as inferior or insignificant, Gouge and his fellow Puritans held the wife and mother in the highest esteem. They saw her calling as noble, essential, and worthy of the deepest respect.

Gouge begins by establishing the wife's dignity. She is created in the image of God, redeemed by the blood of Christ, and indwelt by the Holy Spirit. She is an heir together with her husband of the grace of life. She is not a servant or a slave but a companion, a helpmeet, one who stands beside her husband as his closest counselor and dearest friend. Gouge draws from Genesis 2, noting that God created the woman not from Adam's head, to rule over him, nor from his feet, to be trampled by him, but from his side, to walk beside him, near his heart, to be loved by him.

The virtuous woman of Proverbs 31 is Gouge's model. She is a woman of industry, wisdom, generosity, and spiritual depth. She is not idle but active, not foolish but prudent, not selfish but generous, not anxious but confident in the Lord. "Strength and honour are her clothing; and she shall rejoice in time to come." Her strength is not the world's strength — it is the quiet, unshakeable strength that comes from trusting in God. Her honor is not the world's honor — it is the honor that comes from faithful service to her family and her Lord.

Gouge identifies several duties of the wife. The first is reverence toward her husband. This is not servile fear but respectful regard — honoring the office God has given him, supporting his leadership, and encouraging him in his duties. Gouge is careful to note that this reverence is rendered "in the Lord" — that is, it flows from the wife's relationship with Christ, not from the husband's personal merit. A wife reverences her husband not because he is perfect but because God has placed them together in a relationship that reflects the great mystery of Christ and the church.

The second duty is partnership. The Puritan wife was not a passive figure in the background of household life. She was an active partner in the management of the home, the education of children, the hospitality of the household, and the spiritual life of the family. Gouge describes the wife as the husband's "yoke-fellow" — sharing the burdens, the decisions, and the work of building a godly home. In practical terms, the Proverbs 31 woman buys and sells, plants and harvests, manages servants and business affairs, and opens her hand to the poor. She is competent, capable, and fully engaged in the life of her household.

The third duty is the education and nurture of children. Gouge writes at length about the mother's unique influence on her children, particularly in their earliest years. While both parents share the responsibility of instruction, the mother's daily presence, her tender words, her patient corrections, and her example of faith leave an indelible mark on the hearts of her children. "Her children arise up, and call her blessed" — not because she gave them everything they wanted, but because she gave them what they needed most: the knowledge of God, the discipline of love, and the model of a life lived in faith.

Gouge does not romanticize the wife's calling. He acknowledges that it is demanding, often thankless, and filled with difficulties. But he insists that it is a calling of the highest order — a calling that shapes souls, builds the church, and glorifies God. The woman who pours herself into the sacred work of the home is doing eternal work, whether the world recognizes it or not.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: The Proverbs 31 woman is described as clothed in "strength and honour." What does this kind of strength look like in everyday life? How is it different from the world's idea of strength?

Question 2: Gouge calls the wife her husband's "yoke-fellow." What does this word picture suggest about the nature of marriage? How do partners share the work of building a home?

Question 3: Why does Gouge consider the mother's role in educating children so important? What are some things only a mother's daily presence and example can teach?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Strength and honour are her clothing; and she shall rejoice in time to come." -- Proverbs 31:25

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for the gift of mothers and wives. We thank You for the noble calling You have given to women in the household of faith. Strengthen every woman in this family for the work You have set before her. Give her wisdom, patience, joy, and the quiet confidence that comes from trusting in You. Crown her labors with Your blessing. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
