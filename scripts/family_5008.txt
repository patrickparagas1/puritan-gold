PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 8
"God's Providence Over Your Family"
John Flavel — Providence and Family Trust

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to a new week of Puritan Gold. This week we sit at the feet of John Flavel, one of the warmest and most beloved Puritan pastors, as he teaches us about the providence of God and how it applies to our family's journey of trust. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Sovereign Lord, we confess that we do not always understand Your ways. But we trust that You are working all things together for our good. Open our eyes this week to see Your providential hand in every detail of our family's life. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Romans 8:28. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose."

That is Romans 8:28. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

John Flavel was a pastor in Dartmouth, England, during one of the most tumultuous periods in English history. He was ejected from his pulpit under the Act of Uniformity in 1662, forced to preach in secret, and endured years of persecution, poverty, and personal loss. He buried two wives and knew the sharp edge of grief as well as any man of his generation. And yet, through it all, Flavel became one of the most eloquent and passionate defenders of the doctrine of divine providence in all of Christian literature.

Providence is the teaching that God governs all events in His creation — from the rise and fall of empires to the falling of a sparrow — with infinite wisdom, perfect justice, and tender love. Nothing happens by chance. Nothing escapes God's notice. Nothing falls outside His sovereign control. Flavel devoted an entire treatise to this subject, and it remains one of the most comforting and challenging works in the Puritan library.

For families, the doctrine of providence is like a strong anchor in stormy seas. Life is unpredictable. Plans fail. Health falters. Jobs are lost. Relationships strain. Children face difficulties that parents cannot fix. In such moments, the family that understands providence has a resource that the world does not: the unshakable conviction that God is in control and that He is working all things — including the painful things — for their ultimate good.

Flavel distinguished between what he called "general providence" and "special providence." General providence refers to God's governance of the entire created order — the seasons, the weather, the laws of nature. Special providence refers to God's particular care for His children — the specific events, circumstances, and encounters that shape their lives in ways that advance His purposes.

For your family tonight, consider the special providences you have experienced. Think back over your family's history. Can you see moments when God directed your steps in ways you did not expect? A job loss that led to a better opportunity? An illness that drew the family closer together? A move to a new city that brought you to a church where your faith was strengthened? Flavel would say that none of these events were accidents. They were all part of God's providential plan for your family.

But Flavel was also honest about the difficulty of trusting providence in the midst of pain. He acknowledged that many of God's providences are mysterious. They do not make sense at the time. They seem to contradict God's promises. They feel more like punishment than love. In such seasons, Flavel counseled families to walk by faith, not by sight. He urged them to trust the character of God even when they could not trace His hand. He reminded them that a good God does not cease to be good simply because His children cannot understand what He is doing.

Flavel used a beautiful image: he compared God's providence to a piece of embroidery. On the underside, the threads seem tangled and purposeless — a mess of loose ends and knots. But when you turn the fabric over, you see the beautiful design that the artisan intended all along. In this life, we see the underside. We see the tangles and the knots. But one day, in heaven, we will turn the fabric over and see the breathtaking design that God was creating through every trial, every loss, every unexpected turn in the road.

Tonight, begin to cultivate in your family a habit of noticing God's providence. At the dinner table, at bedtime, on your drives together, ask one another: "Where did we see God at work today?" This simple practice can transform the way your family experiences daily life. Instead of seeing random events, you begin to see a loving Father arranging every detail for your good and His glory.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: What is providence? How is it different from luck or chance?

Question 2: Can you think of a time in our family's history when something that seemed bad at the time turned out to be part of God's good plan for us?

Question 3: Flavel compared providence to embroidery — tangled on one side, beautiful on the other. How does this image help you trust God when things do not make sense?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose." -- Romans 8:28

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Sovereign Father, we thank You that nothing in our family's life is random or purposeless. You hold every detail in Your wise and loving hands. Help us to trust Your providence, even when we cannot understand it. Give us eyes to see Your hand at work in every circumstance. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that the God who governs the stars also governs the smallest details of your family's story.
