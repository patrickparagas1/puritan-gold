PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Doctrine Deep-Dive, Day 26
"The New Heavens and New Earth"
Eschatological Hope — The Renewal of All Things

---

Good evening, friend. Tonight we lift our eyes to the horizon of history itself and consider one of the most glorious promises in all of Scripture: the new heavens and the new earth. The Puritans were not escapists who longed merely to flee this world. They were pilgrims who knew that this world itself would one day be made new. Let us explore this breathtaking hope together.

--- Scripture ---

Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Revelation 21:1-4:

"And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea. And I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God. And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.

--- Doctrine Study ---

The doctrine of the new heavens and new earth teaches that the final destiny of the redeemed is not a disembodied existence in some ethereal realm but a renewed, glorified, physical creation in which God dwells with His people forever. This is not the destruction of the world but its transformation — its liberation from the curse of sin and its restoration to the glory God always intended for it.

The apostle Peter writes, "Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness" (2 Peter 3:13). The Puritans seized upon that final phrase: "wherein dwelleth righteousness." In this present world, righteousness is a stranger, an exile, a pilgrim. In the new creation, righteousness will be at home. Sin will not merely be restrained; it will be absent. Evil will not merely be punished; it will be eradicated. The world that God originally pronounced "very good" will be restored to a goodness that exceeds even Eden, for it will be a goodness that can never be lost.

Thomas Watson reflected on the continuity between this present creation and the one to come. He argued that just as the believer's body will be raised and glorified — not replaced with a different body but transformed into something infinitely better — so the creation itself will be raised and glorified. The same earth that has groaned under the curse will one day rejoice under the blessing. The same skies that have witnessed centuries of human wickedness will one day shine with the undimmed glory of God. Paul teaches in Romans 8:21 that the creation itself shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God.

Stephen Charnock developed the theological significance of God's promise to dwell with His people. In Eden, God walked with man in the cool of the day. In the tabernacle and temple, God's presence was mediated through symbols, rituals, and a heavy curtain. In the incarnation, the Word became flesh and dwelt among us. But in the new creation, all mediating structures will be removed. There will be no temple, John tells us, for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple. The presence of God will be immediate, unbroken, and all-encompassing. Every moment will be communion. Every breath will be worship.

The Puritans also emphasized what will be absent from the new creation. John's vision catalogs the things that will be no more: death, sorrow, crying, pain. These are not minor details. They are the sum total of everything that makes this present life so hard. Every tear that has ever fallen from a human eye will be wiped away — not by the passage of time or the fading of memory but by the hand of God Himself. Richard Sibbes wrote that heaven is not merely the absence of sorrow but the presence of a joy so complete that sorrow becomes impossible.

The practical effect of this doctrine upon the Puritans was not otherworldly passivity but present-tense faithfulness. Because they knew that this world was headed for renewal, not annihilation, they took their earthly callings seriously. They worked, they built, they planted, they governed — all with the knowledge that their labor was not in vain. Paul concludes his great resurrection chapter with these words: "Therefore, my beloved brethren, be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord" (1 Corinthians 15:58). The hope of the new creation does not diminish the value of present labor; it guarantees it.

--- Application ---

First, cultivate a habit of thinking about the new creation. When you see beauty in this world — a sunset, a mountain, the face of someone you love — let it remind you that this beauty is only a foretaste. The best of this world is the worst of the next.

Second, let the hope of the new creation comfort you in present suffering. Whatever you are enduring right now is temporary. The sorrow will end. The pain will cease. The tears will be wiped away. Hold on, friend. The renewal is coming.

Third, let this hope motivate your faithfulness in ordinary callings. Your work matters. Your relationships matter. Your small acts of obedience matter. Nothing done for the Lord in this life will be wasted in the life to come.

--- Closing Prayer ---

O God of all hope, we thank Thee for the promise of a new heavens and a new earth wherein dwelleth righteousness. When this present world weighs heavy upon us, lift our eyes to the glory that is coming. Give us patience in suffering, faithfulness in labor, and an unshakeable confidence that the best is yet to come. Come, Lord Jesus. Make all things new. Amen.

Sleep in hope tonight, friend. The dawn of the new creation is closer than you think.
