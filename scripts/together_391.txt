PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 30
"Domestical Duties — William Gouge"
Puritan Marriage Wisdom — William Gouge's Vision for the Household

---

Welcome, dear couple. Tonight we sit at the feet of William Gouge (1575-1653), whose massive work Of Domestical Duties remains one of the most comprehensive treatments of family life in the English language. Gouge was pastor of St. Anne Blackfriars in London for over forty years, and his counsel was forged in decades of pastoral care for real families with real problems.

Let us read Ephesians 5:21-25 (KJV):

"Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God. Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord. For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body. Therefore as the church is subject unto Christ, so let the wives be to their own husbands in every thing. Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it."

--- Teaching ---

William Gouge preached through Ephesians over the course of several years, and his sermons on the household passages became the foundation for Of Domestical Duties. What makes Gouge remarkable is his balance. In an age when many reduced Ephesians 5 to a single command — wives, submit — Gouge insisted on reading the entire passage, giving equal weight to the husband's duty to love sacrificially and the mutual duty to submit to one another in the fear of God.

Gouge identified what he called "the three bonds of marriage." The first bond is the bond of covenant. Marriage is not merely a social arrangement or a romantic partnership. It is a covenant — a solemn, binding, sacred agreement made before God and witnesses. Gouge wrote that the covenant nature of marriage means that feelings alone do not sustain it. There will be days when you do not feel like loving your spouse. There will be seasons when romance is distant and duty is all you have. In those seasons, the covenant holds. It is the steel frame beneath the beautiful exterior. Without it, the house collapses at the first storm.

The second bond is the bond of companionship. Gouge placed enormous emphasis on friendship between husband and wife. He wrote that many marriages fail not because of great sins but because of great boredom — the slow erosion of interest, conversation, and shared life. He urged couples to be each other's "chief companions" — to prefer each other's company above all others, to seek each other out for conversation, and to find genuine pleasure in simply being together.

The third bond is the bond of physical union. Gouge was remarkably frank about the physical dimension of marriage. He insisted that both partners have equal rights and responsibilities in this area. He rejected the notion that one partner could use physical intimacy as a bargaining chip or a tool of control. He wrote that "the bodies of married persons belong mutually to each other" and that withholding physical affection is a form of fraud. This was radical in his day and remains important in ours.

Gouge also wrote extensively about the "common duties" that both husband and wife share equally. These include: the duty of love — expressed in words, actions, and attitudes; the duty of faithfulness — absolute exclusivity in body, mind, and heart; the duty of helpfulness — assisting each other in all aspects of life, both practical and spiritual; the duty of respect — treating each other with honor, never speaking dismissively or contemptuously; and the duty of forbearance — bearing with each other's weaknesses patiently, as Christ bears with ours.

On the subject of husband's duties specifically, Gouge was emphatic. He wrote that leadership in the home is not domination but service. The husband who leads like Christ leads with self-sacrifice, not self-assertion. He lays down his preferences for his wife's flourishing. He consults her wisdom before making decisions. He protects her dignity at all costs. Gouge warned that the husband who uses the language of headship to justify selfishness has "turned the grace of God into a license for tyranny."

On the subject of the wife's role, Gouge was equally nuanced. He wrote that the wife's submission is not slavish obedience but willing, intelligent, active partnership. She is the husband's adviser, his helper, his complement. She brings gifts and perspectives that he lacks. Her submission is modeled not on a servant before a master but on the church before Christ — a relationship of trust, love, and mutual devotion.

Gouge's greatest contribution may be his insistence that marriage is a school — a daily classroom where both partners are simultaneously teachers and students. In marriage, you learn patience by living with an imperfect person. You learn sacrifice by putting someone else's needs before your own. You learn forgiveness by needing it and giving it, day after day. Marriage, for Gouge, was not merely a gift to enjoy but a discipline to master — and the mastery takes a lifetime.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Turn to each other and consider these questions.

Question 1: Of Gouge's three bonds — covenant, companionship, and physical union — which is strongest in your marriage? Which needs the most attention?

Question 2: Gouge says that marriage is a school. What is your marriage currently teaching you? What lesson has been hardest to learn?

Question 3: Gouge insists on mutual respect and forbearance. Is there an area where you have been disrespectful or impatient with your spouse — even subtly? Can you acknowledge it tonight?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray over the three bonds of your marriage — covenant, companionship, and physical union. Ask God to strengthen each one. Pray that your marriage would be a school of grace where both of you grow in Christ-likeness through the daily discipline of loving each other well.

--- Closing ---

William Gouge served one congregation for over forty years, and his faithfulness in that small sphere changed the world. May your faithfulness in the small sphere of your marriage do the same. Goodnight, dear couple.
