PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 26
"The Godly Person Shows Charity"
Thomas Watson — The Godly Man's Picture


Hello and welcome to our family devotional. Come, young and old alike. God's Word has something for every member of this family tonight. Let us draw near to Him.

Shall we pray? Our Father, we gather tonight hungry for Your truth. We know that man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from Your mouth. Feed us now, Lord. Bless our family time together. In the name of Christ we pray. Amen.

God has preserved His Word for thousands of years so that families like ours could hear it tonight. Let us read from 1 John 3:17-18:

"But whoso hath this world's good, and seeth his brother have need, and shutteth up his bowels of compassion from him, how dwelleth the love of God in him? My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth."

That is 1 John 3:17-18. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Today Thomas Watson paints the next stroke in his portrait of the godly person: charity, which in biblical language means practical, generous love for others. The Apostle John writes with searching directness in 1 John 3:17-18.

'But whoso hath this world's good, and seeth his brother have need, and shutteth up his bowels of compassion from him, how dwelleth the love of God in him?' John is asking a devastating question. If you have the means to help someone in need and you choose not to, can you really claim that God's love is in you? The answer, clearly, is no.

The phrase 'shutteth up his bowels of compassion' is vivid. In the ancient world, the bowels (intestines, the gut) were considered the seat of deep emotion — much like we say 'my heart goes out to you.' To shut up your compassion is to see suffering and deliberately close your heart to it. It is to look away, to cross to the other side of the road, to find excuses for not helping.

Then John gives the positive command: 'My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth.' Love must not stay in the realm of words and feelings. It must move to deeds and truth. Saying 'I will pray for you' to a hungry person without offering food is empty love. James makes the same point in James 2:15-16: 'If a brother or sister be naked, and destitute of daily food, And one of you say unto them, Depart in peace, be ye warmed and filled; notwithstanding ye give them not those things which are needful to the body; what doth it profit?'

Jesus taught this supremely in the parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:30-37). A man lay beaten and bleeding by the roadside. A priest walked by — he saw the man and passed by on the other side. A Levite did the same. But a Samaritan — despised by the Jews — stopped, bandaged the man's wounds, took him to an inn, and paid for his care. Jesus asked, 'Which now of these three, thinkest thou, was neighbour unto him that fell among the thieves?' The answer was obvious: 'He that shewed mercy on him.' And Jesus said, 'Go, and do thou likewise.'

In Matthew 25:35-40, Jesus gives one of His most sobering teachings. At the final judgment, the King will say to the righteous, 'For I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat: I was thirsty, and ye gave me drink: I was a stranger, and ye took me in: Naked, and ye clothed me: I was sick, and ye visited me: I was in prison, and ye came unto me.' When they ask, 'Lord, when did we do these things for you?' He answers, 'Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.' Every act of compassion toward a person in need is an act of service to Christ Himself.

Thomas Watson wrote that the godly person is full of charity. His heart is not a frozen sea but a flowing fountain. He does not hoard his mercies but distributes them. His hand is the channel through which God's bounty flows to the needy.

Watson identified several marks of true charity. First, it is willing — not given grudgingly or under compulsion, but cheerfully. 'God loveth a cheerful giver' (2 Corinthians 9:7). Second, it is proportionate — we give according to what we have, not beyond what we can. Third, it is indiscriminate — we help those in need regardless of whether they are from our group, our church, or our social circle. Fourth, it is quiet — true charity does not seek praise or recognition. Jesus said, 'Let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doeth' (Matthew 6:3).

Watson also warned against the opposite error — stinginess disguised as prudence. Some people convince themselves they cannot afford to be generous, when in reality they simply do not want to be. Watson reminded his readers that everything we have comes from God, and we are stewards, not owners. When we give generously, we are simply returning to God what was already His.

For our youngest: God loves it when we share with others, especially with people who need help. Even a small act of kindness makes God happy! For older children: look around this week. Is there someone in your school, neighborhood, or church who needs help? What could you do? For the whole family: let us find one concrete way to show charity this week — not just in words but in deeds.


There is much we can learn from those who walked with God before us. Consider these words.

Thomas Watson, in his work "The Godly Man's Picture," wrote these words:

"The godly man is full of charity. His heart is not a frozen sea but a flowing fountain. He does not hoard his mercies but distributes them. His hand is the channel through which God's bounty flows to the needy."

What Thomas Watson is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in 1 John 3:17-18 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Family, let us talk about what we have learned. Consider these questions together.

Question 1: What does it mean to love 'in deed and in truth' instead of just in words?

Question 2: In the parable of the Good Samaritan, why do you think the priest and the Levite walked past the hurt man?

Question 3: Jesus says that when we help someone in need, it is like helping Him. How does that change the way we think about serving others?

Question 4: What is one way our family can show real, practical love to someone in need this week?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth." -- 1 John 3:18

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Learning sticks better when we do something with it. Try this activity as a family:

As a family, assemble a 'blessing bag' — a small bag filled with useful items like snacks, socks, a water bottle, and a note of encouragement. Keep it in the car to give to someone in need when you see them. Or choose a charity project to do together, like preparing a meal for a neighbor.


Lord, as we close tonight's devotional, we place our family in Your hands. Watch over us as we sleep. Guard our dreams. Prepare our hearts for tomorrow. May the truths from 1 John 3:17-18 follow us into the new day. We love You, Lord. Amen.

Goodnight. Remember your memory verse, and carry it with you tomorrow.
