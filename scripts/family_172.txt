PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 11
"Piety in the Daily Round"
John Angell James — Female Piety


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight James shows us that true godliness is not reserved for Sundays and special occasions — it fills the ordinary hours of daily life with sacred purpose. Let us draw near to God.

Let us pray. Lord, we confess that we sometimes think of godliness as something separate from our daily tasks. Teach us tonight that every duty, however small, can be done for Your glory. Help us to see the sacred in the ordinary. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Colossians 3:17, 23-24. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"And whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God and the Father by him. And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men; Knowing that of the Lord ye shall receive the reward of the inheritance: for ye serve the Lord Christ."

That is Colossians 3:17, 23-24. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


One of the most valuable features of John Angell James's "Female Piety" is his insistence that godliness is not a Sunday-only affair. True piety, he writes, permeates every corner of daily life. It is found not only in the prayer closet and the pew but in the kitchen and the market, in the nursery and the sickroom, in the conversation at table and the quiet labor of the afternoon.

James draws on Paul's sweeping command in Colossians 3: "Whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus." The scope of this command is breathtaking. Not some things. Not religious things. Whatsoever. Every word, every deed, every task, every interaction — all of it is to be done in the name of Jesus, as an act of worship and thanksgiving. This is the Puritan vision of the sanctified life, and it applies to every member of the household, but James gives it particular application to the daily experience of women.

Consider the daily round of a mother's life. She rises early, prepares the household, feeds and dresses children, teaches, cleans, manages, counsels, comforts, corrects, plans, purchases, mends, and hosts — and by the end of the day, she may feel that she has done nothing of significance. The world certainly does not honor these tasks. They do not make headlines. They earn no awards. They produce no visible monuments. But James insists that these are among the most significant labors a human being can perform, because they are done in service of the most significant institution God ever created — the family.

James writes that the key to transforming mundane duties into acts of worship is the motive of the heart. A meal prepared with gratitude and offered to God's glory is an act of worship as real as any prayer spoken in a cathedral. A child's wound bandaged with tenderness and silent prayer is a ministry as sacred as any performed by a minister of the gospel. A floor swept with a cheerful heart and a consciousness of God's presence is ground as holy as any church sanctuary. Brother Lawrence, the seventeenth-century monk, spoke of "practicing the presence of God" in the kitchen, and James commends the same practice to every Christian woman and to every member of the household.

James also speaks about the sanctification of suffering in daily life. He recognizes that the daily round often includes not just ordinary duties but ordinary difficulties — the irritation of a child's disobedience, the frustration of plans that go awry, the exhaustion of sleepless nights, the loneliness of unrecognized labor. These are the small crosses of daily life, and James urges believers to bear them as Christ bore His cross — with patience, with trust, and with the knowledge that God is using every trial to refine and purify the soul.

Paul adds the great motivation: "Knowing that of the Lord ye shall receive the reward of the inheritance: for ye serve the Lord Christ." When you serve your family, you are serving the Lord Christ. When you wash dishes, you wash them for Christ. When you help with homework, you help Christ. When you listen patiently to a child's long and rambling story, you listen to Christ. This is not pious exaggeration — it is the plain teaching of Scripture. Jesus said, "Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me."

James concludes with an encouragement that has sustained generations of faithful women and men: the most powerful lives are often the most hidden. The world measures greatness by visibility, by fame, by the size of one's platform. But God measures greatness by faithfulness, by love, by the quality of the heart. The most influential person in a household may well be the one who is never on a stage, never in a spotlight, never applauded by crowds — but who day after day, year after year, faithfully serves, quietly prays, and steadily points her family toward Christ.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Paul says, "Whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord." How does thinking of our daily chores as service to Christ change the way we approach them?

Question 2: James says the most powerful lives are often the most hidden. Do you agree? Can you think of someone whose quiet faithfulness has had a great impact?

Question 3: What is one ordinary task in our family routine that we could begin to do more intentionally "as to the Lord" this week?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men." -- Colossians 3:23

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord Jesus, forgive us for despising the small and ordinary duties of daily life. Open our eyes to see that every task done for love of You is sacred. Help us to serve our family with cheerfulness, with gratitude, and with the consciousness that we serve You. Transform our daily round into daily worship. In Your holy name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
