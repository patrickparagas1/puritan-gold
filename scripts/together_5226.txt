PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 26
"Her Children Rise and Call Her Blessed"
Proverbs 31:28

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we come to one of the most moving verses in the Proverbs 31 portrait, a verse that speaks to the legacy a godly wife and mother leaves in the hearts of her children and her husband. This is not merely about receiving praise but about the kind of life that naturally and inevitably calls forth gratitude from those who have been blessed by it.

Let us read our passage. Proverbs 31:28-29 (KJV):

"Her children arise up, and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praiseth her. Many daughters have done virtuously, but thou excellest them all."

--- Teaching ---

There is something deeply instructive about the order of events in this verse. The children rise up and call her blessed. The husband praises her. This praise is not extracted or demanded. It flows naturally from hearts that have been shaped by her faithful love over many years. The children who call their mother blessed are children who have experienced, day after day, her wisdom, her patience, her sacrifice, and her unwavering devotion. The husband who praises her is a man who has watched her faithfulness unfold across the seasons of their life together and has come to recognize, with increasing wonder, the magnitude of what God has given him.

Richard Baxter, in his writings on the family, made a striking observation about the nature of a mother's influence. He noted that a mother's character is imprinted upon her children more deeply than almost any other human influence. Children may forget their mother's specific words, but they will never forget her spirit. They will carry with them, for the rest of their lives, the memory of whether their home was a place of peace or turmoil, whether their mother's face was marked by joy or by bitterness, whether she pointed them toward Christ or away from Him. The mother who lives in the fear of God plants seeds in her children's hearts that will bear fruit for generations.

William Gouge emphasized that the husband's praise of his wife is not optional but obligatory. It is a duty, he argued, for the husband to recognize and honor the labors of his wife, especially in the raising of children. Too many husbands, Gouge lamented, take their wife's sacrifices for granted. They notice when something goes wrong but fail to acknowledge the countless things that go right because of her diligent and loving care. The husband who praises his wife publicly and privately strengthens her hands for the work God has given her and models for his children the honor that is due to their mother.

Thomas Watson added that the praise described in this verse is not mere flattery but genuine recognition of godly character. The husband does not say to his wife, "Thou art beautiful" or "Thou art clever," though she may be both. He says, "Many daughters have done virtuously, but thou excellest them all." This is praise rooted in moral and spiritual admiration. It honors not her appearance or her accomplishments but her character, her fear of God, her faithfulness in the ordinary and extraordinary duties of life.

Dear husband, when was the last time you praised your wife, not for what she does but for who she is? When was the last time you spoke words of admiration that went beyond her cooking or her appearance and touched on her character, her faith, her steadfastness? Tonight is an opportunity to speak that praise aloud.

Dear wife, know that your labors are seen, even when they feel invisible. God sees every sacrifice, every prayer whispered over a sleeping child, every tear shed in secret. And the fruit of your faithfulness will rise up, in God's time, and call you blessed.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: Husband, take a moment tonight to offer your wife specific, heartfelt praise. What aspects of her character and faithfulness do you most admire?

Question 2: Wife, how does it affect you when your husband expresses genuine admiration? What kind of praise means the most to you?

Question 3: If you have children, how are you both modeling for them the practice of honoring and praising one another?

--- Prayer ---

Heavenly Father, we thank Thee for the gift of motherhood and for the faithful labors of every wife who pours herself out for her family. Open the husband's eyes to see what Thou seest, and loosen his tongue to speak the praise that his wife deserves. May the children of this home learn, by the example of both parents, to honor those who have loved them well. And may this family be a place where faithfulness is recognized, gratitude is freely expressed, and praise ascends to Thee, the giver of every good gift. Through Christ our Lord, Amen.
