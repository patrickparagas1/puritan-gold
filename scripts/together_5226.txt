PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 26
"Her Children Rise and Call Her Blessed"
Proverbs 31:28-29 — The Virtuous Wife

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we come to one of the most tender and moving verses in Proverbs 31. It describes a moment that every godly wife and mother longs for: the day when those she has served recognize her worth and rise to honor her.

Let us read our passage. Proverbs 31:28-29 (KJV):

"Her children arise up, and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praiseth her. Many daughters have done virtuously, but thou excellest them all."

--- Teaching ---

Picture the scene that Solomon paints. A woman has spent her years in faithful, often unnoticed service. She has risen early and gone to bed late. She has fed, clothed, taught, corrected, comforted, and prayed for her family day after day, year after year. Much of her work has been invisible. No one handed her an award for the meals she cooked, the tears she dried, the late-night prayers she offered on her knees. But now, in this beautiful moment, her children rise up. They stand. They open their mouths. And they call her blessed.

This is not flattery. This is recognition. The children see clearly what they may have taken for granted in their youth: their mother's faithfulness was the very foundation of their lives. Every good thing they have, every virtue they possess, every strength they carry into the world can be traced back in some measure to the woman who loved them with tireless devotion. And they are moved to honor her.

But notice that the children are not alone in their praise. "Her husband also, and he praiseth her." The husband, who has been the closest witness to her daily faithfulness, adds his voice. He does not simply agree with the children's assessment. He goes further: "Many daughters have done virtuously, but thou excellest them all." This is not a careless compliment. This is a deliberate, public, passionate declaration that his wife is the most excellent woman he has ever known.

William Gouge drew a challenging application from this verse for husbands. He asked: when was the last time you praised your wife, not in private only, but in the presence of your children and others? Gouge observed that many husbands are quick to criticize their wives before others but slow to praise them. This is a grave injustice. The husband who speaks well of his wife in public is giving her a gift that no amount of private affection can replace. It is one thing to tell your wife you love her when the two of you are alone. It is another thing entirely to tell your children, your friends, your church, and anyone who will listen that your wife is the most excellent woman you know.

Thomas Gataker noted that the praise described in Proverbs 31 is earned over a lifetime. The virtuous wife does not seek praise; she seeks faithfulness. She does not perform for applause; she serves for the glory of God. But the beautiful irony is that the woman who does not seek praise is the woman who most deserves it. Her virtue is proven not by a single moment of brilliance but by decades of quiet consistency. And when that praise finally comes, from the mouths of grown children who now understand what she sacrificed, it is the sweetest sound in all the earth.

Richard Baxter counseled wives not to grow discouraged if the praise is slow in coming. Some children do not recognize their mother's worth until they have children of their own. Some husbands do not fully appreciate their wife's faithfulness until a crisis reveals how much they depend on her. But God sees everything. He has been watching your faithfulness since the day you said "I do." And His "well done" will be worth more than all the praise of men.

For the husband, tonight is an opportunity. Do not wait until your wife's hair is gray to praise her. Do not wait until your children are grown to tell them how blessed they are to have such a mother. Speak now. Praise her tonight, in the hearing of your children if they are present, and if not, then in the hearing of God who records every word. Tell her what she means to you. Tell her that among all the women in the world, she excels them all in your eyes.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: Husband, when was the last time you publicly praised your wife, in front of your children, your family, or your friends?

Question 2: Wife, do you feel that your daily faithfulness is seen and appreciated? What kind of recognition means the most to you?

Question 3: How can we build a culture in our home where the contributions of each family member are regularly acknowledged and celebrated?

--- Prayer ---

Father of all mercy, we thank Thee for the faithful wife and mother whose daily devotion builds the very foundation of our home. Forgive us for the times we have taken her for granted. Open the eyes of this husband to see her worth and open his mouth to praise her. Let her children rise and call her blessed, not merely one day in the future, but today and every day. May our home be a place where faithfulness is recognized, virtue is celebrated, and praise is freely given. In the name of Jesus Christ, Amen.
