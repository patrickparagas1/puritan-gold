PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"The Earth Is the Lord's"
May 4, 2026

---

"The earth is the Lord's, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein. For he hath founded it upon the seas, and established it upon the floods. Who shall ascend into the hill of the Lord? or who shall stand in his holy place? He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart; who hath not lifted up his soul unto vanity, nor sworn deceitfully." -- Psalm 24:1-4

This magnificent psalm opens with a declaration so vast in its scope that the mind of man can scarcely contain it. "The earth is the Lord's, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein." Every grain of sand upon the seashore, every star that burns in the firmament, every creature that moves upon the face of the earth, every soul that draws breath beneath the canopy of heaven, all belong to God by the most absolute and incontestable right. He is the Maker of all things, and therefore He is the Owner of all things. The gold in the mountains is His. The silver in the streams is His. The cattle upon a thousand hills are His. And every man, woman, and child who has ever lived, or who shall ever live, belongs to Him by right of creation, whether they acknowledge His lordship or not.

This truth, which seems so simple when stated, is in reality one of the most revolutionary and far-reaching doctrines in all of Scripture. For if the earth is the Lord's, then no man is truly the absolute owner of anything. We are stewards, not proprietors. We are tenants, not landlords. Every good thing we possess, from the bread upon our tables to the breath in our lungs, is a loan from the hand of the Almighty, and we shall one day give an account of how we have used what He entrusted to us. The Puritan Richard Baxter was fond of reminding his congregation that a man's relationship to his possessions reveals more about the state of his soul than almost anything else. The man who holds his goods with an open hand, knowing that they belong to God and are to be used for God's glory, gives evidence of a heart that has been renewed by grace. But the man who clutches his possessions with a tight fist, as though they were his by some absolute right, gives evidence of a heart that has not yet learned the first lesson of the gospel.

But the psalm does not merely declare God's ownership of the earth. It moves swiftly to a searching question: "Who shall ascend into the hill of the Lord? or who shall stand in his holy place?" If God is the sovereign Lord of all creation, then the question naturally arises: Who is fit to come into His presence? Who is worthy to stand before this majestic Creator and worship Him? The hill of the Lord is Mount Zion, the place of the temple, the dwelling place of God among His people. To ascend that hill was the highest privilege any Israelite could enjoy. But it was not a privilege to be claimed carelessly or presumed upon lightly. There were qualifications for entering the presence of the Holy One, and they were not qualifications that any fallen son of Adam could meet in his own strength.

"He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart; who hath not lifted up his soul unto vanity, nor sworn deceitfully." Here are the four marks of the man who may stand in God's holy place. Clean hands speak of outward conduct, a life free from the defilement of sinful actions. A pure heart speaks of inward character, a soul cleansed from the pollution of sinful desires and motives. Not lifting up the soul unto vanity speaks of right worship, a spirit that has not given itself to the adoration of false gods or empty idols. And not swearing deceitfully speaks of integrity, a tongue that does not make promises it never intends to keep or invoke the name of God to cover a lie.

Now, who among us can read these requirements and not tremble? Who has perfectly clean hands? Who possesses a truly pure heart? Who has never lifted up his soul unto vanity? Who has never spoken a deceitful word? If entrance into God's presence depends upon our meeting these standards in our own righteousness, then the gates of heaven are shut against every one of us, and the hill of the Lord is a mountain that no sinner can ever climb.

And here, beloved, is where the gospel shines forth with its most brilliant and comforting light. For there is One who has perfectly met every one of these requirements. There is One whose hands were without spot, whose heart was absolutely pure, whose soul was never given to vanity, whose lips never uttered a deceitful word. That One is the Lord Jesus Christ, the spotless Lamb of God. And He has ascended the hill of the Lord, not for Himself alone, but for all who trust in Him. He is the King of glory whom the psalmist describes in the closing verses of this psalm, the Lord strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in battle, who has passed through the gates of death and ascended to the right hand of the Father, there to intercede for His people and to prepare a place for them.

William Gurnall, that valiant soldier of Puritan theology, wrote that the believer's confidence in approaching God rests not upon any worthiness of his own, but entirely upon the worthiness of Christ. When we come before God in prayer, we come not in our own name, but in the name of Jesus. When we enter the sanctuary of worship, we enter not upon the ground of our own holiness, but upon the ground of His holiness imputed to us by faith. Our hands are made clean by His blood. Our hearts are made pure by His Spirit. Our souls are lifted above vanity by His grace. And our tongues are taught to speak truth by His Word.

The psalm concludes with a glorious antiphonal shout: "Lift up your heads, O ye gates; and be ye lift up, ye everlasting doors; and the King of glory shall come in." The early church fathers understood these words as a description of Christ's triumphant ascension into heaven, when the gates of glory were flung wide to receive the conquering King. But they also apply to every believing heart that opens itself to receive the Lord Jesus. For Christ does not merely ascend to a distant heaven. He comes to dwell in the hearts of His people by His Spirit. And every soul that opens the door to Him becomes a temple of the living God, a holy place where the King of glory takes up His eternal abode.

Let us therefore examine our hands and our hearts today. Let us not presume upon the grace of God, as though it were a license for careless living. But let us also not despair, as though the standard of holiness were beyond our reach. For the King of glory Himself is our righteousness, and in Him we stand accepted, forgiven, and fit to ascend the holy hill of God. Amen.
