PURITAN GOLD -- Study Topic: The Book of Melchizedek, Part 8 of 8
"King of Righteousness, King of Peace"
The Book of Melchizedek — The full typology fulfilled in Christ

---

Beloved friends, we have come to the final episode of our study on Melchizedek. Over the past seven sessions, we have traced this extraordinary figure from his sudden appearance in Genesis 14, through the prophetic oath of Psalm 110, through the detailed exposition of Hebrews 7, and through the rich reflections of the Puritan theologians. Today we gather up the threads of our study and weave them together into a single tapestry — a portrait of Christ as the true and eternal Melchizedek, the King of Righteousness and the King of Peace.

Let us read from Isaiah 9:6-7:

"For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace. Of the increase of his government and peace there shall be no end, upon the throne of David, and upon his kingdom, to order it, and to establish it with judgment and with righteousness from henceforth even for ever. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will perform this."

--- Teaching ---

In the ancient valley of Shaveh, after the slaughter of the kings, a figure emerged from the obscurity of Canaanite Salem bearing bread and wine — a king who was also a priest, a man whose name meant Righteousness and whose city meant Peace. He blessed the father of the faithful, received his tithes, and then disappeared from the narrative as suddenly as he had appeared. For over a thousand years, his name was not mentioned again in Scripture — until David, moved by the Holy Spirit, wrote in Psalm 110: "The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek."

And then another thousand years of silence. Generation after generation of Levitical priests served at the altar, offered their sacrifices, sprinkled the blood, entered the holy place — all of them mortal, all of them sinful, all of them temporary. The shadow priesthood continued its faithful but insufficient work, pointing always to something greater, something better, something that had not yet come.

And then He came.

In the fullness of time, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under the law (Galatians 4:4). He came from the tribe of Judah — not Levi. He came with no priestly genealogy, no Levitical credentials, no claim to the Aaronic succession. And yet He was the true High Priest — the one to whom every Levitical sacrifice had pointed, the one for whom every priestly garment had been designed, the one whose sacrifice would accomplish what ten thousand bulls and goats could never achieve.

Let us now trace the full typology of Melchizedek and see how every detail finds its fulfillment in Jesus Christ.

Melchizedek was king of Salem. Christ is the King of the New Jerusalem — the heavenly city that is above, which is the mother of us all (Galatians 4:26). His kingdom is not of this world (John 18:36), and yet it shall one day encompass all the kingdoms of the earth. "The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever" (Revelation 11:15).

Melchizedek was priest of the most high God. Christ is the great High Priest who has passed through the heavens (Hebrews 4:14). He entered not into a tabernacle made with hands but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us (Hebrews 9:24). His priesthood is exercised not in an earthly temple but at the very throne of the Almighty.

Melchizedek brought forth bread and wine. Christ instituted the Lord's Supper with bread and wine — the signs and seals of the new covenant in His blood. "This is my body, which is broken for you: this do in remembrance of me. This cup is the new testament in my blood: this do ye, as oft as ye drink it, in remembrance of me" (1 Corinthians 11:24-25). Every time we come to the Lord's Table, we participate in a meal that stretches back typologically to the valley of Shaveh and forward eschatologically to the marriage supper of the Lamb.

Melchizedek blessed Abraham. Christ blesses all who come to Him in faith. "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ" (Ephesians 1:3). The blessing of Melchizedek was a temporal benediction upon a victorious warrior; the blessing of Christ is an eternal benediction upon all who believe — justification, adoption, sanctification, glorification, and every spiritual gift that flows from union with Him.

Melchizedek received tithes from Abraham. Christ receives the worship and devotion of all the redeemed. As Abraham acknowledged Melchizedek's greatness by giving him a tenth of the spoils, so believers acknowledge Christ's lordship by offering Him their whole lives — "a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service" (Romans 12:1).

Melchizedek was "King of Righteousness." Christ is THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS (Jeremiah 23:6). He is the one who "knew no sin" yet was "made sin for us, that we might be made the righteousness of God in him" (2 Corinthians 5:21). The righteousness that Melchizedek's name merely signified, Christ actually provides. He is not merely called righteous; He is righteousness itself — and He imputes His righteousness to all who believe.

Melchizedek was "King of Peace." Christ is "our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us" (Ephesians 2:14). He made peace "through the blood of his cross" (Colossians 1:20). The peace that Salem merely symbolized, Christ actually accomplishes. He reconciles God and sinners. He reconciles Jew and Gentile. He gives a peace that the world cannot give and cannot take away (John 14:27).

Melchizedek was without recorded genealogy, without beginning of days, without end of life. Christ truly and literally has no beginning of days and no end of life. "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (John 1:1). He is "the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever" (Hebrews 13:8). What was typologically presented in Melchizedek is ontologically true of Christ.

Melchizedek abides a priest continually. Christ "ever liveth to make intercession" (Hebrews 7:25). His priestly ministry knows no interruption and no conclusion. At this very moment, He is interceding for you. He will be interceding for you tomorrow. He will be interceding for you in ten thousand years. He will be interceding for you when the stars have ceased to burn and the mountains have crumbled to dust. His priesthood is as eternal as His person.

John Owen, summarizing the entire typology, wrote that Melchizedek was "a signal representation of the Lord Christ in His kingly and priestly offices" — a figure designed by God from the foundation of the world to foreshadow the glory of the Redeemer. Every feature of the type was a ray of light pointing to the Sun; every attribute of Melchizedek was a whisper anticipating the voice of Christ.

Thomas Goodwin, with his characteristic warmth, urged believers to make personal application of the Melchizedek typology. It is not enough to know that Christ is a priest after the order of Melchizedek; you must come to Him as such. You must bring your sins to Him for cleansing. You must bring your sorrows to Him for comfort. You must bring your weakness to Him for strength. You must bring your whole life to Him for blessing. He is waiting, not reluctantly but eagerly — for He is a priest who delights in His office and a king who rejoices over His people.

And so we conclude where we began — in the valley of Shaveh, where a mysterious king-priest emerged from the shadows to bless the father of the faithful. But now we see more than Abraham saw. We see in Melchizedek the outline of a greater King, a greater Priest, a greater Blessing — one who is not merely a type but the very substance of all that God has promised. We see Jesus — the King of Righteousness and the King of Peace, our eternal High Priest, our sovereign Lord, our all-sufficient Savior.

--- Application ---

1. Come to Christ as your Melchizedek — your King and your Priest. Do not approach Him merely as a historical figure or a theological concept. Come to Him as a living person who exercises His offices on your behalf at this very moment.

2. Let the bread and wine of communion be a fresh encounter with your High Priest. When you eat the bread and drink the cup, remember that you are participating in a mystery that stretches from Melchizedek to Calvary to the marriage supper of the Lamb.

3. Offer your whole life as an act of worship to Christ. As Abraham gave tithes to Melchizedek, so give yourself — your time, your talents, your treasures, your very being — to the One who is infinitely greater than Abraham's Melchizedek.

4. Rest in the completed typology. Everything that Melchizedek foreshadowed, Christ has fulfilled. There is nothing left to be accomplished, nothing left to be added, nothing left to be desired. In Christ, you have everything.

--- Closing ---

Lord Jesus Christ — our King of Righteousness, our King of Peace, our eternal High Priest after the order of Melchizedek — we worship Thee with all our hearts. Thou art the fulfillment of every type, the substance of every shadow, the answer to every prayer, and the satisfaction of every longing. We come to Thee with our sins and find cleansing. We come to Thee with our sorrows and find comfort. We come to Thee with our empty hands and find them filled with every spiritual blessing. All glory, honor, and praise be unto Thee, now and forever and ever. Amen.

And so, beloved, we bring this study to its close. May the figure of Melchizedek, now illuminated by the full light of the gospel, draw you ever closer to the true King-Priest who reigns at the Father's right hand. May His righteousness be your covering, His peace be your portion, and His intercession be your confidence, until you see Him face to face. Grace and peace be with you all, in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.
