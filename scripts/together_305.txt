PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 5
"Faithfulness in the Garden of Marriage"
Proverbs 5:15-19

---

Hello and welcome to your couples devotional. Whether you are sitting side by side on the couch or listening together at the end of a long day, God is here with you both. He meets married couples in a special way when they turn their hearts to Him together.

Let me read our passage for this evening. It comes from Proverbs 5:15-19:

"Drink waters out of thine own cistern, and running waters out of thine own well. Let thy fountains be dispersed abroad, and rivers of waters in the streets. Let them be only thine own, and not strangers' with thee. Let thy fountain be blessed: and rejoice with the wife of thy youth. Let her be as the loving hind and pleasant roe; let her breasts satisfy thee at all times; and be thou ravished always with her love."

That is Proverbs 5:15-19. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Matthew Henry was a faithful pastor and theologian whose words still carry weight centuries later. Listen to what the Puritans had to say about the truths in tonight's passage.

Here Solomon employs the most vivid imagery to commend marital faithfulness and delight. The metaphor of the cistern, well, and fountain speaks of the marriage relationship as a source of refreshment, satisfaction, and life. Matthew Henry writes: 'Let thy wife, thy own wife, be thy fountain of pleasure, and let the waters that flow from that fountain be sacred to thyself.'

Henry is careful to note that this passage does not merely forbid adultery; it positively commands delight in one's own spouse. The language is warm and tender: 'Rejoice with the wife of thy youth.' Henry observes: 'It is not enough that thou have a wife, but thou must rejoice with her, take pleasure in her conversation and society, and let that be thy recreation.' The Puritans, far from the cold asceticism sometimes attributed to them, were champions of marital joy.

The water imagery is rich. A cistern collects and stores water; a well draws from deep underground springs; a fountain flows freely. Marriage is all three: it stores the accumulated history of shared life, it draws from deep wells of commitment and covenant, and it flows freely with ever-renewed affection. But the water must not be scattered to strangers. Henry warns that unfaithfulness is like breaking the aqueduct and letting the water flow into the streets: it wastes what was meant to nourish one garden.

Charles Bridges adds that 'the command to be ravished always with her love implies a duty of cultivation.' The garden of marriage must be tended. The husband who neglects his wife's heart, who takes her for granted, who pours his best energy into work or recreation and leaves only the dregs for his marriage, is failing to drink from his own cistern. The wife who withholds affection, who grows cold and distant, who seeks emotional satisfaction elsewhere, is letting her fountain flow into the streets.

--- Marriage Application ---

This passage calls every married couple to two things: exclusivity and delight. Your marriage is a garden with a wall around it, and within that wall is meant to be a paradise of mutual love, satisfaction, and refreshment.

Exclusivity means guarding your marriage from every rival affection. In an age of constant distraction and temptation, this requires intentional effort. Guard your eyes, your conversations, your emotional connections. Do not pour into other relationships what belongs to your spouse.

Delight means actively cultivating joy in one another. Do not let your marriage become a mere business partnership or parenting cooperative. Remember why you married. Pursue one another. Speak words of affirmation. Set aside time for intimacy, both emotional and physical. The command to 'rejoice with the wife of thy youth' is a present-tense, ongoing obligation: rejoice today, not merely in memory of yesterday.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Let us reflect together. These questions are designed to help you go deeper, both with God and with each other.

Question 1: Are we drinking deeply from our own cistern, or have we allowed our affections to become scattered?

Question 2: What is one specific way I can better cultivate delight in my spouse this week?

Question 3: Are there any threats to our exclusivity that we need to address honestly and urgently?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

As we near the end of tonight's devotional, let us bring our hearts before God in prayer. Pray for each other. Pray for your marriage. Pray for your home.

Tonight's prayer focus: Thank God for the gift of your spouse and ask Him to renew your delight in one another. Pray for protection against every form of unfaithfulness, and ask for grace to cultivate the garden of your marriage with tenderness and joy.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord who established marriage as a picture of His covenant love cause your fountain to be blessed, and may you rejoice in one another all the days of your life. Amen.

The Lord bless you and keep you. The Lord make His face shine upon you and be gracious to you. The Lord lift up His countenance upon you both, and give you peace. Amen.
