"The Application of Redemption — Calling, Justification, and Adoption"
Westminster Standards — Lesson 7

---

Welcome back to Puritan Gold's homeschool program. Today we explore how the salvation Christ accomplished is applied to individual believers through the doctrines of effectual calling, justification, and adoption. Let us pray and begin.

Gracious God, salvation is Your work from beginning to end. As we study how You apply Christ's redemption to sinners, fill us with wonder at Your grace. In Christ's name, Amen.

--- Lesson ---

Christ accomplished salvation on the cross. But how does that salvation become ours? How does the work done two thousand years ago in Jerusalem reach into the twenty-first century and transform individual human hearts? The Westminster Standards address this question with meticulous care in their treatment of the "application of redemption" — the process by which the Holy Spirit applies the benefits of Christ's work to those whom the Father has chosen.

The Shorter Catechism, Question 29, provides an overview: "We are made partakers of the redemption purchased by Christ, by the effectual application of it to us by his Holy Spirit." Salvation is Trinitarian: the Father plans it, the Son accomplishes it, and the Spirit applies it. Without the Spirit's work, the cross would remain a historical event with no personal impact. It is the Spirit who brings the blessings of Christ's death and resurrection into the experience of individual believers.

The first step in the application of redemption is effectual calling. The Shorter Catechism, Question 31, asks, "What is effectual calling?" and answers: "Effectual calling is the work of God's Spirit, whereby, convincing us of our sin and misery, enlightening our minds in the knowledge of Christ, and renewing our wills, he doth persuade and enable us to embrace Jesus Christ, freely offered to us in the gospel."

Notice what happens in effectual calling. The Spirit convinces us of our sin — showing us that we are guilty before God and unable to save ourselves. The Spirit enlightens our minds — opening our eyes to see the beauty and sufficiency of Christ. The Spirit renews our wills — changing our hearts so that we actually desire to come to Christ. And the Spirit persuades and enables us to embrace Christ — making it possible for us to believe.

This is not a mere invitation that we are free to accept or reject. It is an effectual work of the Spirit that actually accomplishes what it intends. Those whom God calls are not merely given an opportunity — they are given the ability and the desire to believe. This is why the Puritans called it "effectual" calling — it always produces its intended effect.

The second great benefit is justification. The Shorter Catechism, Question 33, asks, "What is justification?" and answers: "Justification is an act of God's free grace, wherein he pardoneth all our sins, and accepteth us as righteous in his sight, only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us, and received by faith alone."

Justification is the doctrine at the heart of the Protestant Reformation. It teaches that sinners are declared righteous before God — not because of anything they have done, but solely because of what Christ has done. In justification, two things happen simultaneously. First, God pardons all our sins — past, present, and future. The guilt of every offense against God's law is removed, and we are forgiven completely. Second, God credits to our account the perfect righteousness of Christ. Christ's lifelong obedience to God's law — His perfect keeping of every commandment — is counted as ours. We are clothed in His righteousness, and when God looks at us, He sees not our sin but Christ's perfection.

This is received "by faith alone." Faith is the instrument by which we receive justification — the empty hand that grasps the gift of Christ's righteousness. Faith itself is not a meritorious work. It is simply trusting in Christ and His finished work.

The third great benefit is adoption. The Shorter Catechism, Question 34, asks, "What is adoption?" and answers: "Adoption is an act of God's free grace, whereby we are received into the number, and have a right to all the privileges, of the sons of God."

Adoption is one of the most glorious and underappreciated doctrines in the Christian faith. In justification, we are pardoned criminals. In adoption, we are welcomed children. We are brought into God's family. We can call Him Father. We become heirs of His kingdom — "heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ" (Romans 8:17). We receive the Spirit of adoption, who cries out within us, "Abba, Father" — the intimate, affectionate cry of a child to its beloved parent.

The Westminster Confession, Chapter 12, elaborates: "All those that are justified, God vouchsafeth, in and for his only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the grace of adoption; by which they are taken into the number and enjoy the liberties and privileges of the children of God." The privileges of adoption include God's fatherly love, His protection, His provision, His discipline (which is itself a mark of love), and the inheritance of eternal life.

These three doctrines — effectual calling, justification, and adoption — describe the miracle of salvation from the sinner's perspective. The Spirit calls, God justifies, and the Father adopts. And all of it is by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone, to the glory of God alone.

--- Review Questions ---

Review Question 1: What is effectual calling, and how does it differ from a general invitation to believe?

Review Question 2: Explain the two aspects of justification — pardon and imputed righteousness. Why is each essential?

Review Question 3: What is adoption, and what privileges does it bring? How is adoption different from justification?

--- Activity ---

Create a flowchart or diagram showing the order of salvation as presented in the Westminster Standards: effectual calling leads to faith and repentance, which leads to justification and adoption. Include a brief description of each step, a relevant Scripture verse, and arrows showing the logical progression. Write a personal reflection on which of these doctrines is most meaningful to you and why.

--- Closing ---

The application of redemption is the work of the Holy Spirit bringing the blessings of Christ into your life. If you have been called, justified, and adopted, you have reason for eternal gratitude. If you have not yet believed, the invitation stands open: come to Christ and receive all that He has purchased. Until next time, students.
