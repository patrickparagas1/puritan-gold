PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"O Clap Your Hands, All Ye People"
June 23, 2026

---

"O clap your hands, all ye people; shout unto God with the voice of triumph. For the Lord most high is terrible; he is a great King over all the earth. He shall subdue the people under us, and the nations under our feet. He shall choose our inheritance for us, the excellency of Jacob whom he loved. Selah." -- Psalm 47:1-4

This psalm bursts upon us like a fanfare of trumpets, summoning not merely Israel but all the peoples of the earth to a universal celebration of the sovereignty of God. There is nothing quiet or reserved about this song. It is a call to clap, to shout, to make a joyful noise with the voice of triumph. And the ground of this exuberant praise is the most magnificent truth in all theology: that the Lord most high is King over all the earth.

Spurgeon loved this psalm and preached upon it with infectious enthusiasm. He noted that the early Christians were not the quiet, subdued, timid worshippers that some modern believers seem to think they should be. They were men and women who had been seized by the most thrilling news the world had ever heard -- that God had invaded history, that Christ had conquered death, that the tomb was empty, and that the King of kings was alive and reigning at the right hand of the Father. And when such news takes hold of the heart, the response is not a polite nod of acknowledgement but a shout of triumph.

"For the Lord most high is terrible; he is a great King over all the earth." The word "terrible" in the language of the King James Version means awe-inspiring, majestic, fearful in His greatness. God is not merely a local deity, the patron god of one nation among many. He is the Lord most high, exalted above all the heavens, sovereign over every kingdom and every empire and every government that has ever existed or ever shall exist. The kings of the earth may strut upon the stage of history and imagine themselves to be masters of their own destiny, but they are all under the government of the Most High, and He does among them whatsoever He pleases.

Thomas Manton preached with great clarity upon the universal sovereignty of God, arguing that this doctrine is not a speculative abstraction but a practical comfort of the highest order. If God is King over all the earth, then no earthly power can frustrate His purposes. If God is King over all the earth, then no political revolution can overthrow His government. If God is King over all the earth, then the affairs of nations, however chaotic and threatening they may appear, are all moving according to His eternal plan, and the final outcome is as certain as His own existence.

"He shall choose our inheritance for us." Here is a truth that speaks directly to one of the deepest anxieties of the human heart: the fear that our future is uncertain, that we do not know what lies ahead, that the path before us is dark and unmapped. But the psalmist declares that God chooses our inheritance. He selects the portion that is best for us. He determines the path that we shall walk, the place where we shall dwell, the circumstances in which we shall live and serve. And His choice is infinitely wiser and better than any choice we could make for ourselves.

John Flavel wrote a lengthy treatise on the mystery of divine providence, in which he urged believers to submit cheerfully to the choices that God makes for them, even when those choices seem hard or perplexing. The child does not choose his own inheritance; the father chooses it for him. And a wise child trusts his father's judgment, knowing that the father sees further, knows more, and loves more deeply than the child can comprehend. So it is with God and His people. We do not always understand why He leads us through one valley rather than another, why He opens one door and closes another, why He gives us this inheritance rather than that. But we trust Him, because He is our Father, and because He is wise, and because He loves us with an everlasting love.

William Gurnall reminded the church that the sovereignty of God over the nations is not a cold and impersonal sovereignty, like the rule of a distant emperor over provinces he has never visited. It is a personal, intimate, fatherly sovereignty that extends to the smallest details of every individual life. The God who subdues nations under His feet is the same God who numbers the hairs of your head. The King who rules over all the earth is the same King who chooses your inheritance with the tender care of a father selecting the best portion for his beloved child.

Let us therefore clap our hands and shout unto God with the voice of triumph. Let us celebrate the kingship of our God over all the earth. Let us trust His sovereign choice for our lives, knowing that the inheritance He selects for us is the excellency of Jacob whom He loved. For the Lord most high is terrible in majesty, and great in power, and perfect in wisdom, and infinite in love. To Him be praise and dominion forever. Amen.
