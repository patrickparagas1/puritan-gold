PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 11
"Seeking Counsel Together"
Proverbs 11:14

---

Good evening. What a blessing it is to see a husband and wife seeking the Lord together. The Puritans believed that a praying couple is an unstoppable force for the kingdom of God. Let us pray together tonight.

The Word of God has sustained marriages for thousands of years. Tonight it speaks to yours from Proverbs 11:14:

"Where no counsel is, the people fall: but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety."

That is Proverbs 11:14. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher. Matthew Henry reflected deeply on the truths of Scripture and how they apply to the covenant of marriage.

Solomon here speaks a truth that applies to nations, churches, and marriages alike. Matthew Henry observes that 'counsel' in this verse refers to wise, godly direction: the kind of guidance that comes from those who fear the Lord and understand His ways. Without such counsel, people 'fall' — not merely stumble, but collapse entirely.

Henry writes: 'A man may be ever so well versed in the theory of government, but if he have not prudent counsellors about him, he will be in danger of taking false steps.' This is equally true in marriage. A couple who relies solely on their own wisdom, who never seeks input from pastors, mentors, or mature believers, is dangerously exposed. Pride says, 'We can figure this out ourselves.' Wisdom says, 'In the multitude of counsellors there is safety.'

Charles Bridges adds that the 'multitude' does not mean consulting everyone indiscriminately. Rather, it means having multiple sources of godly wisdom: a pastor who knows your situation, older couples who have walked the road before you, friends who will speak the truth in love rather than merely tell you what you want to hear. Bridges writes that 'the safety is not in the number of counsellors, but in the wisdom of the counsel.'

Henry further observes that seeking counsel requires humility. The couple who thinks they have no need of outside perspective is the couple most in danger. 'Those that are very confident of their own judgment are commonly most out of the way,' Henry warns. The willingness to seek and receive counsel is itself a mark of wisdom.

--- Marriage Application ---

Do you have counsellors for your marriage? Not merely friends who will sympathize with your frustrations, but wise, godly people who will challenge you, correct you, and point you to Christ?

Many couples struggle in isolation, either because they are too proud to admit they need help or because they are too private to let anyone into their struggles. But God has designed the Christian life, and therefore the Christian marriage, to be lived in community. You need the church. You need older, wiser couples. You need a pastor who can speak into your life.

Make it a practice to seek counsel before you face a crisis, not only after. Regularly invite trusted mentors into your life. Attend marriage enrichment events at your church. Read together the wisdom of those who have gone before you. In the multitude of counsellors, there is safety for your marriage.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Here are some questions to guide your conversation tonight. Take turns sharing, and give each other the gift of your full attention.

Question 1: Do we have godly counsellors speaking into our marriage, or are we trying to navigate everything alone?

Question 2: Is there a current challenge in our marriage where we would benefit from seeking wise counsel?

Question 3: What holds us back from being more open about our marriage with trusted mentors, and how can we overcome that?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Prayer is the heartbeat of a Christian marriage. Let us pray together now. You do not need eloquent words, just honest ones.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to provide your marriage with wise counsellors: a faithful pastor, mature mentors, and honest friends. Pray for the humility to seek and receive counsel, and for discernment to know whose wisdom to trust.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord surround your marriage with wise and faithful counsellors, that in the safety of godly community you may stand firm through every season. Amen.

May your love for each other grow deeper with every passing day, and may your love for God be the source and the strength of it all. Goodnight, dear ones.
