PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 4
"The Gift of Sabbath Rest for Families"
Thomas Watson — The Ten Commandments


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Thomas Watson opens up the fourth commandment for us — the gift of Sabbath rest and what it means for our family's rhythm of worship and renewal. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord of the Sabbath, we thank You for the gift of rest. In a world that never stops, You call us to pause, to worship, and to be renewed in Your presence. Open our hearts tonight to receive the blessing of this commandment. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Exodus 20:8-11. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy. Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work: But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates: For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it."

That is Exodus 20:8-11. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Thomas Watson called the Sabbath "the market day of the soul." It is the day when heaven's goods are displayed and the soul may stock up on grace for the week ahead. Watson loved the Sabbath, and he wanted families to love it too — not as a burden of restrictions, but as a gift of unparalleled blessing.

The fourth commandment is unique among the ten. It is the only one that begins with the word "Remember," as if God knew that this commandment would be the one most easily forgotten. And indeed, in our hurried, restless, productivity-obsessed culture, the principle of Sabbath rest has been nearly abandoned. Families rush from one activity to the next without pause. Children are enrolled in sports leagues and enrichment programs that consume every weekend. Parents work seven days a week, either at the office or on their devices. The very idea of setting aside one day for worship and rest seems quaint, even irresponsible.

But Watson would argue that neglecting the Sabbath is what is truly irresponsible. God designed human beings for a rhythm of work and rest, and when we ignore that rhythm, we damage our souls, our bodies, and our families. Watson observed that the Sabbath is not merely a rule to follow but a gift to receive. It is God's provision for the renewal of our whole being — body, mind, and spirit.

For families, the Sabbath is especially precious. It is the one day when the whole household can slow down together. It is the day when parents can give their children undivided attention, when the family can worship together without the pressure of schedules and deadlines, when conversations can go deeper because there is no rush to move on to the next thing. Watson believed that the health of a family could be measured by the quality of its Sabbath keeping. A family that keeps the Sabbath well is a family that has its priorities in order.

Watson taught that the Sabbath has both a negative and a positive dimension. Negatively, it means ceasing from ordinary work and worldly pursuits. This is not legalism but wisdom. The soul cannot worship when the mind is consumed with to-do lists and business concerns. Setting aside secular work creates space for sacred activities. Positively, the Sabbath means filling the day with worship, prayer, Scripture reading, works of mercy, and spiritual fellowship. Watson was not in favor of a Sabbath spent in idleness or mere entertainment. He wanted the day to be full — full of God.

Notice also that the commandment extends the rest to the entire household: sons, daughters, servants, and even animals. Watson saw in this a principle of family leadership. Parents are responsible for setting the tone of the Sabbath in their homes. If the parents treat the Lord's Day as just another day, the children will too. But if the parents create an atmosphere of joyful worship and restful fellowship, the children will grow up treasuring the Sabbath as one of the great delights of their childhood.

What might a Watson-inspired Sabbath look like for your family? Perhaps it begins with morning worship at church, followed by a leisurely family meal where you talk about the sermon. Perhaps the afternoon includes a walk in nature, a time of quiet reading, or a visit to someone who is sick or lonely. Perhaps the evening closes with family worship — singing, prayer, and reading Scripture together. The specifics will vary from family to family, but the principle remains: set apart one day for God, and watch how He blesses both the day and the rest of the week.

Watson would also gently remind us that the Sabbath points forward to an eternal rest. The weekly rhythm of six days of labor and one day of rest is a picture of the greater story: the toil of this present life followed by the eternal rest of heaven. Every Sabbath is a foretaste of glory, a rehearsal for the day when work and weariness will be no more, and the whole family of God will rest in His presence forever.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Watson called the Sabbath "the market day of the soul." What do you think he meant by that? How is the Lord's Day an opportunity to stock up on grace for the week ahead?

Question 2: In our busy culture, what are the biggest obstacles to keeping a restful, worshipful Sabbath as a family? How might we overcome them?

Question 3: If we were to redesign our Sundays to be more faithful to the spirit of the fourth commandment, what would we add and what would we remove?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy." -- Exodus 20:8

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord, thank You for the gift of rest. Forgive us for treating Your holy day as merely our day. Teach our family to treasure the Sabbath and to find our deepest refreshment in Your presence. May our Lord's Days be a foretaste of the eternal rest that awaits Your people. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, resting in the God who made the Sabbath for your good.
