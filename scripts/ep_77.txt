PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"O Sing Unto the Lord a New Song"
May 16, 2026

---

"O sing unto the Lord a new song: sing unto the Lord, all the earth. Sing unto the Lord, bless his name; shew forth his salvation from day to day. Declare his glory among the heathen, his wonders among all people. For the Lord is great, and greatly to be praised: he is to be feared above all gods. For all the gods of the nations are idols: but the Lord made the heavens." -- Psalm 96:1-5

Three times in the opening verses of this psalm, the command rings out: Sing unto the Lord! Sing! Sing! It is as though the Spirit of God cannot contain Himself. The praise of the Almighty demands not a whisper but a shout, not a reluctant murmur but a full-throated song, and not merely from a select choir but from all the earth. Every nation, every tribe, every tongue, every creature that has breath is summoned to join in this universal anthem of adoration. And it is to be a new song, not a stale repetition of worn-out phrases, but a fresh and living expression of worship that springs from a heart continually discovering new reasons to praise the God of heaven.

Why a new song? Because the mercies of God are new every morning, and they deserve new praise. Because the glory of God is inexhaustible, and no single song can compass it. Because the people of God are new creatures in Christ, and new creatures sing new songs. The old songs of complaint, of despair, of worldly sorrow, have given way to songs of redemption, of hope, of everlasting joy. And even the oldest hymns of the church become new when they are sung by hearts that have been freshly touched by the power of the Holy Spirit.

"Shew forth his salvation from day to day." The praise of God is not a weekly obligation to be discharged at the Sunday service and then set aside until the following Lord's Day. It is a daily occupation. From day to day, the believer is to declare the saving works of God, to make known His mercies, to publish His faithfulness. And this daily proclamation of salvation is itself one of the chief means by which the knowledge of God is spread throughout the earth. For when a man praises God with genuine gratitude and evident joy, those who hear him are compelled to ask, "What manner of God is this, who inspires such devotion in His servants?"

"Declare his glory among the heathen, his wonders among all people." Here is the missionary heart of the psalm. The God of Israel is not a tribal deity, confined to a single nation or a single land. He is the God of the whole earth, and His glory is to be proclaimed to every people under heaven. The Puritans understood this with a clarity that many in our own day have lost. They were not narrow isolationists who cared only for their own spiritual comfort. They were men and women consumed with a passion for the glory of God among the nations. John Eliot gave his life to preaching the gospel among the native peoples of New England. David Brainerd exhausted his frail body in missionary labours among the Indians of the American frontier. And they did so because they believed what this psalm declares: that the Lord is great, and greatly to be praised, and that His glory must be declared among all people.

"For the Lord is great, and greatly to be praised: he is to be feared above all gods." The greatness of God is the foundation of all true worship. We do not praise God because He needs our praise. The eternal, self-sufficient God has no needs. We praise Him because He deserves our praise. His greatness demands it. His goodness compels it. His glory requires it. And when we fail to praise Him, we do not diminish His greatness. We diminish ourselves. For the creature that refuses to praise its Creator is a creature that has forgotten its own identity, its own purpose, and its own highest good.

"For all the gods of the nations are idols: but the Lord made the heavens." Here is the acid test that distinguishes the true God from every counterfeit. The gods of the nations, whatever form they may take, are idols, things of nothing, vanities, emptinesses dressed up in religious clothing. They cannot speak. They cannot hear. They cannot act. They cannot save. They are the projections of human imagination, the products of human craftsmanship, the embodiments of human fear and human desire. But the Lord, the God of Israel, the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, made the heavens. He spoke, and the stars blazed into existence. He breathed, and the galaxies spun into their orbits. He is the Creator, and all else is creature. And between the Creator and the creature there is an infinite, unbridgeable gulf that no idol can ever cross.

"Honour and majesty are before him: strength and beauty are in his sanctuary." The sanctuary of God is not a dull, grey, oppressive place, as the enemies of religion would have us believe. It is a place of strength and beauty, of honour and majesty. The worship of the true God is not the enemy of beauty but its source. All the beauty that exists in the created world is but a dim reflection of the beauty that dwells in the sanctuary of the Most High. The sunset that takes our breath away, the mountain that fills us with awe, the ocean that humbles us with its vastness, these are but whispers of the glory that fills the temple of God.

"Let the heavens rejoice, and let the earth be glad; let the sea roar, and the fulness thereof. Let the field be joyful, and all that is therein: then shall all the trees of the wood rejoice before the Lord: for he cometh, for he cometh to judge the earth: he shall judge the world with righteousness, and the people with his truth." The psalm concludes with a vision of cosmic praise that sweeps up all creation, from the highest heavens to the deepest seas, into one magnificent symphony of adoration. And the occasion of this praise is not merely the past mercies of God but His future coming. He cometh! He cometh to judge the earth! And this coming is cause for rejoicing, not for terror, because He shall judge with righteousness and truth. The day of judgment, which fills the ungodly with dread, fills the children of God with hope. For on that day, every wrong shall be righted, every tear shall be dried, every injustice shall be corrected, and the righteous Judge shall make all things new. O sing unto the Lord a new song, for He is worthy! Amen.
