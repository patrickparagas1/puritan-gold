PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 15
"The Power of a Soft Answer"
Proverbs 15:1

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to tonight's couples devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for setting aside this time to draw near to God and to each other. The moments you invest in your marriage before the Lord are among the most important moments of your day.

Let us open God's Word together. Our passage tonight comes from Proverbs 15:1:

"A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger."

That is Proverbs 15:1. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Now let us hear from Matthew Henry, one of the great Puritan voices on this subject. The Puritans wrote extensively about marriage because they saw it as one of God's most profound gifts.

This proverb is among the most practical and frequently tested truths in all of Scripture, and nowhere is it tested more regularly than in marriage. Matthew Henry writes: 'Nothing is more natural than for passion to beget passion, and for angry words to provoke angry answers. But grace enables a man to bridle his tongue and to give a soft answer.'

Henry is careful to distinguish a soft answer from a weak answer. Softness is not capitulation; it is control. The soft answer may still speak truth, may still address wrong, may still maintain a position. But it does so without inflammatory language, without raised voices, without the desire to wound. Henry writes: 'If wrath be risen like a threatening storm, a soft answer will turn it away, as the pilot turns the ship from the wind.'

The contrast is 'grievous words' — words designed to hurt, to punish, to retaliate. Henry observes that grievous words do not merely fail to resolve conflict; they 'stir up anger,' actively making the situation worse. One harsh word provokes another, and the cycle of escalation can destroy in minutes what took months to build.

Charles Bridges adds that the soft answer requires spiritual strength, not weakness. 'It is far easier to answer wrath with wrath than to answer it with gentleness,' Bridges writes. The soft answer is the fruit of the Spirit's work in a heart that has been disciplined by grace. It is the evidence of a soul that fears God more than it fears losing an argument. Bridges counsels: 'In the heat of conflict, pause before you speak. Ask yourself not what will satisfy your anger, but what will glorify your God and preserve your brother.'

--- Marriage Application ---

If there is one proverb that could transform your marriage overnight, it may be this one. How many arguments have escalated not because of the original issue but because of the way you spoke to one another in the heat of the moment?

A soft answer in marriage means choosing your words with care, especially when you are angry. It means lowering your voice when you want to raise it. It means pausing before you respond to something hurtful. It means asking, 'How would I want to be spoken to right now?' and then extending that same grace.

This does not mean avoiding difficult conversations. The soft answer is not silence; it is truth spoken gently. You can address sin, express hurt, and advocate for change — all with a soft tone that turns away wrath rather than stirring it up. Agree together that in your next conflict, you will fight for the soft answer.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Now let us pause for reflection. Here are some questions for you and your spouse to discuss together. Be honest. Be gentle. Listen more than you speak.

Question 1: When we disagree, am I more inclined to give a soft answer or grievous words? What about my spouse?

Question 2: Can you think of a recent conflict that was made worse by harsh words? How might a soft answer have changed the outcome?

Question 3: What practical steps can we take to train ourselves toward soft answers in the heat of conflict?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Let us move into a time of prayer together. If you are able, hold hands as you pray. There is something powerful about a husband and wife joining hands before the throne of grace.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray for the Spirit's fruit of self-control in your speech. Ask God to put a guard over your mouths, that when anger rises, you would respond with grace rather than grievous words. Pray specifically for your next disagreement — that it would be marked by soft answers.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord who is slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love fill your mouths with soft answers, turning away wrath and filling your home with peace. Amen.

May the Lord bless your marriage and keep it strong. May He make His face to shine upon your home, and give you peace. Goodnight, dear couple. Walk together in His love.
