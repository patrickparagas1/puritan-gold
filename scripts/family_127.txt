PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 27
"The Godly Person Pursues Holiness"
Thomas Watson — The Godly Man's Picture


Good evening, family. There is no place God loves to meet His people more than when families gather in His name. Welcome to Puritan Gold. Let us open our Bibles and our hearts.

Let us begin in prayer. Lord Jesus, You said that where two or three are gathered in Your name, there You are in the midst of them. We claim that promise tonight as a family. Be here with us. Teach us. Shape us. We pray in Your holy name. Amen.

Every word of Scripture is breathed out by God. Tonight we receive His breath from Hebrews 12:14:

"Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord."

That is Hebrews 12:14. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Today we come to a word that may sound old-fashioned but is absolutely central to the Christian life: holiness. Hebrews 12:14 says plainly, 'Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord.'

That last phrase is sobering: 'without which no man shall see the Lord.' Holiness is not optional for the Christian. It is not an advanced course for spiritual elites. It is essential. Without holiness — without a genuine pursuit of purity and godliness — no one will see the Lord.

But what is holiness? The Hebrew word 'qadosh' and the Greek word 'hagios' both mean 'set apart.' Something holy is separated from common use and dedicated to God. When God says, 'Be ye holy; for I am holy' (1 Peter 1:16, quoting Leviticus 11:44), He is calling us to be set apart — different from the world around us, dedicated to His purposes, reflecting His character.

Holiness is not primarily about what we avoid. It is about what we pursue. Paul writes in 1 Timothy 6:11, 'But thou, O man of God, flee these things; and follow after righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, meekness.' Yes, we flee from sin. But then we run toward righteousness. Holiness is not just the absence of evil but the presence of good.

In Romans 12:1-2, Paul calls us to present our bodies as 'a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God.' Under the old covenant, sacrifices were dead animals laid on an altar. Under the new covenant, we are living sacrifices — offering our whole selves, every part of our lives, to God. And Paul says we should 'be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind.' Holiness means being transformed — changed from the inside out — rather than being squeezed into the mold of the world.

Peter writes in 2 Peter 1:5-7, 'And beside this, giving all diligence, add to your faith virtue; and to virtue knowledge; And to knowledge temperance; and to temperance patience; and to patience godliness; And to godliness brotherly kindness; and to brotherly kindness charity.' Holiness is a growth process — we add one quality to another, building a life of increasing godliness.

Thomas Watson wrote that holiness is the beauty of a Christian. Just as a painting is valued for its beauty and a jewel for its brilliance, a Christian is most beautiful when living a holy life. Watson said that the godly person is not sinless — no one is in this life — but he is a person who cannot rest in sin. When he falls, he gets back up. When he sins, he repents. He is always moving toward God, even when the journey is slow and difficult.

Watson described holiness as having two dimensions. There is imputed holiness — the righteousness of Christ credited to us when we believe. This is our standing before God, perfect and complete, because Christ's perfect righteousness covers us. And there is practical holiness — the daily process of becoming more like Christ in our thoughts, words, and actions. This is our walk, which is always imperfect but always progressing.

Watson urged Christians not to be discouraged by their slowness in holiness. He compared the Christian life to the sun rising — it may be gradual, but it is always moving toward full brightness. What matters is the direction, not the speed. Are you moving toward holiness, however slowly? Then take heart. God who began a good work in you will complete it (Philippians 1:6).

Watson also warned against the danger of pursuing holiness in your own strength. Holiness is a work of the Holy Spirit in us (hence the name 'Holy' Spirit). We cooperate with God's grace, but we cannot manufacture holiness by willpower alone. We need God's Spirit, God's Word, God's people, and God's grace every step of the way.

For our youngest ones: being holy means trying to be like God — kind, honest, loving, and good. We cannot do it perfectly, but we try because we love God. For older children: holiness is not about being weird or boring. It is about being set apart for something beautiful — God's purposes. For the whole family: let us pursue holiness together, not through rigid rules but through genuine love for God and daily dependence on His grace.


One of the great blessings of the Christian tradition is that we stand on the shoulders of faithful saints who came before us.

Thomas Watson, in his work "The Godly Man's Picture," wrote these words:

"Holiness is the beauty of a Christian. It is not a perfection we achieve but a direction we pursue. The godly man is not sinless, but he is a man who cannot rest in his sin."

What Thomas Watson is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Hebrews 12:14 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Now we get to the best part: talking about God's Word together as a family. Here are tonight's questions.

Question 1: What does 'holy' mean? (Set apart for God!) Can you think of something that is set apart for a special purpose?

Question 2: The Bible says 'without holiness no one will see the Lord.' Does that mean we have to be perfect? What does it mean?

Question 3: Watson says the godly person is not sinless but cannot rest in sin. What is the difference between being perfect and always trying to grow?

Question 4: What is one area of your life where you would like to grow in holiness? How can our family help each other?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord." -- Hebrews 12:14

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a practical way to live out tonight's devotional:

Set apart one ordinary object in the house for a special purpose this week — perhaps a mug that becomes 'the prayer mug' (only used during devotion time) or a chair that becomes 'the reading chair' (for Bible time only). Discuss how being 'set apart' makes something special — and that is what God does with us.


Gracious God, thank You for meeting with our family tonight. Your Word never returns void, and we trust that what we have heard will bear fruit in each of our lives. Strengthen our family bonds, deepen our faith, and make us a light in this world. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

May the peace of God guard your hearts and minds tonight. Goodnight, family.
