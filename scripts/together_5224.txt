PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 24
"A Wife of Noble Character"
Proverbs 31:10-12 — The Virtuous Wife


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we begin our journey into the great portrait of the virtuous woman in Proverbs 31. This passage has been a source of both inspiration and encouragement for wives throughout the centuries. Let us approach it with grace, understanding that it is a portrait of aspiration, not a standard of perfection.

Let us read our passage. Proverbs 31:10-12 (KJV):

"Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies. The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her, so that he shall have no need of spoil. She will do him good and not evil all the days of her life."


The opening question rings through the centuries with undiminished power: "Who can find a virtuous woman?" The word translated "virtuous" in the King James Version is the Hebrew word chayil, which means strength, valor, and capability. This is not a fragile, passive woman. This is a woman of power, of substance, of moral and practical strength. Her price, Solomon says, is far above rubies. In the ancient world, rubies were among the most precious and rare of gems. The virtuous wife is rarer and more valuable still.

William Gouge meditated on the phrase "her price is far above rubies" and drew a sobering conclusion. If the virtuous wife is this valuable, then the husband who treats her carelessly, who takes her for granted, who fails to recognize her worth, is a fool of the highest order. A man who possesses a priceless jewel and throws it in the dirt is beneath contempt. Yet how many husbands treat their wives with less care than they give their careers, their hobbies, or their reputations? The husband who reads Proverbs 31 should be filled not only with admiration for the virtuous wife but with a holy determination to honor her as the treasure she is.

The next verse reveals one of the most beautiful qualities of the virtuous wife: trustworthiness. "The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her." This is the trust of a man who knows his wife will not betray him, not with infidelity, not with careless words, not with financial irresponsibility, not with a divided heart. She is a safe harbor. He can open his heart to her without fear. He can share his struggles, his failures, and his weaknesses, knowing that she will guard his confidence and respond with compassion rather than contempt.

Thomas Gataker observed that trust is the currency of marriage. Where trust exists, love flourishes. Where trust is broken, love withers. The virtuous wife builds trust through consistent faithfulness in small things and great things alike. She keeps her word. She guards her husband's reputation. She manages the household resources with integrity. She is the same woman in private that she is in public. This consistency creates a depth of trust that allows the marriage to go deeper than any other human relationship.

The final phrase of our passage is perhaps the most comprehensive: "She will do him good and not evil all the days of her life." Not some days. Not when she feels like it. Not when he deserves it. All the days of her life. This is a commitment of total loyalty, of unwavering benevolence, of lifelong devotion to the good of her husband. It does not mean she never corrects him; correction, when done in love, is a form of doing good. It does not mean she never disagrees; honest disagreement can prevent great harm. But it means that in everything she does, her orientation is toward his benefit, his flourishing, and his joy.

Richard Baxter reminded husbands that this lifelong commitment from their wives calls for an equally lifelong response. If your wife has pledged to do you good all the days of her life, what have you pledged to her? Have you committed to do her good with the same constancy, the same unwavering devotion, the same total orientation toward her flourishing? The virtuous wife described in Proverbs 31 is not meant to be an isolated figure. She is meant to be matched by a virtuous husband who is equally committed to her good.


Question 1: Husband, does your heart safely trust in your wife? If so, tell her what that means to you. If trust needs to grow, share what would help.

Question 2: Wife, do you feel that you are valued as someone whose price is far above rubies? What helps you feel most treasured?

Question 3: What does it look like for each of us to commit to doing the other good and not evil all the days of our lives?


Lord God, we are awed by the beauty of the virtuous wife described in Thy Word. Her worth is beyond all earthly treasure. We thank Thee for the wife in this marriage, for her faithfulness, her strength, and her devotion. Help the husband to recognize and honor her value. Help the wife to grow in the virtues that Thy Word commends. May the trust between us deepen with each passing year, and may we both be committed to doing each other good all the days of our lives. In Jesus' name, Amen.
