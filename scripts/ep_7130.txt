From "The Bruised Reed" by Richard Sibbes:

PURITAN GOLD -- The Bruised Reed: Complete Summary A Comprehensive Overview of Richard Sibbes's Classic Work The Bruised Reed. By Richard Sibbes. A Complete Summary. Welcome to this summary of Richard Sibbes's The Bruised Reed, one of the tenderest and most comforting works in all of Christian literature. Based on Isaiah 42:3 and Matthew 12:20, Sibbes unfolds the character of Christ as the gentle Savior who will not break the bruised reed nor quench the smoking flax. Sibbes begins by explaining the bruised reed and the smoking flax as images of weak and struggling believers. The bruised reed represents a soul that has been crushed by conviction of sin, by affliction, or by temptation. The smoking flax represents the faintest spark of grace — faith that is weak, love that is cold, hope that is dim. Christ's great promise is that He will not break what is already bruised, and He will not quench what is already smoking. On the contrary, He tenderly nurtures the weakest beginnings of grace until they grow strong and bright. Sibbes explores Christ's dealing with weak believers with pastoral warmth. He shows that the least measure of true grace is precious in Christ's sight. A spark of heavenly fire is more valuable than all the blazing fires of nature. Christ cherishes the smallest beginnings of faith and fans them into flame. The marks of the smoking flax are identified so that doubting Christians can recognize grace in themselves. Sibbes helps the reader distinguish between true grace (however weak) and mere natural affection. He addresses the temptations that hinder comfort and the discouragements that assail weak Christians. The duty of believers toward one another is emphasized: we must not despise or discourage those who are weak in faith but support and strengthen them, following Christ's own example. Sibbes then turns to Christ's judgment and victory — how He establishes His kingdom not by external force but by the gentle government of grace. Pardon leads to obedience, justification leads to sanctification, and grace ultimately reigns over sin. The book concludes with the assurance that Christ's kingdom must and shall prevail. Though the enemies seem strong and the church seems weak, the victory is certain. Every Christian may take courage from the knowledge that the one who began a good work will bring it to completion. This work remains a balm for wounded consciences and a source of deep encouragement for every believer who feels weak in faith or cold in love.
