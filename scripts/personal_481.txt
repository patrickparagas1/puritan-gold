PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 20
"The Stewardship of Time"
Pastoral Day — Redeeming the Days


Welcome. Today is a Pastoral day. Our subject is the stewardship of time — a resource the Puritans valued above gold, because once spent, it can never be recovered.

Let us read Ephesians 5:15-16 (KJV):

"See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, Redeeming the time, because the days are evil."


The Puritans were acutely conscious of time. They lived with the awareness that every moment is a gift from God and that every moment will be accounted for. Richard Baxter, who lived with chronic illness and the constant awareness that death might be near, wrote with particular urgency about the use of time. He warned that the greatest robbery most people commit is not the theft of money but the waste of time — minutes, hours, and days squandered on trivialities while eternity draws near.

Jonathan Edwards famously composed a series of resolutions in his youth, several of which addressed the use of time. He resolved never to lose a moment of time, but to improve it in the most profitable way possible. He resolved to live with all his might while he lived. He resolved never to do anything that he would not be willing to do if it were the last hour of his life.

The Puritans offered several practical counsels on the stewardship of time.

First, begin each day intentionally. Do not let the morning drift away in aimless scrolling, daydreaming, or procrastination. The Puritans rose early — many before dawn — and gave the first hours of the day to prayer, Scripture reading, and the planning of the day's duties. Baxter wrote that the way you begin the day sets the trajectory for everything that follows.

Second, distinguish between the urgent and the important. Many people spend their lives responding to urgencies — demands, interruptions, crises — while neglecting the truly important things: prayer, study, family, long-term growth. The Puritans counseled wisdom in prioritization. Thomas Watson warned against letting the good become the enemy of the best.

Third, guard against the time-wasters. The Puritans identified several: excessive entertainment, idle conversation, unnecessary social obligations, the cultivation of trivial knowledge at the expense of divine knowledge, and what they called slothfulness — the sin of laziness. While they did not oppose rest and recreation — indeed, they commended both as necessary for the soul and body — they warned against the excess that turns recreation into dissipation.

Fourth, fill the margins. The Puritans were masters of using small pockets of time productively. While walking, they meditated. While waiting, they prayed. While traveling, they read. They understood that great things are accomplished not in dramatic bursts but in the faithful accumulation of small moments.

Fifth, live with the end in view. The Puritans regularly contemplated death — not morbidly but wisely. The awareness that life is brief and death is certain gave them an urgency that is largely absent from modern Christianity. Psalm 90:12 was a constant companion: "So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom."

Sixth, rest without guilt. While the Puritans valued industriousness, they also valued rest. They understood the Sabbath principle — that God Himself rested, and that rest is built into the fabric of creation. The person who refuses to rest is not more spiritual; they are more proud. Richard Sibbes wrote that "the soul that never rests never reflects, and the soul that never reflects never grows."

The ultimate motivation for the stewardship of time is not productivity but glory. The Puritans did not manage their time in order to accomplish more worldly success. They managed their time in order to glorify God more fully. Every hour given to prayer glorifies God. Every hour given to service glorifies God. Every hour given to study glorifies God. Even the hours given to rest and recreation glorify God when they are received as gifts from His hand and used for the refreshment of the body and soul He created.


Time is the currency of eternity. Spend it wisely. Lord, I confess that I have wasted time that You gave me as a sacred trust. Teach me to number my days. Help me to redeem the time — to fill each hour with what matters most. Not busyness for busyness's sake, but faithful stewardship of the life You have given me. Amen.
