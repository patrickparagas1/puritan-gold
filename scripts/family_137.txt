PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 6
"The Palace Beautiful"
Pilgrim Families — Week 1, Day 6

---

Good evening, family. There is no place God loves to meet His people more than when families gather in His name. Welcome to Puritan Gold. Let us open our Bibles and our hearts.

Let us begin in prayer. Lord Jesus, You said that where two or three are gathered in Your name, there You are in the midst of them. We claim that promise tonight as a family. Be here with us. Teach us. Shape us. We pray in Your holy name. Amen.

Every word of Scripture is breathed out by God. Tonight we receive His breath from Hebrews 10:24-25; Psalm 133:1:

"And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching. Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!"

That is Hebrews 10:24-25; Psalm 133:1. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

After climbing the Hill Difficulty, Christian arrives at a magnificent place called the Palace Beautiful. Here he is welcomed by godly women named Discretion, Prudence, Piety, and Charity. They feed him, give him rest, show him the armory of spiritual weapons, and let him look out from the palace rooftop toward the Delectable Mountains — a distant view of the glory that awaits.

The Palace Beautiful is a picture of the local church — the fellowship of believers where weary pilgrims find refreshment, encouragement, and equipping for the journey ahead.

Our Scripture today establishes why this fellowship matters. Hebrews 10:24-25 commands us: "Let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching." And the Psalmist exclaims in Psalm 133:1: "Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!"

God never intended the Christian life to be a solo journey. Christian had been traveling alone through much of his pilgrimage — and it was hard. He fell into the Slough, he was tricked by Mr. Worldly Wiseman, he fell asleep on the Hill Difficulty. Many of these struggles could have been avoided or lessened if he had faithful companions walking alongside him. The Palace Beautiful shows what happens when we gather with other believers: we are strengthened, nourished, and prepared.

At the Palace Beautiful, Christian was fed a meal of rich food and good wine. This represents the spiritual nourishment we receive from the preaching of God's Word and the Lord's Supper. He was shown the armory — swords, shields, helmets, breastplates — all the weapons God provides for spiritual warfare. This represents the equipping ministry of the church, where we learn to fight the battles of faith. And he was given a room called Peace, where he slept soundly until morning. This represents the rest and comfort that come from being among God's people.

Hebrews tells us that the purpose of gathering together is to "provoke unto love and to good works." The word "provoke" means to stir up, to urge, to spur forward. We need other Christians to stir us up when we grow cold, to encourage us when we grow weary, to correct us when we wander. No one can do this for themselves. We need each other.

The Psalmist says it is both good and pleasant when brethren dwell together in unity. Good — meaning it is morally right and spiritually beneficial. And pleasant — meaning it brings genuine joy and delight. Christian fellowship is not a burden or an obligation. It is a gift from God. When believers come together in love, something beautiful happens that cannot happen anywhere else on earth.

Bunyan also gives us a warning in this scene. Before Christian reached the Palace Beautiful, he had to pass by two lions chained on either side of the path. The lions roared fiercely, and Christian nearly turned back in fear. But the palace porter, named Watchful, called out to him: "Fear not the lions, for they are chained!" The enemy places fear at the entrance of every good thing God has for us. Fear of commitment. Fear of vulnerability. Fear of being hurt by other Christians. But the lions are chained. God is in control.

Families, the church is not optional. It is the Palace Beautiful that God has provided for every pilgrim family. Yes, the church is imperfect. Yes, Christians disappoint each other. But the answer is not to abandon fellowship — it is to lean into it more deeply. Your family needs a church home where you are fed, equipped, encouraged, and loved. And you need to be that for other families as well.

Consider one another. Provoke one another to love. Do not forsake the assembling. The journey is too long and too hard to walk alone. Christian left the Palace Beautiful refreshed, rested, and armed for the battles ahead. Your family can experience the same refreshment every time you gather with God's people.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

One of the great blessings of the Christian tradition is that we stand on the shoulders of faithful saints who came before us.

John Bunyan, in his work "The Pilgrim's Progress," wrote these words:

"Then I saw in my dream, that the Interpreter took Christian by the hand, and led him into a place where was a fire burning against a wall, and one standing by it, always casting much water upon it, to quench it; yet did the fire burn higher and hotter."

What John Bunyan is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Hebrews 10:24-25; Psalm 133:1 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Now we get to the best part: talking about God's Word together as a family. Here are tonight's questions.

Question 1: Why do you think God gave us the church? What would the Christian life be like without fellowship?

Question 2: What are the 'lions' that keep people from being involved in a church? How do we overcome that fear?

Question 3: How has our church family helped us grow as a family? Can you think of specific examples?

Question 4: What does it mean to 'provoke one another to love and good works'? How can we do this for others?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!" -- Psalm 133:1

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Here is a practical way to live out tonight's devotional:

Write thank-you notes to three people at your church who have encouraged your family. Deliver them this week. Talk about how encouraging others is one way we build the 'Palace Beautiful' for fellow pilgrims.

--- Closing ---

Gracious God, thank You for meeting with our family tonight. Your Word never returns void, and we trust that what we have heard will bear fruit in each of our lives. Strengthen our family bonds, deepen our faith, and make us a light in this world. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

May the peace of God guard your hearts and minds tonight. Goodnight, family.
