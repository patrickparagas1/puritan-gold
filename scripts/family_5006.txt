PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 6
"Protecting Life and Nurturing Kindness"
Thomas Watson — The Ten Commandments

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Thomas Watson leads us to the sixth commandment and its searching application to the way we treat one another within the walls of our home. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord of life, You are the giver of every breath. Teach us tonight to value human life as You do, and to guard the well-being of every person in our family — not only their physical safety, but also their emotional and spiritual health. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Exodus 20:13 and Matthew 5:21-24. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Thou shalt not kill. Ye have heard that it was said by them of old time, Thou shalt not kill; and whosoever shall kill shall be in danger of the judgment: But I say unto you, That whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment: and whosoever shall say to his brother, Raca, shall be in danger of the council: but whosoever shall say, Thou fool, shall be in danger of hell fire. Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift."

That is Exodus 20:13 and Matthew 5:21-24. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

At first glance, the sixth commandment might seem to have little to do with family life. Most of us are not in danger of committing physical murder within our homes. But Thomas Watson, following Jesus Himself, taught that this commandment reaches far deeper than the outward act. It reaches into the heart, and it touches the very atmosphere of our family relationships.

Jesus expanded the sixth commandment in the Sermon on the Mount, teaching that anger, contempt, and hateful speech are the roots from which murder grows. When we harbor anger against a family member, when we speak words of contempt — "Raca," meaning "worthless one" — when we call someone a fool, we are violating the spirit of the sixth commandment. Watson understood that murder does not begin with a weapon in the hand. It begins with malice in the heart.

This means that the sixth commandment speaks directly to the emotional climate of your home. How do family members speak to one another when they are frustrated? Do parents resort to harsh, cutting words when disciplining their children? Do siblings hurl insults at each other in moments of conflict? Do spouses let anger simmer into cold contempt? Watson would say that every cruel word, every contemptuous glance, every act of emotional neglect is a small violation of the sixth commandment. It may not take physical life, but it wounds the spirit, and spiritual wounds can be as devastating as physical ones.

Watson taught that the sixth commandment requires not merely the absence of harm but the active pursuit of good. We are called not only to refrain from hurting others but to protect, nurture, and build them up. For families, this means creating a culture of kindness. It means speaking words that heal rather than words that wound. It means being quick to listen, slow to speak, and slow to wrath. It means pursuing reconciliation when conflict arises rather than letting bitterness take root.

Consider the power of words in a family. A parent's words of encouragement can shape a child's entire understanding of themselves and their God. "I am proud of you." "God made you with a purpose." "I love you no matter what." These are words of life. Conversely, a parent's words of constant criticism, disappointment, or rejection can inflict wounds that take decades to heal. Watson would say that parents who consistently tear their children down with their words are, in a spiritual sense, committing a kind of violence against the souls of their children.

The same principle applies to siblings. Brothers and sisters who learn to speak kindly to one another, to resolve conflicts peacefully, and to protect one another's dignity are learning lessons that will serve them for the rest of their lives. And the habits they form at home will shape how they treat every person they encounter in the world beyond.

Watson also connected the sixth commandment to the duty of self-care. We are not only forbidden from harming others; we are also called to be good stewards of our own bodies and souls. Parents who destroy their health through overwork, stress, or neglect are harming the family that depends on them. The sixth commandment calls us to care for ourselves — to rest when we need rest, to eat wisely, to guard our mental and emotional health — so that we can serve our families well.

Tonight, let your family become a place where life is cherished, where words are chosen with care, where anger is managed and forgiveness is practiced, and where every member feels safe, valued, and loved.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Jesus said that anger and contemptuous speech violate the spirit of the sixth commandment. What does this teach us about the power of words in our home?

Question 2: Watson said the sixth commandment requires not just the absence of harm but the active pursuit of good. What are some ways our family can actively build one another up this week?

Question 3: Is there anyone in our family who needs to hear a word of encouragement or reconciliation tonight? Can we take a moment to speak those words right now?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger." -- Proverbs 15:1

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord, forgive us for the times we have wounded one another with our words. Create in us clean hearts and gentle tongues. Help us to be a family that protects life, nurtures love, and speaks with kindness. May our home be a sanctuary where every member is safe and cherished. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, sheltered in the love of the God who gave you life.
