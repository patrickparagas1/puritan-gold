PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"Why Boastest Thou Thyself in Mischief, O Mighty Man?"
June 27, 2026

---

"Why boastest thou thyself in mischief, O mighty man? the goodness of God endureth continually. Thy tongue deviseth mischiefs; like a sharp razor, working deceitfully. Thou lovest evil more than good; and lying rather than to speak righteousness. Selah. Thou lovest all devouring words, O thou deceitful tongue." -- Psalm 52:1-4

This psalm was born in a moment of sharp indignation. David had learned that Doeg the Edomite, chief herdsman of Saul, had betrayed the priests of Nob to the king, and that as a result eighty-five innocent men had been slaughtered. And David, moved by the Holy Spirit, turns his gaze upon this treacherous informer and addresses him with a question of withering contempt. "Why boastest thou thyself in mischief, O mighty man?" It is a question that strips the mask from every bully, every tyrant, every man who uses his position and power to crush the innocent and then congratulates himself upon his cleverness.

Spurgeon observed that the world's idea of a mighty man is often nothing more than a mighty villain. The man who climbs to power by treachery, who maintains his position by cruelty, who silences his critics by violence -- such a man is called "mighty" by those who measure greatness by brute force. But in the eyes of God, he is a contemptible coward who uses his strength against the defenceless and his cunning against the unsuspecting. True might is the might of righteousness, and the man who wields his power for justice and mercy is mightier in the estimation of heaven than all the conquerors who ever rode at the head of their armies.

"Thy tongue deviseth mischiefs; like a sharp razor, working deceitfully." The comparison of the tongue to a razor is brilliantly apt. A razor is a tool designed for cutting, and in the hands of a skilled barber it performs a useful service. But in the hands of a malicious man, it becomes a weapon of destruction. And the treacherous tongue is worse than a razor in one crucial respect: the razor cuts openly, but the deceitful tongue cuts while pretending to be harmless. The flatterer smiles while he slanders. The informer professes friendship while he betrays. The gossip expresses concern while she destroys.

Thomas Watson wrote with characteristic sharpness upon the sins of the tongue, cataloguing the various forms of verbal wickedness with a precision that leaves no room for self-excuse. There is the tongue of the liar, who calls black white and white black. There is the tongue of the flatterer, who tells men what they wish to hear rather than what they need to hear. There is the tongue of the gossip, who spreads rumours as a farmer spreads manure, contaminating everything it touches. There is the tongue of the slanderer, who attacks the reputation of his neighbour behind his back. And there is the tongue of the informer, like Doeg, who betrays the innocent to the powerful for personal advantage.

"Thou lovest evil more than good; and lying rather than to speak righteousness." Here the psalmist lays bare the fundamental perversity of the wicked man's character. It is not merely that he sometimes does evil when he would prefer to do good, as even the best of men may do in moments of weakness. No, he loves evil. He prefers it. Given the choice between truth and falsehood, he chooses falsehood. Given the choice between righteousness and wickedness, he chooses wickedness. His moral compass is not merely broken; it is reversed. He calls evil good and good evil, and he does so not out of ignorance but out of a settled and deliberate preference for darkness over light.

John Owen analysed this phenomenon with theological precision, tracing it to the corruption of the will by indwelling sin. The unregenerate man does not sin reluctantly, as though forced by circumstances beyond his control. He sins willingly, because his will is enslaved to sin, and the things that are pleasing to God are distasteful to him. He loves evil because evil is congenial to his fallen nature, as darkness is congenial to the bat and filth is congenial to the swine. And until the sovereign grace of God intervenes to change his nature, to give him a new heart and a new spirit, he will continue to love evil and to hate good, no matter how much moral instruction he receives or how many good examples are set before him.

But the psalm does not leave the mighty man triumphant. It ends with a vision of his destruction and the vindication of the righteous. "God shall likewise destroy thee for ever, he shall take thee away, and pluck thee out of thy dwelling place, and root thee out of the land of the living." The mighty man who boasts in mischief shall be uprooted like a noxious weed and cast into the fire. But the righteous man "shall be like a green olive tree in the house of God," flourishing in the courts of the Lord, bearing fruit in due season, trusting in the mercy of God forever and ever.

Let us take warning from Doeg and all who follow his way. The tongue that devises mischief will be silenced. The man who boasts in evil will be brought low. But the man who trusts in the goodness of God shall flourish like a green olive tree, and his leaf shall not wither. Amen.
