PURITAN GOLD — Episode 13
"Wisdom's Call"
Week 2, Day 6 (Saturday) — Proverbs 8

---

Can you imagine wisdom... shouting?

Not whispering in a library. Not hidden behind the walls of a university. Not locked away in some exclusive meeting room where only the elite are invited. But standing at the crossroads of the busiest street in town, raising her voice above the noise of the market, calling out to anyone — everyone — who will listen?

That's how Proverbs 8 begins. And it's one of the most stunning images in all of Scripture.

Proverbs 8, verses 1 through 4:

"Doth not wisdom cry? And understanding put forth her voice? She standeth in the top of high places, by the way in the places of the paths. She crieth at the gates, at the entry of the city, at the coming in at the doors. Unto you, O men, I call; and my voice is to the sons of man."

This is Puritan Gold, and today we arrive at a chapter that the church fathers, the medieval theologians, the Reformers, and the Puritans all considered one of the most magnificent passages in the Old Testament. Proverbs 8 is not merely a collection of wise sayings. It is a revelation. A vision. A portrait of Wisdom personified — Wisdom as a living, speaking, eternal reality who calls out to the human race with an urgency that takes your breath away.

And what makes this passage so extraordinary is the contrast with what came just before it. In Proverbs 7, we heard the voice of the adulteress — the seductress who whispered in the shadows, who waited on dark corners, who lured the naive young man to his destruction. Her voice was private, secretive, flattering.

But Wisdom's voice? Wisdom doesn't whisper. She cries. She doesn't hide in shadows. She stands on the hilltops. She doesn't wait for you to stumble upon her. She goes to where you are — the gates, the crossroads, the busiest intersections of your life — and she calls out to you. Publicly. Boldly. Without apology.

And her message could not be more different from the seductress's. Listen to verses 6 through 11:

"Hear; for I will speak of excellent things; and the opening of my lips shall be right things. For my mouth shall speak truth; and wickedness is an abomination to my lips. All the words of my mouth are in righteousness; there is nothing froward or perverse in them. They are all plain to him that understandeth, and right to them that find knowledge. Receive my instruction, and not silver; and knowledge rather than choice gold. For wisdom is better than rubies; and all the things that may be desired are not to be compared to it."

Where the adulteress offered pleasure, Wisdom offers truth. Where the adulteress spoke with craft and subtlety, Wisdom speaks plainly. Where the adulteress's words were "smoother than oil," Wisdom's words are "right" and "righteous" — no hidden agenda, no manipulation, no concealed hook. Just... truth. Clear, beautiful, life-giving truth.

And notice the value comparison. Wisdom is better than silver. Better than gold. Better than rubies. Better than anything that can be desired. This is an astounding claim. Solomon — the richest man in the world, a man who had literally everything material wealth could buy — is saying that wisdom surpasses it all. If you had to choose between every treasure on earth and wisdom... choose wisdom. It's not even close.

A.W. Pink, in Studies in the Scriptures, wrote about this passage with great reverence. He noted that while the immediate subject of Proverbs 8 is wisdom personified, the New Testament gives us reason to see something — Someone — deeper. The Apostle Paul called Christ "the wisdom of God" in First Corinthians. And in Colossians, he wrote that in Christ "are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge." The early church fathers, reading Proverbs 8, saw in Wisdom's voice the voice of the eternal Son of God — calling to humanity before His incarnation, declaring His worth, inviting all who would hear to come and find life.

Whether or not you read this chapter as directly Christological, the connection is undeniable. Wisdom in Proverbs 8 does everything that Christ does in the Gospels. She calls to the simple and the foolish. She offers life to those who seek her. She stands in public places and declares truth without fear. And she warns that those who reject her are choosing death.

Now let's move to the heart of the chapter — one of the most breathtaking passages in all of Scripture. Verses 22 through 31:

"The Lord possessed me in the beginning of His way, before His works of old. I was set up from everlasting, from the beginning, or ever the earth was. When there were no depths, I was brought forth; when there were no fountains abounding with water. Before the mountains were settled, before the hills was I brought forth: While as yet He had not made the earth, nor the fields, nor the highest part of the dust of the world. When He prepared the heavens, I was there: when He set a compass upon the face of the depth: When He established the clouds above: when He strengthened the fountains of the deep: When He gave to the sea His decree, that the waters should not pass His commandment: when He appointed the foundations of the earth: Then I was by Him, as one brought up with Him: and I was daily His delight, rejoicing always before Him; Rejoicing in the habitable part of His earth; and my delights were with the sons of men."

Do you feel the weight of that? Wisdom was there... at the beginning. Before the earth existed. Before the mountains. Before the oceans. Before the first sunrise. When God set a compass upon the face of the deep — when He drew the circle of the horizon, when He laid the foundations of the world — Wisdom was there. Not as a spectator, but as a participant. By His side. His daily delight.

This is not merely a poetic device. This is a revelation about the nature of reality itself. Wisdom is not something invented by human beings. It's not a cultural construct or a philosophical tradition. Wisdom is woven into the fabric of creation. It was there before creation. The universe was built by it, through it, in accordance with it. When you live wisely, you are not just following good advice — you are aligning yourself with the fundamental structure of reality. You are living in harmony with the way God made the world to work.

And when you live foolishly? You are fighting against reality itself. You are swimming against the current of creation. And eventually, inevitably, reality wins.

Pink observed that this passage reveals why wisdom is so reliable. It's not reliable because clever people endorse it. It's reliable because the God who made the universe built it into the blueprint. Gravity works whether you believe in it or not. And wisdom works whether you follow it or not — the only question is whether you'll work with it or against it.

Now look at those last two verses again: "I was daily His delight, rejoicing always before Him; rejoicing in the habitable part of His earth; and my delights were with the sons of men."

There is joy here. Deep, overflowing, cosmic joy. Wisdom delights in God. God delights in Wisdom. And Wisdom delights in... us. In the sons of men. In the habitable part of the earth — the world where human beings live and love and struggle and seek. Wisdom is not cold. Not clinical. Not academic. Wisdom is joyful. And she finds her joy in the company of ordinary people like you and me.

This changes everything about how we pursue wisdom. We're not pursuing a dry, abstract principle. We're pursuing a living reality that delights in being found. That wants to be known. That stands on the hilltop and shouts, "Come! Come to me! I'm here! I'm calling you!"

And that leads us to the climax of the chapter. Verses 34 and 35:

"Blessed is the man that heareth me, watching daily at my gates, waiting at the posts of my doors. For whoso findeth me findeth life, and shall obtain favour of the Lord."

Blessed is the man who watches daily at wisdom's gates. Not weekly. Not occasionally. Daily. The picture is of someone who positions himself at the doorway of wisdom every single morning, waiting to hear her speak. Watching for her. Eager for her instruction. This is a picture of the devoted student, the hungry disciple, the man or woman who has tasted wisdom and found it so satisfying that they can't stay away.

And the reward? "Whoso findeth me findeth life." Not just information. Not just good decisions. Life. Real, abundant, God-honoring life. And "favour of the Lord" — the smile of God. His approval. His blessing.

But then comes the warning, in verse 36:

"But he that sinneth against me wrongeth his own soul: all they that hate me love death."

All they that hate wisdom love death. That is a sobering sentence. There are only two options. Find wisdom, find life. Reject wisdom, embrace death. There is no third category. No neutral ground. No "I'll think about it later." You are either moving toward wisdom or away from it. And to move away from it — whether through active rebellion or passive neglect — is to move toward death.

So how do we cultivate wisdom in our daily lives? Let me offer four practical steps as we close.

First, listen for wisdom's call. She is not hiding. She's in the public square. She's in the open Scriptures. She's in the counsel of godly friends. She's in the quiet promptings of the Holy Spirit. But you have to have ears to hear. And that means slowing down. Turning off the noise. Creating space in your life for her voice to be heard above the clamor.

Second, value wisdom above everything else. This is a daily battle in a materialistic world. Every advertisement, every cultural message tells you that money, success, pleasure, and comfort are the highest goods. Solomon says they're not. They're not even close. Wisdom is better than rubies. Do you believe that? Do you live as if you believe that?

Third, watch daily at wisdom's gates. Make the pursuit of wisdom a daily discipline. Open your Bible every morning. Pray for understanding. Read the works of wise men and women — the Puritans, the Reformers, teachers like Pink who have gone deep into the truth of God. Don't be a sporadic seeker. Be a daily watcher.

Fourth, align your life with reality. Since wisdom is woven into the fabric of creation, living wisely is not restrictive — it's liberating. It's living in harmony with how things actually work. When you tell the truth, you're living in harmony with the God of truth. When you love your neighbor, you're living in harmony with the God of love. When you work diligently, rest appropriately, give generously, and forgive freely — you are surfing the wave of reality rather than being pounded by it.

Wisdom is calling, friends. Right now. In this moment. She's standing at the crossroads of your life, raising her voice above the noise. She's not selling you something. She's not manipulating you. She's offering you life. Real life. The kind of life that begins in the fear of the Lord and never, ever ends.

Will you answer?

Let's pray.

Father of all wisdom, we hear the call. Through the noise of our busy lives, through the competing voices that demand our attention, we hear Wisdom crying out. Give us ears to hear her and hearts to respond. Teach us to value wisdom above silver, above gold, above all the things this world says we should want. Help us to watch daily at her gates — to be students of Your truth, lovers of Your Word, seekers of understanding. And as we find wisdom, may we find life... and favor with You. Through Jesus Christ, in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden. Amen.
