PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 14
"Who Shall Deliver Us?"
Romans for Marriage — Day 14


Welcome, husband and wife. There is something powerful about two hearts turning toward God at the same time. When you both face the same direction, toward Christ, everything in your marriage finds its proper alignment. Let us do that now.

Every marriage needs a foundation that does not shift. Let us hear from that foundation now, from Romans 7:24-25:

"O wretched man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death? I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord. So then with the mind I myself serve the law of God; but with the flesh the law of sin."

That is Romans 7:24-25. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


The Puritans treasured marriage as a covenant made before God. John Bunyan wrote beautifully about how Scripture speaks to the hearts of married couples.

Paul's cry in Romans 7:24 is the most desperate plea in all his writings: 'O wretched man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death?' This is not the cry of an unbeliever — it is the cry of a mature apostle who feels the full weight of indwelling sin. He calls himself wretched. He feels trapped in a body of death. He cannot deliver himself.

John Bunyan knew this cry intimately. His autobiography, Grace Abounding to the Chief of Sinners, records years of agonizing struggle with doubt, sin, and spiritual torment. Bunyan felt the wretchedness Paul describes — the horror of knowing what is right and being unable to consistently do it. He understood that the Christian life, on this side of glory, includes genuine spiritual anguish.

But notice how quickly the cry turns to triumph: 'I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord!' The answer to 'Who shall deliver me?' is not self-improvement, not willpower, not a better strategy — it is Jesus Christ our Lord. Deliverance comes from a Person, not a program. It comes from grace, not from gritting your teeth harder.

Bunyan's entire literary career was built on this truth: we are helpless apart from Christ, but in Christ we are delivered. Christian in The Pilgrim's Progress could not remove his own burden. He had to come to the cross. And at the cross, the burden rolled away — not by Christian's effort, but by Christ's sacrifice.

For marriage, this truth is life-giving. There are problems in your marriage that you cannot fix. There are patterns you cannot break in your own strength. There are wounds you cannot heal by trying harder. But Jesus Christ can. The one who delivered Paul from the body of death can deliver your marriage from whatever is holding it captive. The cry of the desperate heart is not a sign of failure — it is the beginning of deliverance. When you stop trusting in yourself and start trusting in Christ, everything changes.


Have you reached the end of yourselves in any area of your marriage? Good. That is exactly where God meets you. Paul's cry of wretchedness was immediately followed by thanksgiving — because the end of self is the beginning of Christ's work.

Stop trying to fix your marriage in your own power. Stop relying on your own wisdom, your own patience, your own goodness. Cry out together: 'Who shall deliver us?' And then answer together: 'Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord!'

This is not giving up — it is giving over. You are surrendering the impossible to the One for whom nothing is impossible. Let tonight be the night your marriage moves from self-reliance to Christ-dependence.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Now turn to each other. Here are tonight's reflection questions. Answer from the heart, not from the head.

Question 1: Is there an area of your marriage where you have been trying to 'deliver yourselves' by your own effort? How is that working?

Question 2: What does it look like to move from self-reliance to Christ-dependence in your marriage?

Question 3: How does Paul's immediate transition from 'wretched man' to 'I thank God' give you hope tonight?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


The Puritans ended every evening with prayer. Let us follow their example and bring our marriage before the Lord.

Tonight's prayer focus: Cry out to God together for deliverance — from destructive patterns, from recurring sin, from the weight of your own limitations. Then thank Him through Jesus Christ, who is your deliverer. Rest in His ability, not your own.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the Deliverer meet you in your weakness tonight. May Jesus Christ be the answer to every 'who shall deliver us' in your marriage. Thanks be to God — He is able.

May the words you have heard tonight bear fruit in your marriage for years to come. The Lord be with you both. Until next time.
