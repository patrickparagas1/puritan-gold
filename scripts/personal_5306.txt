PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Pastoral Encouragement, Day 6
"Spiritual Warfare"
Fighting the Good Fight — The Believer's Battle


Welcome, friend. Tonight we take up a subject that every serious Christian must face: the reality of spiritual warfare. The Puritans did not romanticize the Christian life. They knew it was a battle, and they prepared for it accordingly. Let us sit under their counsel tonight and take up the whole armor of God.


Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Ephesians 6:11-12:

"Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil. For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.


The Puritans took spiritual warfare seriously because they took the Bible seriously. Scripture is clear: the believer has enemies. Not merely human adversaries, not merely internal struggles, but a personal, intelligent, malevolent enemy who seeks the destruction of the saints. Paul names him plainly: the devil. And Paul describes his method: wiles — cunning stratagems, subtle deceptions, carefully laid traps.

William Gurnall, in his massive work "The Christian in Complete Armour," devotes over a thousand pages to the exposition of Ephesians 6:10-20. Gurnall knew that the Christian life is a war from beginning to end. He writes that the believer who does not understand this will be perpetually confused by the difficulties of the Christian life. Why is prayer so hard? Because an enemy opposes you. Why does temptation come so fiercely after spiritual victories? Because the enemy counterattacks. Why does doubt assail you in your weakest moments? Because the enemy knows when to strike.

Gurnall identifies several of Satan's primary strategies. The first is accusation. The devil is called "the accuser of our brethren" in Revelation 12:10. He takes the believer's real sins and uses them to undermine his assurance. He whispers, "You are not truly saved. A real Christian would not struggle with this sin. God cannot love someone as wicked as you." The Puritan response to accusation is not denial but the gospel. Yes, I am a sinner. But Christ died for sinners. My standing before God rests not on my performance but on His Son's righteousness imputed to me.

The second strategy is deception. Satan is a liar and the father of lies (John 8:44). He deceives about the nature of sin, making it appear harmless, attractive, or necessary. He deceives about the character of God, making Him appear harsh, distant, or indifferent. He deceives about the promises of Scripture, making them seem unreliable or inapplicable to one's particular situation. Thomas Brooks, in "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices," catalogues dozens of these deceptions and provides specific biblical antidotes for each one.

The third strategy is distraction. Satan knows that he cannot destroy the believer — the elect are secure in Christ — but he can render the believer ineffective. He does this by filling the mind with trivial concerns, by stoking anxieties about the future, by fostering conflicts within the church, and by promoting a spirit of busyness that crowds out prayer, Scripture reading, and fellowship. The believer who is too busy to pray is exactly where the enemy wants him.

How then shall we fight? The Puritans offer counsel that is as practical as it is spiritual.

First, know your enemy. Do not be ignorant of Satan's devices. Study the Scriptures that reveal his character and methods. Read the Puritans who have mapped his strategies. The soldier who knows the enemy's tactics is far better prepared than the one who goes into battle blind.

Second, put on the whole armor of God. Paul's metaphor in Ephesians 6 is not decorative. Each piece of armor represents a spiritual reality: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, and the Word of God. The belt of truth means living in honesty before God and men. The breastplate of righteousness means both the imputed righteousness of Christ and the practical righteousness of daily obedience. The shield of faith means trusting God's promises against every fiery dart of doubt, temptation, and accusation.

Third, pray without ceasing. Paul ends the armor passage with an exhortation to prayer: "Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit" (Ephesians 6:18). Prayer is not merely one weapon among many. It is the atmosphere in which all the other weapons are wielded. The believer who does not pray is a soldier without orders, fighting blindly and without reinforcement.

Fourth, fight in community. The Puritans knew that isolated Christians are vulnerable Christians. Satan picks off the straggler. The believer who is rooted in a faithful church, surrounded by godly friends, and accountable to wise counselors is far harder to bring down than the one who tries to go it alone.

Fifth, remember that the war has already been won. Christ's death and resurrection dealt the decisive blow to Satan's kingdom. The enemy we face is a defeated foe. He still has power, but he does not have sovereignty. He still has cunning, but he does not have the final word. The outcome of the war is not in doubt. We fight from victory, not toward it.

Take courage tonight. The battle is real, but so is the Commander who leads you. Greater is He that is in you than he that is in the world.


Before we close, sit with these questions.

Question 1: Am I aware of the specific strategies Satan uses against me, or have I been fighting blindly?

Question 2: Which piece of the armor of God am I most neglecting — truth, righteousness, faith, the Word, prayer?

Question 3: Am I fighting in community, or have I allowed myself to become isolated and therefore vulnerable?

Do not rush past these questions. Sit with them. Journal about them if you can. The Puritans believed that truth must be digested, not merely tasted.


Almighty God, we acknowledge that we wrestle not against flesh and blood but against spiritual powers. Clothe us in Thy armor. Strengthen us by Thy Spirit. Remind us that the victory belongs to Christ. Keep us vigilant, keep us humble, and keep us fighting until the battle is done. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Go in peace. The God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly.
