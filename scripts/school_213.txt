Welcome to today's lesson. The Puritans were some of the most learned men and women of their age, and yet they counted all their learning as nothing compared to knowing Christ. Let us follow their example today.

God of all knowledge, we begin with You. Without Your light, even the brightest minds walk in darkness. Shine Your truth into our studies today. Help us to love learning and to learn in love. For the glory of Your name. Amen.

Let us read a passage slowly, letting each word settle into our minds.

Our Scripture for today is Revelation 19:16:

"And he hath on his vesture and on his thigh a name written, KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS."

Begin this day by bowing your heart before Christ your King. He is not merely your Savior and Friend — He is your sovereign Lord, the King of kings, before whom every knee will bow. Yet His kingship is not harsh or tyrannical; it is gracious and life-giving. His yoke is easy. His burden is light. To serve Him is perfect freedom. Surrender every area of your life to His authority today — your plans, your relationships, your words, your thoughts. Let Him reign in your heart without rival or competitor. As we prepare for today's lesson, remember that everything we study flows from this one fountain: the Word of God. Our lesson today is part of The Work of Christ, and it is titled: Christ as King — His Rule in Our Lives. Let us proceed with minds engaged and hearts open.

SEGMENT 2: MAIN LESSON PART 1 -- BIBLICAL FOUNDATION
----------------------------------------

Let us proceed to the main body of today's teaching.

The first part of our lesson establishes the biblical foundation for today's topic. We will examine what God's Word teaches us about the themes in Revelation 19:16.

The kingship of Christ is the third and culminating aspect of His threefold office. As Prophet, He reveals God's truth. As Priest, He reconciles us to God. As King, He rules over all things for the good of His people and the glory of His Father. Revelation 19:16 presents Christ in His full regal glory: "And he hath on his vesture and on his thigh a name written, KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS." This title declares Christ's absolute supremacy over every earthly ruler, every angelic being, and every power in the universe. There is no king above Him. There is no lord who outranks Him. He is the sovereign of sovereigns, the ruler of rulers, the supreme authority in heaven and on earth.

Philippians 2:9-11 describes the exaltation that followed Christ's humiliation: "Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name: That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father." The scope of Christ's kingship is universal — it extends to heaven, earth, and under the earth. Every creature will ultimately acknowledge His lordship. Some will bow willingly in worship; others will bow unwillingly in judgment. But every knee will bow, and every tongue will confess that Jesus Christ is Lord.

Psalm 2:6-8 records the Father's decree concerning the Son's kingship: "Yet have I set my king upon my holy hill of Zion. I will declare the decree: the LORD hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee. Ask of me, and I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance, and the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession." Christ's kingship is not self-assumed but divinely appointed. The Father has set Him as King upon Zion — a reference to the heavenly city of which earthly Jerusalem was a type. And the Father has given Him the nations as His inheritance. The entire earth belongs to Christ by divine decree.

Matthew 28:18 records Christ's own declaration after His resurrection: "All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth." The word "power" is the Greek "exousia," which means authority — the right to rule and command. This authority is not partial but total: "all power." It is not limited to one realm but extends to both "heaven and earth." The risen Christ possesses absolute authority over every dimension of reality. This is the foundation of the Great Commission that follows: "Go ye therefore, and teach all nations" (v. 19). Because Christ has all authority, His disciples can go to all nations with confidence that their Lord reigns supreme.

Christ's kingship has two aspects that must be carefully distinguished. First, there is His universal kingship — His sovereign rule over all creation. In this sense, Christ is King whether people acknowledge it or not. He governs the nations, directs the course of history, and controls the forces of nature. "The king's heart is in the hand of the LORD, as the rivers of water: he turneth it whithersoever he will" (Proverbs 21:1). Even rulers who do not know Christ or who actively oppose Him are under His sovereign control.

Second, there is His mediatorial or spiritual kingship — His gracious rule in the hearts and lives of His people. In this sense, Christ becomes King in a person's life when that person is converted and submits to His lordship. This is the kingdom that Jesus preached: "The kingdom of God is within you" (Luke 17:21). It is a kingdom entered by the new birth (John 3:5), advanced by the preaching of the gospel, and characterized by "righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost" (Romans 14:17).

The implications of Christ's kingship for the believer are comprehensive. If Christ is King, then He has the right to govern every area of our lives — our thoughts, our words, our actions, our relationships, our finances, our ambitions, our entertainment. There is no sphere of life over which Christ does not claim authority. Paul expressed this total surrender in 2 Corinthians 10:5: "Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ." Even our thought life is subject to the King's authority.

Christ's kingship also provides the believer with protection and security. Because Christ is King of kings, no enemy can ultimately prevail against His people. Romans 8:31 declares, "If God be for us, who can be against us?" The forces arrayed against the believer — sin, Satan, the world, death itself — are all subject to Christ's sovereign authority. He has already defeated them at the cross and will fully manifest their defeat at His return. Until then, His people live under the protection of the mightiest King who has ever reigned or will ever reign.

The kingship of Christ will be fully manifested at His second coming, when He will return in glory to judge the living and the dead and to establish His kingdom in its consummate form. Revelation 11:15 anticipates that day: "The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever." Until that day, believers live as citizens of a kingdom that is already present but not yet fully realized — already reigning with Christ by faith, yet still awaiting the day when His reign will be visible to all.

This biblical foundation is essential. Without it, everything else we study today would be like a house built on sand. But with it, we stand on solid rock.

SEGMENT 3: MAIN LESSON PART 2 -- PURITAN TEACHING
----------------------------------------

Now we turn to the Puritan perspective. John Flavel wrote extensively on this subject in "The Fountain of Life." The Puritans were not merely theologians in ivory towers. They were pastors, teachers, and parents who applied God's truth to every dimension of life.

John Flavel's exposition of Christ's kingly office in "The Fountain of Life" reflects the characteristically Puritan emphasis on the practical lordship of Christ over every dimension of the believer's life. For the Puritans, the kingship of Christ was not merely a theological category but a daily, lived reality. Flavel wrote that to confess Christ as King means to place the entire life — every thought, every desire, every choice, every relationship — under His sovereign authority. There can be no area of life where Christ is not Lord, for a partial lordship is no lordship at all.

Flavel distinguished three aspects of Christ's kingly work. First, Christ conquers — He subdues the hearts of sinners by His grace, overcoming their resistance and bringing them willingly under His authority. This is the work of conversion, where Christ the King invades the rebel territory of the human heart and establishes His throne. Flavel described conversion as a spiritual conquest: the sinner is a rebel in arms against God, and Christ, by the power of the Holy Spirit, storms the citadel of the heart, casts down the banners of self-will and sin, and raises His own standard in their place. Yet this conquest is accomplished not by force but by love. Christ wins the hearts of His people by revealing His beauty, His grace, and His mercy. As Flavel wrote: "Christ conquers not by the sword of His power but by the scepter of His grace."

Second, Christ protects — He defends His people against their spiritual enemies. The Christian life is warfare (Ephesians 6:12), and the believer faces formidable adversaries: the world, the flesh, and the devil. But Christ the King provides protection. He sends His angels to guard His people (Psalm 91:11). He limits the power of Satan (Job 1:12). He provides spiritual armor (Ephesians 6:13-17). He ensures that no temptation will be more than His people can bear (1 Corinthians 10:13). Flavel wrote that the believer should take great comfort from the fact that his King is also his Defender: "If Christ be your King, then all His royal power is engaged for your protection. You need not fear what man can do unto you, for the King of heaven has promised to keep you as the apple of His eye."

Third, Christ governs — He directs the daily affairs of His people by His Word and Spirit. Flavel taught that Christ's kingly government over the believer is exercised primarily through the Scriptures and the internal work of the Holy Spirit. The Bible is the King's law — it reveals His will, establishes His standards, and guides the believer in every area of life. The Holy Spirit is the King's representative — He applies Christ's rule to the individual heart, convicting of sin, illuminating truth, and empowering obedience.

Flavel explored the nature of Christ's kingdom with great care. He emphasized that Christ's kingdom is fundamentally different from earthly kingdoms. It is spiritual, not temporal. It advances by the preaching of the Word, not by the force of arms. Its weapons are truth, love, and holiness, not swords and spears. Its citizens are the redeemed from every nation, tongue, and tribe. Its laws are written not on tablets of stone but on the hearts of believers. Its rewards are not earthly wealth and power but eternal life, joy, and glory.

Yet Flavel was also clear that Christ's spiritual kingdom has implications for every sphere of earthly life. The Puritans believed that because Christ is Lord of all, there is no area of human existence — politics, economics, education, art, science, family life — that is outside the scope of His authority. They did not seek to establish a theocracy (the direct rule of clergy over the state), but they did insist that all human institutions should be governed by biblical principles and submit to the moral law of God as proclaimed by Christ the King.

Flavel paid particular attention to the subjective experience of Christ's kingship in the believer's heart. He observed that the hallmark of a true subject of Christ is willing obedience. A person may be compelled to obey an earthly king through fear of punishment, but Christ's true subjects obey Him out of love, gratitude, and delight. Flavel quoted Psalm 110:3: "Thy people shall be willing in the day of thy power." Christ's kingship does not destroy human freedom; it transforms it. Before conversion, the sinner is a slave to sin, unable to obey God even if he wanted to. After conversion, the believer is freed from the tyranny of sin and enabled to serve Christ joyfully. Flavel wrote: "The service of Christ is perfect freedom. His yoke is easy and His burden is light, not because He demands little, but because He gives much. He gives the grace to do what He commands."

The Puritan vision of Christ's kingship also included the hope of His future return and the consummation of His kingdom. Flavel wrote with great anticipation about the day when Christ would return in visible glory to judge the nations and establish His kingdom in its fullness. He taught that this hope should motivate believers to faithful obedience in the present — not out of fear but out of eager desire to please their coming King. The Puritans lived with a strong sense of eternal perspective, measuring everything in this life against the standard of the age to come. Their commitment to Christ's lordship in every area of life was rooted not in legalism but in love — the love of loyal subjects who live in joyful anticipation of their King's glorious appearing.

The Puritan approach reminds us that theology is never abstract. It is always personal and always practical. What we believe about God shapes everything we do.

SEGMENT 4: DISCUSSION AND APPLICATION
----------------------------------------

Let us consider together what these truths mean for our lives.

Discussion Question 1: What does it mean practically that Christ is 'King of kings and Lord of lords'? How should this title affect the way we view earthly authorities and power structures?

Discussion Question 2: Philippians 2:9-11 says every knee will bow and every tongue confess. What is the difference between bowing willingly in worship and bowing unwillingly in judgment?

Discussion Question 3: Flavel said Christ conquers hearts 'not by the sword of His power but by the scepter of His grace.' What does this mean for how we understand conversion?

Discussion Question 4: If Christ has 'all power in heaven and in earth' (Matthew 28:18), what are the implications for areas of life we tend to keep under our own control?

Discussion Question 5: How does the hope of Christ's future return and the consummation of His kingdom affect the way we live today?

Take time with each question. The Puritans valued what they called "holy conference," the practice of discussing spiritual truths together. Do not settle for quick, surface answers. Push deeper. Ask follow-up questions. Listen carefully to one another.

Application is where truth becomes life. It is not enough to know what Revelation 19:16 says. We must ask: how does this change the way I live today? How does this shape my thoughts, my words, my actions? The Puritans insisted that a truth that does not transform is a truth that has not been truly understood.

SEGMENT 5: AFTERNOON ACTIVITY
----------------------------------------

Let us transition from study to practice. Here is your afternoon activity.

Students will write a declaration of allegiance to Christ the King. Modeled on historic declarations and covenants (such as the Scottish National Covenant or the Puritan church covenants), students will compose a formal, thoughtful written statement declaring their submission to the lordship of Christ over specific areas of their lives. The declaration should include: (1) an acknowledgment of who Christ is (His qualifications as King), (2) specific areas of life they are placing under His authority (academics, relationships, entertainment, future plans, thought life), (3) a commitment to obey His Word as their supreme rule of life, and (4) a reliance on His grace for the strength to fulfill this commitment. Students should write seriously and reverently, as this is a spiritual exercise, not merely an academic one.

Materials needed: Quality paper or parchment-style paper, pens (consider calligraphy pens for a more formal feel), Bibles for reference, sample historical covenants for format inspiration

As you complete this activity, think about how it connects to what you studied in today's lesson. The goal is not just to do something, but to reinforce the truths you have been learning. The Puritans believed that education should engage the whole person: mind, heart, and hands.

Take your time with this activity. Quality matters more than speed. When you are finished, reflect on what you learned through the process of doing. Often we understand something far better after we have tried to put it into practice.

SEGMENT 6: CROSS-SUBJECT CONNECTIONS
----------------------------------------

One of the great insights of the Puritan educational tradition is that all subjects are interconnected under the lordship of Christ. There is no such thing as a purely secular subject. History, literature, and even the natural sciences all reveal the glory and wisdom of God. Let us explore those connections now.

History Connection:

The concept of kingship has evolved dramatically throughout human history. Study the development of monarchy from ancient Near Eastern god-kings through the feudal system of medieval Europe to the constitutional monarchies and democratic revolutions of the modern era. Discuss how the biblical concept of Christ's kingship — a righteous, just, and compassionate rule — served as both a critique of unjust human rulers and an inspiration for better forms of government.

Literature Connection:

The theme of the returning king is one of the most powerful archetypes in world literature. J.R.R. Tolkien's 'The Return of the King' draws explicitly on the biblical pattern of the rightful king who comes to reclaim his throne and establish justice. C.S. Lewis's Aslan functions as a Christ-figure whose reign brings peace and restoration to Narnia. Study how these literary treatments of kingship reflect and illuminate the biblical teaching about Christ's present and future reign.

Science Connection:

The concept of hierarchy and order is fundamental to the natural sciences. In ecology, food chains and ecosystems are organized in hierarchical structures. In chemistry, the periodic table organizes elements in order of increasing complexity. In astronomy, celestial bodies are organized in hierarchical systems (planets orbit stars, stars orbit galactic centers). These patterns of order and hierarchy in creation reflect the sovereign ordering of a King who created and sustains all things by the word of His power.

As you continue your studies in other subjects, look for connections to what you have learned today. The Puritans saw every branch of knowledge as a thread in the great tapestry of God's truth. Train your eye to see those connections, and your education will be richer for it.

We have reached the final segment of today's lesson. The Puritans placed great value on review and memorization. They knew that what is not reviewed is soon forgotten. Let us take time now to consolidate what we have learned.

Memory Verse:

"And he hath on his vesture and on his thigh a name written, KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS." -- Revelation 19:16

Read this verse aloud five times. Then close your eyes and try to recite it from memory. Write it out by hand on a card or in your journal. The Puritans memorized vast portions of Scripture, and it was one of the great sources of their spiritual strength. Even one verse, truly hidden in the heart, can sustain you in times of trial.

We have covered much ground today. Take time this evening to reflect on what stood out to you most. Talk about it with your family. Write it in your journal. Let these truths take root. Until next time, students.
