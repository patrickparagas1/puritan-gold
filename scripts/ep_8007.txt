PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"Dead in Trespasses and Sins"
July 7, 2026

---

"And you hath he quickened, who were dead in trespasses and sins; wherein in time past ye walked according to the course of this world, according to the prince of the power of the air, the spirit that now worketh in the children of disobedience: among whom also we all had our conversation in times past in the lusts of our flesh, fulfilling the desires of the flesh and of the mind; and were by nature the children of wrath, even as others." -- Ephesians 2:1-3

After the glorious heights of chapter one, Paul now takes us into the depths -- the dark valley of our natural condition apart from grace. 'You were dead in trespasses and sins.' Not sick, not weak, not struggling -- dead. This is the Bible's diagnosis of the human condition, and it is the foundation upon which all true theology must be built.

The Puritans understood the doctrine of total depravity with a clarity and seriousness that is largely lost in our superficial age. They did not mean that every person is as bad as they could possibly be, but that every part of every person -- mind, will, affections, conscience -- is corrupted by sin and unable to contribute anything toward salvation. As Thomas Boston wrote in his masterwork 'Human Nature in Its Fourfold State,' man in his natural condition is not merely at a disadvantage; he is utterly helpless, as incapable of spiritual life as a corpse in a coffin is incapable of rising and walking.

Paul identifies three masters that governed our lives before grace: the world, the devil, and the flesh. We walked according to the course of this world -- following its fashions, imbibing its values, pursuing its pleasures. We were under the influence of the prince of the power of the air -- Satan himself, that ancient serpent who blinds the minds of the unbelieving and leads them captive at his will. And we were slaves to the lusts of our flesh -- not merely outward sins of the body, but inward corruptions of the mind.

And the verdict? 'Children of wrath, even as others.' By nature, not merely by practice, we were objects of God's just displeasure. We did not become children of wrath by sinning; we sinned because we were children of wrath. The corruption was in the root before it appeared in the fruit.

Why does Paul rehearse this dismal catalogue? Because we can never appreciate the height of grace until we understand the depth from which grace has lifted us. The man who thinks he was merely a little confused, a little mistaken, a little off course, will never marvel at his salvation. But the man who knows he was dead, enslaved, condemned -- that man will sing of grace forever. Amen.
