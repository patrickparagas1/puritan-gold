PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 24
"By Wisdom a House Is Built"
Proverbs 24:3-4

---

Welcome, dear ones. Marriage is a gift from God, and so is this time together in His presence. Whether this has been an easy day or a hard one, you are here, together, and that matters more than you know.

Our Scripture reading this evening is from Proverbs 24:3-4. Let us receive it together:

"Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established: And by knowledge shall the chambers be filled with all precious and pleasant riches."

That is Proverbs 24:3-4. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

The Puritans understood marriage in a way that the modern world has largely forgotten. Let us hear from Matthew Henry, who wrote with great wisdom on this subject.

Matthew Henry sees in these verses a comprehensive blueprint for the godly home. Three divine qualities build, establish, and furnish it: wisdom, understanding, and knowledge. Henry writes: 'Wisdom, understanding, and knowledge are the three pillars upon which the house of the godly stands. Take away any one, and the structure is weakened; take away all three, and it falls.'

Henry distinguishes the three: wisdom is the master builder, the one who designs and directs. Understanding is the one who stabilizes, who takes the raw materials and establishes them in proper order. Knowledge fills the rooms with the treasures of truth, grace, and godly living. Together, these three produce a home that is not merely standing but flourishing.

Henry applies this directly to the domestic sphere: 'The house here is not merely the building, but the family. It is built — that is, the family is raised and maintained — by wisdom.' This means that building a family requires more than love and good intentions; it requires the application of divine wisdom to every aspect of domestic life: financial management, conflict resolution, child-rearing, spiritual leadership, and the cultivation of hospitality.

Charles Bridges adds a beautiful comment on the 'precious and pleasant riches' that fill the chambers: these are not primarily material goods but spiritual blessings. 'The true riches of a home are peace, love, joy, and the knowledge of God,' Bridges writes. 'A house may be splendidly furnished with the goods of this world yet empty of all that truly matters. But the house built by wisdom is filled with treasures that moth and rust cannot corrupt.'

--- Marriage Application ---

This passage gives you a vision for your home that goes beyond mere survival. God's design is not that your household would merely endure but that it would be filled with precious and pleasant riches: the riches of mutual love, of shared faith, of children raised in the knowledge of God, of hospitality extended to others, of generosity flowing outward.

How are you building? Are you building with wisdom — seeking God's design for your home, not the world's? Are you establishing with understanding — applying truth consistently and thoughtfully? Are you filling the rooms with knowledge — making your home a place where God is known, discussed, worshipped, and loved?

Consider the 'chambers' of your home: your bedroom, your living room, your kitchen table, your children's spaces. What fills them? The noise of screens, the tension of unresolved conflict, the emptiness of emotional distance? Or the richness of conversation, the warmth of shared prayer, the beauty of a life lived together before God?

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

The Puritans practiced what they called holy conference, speaking honestly about spiritual things with those they loved most. Do that now with these questions.

Question 1: Which of the three — wisdom, understanding, or knowledge — does our household most need to grow in right now?

Question 2: What are the 'riches' that currently fill the chambers of our home, and are they precious and pleasant?

Question 3: What is one specific way we can apply wisdom to an area of our domestic life that has been neglected?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

It is time to bring what we have heard and discussed before the Lord in prayer. Pray out loud if you can. Let your spouse hear your heart.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to be the architect of your home, building it with His wisdom, establishing it with His understanding, and filling every room with the precious riches of His grace. Pray for a home that overflows with spiritual abundance.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord who builds by wisdom and establishes by understanding fill every chamber of your home with riches that endure forever. Amen.

May the God who brought you together continue to bind you together in love, in faith, and in purpose. Sleep well tonight. He watches over your marriage.
