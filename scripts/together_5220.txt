PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 20
"Praying Together Daily"
Domestical Duties — Puritan Household Duties

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we come to what William Gouge considered the single most important practice in the Christian home: prayer together. If you have already been praying together each evening as part of this devotional, you have been practicing what the Puritans regarded as the lifeblood of a godly marriage.

Let us read our passage. Matthew 18:19-20 (KJV):

"Again I say unto you, That if two of you shall agree on earth as touching any thing that they shall ask, it shall be done for them of my Father which is in heaven. For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them."

--- Teaching ---

William Gouge lamented that many couples who professed faith in Christ seldom prayed together. They prayed privately, which was good. They prayed at meals, which was a beginning. But they rarely knelt side by side, opened their hearts before God, and interceded for each other, for their children, for their household, and for the world. Gouge considered this a grave neglect. He argued that joint prayer is not merely one duty among many; it is the duty that sustains all the others. The couple that prays together will find strength to love, to forgive, to submit, to serve, and to endure. The couple that neglects prayer together will find all of these duties increasingly difficult.

Consider the promise of our Lord in Matthew 18:19-20. When two agree in prayer, the Father hears and answers. And when two or three are gathered in His name, He is present in their midst. What more powerful encouragement could there be for a husband and wife to pray together? When you kneel together in prayer, Christ Himself stands in your room. When you agree together in petition, the Father inclines His ear. Your bedroom becomes the throne room of God.

Gouge offered practical counsel for couples who found joint prayer difficult. He understood that for many, praying out loud in front of another person is intimidating. He counseled couples to begin simply. Start with short prayers. Start with sentence prayers. Start with reading a psalm together and then offering a brief response to God. Do not wait until you feel eloquent or spiritual. Pray in your own words, in your own way, with the simplicity of children speaking to their Father.

Gouge also addressed the issue of consistency. He knew that the greatest enemy of joint prayer is not hostility but busyness. Couples intend to pray together, but the evening slips away, and before they know it, one of them is asleep. Gouge counseled couples to set a fixed time for prayer and to guard it as they would guard any other essential appointment. It need not be long. Even five minutes of sincere, focused prayer together is immeasurably better than none at all.

Richard Baxter added a beautiful observation. He noted that there is no act in marriage that creates deeper intimacy than prayer. When a husband prays for his wife in her presence, she hears what is in his heart. She hears his gratitude for her. She hears his concerns for her. She hears his dependence on God for the strength to love her well. When a wife prays for her husband in his presence, he hears the same. These are words that might never be spoken in ordinary conversation but flow naturally in the presence of God. Prayer breaks down walls that no amount of talking can breach.

Baxter also warned that prayerlessness in marriage is a symptom of spiritual decline. When a couple stops praying together, it is often a sign that they are drifting apart. The return to joint prayer is often the first step in healing a struggling marriage. Before you try to fix your communication, your finances, or your conflicts, try praying together. The presence of God has a way of putting everything else in its proper perspective.

Dear couple, if you are already praying together, be encouraged. You are doing one of the most powerful things you can do for your marriage. If you have not yet established this practice, tonight is the night to begin. Do not be intimidated. Do not wait until you are ready. Simply bow your heads, hold each other's hands, and speak to God as children speak to their Father. He is listening. He is present. And He delights in the prayers of a husband and wife who come to Him together.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: How consistent are we in praying together? What obstacles prevent us from making it a daily practice?

Question 2: What would we like to be praying about together regularly? Let us make a short list of shared prayer concerns.

Question 3: How has praying together, when we have done it, affected our relationship? What changes have we noticed?

--- Prayer ---

Father in heaven, we come before Thee tonight as husband and wife, and we confess that we have not prayed together as faithfully as we ought. Forgive us for the evenings we let slip away without seeking Thy face. Help us to establish the holy habit of daily prayer together. Overcome our fears, our excuses, and our busyness. Meet us in this room tonight, as Thou hast promised. Draw us closer to Thee and to each other through the discipline of prayer. May our home be known in heaven as a house of prayer. In the name of Jesus, who ever liveth to make intercession for us, Amen.
