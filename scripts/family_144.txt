PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 13
"Contentment in Family Trials"
Pilgrim Families — Week 2, Day 6

---

Good evening, family. There is no place God loves to meet His people more than when families gather in His name. Welcome to Puritan Gold. Let us open our Bibles and our hearts.

Let us begin in prayer. Lord Jesus, You said that where two or three are gathered in Your name, there You are in the midst of them. We claim that promise tonight as a family. Be here with us. Teach us. Shape us. We pray in Your holy name. Amen.

Every word of Scripture is breathed out by God. Tonight we receive His breath from Romans 8:28; Job 1:21:

"And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose. And said, Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD."

That is Romans 8:28; Job 1:21. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Every family faces trials. It is not a matter of if, but when. And in those trials, the doctrine of contentment is tested most severely. It is easy to talk about contentment when life is comfortable. But what about when the doctor calls with bad news? When a child rebels? When a marriage is strained? When finances collapse? These are the moments when contentment must be more than a theory — it must be a lived reality.

Romans 8:28 is one of the most beloved and most tested verses in Scripture: "And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose." Paul says "we know" — this is not a guess or a hope. It is settled, confident knowledge. All things — not some things, not the pleasant things, but all things — work together for good. This includes the trials, the sorrows, the losses, the disappointments.

But notice the qualifiers. This promise is for "them that love God" and are "called according to his purpose." This is not a blanket promise that everything turns out fine for everyone. It is a specific promise for believers who are in a covenant relationship with God through Christ. And the "good" God is working toward is not necessarily our comfort — it is our conformity to the image of His Son (Romans 8:29). God's primary goal is not to make us comfortable but to make us like Jesus.

Job's response to devastating loss is the supreme example of contentment in trial. In a single day, Job lost his livestock, his servants, and all ten of his children. The devastation was total. And yet Job fell to the ground and worshipped: "Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD."

Three truths anchor Job's response. First, "the LORD gave." Everything Job had was a gift from God in the first place. He did not earn his children. He did not create his wealth. God gave it all. When we remember that everything good in our lives is a gift, we hold it more loosely.

Second, "the LORD hath taken away." Not Satan, not chance, not bad luck — the LORD. Job understood that nothing happens outside of God's sovereign control. Even Satan could not touch Job without God's permission (Job 1:12). This is deeply comforting, though it is hard to understand. If God is the one who allows the trial, then the trial has a purpose. It is not random. It is not meaningless. A purposeful God allows purposeful suffering.

Third, "blessed be the name of the LORD." In the deepest pit of his grief, Job blessed God. He did not curse. He did not demand an explanation. He worshipped. This is the pinnacle of contentment — blessing God not because of what He gives, but because of who He is.

Burroughs taught that contentment in trials comes from bringing your heart to your condition rather than demanding that your condition match your heart. The world says, "Change your circumstances until they satisfy you." The Gospel says, "Let God change your heart until you are satisfied in Him, regardless of circumstances." This is not passivity — it is the deepest form of active trust.

For families walking through trials together, several practices help cultivate contentment. First, remember God's past faithfulness. In the middle of a new trial, review the trials God has already brought you through. He was faithful then; He will be faithful now. Second, hold each other up. When one family member is struggling to be content, the others can carry them in prayer and encouragement. Third, keep worshipping. Do not let trials shut down your family worship. Keep reading Scripture together, keep praying, keep singing. These are the anchors that hold when the storms rage.

Burroughs himself lived during the English Civil War — a time of immense upheaval, danger, and uncertainty. He was ejected from his pulpit for nonconformity and spent years preaching in secret. Yet from that experience of deprivation, he wrote one of the most joyful books on contentment the church has ever known. His contentment was not academic — it was forged in the furnace of real suffering. And the same furnace can forge the same contentment in your family.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

One of the great blessings of the Christian tradition is that we stand on the shoulders of faithful saints who came before us.

Jeremiah Burroughs, in his work "The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment," wrote these words:

"God did not bring thee into the world to live for thyself alone but for thy generation; and therefore the work of contentment is to bring thy heart to thy condition, and not thy condition to thy heart."

What Jeremiah Burroughs is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Romans 8:28; Job 1:21 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Now we get to the best part: talking about God's Word together as a family. Here are tonight's questions.

Question 1: What does it mean that 'all things work together for good'? Does this mean everything that happens is good?

Question 2: Job lost everything in a single day, yet he blessed God. What do you think gave him the strength to respond this way?

Question 3: Can you think of a difficult time in our family's past that God used for good? How did He bring good out of it?

Question 4: How can we help each other be content when one person in the family is going through a hard time?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose." -- Romans 8:28

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Here is a practical way to live out tonight's devotional:

Draw a timeline of your family's journey together. Mark the difficult times with one color and the blessings with another. Then draw lines showing how God used the difficult times to bring about good things. See how God has been faithful throughout your family's story.

--- Closing ---

Gracious God, thank You for meeting with our family tonight. Your Word never returns void, and we trust that what we have heard will bear fruit in each of our lives. Strengthen our family bonds, deepen our faith, and make us a light in this world. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

May the peace of God guard your hearts and minds tonight. Goodnight, family.
