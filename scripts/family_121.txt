PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 21
"Watching Your Words"
William Secker — The Nonsuch Professor


Welcome, dear family. Another day has passed, and God has been faithful through it all. Now let us close this day together by sitting at the feet of Jesus and hearing His Word.

Let us pray. Almighty God, You are the giver of every good gift, and one of the greatest gifts You have given us is this family. We dedicate this time to You. Help us to listen well and to grow in wisdom together. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

Our passage tonight is Proverbs 18:21. Listen with expectant hearts:

"Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof."

That is Proverbs 18:21. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


As we close our week with William Secker, we turn to a subject that touches every family every single day — our words. Proverbs 18:21 makes an astonishing claim: 'Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof.'

Death and life. Not just influence, not just impact — death and life. Our words have the power to give life or to kill. They can build up or tear down. They can heal or wound. They can encourage a struggling soul or crush a tender spirit. This is not poetic exaggeration. It is the sober truth about the power God has placed in human speech.

The book of James devotes a large section to the tongue. In James 3:5-6, James writes, 'Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth! And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity: so is the tongue among our members, that it defileth the whole body, and setteth on fire the course of nature; and it is set on fire of hell.' A tiny spark can burn down an entire forest. A tiny tongue can destroy a reputation, a relationship, or a family.

James continues in verses 9-10 with a painful observation: 'Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God. Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.' How inconsistent it is to praise God on Sunday and speak harshly to our children on Monday. To pray for our neighbors at church and gossip about them at lunch.

Jesus traced the problem to its root in Matthew 12:34, 'Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.' Our words reveal what is in our hearts. If our hearts are full of love, our words will be loving. If our hearts are full of bitterness, our words will be bitter. The tongue is a thermometer that measures the temperature of the soul.

But the Bible does not only warn about the danger of words. It also celebrates the power of good words. Proverbs 12:18 says, 'There is that speaketh like the piercings of a sword: but the tongue of the wise is health.' Wise words bring healing. Proverbs 15:1 says, 'A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger.' Gentle words defuse conflict. Proverbs 25:11 says, 'A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver.' A well-timed word of encouragement is like a beautiful, precious gift.

Ephesians 4:29 gives us a practical standard: 'Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.' Before we speak, we should ask: will this build up the listener? Will it give grace? If not, perhaps it should not be said.

William Secker wrote that the tongue is but a small member, yet it is either a tree of life or a root of bitterness. He urged Christians to watch their words carefully, knowing that words can build up or tear down, heal or wound, bless or curse.

Secker had a gift for memorable sayings about speech. He wrote that the tongue should be like a sundial — only marking bright hours. He said that a wise person speaks because they have something to say, but a fool speaks because they have to say something. And he counseled that silence is often wiser than speech, for we never need to repent of words we did not say.

Secker also applied this to family life with particular force. In a family, words carry extra weight because they come from the people we love and trust most. A critical word from a parent cuts deeper than the same word from a stranger. An encouraging word from a sibling means more than a compliment from an acquaintance. In the home, our words can create either an atmosphere of warmth and safety or an atmosphere of tension and fear.

For our youngest family members: your words are powerful! Kind words make people feel happy and loved. Mean words make people feel sad and hurt. Choose kind words! For older children: think about the last few days. Were your words mostly encouraging or mostly critical? Were they building people up or tearing them down? For the whole family: let us make a commitment this week to speak words of life in our home — encouragement, gratitude, kindness, and grace.


Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher and learn something that will deepen our understanding.

William Secker, in his work "The Nonsuch Professor in His Meridian Splendour," wrote these words:

"The tongue is but a small member, yet it is either a tree of life or a root of bitterness. Let the Christian watch his words, for by them he may build up or tear down, heal or wound, bless or curse."

What William Secker is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Proverbs 18:21 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Let us discuss what we have heard. Parents, encourage even the youngest to share what they are thinking.

Question 1: Can words really hurt people even though they are just words? Can you think of a time someone's words made you feel really good or really bad?

Question 2: Why does James compare the tongue to a fire? How can a small thing cause big damage?

Question 3: What does Jesus mean when He says 'out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh'?

Question 4: What are some practical ways we can use our words to give life and encouragement in our family?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof." -- Proverbs 18:21

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Before we close, here is a family challenge for the days ahead:

Squeeze a tube of toothpaste onto a plate. Then try to put it back in. (You cannot!) Discuss: words are like toothpaste — once they come out, you cannot take them back. That is why we should think before we speak. Then, go around the family and have each person say one kind, life-giving word to every other family member.


Heavenly Father, we end where we began, in gratitude. Thank You for Your Word, for this family, and for the hope we have in Christ. May tonight's lesson from Proverbs 18:21 shape the way we live, the way we speak, and the way we love one another. Amen and amen.

Until next time, dear family. The Lord goes before you. Walk with Him.
