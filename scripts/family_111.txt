PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 11
"Trusting God in Trials"
Matthew Henry — Commentary on the Psalms


Good evening, beloved family. The Puritans believed that the family altar was the most important altar in all the land. Tonight we gather at that altar together. Welcome to Puritan Gold.

Let us come before the Lord. Gracious God, thank You for the gift of family. Thank You that we can come to You together. We pray that You would teach us from Your Word this evening. Let every word sink deep into our hearts. We love You, Lord. In Jesus' name, Amen.

The Word of God is a lamp to our feet and a light to our path. Tonight that light shines from Psalm 46:1-2:

"God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea."

That is Psalm 46:1-2. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Life brings storms. Every family knows this. There are seasons of sickness, loss, financial hardship, relational pain, and uncertainty. The question is not whether storms will come but whether we will be prepared when they do. Psalm 46 gives us one of the mightiest declarations of trust in all of Scripture.

'God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea.'

Let us examine each phrase. First, 'God is our refuge.' A refuge is a safe place — a fortress, a shelter, a hiding place. When an ancient city was under attack, the people would flee into the stronghold, the most fortified part of the city, where the walls were thickest and the gates strongest. God Himself is our stronghold. We do not simply run to a place — we run to a Person.

Second, 'and strength.' God is not only the place we hide but the power we need. A refuge keeps us safe. Strength enables us to endure and overcome. God provides both — protection and empowerment.

Third, 'a very present help in trouble.' The word 'very present' is important. It means exceedingly, abundantly found. God is not distant when we are in trouble. He is not hard to find. He is very present — closer than our own breath. When trouble comes, God draws near.

And because God is all of this — refuge, strength, and present help — the psalmist can make a staggering declaration: 'Therefore will not we fear.' Not because the danger is not real. Not because the circumstances are not serious. But because God is greater than any circumstance. Even if the earth itself were shaken and the mountains thrown into the sea — the most extreme, unimaginable catastrophe — we will not fear, because God is with us.

This is echoed in Romans 8:38-39, where Paul writes, 'For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.' Nothing can separate us from God's love. Nothing.

In Isaiah 41:10, God says directly to His people, 'Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.' Notice the three promises: I will strengthen, I will help, I will uphold. God personally commits Himself to us in our weakness.

Matthew Henry wrote that God is not only our refuge to whom we may flee but our strength by whom we are enabled to weather the storm. Henry emphasized that God is a very present help — not distant, not delayed, but immediately available. Henry observed that many Christians know God is their refuge in theory but forget it in practice. When trouble comes, instead of running to God first, they run to worry, to human solutions, to panic. Henry urged believers to make God their first response, not their last resort.

Henry also applied this to families specifically. He wrote that families who pray together in times of trial discover a strength and unity that carries them through. When a family gathers around God's Word during a crisis — a job loss, a health scare, a move, a conflict — they find that God's promises are not empty words but living realities. He said that many families have discovered God to be most real and most precious in the very moments when everything else seemed to be falling apart.

Henry was not speaking from ivory tower theology. His own father, Philip Henry, had faced persecution and hardship for his faith during the Puritan era. Matthew Henry grew up watching his parents trust God through genuine difficulty, and that firsthand experience infused his commentary with warmth and credibility.

For our youngest family members: when you feel scared, remember that God is like the strongest, safest hiding place you can imagine — and He is always right there with you. For older children: think about what scares you most. Now read Psalm 46:1 again. Does it change how you feel? For the whole family: the next time a storm comes — a hard day, a bad report, a frightening situation — let us make our first response to gather together, open God's Word, and remind each other that He is our refuge and strength.


The wisdom of the Puritans remains as fresh today as when it was first written. Listen to this insight.

Matthew Henry, in his work "Commentary on the Whole Bible," wrote these words:

"God is not only our refuge to whom we may flee, but our strength by whom we are supported and enabled to weather the storm. In the time of trouble He is not a distant help but a very present help — closer than any earthly friend."

What Matthew Henry is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Psalm 46:1-2 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Here are some questions for us to think about and discuss together. There are no wrong answers here, just honest hearts.

Question 1: What is a refuge? Where do you go when you feel scared?

Question 2: How is God like a strong fortress that keeps us safe?

Question 3: Why does the psalmist say we do not need to fear even if the earth is shaken? What gives him that confidence?

Question 4: Can you share a time when your family faced something hard and God helped you through it?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble." -- Psalm 46:1

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a hands-on way to remember what we have learned tonight:

Build a 'fortress' together using couch cushions, blankets, and pillows. Sit inside your fortress and read Psalm 46 aloud by flashlight. Talk about how God is an even stronger refuge than the strongest fortress we could build.


Father, we have heard Your Word tonight, and we are grateful. We confess that we cannot live it out in our own strength. Fill us with Your Spirit. Give us wisdom for tomorrow, patience with one another, and a deep love for You. Keep our family close to You always. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Rest well tonight. God is faithful, and His wisdom is sure.
