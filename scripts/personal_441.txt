PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Guided Puritan Reading, Day 10
"Christ Intercedes with Feeling"
Thomas Goodwin — The Heart of Christ, Chapter 3

---

Welcome to our guided reading. The book before us tonight has nourished Christians for centuries. We approach it not as critics but as students, not as experts but as hungry learners. Let us read together, slowly and prayerfully.

Tonight we draw from the work of Thomas Goodwin, specifically from "The Heart of Christ in Heaven Towards Sinners on Earth." This text has been a source of spiritual nourishment for Christians across the centuries, and it remains profoundly relevant today.

--- Scripture ---

Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Hebrews 7:25:

"Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.

--- Guided Reading ---

One of the most glorious truths of the Christian faith is that Christ intercedes for His people. He does not merely save them and then leave them to fend for themselves. He continues, at this very moment, to plead their cause before the Father. But Goodwin presses us to consider not merely the fact of Christ's intercession but the feeling behind it. Christ does not intercede mechanically, like a lawyer performing a contractual obligation. He intercedes passionately, with a heart fully engaged in the welfare of those for whom He prays.

Goodwin begins by establishing the scope of Christ's intercession. It covers every need of every believer in every circumstance. When you are tempted, Christ intercedes for you — as He did for Peter, saying, 'I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not' (Luke 22:32). When you sin, Christ intercedes for you — presenting His blood as the ground of your forgiveness. When you are afflicted, Christ intercedes for you — asking the Father to sustain you and to sanctify your suffering. When you are weak in faith, Christ intercedes for you — pleading for strengthening grace. There is no circumstance of your life that falls outside the scope of His heavenly intercession.

But it is the affections of the Intercessor that Goodwin most wants us to see. Christ's intercession is not cold or dutiful; it is warm, earnest, and deeply felt. Goodwin argues that Christ brings to His intercession all the compassion, all the tenderness, all the experiential sympathy that we have been exploring in this book. When He prays for you, He prays as one who knows what it is to be weary, to be tempted, to be sorrowful, to be forsaken. His prayers for you are not the prayers of a stranger but of a Brother who has walked the same road and emerged victorious.

Goodwin makes a remarkable observation about the relationship between Christ's intercession and our prayers. He argues that Christ's intercession is the foundation of our prayer life, not the other way around. We do not pray and then hope that Christ will add His prayers to ours. Rather, Christ prays first, and our prayers are the echo of His. When the Spirit moves you to pray, it is because Christ in heaven has already begun to intercede for the very thing you are about to ask. Your prayers are, in a sense, catching up with His.

This truth has profound implications for those who struggle with prayer. Many believers feel that their prayers are weak, unworthy, and ineffective. They compare their stumbling words to the eloquent prayers of others and feel discouraged. But Goodwin reminds us that the effectiveness of our prayers does not depend on their eloquence or even their theological precision. It depends on the Intercessor who presents them to the Father. Christ takes our weak, faltering, sin-stained prayers and purifies them, perfects them, and presents them to the Father as though they were His own. Our prayers reach God's ears not because they are good enough but because Christ is good enough.

Goodwin also connects Christ's intercession to the believer's perseverance. Why do believers persevere to the end? Not because their faith is strong enough, not because their will is firm enough, but because Christ's intercession is constant and effectual. He ever liveth to make intercession. His prayers never fail. His mediation never lapses. As long as Christ lives — which is forever — His people are secure. Their security rests not in their grip upon Him but in His grip upon them, expressed through His unceasing intercession.

Goodwin concludes this section with a pastoral application of exceptional beauty. He invites the struggling believer to picture Christ at the right hand of the Father, right now, speaking your name. He is not merely aware of your existence; He is actively engaged in your welfare. He is pleading your case. He is asking the Father to sustain you, strengthen you, forgive you, and bring you safely home. And the Father always hears the Son. Always.

Goodwin adds a further consideration that deepens the comfort of this doctrine. He argues that Christ's intercession is not only effectual but also informed by personal experience. When Christ prays for a tempted believer, He prays as one who was Himself tempted in all points. When He prays for a grieving believer, He prays as the Man of Sorrows who was acquainted with grief. When He prays for a persecuted believer, He prays as one who was rejected, mocked, and crucified. His intercession is not the detached mediation of an uninvolved third party; it is the passionate advocacy of one who has endured every trial from the inside and now brings that experiential knowledge to bear in His heavenly prayers.

Let this truth sustain you today: whatever you are facing, Christ is facing it with you — not merely as a sympathetic observer but as an active, effectual Intercessor whose prayers on your behalf will never go unanswered.

--- Reflection ---

Here are some questions to carry with you into the coming days.

Question 1: Do I think of Christ's intercession as a present, ongoing, personal reality, or as an abstract theological concept?

Question 2: How does the truth that Christ prays for me before I pray for myself change the way I approach my own prayer life?

Question 3: What specific burden am I carrying today that I can entrust to my Intercessor, knowing that He brings it before the Father with feeling and power?

Do not rush past these questions. Sit with them. Journal about them if you can. The Puritans believed that truth must be digested, not merely tasted.

--- Closing Prayer ---

Lord Jesus, my great High Priest, I thank You that You ever live to make intercession for me. I am sustained not by the strength of my own faith but by the power of Your unceasing prayers. Help me to rest in the knowledge that You are speaking my name before the Father even now. I bring my burdens to You, trusting that You will carry them to the throne with compassion and power. Amen.

Rest in His grace tonight. Tomorrow is a new day of growth.
