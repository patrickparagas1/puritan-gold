PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 28
"God Himself Is Our Contentment"
Jeremiah Burroughs — The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment for Families


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we close our week with Burroughs by returning to the deepest truth of all: the ultimate source of Christian contentment is not any circumstance, gift, or blessing — it is God Himself. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, You are our portion. Not Your gifts, not Your blessings, not Your providence — but You Yourself are the treasure of our hearts. Settle this truth deeply in our family tonight. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Psalm 73:25-26. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Whom have I in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee. My flesh and my heart faileth: but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever."

That is Psalm 73:25-26. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


We have spent a rich week with Jeremiah Burroughs, and tonight we come to the crown of his teaching. After explaining the nature of contentment, revealing its mystery, warning against its enemies, and exposing the evil of murmuring, Burroughs arrives at the ultimate secret: God Himself is the fountain of all contentment. Not God's gifts. Not God's providence. Not God's blessings. God Himself.

This distinction is crucial. It is possible to be grateful for God's gifts without being satisfied with God Himself. A family can enjoy health, prosperity, and comfort and give thanks for all of these things while still having a restless, unsatisfied heart because they have not found their ultimate satisfaction in the Giver rather than the gifts. Burroughs would say that such a family, however blessed, has not yet discovered the rare jewel.

The psalmist Asaph captures the essence of true contentment in Psalm 73:25-26. After wrestling with the prosperity of the wicked and the apparent injustice of his own suffering, Asaph arrives at a stunning conclusion: "Whom have I in heaven but thee?" This is not a question of information but of devotion. Asaph is declaring that God alone is his treasure. There is nothing in heaven — no angel, no reward, no crown — that he desires more than God. And there is nothing on earth — no possession, no pleasure, no relationship — that he desires beside God. God is enough. God is more than enough. God is everything.

This is the heart of contentment: the settled conviction that God Himself, apart from any gift He might give, is the supreme good, the highest joy, the deepest satisfaction of the human soul. When a family reaches this conviction — not as an intellectual idea but as a lived reality — contentment becomes unshakable. Circumstances may change. Health may falter. Finances may fluctuate. Relationships may strain. But God remains. And as long as God remains, the family has everything it needs.

Burroughs applied this truth to the daily life of the family in several practical ways. First, he encouraged families to cultivate communion with God as their primary pursuit. This means that family worship is not an afterthought or a duty but the central activity of the home — the place where the family encounters the God who satisfies. When worship becomes perfunctory, contentment drains away. When worship becomes vibrant, contentment flourishes.

Second, Burroughs urged families to develop a habit of turning to God in every circumstance — not just in crisis, but in the everyday moments. When something good happens, turn to God with thanksgiving. When something difficult happens, turn to God for comfort. When a decision must be made, turn to God for wisdom. This constant turning toward God keeps the family's gaze fixed on the Source of their contentment.

Third, Burroughs reminded families that their ultimate destiny is not this world but the world to come. The contentment we experience here is a foretaste of the perfect contentment we will experience in heaven, where we will see God face to face and be satisfied at last. Every earthly contentment is partial and temporary. Only heavenly contentment will be complete and eternal. This forward-looking perspective relativizes the trials of the present and fills the family with a hope that no circumstance can destroy.

As we close our week with Burroughs, let your family declare together: "God is the strength of our heart and our portion forever." Not our house. Not our income. Not our plans. God. He is the rare jewel. And a family that possesses Him possesses everything.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Burroughs said that the ultimate source of contentment is God Himself, not His gifts. What is the difference between being thankful for God's gifts and being satisfied with God Himself?

Question 2: The psalmist declared, "There is none upon earth that I desire beside thee." Is this true of our family? What competes with God for the deepest desires of our hearts?

Question 3: What is the most important lesson about contentment that you want to take away from this week with Burroughs?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Whom have I in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee." -- Psalm 73:25

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord, You are our portion forever. We do not merely want Your gifts — we want You. Satisfy our family with Yourself alone. Let us find in You everything we need for joy, peace, and contentment. Thank You for this week with Jeremiah Burroughs and for the rare jewel of Christian contentment. May it sparkle in our family forever. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, resting in the God who is your eternal portion.
