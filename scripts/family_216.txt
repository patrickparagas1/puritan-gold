PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 24
"Contentment vs. Comparison"
Jeremiah Burroughs — The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment for Families

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Burroughs addresses one of contentment's deadliest enemies — the habit of comparison — and how families can break free from its destructive grip. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, free our family from the trap of comparison. Help us to see Your unique gifts to us without measuring them against what You have given others. Let gratitude replace envy and contentment replace covetousness. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Galatians 6:4-5 and Psalm 16:5-6. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"But let every man prove his own work, and then shall he have rejoicing in himself alone, and not in another. For every man shall bear his own burden. The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance and of my cup: thou maintainest my lot. The lines are fallen unto me in pleasant places; yea, I have a goodly heritage."

That is Galatians 6:4-5 and Psalm 16:5-6. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

If contentment has a mortal enemy, it is comparison. Jeremiah Burroughs understood this well and devoted considerable attention to the corrosive effect that comparing ourselves with others has on the soul. He observed that comparison is one of the most common and most destructive habits of the human heart — and that it is especially damaging to families.

Comparison operates on a simple but poisonous logic: I look at what someone else has and conclude that what I have is not enough. The neighbor's house is bigger, so my house feels small. The friend's children are better behaved, so my children feel disappointing. The colleague's income is higher, so my income feels insufficient. In each case, the thing that was perfectly satisfying a moment ago suddenly becomes a source of frustration — not because anything about it changed, but because I measured it against someone else's portion.

Burroughs would say that comparison is, at its root, a failure to trust God's individual providence. God does not give every family the same gifts, the same circumstances, or the same path. He gives each family exactly what it needs to fulfill its unique calling. When we compare our family's portion with another family's portion, we are implicitly criticizing God's wisdom. We are saying, "God, You gave them a better deal than You gave us." But God does not deal unjustly with any of His children. Every family's portion is custom-designed by an infinitely wise and infinitely loving Father.

The psalmist captured this truth beautifully: "The lines are fallen unto me in pleasant places; yea, I have a goodly heritage." The "lines" refer to boundary lines — the measured-out portion that God has assigned to each person. The psalmist looks at his God-given portion and declares it pleasant and good — not because it is the biggest or the best by worldly standards, but because it is from God, and therefore it is exactly right.

For families, comparison manifests in countless ways. Parents compare their children's academic performance with other children's. Children compare their toys, clothes, and social standing with their peers. Families compare their vacations, their homes, their lifestyles with what they see on social media. And in every case, the result is the same: discontentment, envy, and a loss of gratitude for what God has actually given.

Burroughs prescribed several antidotes to the poison of comparison. First, remember that you see only the outside of other people's lives. The family that looks perfect from the outside may be struggling terribly on the inside. You compare your worst moments with their best moments, and the comparison is never accurate.

Second, focus on your own calling rather than someone else's. God has given your family a unique mission, unique gifts, and unique circumstances. When you spend your energy fulfilling God's call on your life, you have no time or inclination to envy someone else's call.

Third, practice downward comparison rather than upward comparison. Instead of looking at those who have more than you and feeling envious, look at those who have less than you and feel grateful. Even better, look at what you deserve — which, as sinners, is nothing good at all — and recognize that everything you have is undeserved grace.

Fourth, and most importantly, make God Himself your portion. The psalmist says, "The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance." When God is your treasure, your contentment is secure, because God can never be taken away, diminished, or exceeded. No one else has a bigger God than you do.

Tonight, declare war on comparison. When you feel the pull to measure your family against another, stop and redirect your attention to the good gifts God has given you. Count your blessings. Name your unique calling. And rest in the knowledge that your lines have fallen in pleasant places.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: In what areas is our family most tempted to compare ourselves with other families? How does comparison make us feel?

Question 2: Burroughs said we only see the outside of other people's lives. How does remembering this help us resist the temptation to compare?

Question 3: The psalmist said, "The lines are fallen unto me in pleasant places." Can each family member name one way in which God's specific provision for our family is pleasant and good?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"The lines are fallen unto me in pleasant places; yea, I have a goodly heritage." -- Psalm 16:6

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord, forgive us for comparing our family with others. You have given us a unique and precious portion, and we receive it with gratitude tonight. Help us to rejoice in our own calling and to be content with our own circumstances, knowing that You are our inheritance and our greatest treasure. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, content with the pleasant portion God has given your family.
