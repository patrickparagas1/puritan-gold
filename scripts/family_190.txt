PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 29
"Foundations of a Puritan Home"
Synthesis — Building a Puritan Home Today, Day 1


Good evening, dear family. Welcome to the final stretch of our May devotions. Over the past four weeks, we have sat at the feet of four extraordinary guides — William Gouge, John Angell James, Matthew Henry, and Richard Baxter. Tonight we begin drawing their wisdom together and asking: how do we build a Puritan home today? Let us come before the Lord.

Let us pray. Lord God, we have received so much wisdom this month. Now help us to apply it. Show us how to take the timeless truths of these faithful servants and weave them into the fabric of our daily life together. Give us not just knowledge but obedience. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Matthew 7:24-27. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell not: for it was founded upon a rock. And every one that heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them not, shall be likened unto a foolish man, which built his house upon the sand: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell: and great was the fall of it."

That is Matthew 7:24-27. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Jesus closes the Sermon on the Mount with the parable of two builders, and the application is inescapable: the difference between a house that stands and a house that falls is not the storms they face — both face the same rain, the same floods, the same winds — but the foundation on which they are built. The house built on rock endures. The house built on sand collapses. And the rock, Jesus says, is the practice of His words — not merely hearing them but doing them.

This month, we have heard the words. We have heard Gouge on the sacred household, on the husband's duties, the wife's calling, the training of children, the speech of the home, and the practice of family worship. We have heard James on godly womanhood, the mother's influence, daily piety, outward service, guarding the heart, and the eternal weight of glory. We have heard Henry on the commentator's household, the church in the house, searching Scripture together, family prayer, the Lord's Day, catechetical instruction, and the legacy of a faithful home. We have heard Baxter on the atmosphere of the household, winning children's hearts, redemptive discipline, the stewardship of time, family peacemaking, preparing children for the world, and the eternal perspective.

Now the question is: will we build on this foundation?

Let us identify the four great pillars that these teachers share in common — the four non-negotiable elements of the Puritan home.

The first pillar is the centrality of Scripture. Every one of our guides this month placed the Bible at the heart of family life. Gouge called for daily reading. Henry modeled a lifetime of devotional study. Baxter urged comprehensive instruction. James grounded all piety in the fear of the Lord revealed in His Word. A home without the Bible at its center is a home without a compass. It may function, but it will drift.

The second pillar is the practice of prayer. Family prayer — morning and evening, sincere, specific, inclusive, and faith-filled — was the heartbeat of the Puritan household. Henry showed us how. Baxter modeled it. Gouge commanded it. James assumed it. A home without prayer is a home that depends on its own strength, and human strength will always fail.

The third pillar is the pursuit of godly character. Not merely moral behavior imposed from without but genuine virtue cultivated from within — love, humility, patience, forgiveness, self-control, courage, and faithfulness. Baxter taught us to cultivate these in our children through affection, example, instruction, and faithful discipline. James taught us to guard against the enemies that threaten them. Gouge taught us to speak in ways that build them up.

The fourth pillar is the orientation toward eternity. The Puritan home was not built for this world alone. It was built for the world to come. Every duty, every sacrifice, every prayer, every conversation was invested in an inheritance that will never fade. Baxter's eternal perspective and James's vision of the weight of glory remind us that the work we do in our homes today has consequences that reach beyond the grave.

These four pillars — Scripture, prayer, character, and eternity — are the foundation stones of the Christian home. If they are in place, the house will stand when the storms come. If they are absent, no amount of activity, wealth, or good intentions will keep it from falling.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Of the four pillars — Scripture, prayer, character, and eternity — which is strongest in our home right now? Which needs the most attention?

Question 2: Looking back over this month, which teaching or author has had the greatest impact on our family? Why?

Question 3: What is one specific, practical step our family can take this week to strengthen the foundation of our home?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock." -- Matthew 7:24

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord Jesus, we have heard Your words this month — through Scripture and through the faithful witnesses of centuries past. Now help us to do them. Help us to build our house on the rock. Not on the shifting sand of worldly wisdom, but on the solid rock of Your eternal truth. Make our home a house that stands. In Your mighty name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
