PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 12
"The Covenant of Grace"
Theological Day — God's Unbreakable Promise to His People

---

Welcome. Today is a Theological day. We examine the doctrine of the covenant of grace — the foundational framework through which the Puritans understood the entire Bible and the entire Christian life.

Let us read Genesis 17:7 and Hebrews 8:10 (KJV):

"And I will establish my covenant between me and thee and thy seed after thee in their generations for an everlasting covenant, to be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee." "For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people."

--- Teaching ---

The Puritans were covenant theologians. They believed that the relationship between God and His people is structured by covenants — solemn, binding agreements in which God makes promises and establishes the terms of relationship with His creatures.

The Puritans distinguished between the covenant of works and the covenant of grace. The covenant of works was established with Adam in the garden. God promised life upon the condition of perfect obedience: obey, and you will live; disobey, and you will die. Adam failed. He disobeyed. And in his fall, the entire human race fell with him, for Adam was the representative head — the federal head — of all humanity.

The covenant of grace is God's response to the failure of the covenant of works. In this covenant, God promises salvation not on the condition of perfect human obedience — which is now impossible — but on the basis of Christ's obedience. Christ is the second Adam, the new federal head, who accomplishes what the first Adam failed to do. He perfectly obeys the law. He bears the penalty for sin. And His righteousness is credited to all who believe.

The Puritans traced the covenant of grace through the entire Old Testament. They saw it first in Genesis 3:15 — the protoevangelium, the first promise of the Gospel, where God declares that the seed of the woman will crush the serpent's head. They saw it renewed with Noah, confirmed with Abraham, ratified at Sinai (under the administration of the Mosaic law), and promised anew through the prophets. Each administration of the covenant reveals more fully the grace that finds its ultimate expression in Christ.

The beauty of the covenant of grace is that it rests entirely on God's faithfulness, not on ours. The Westminster Confession states that in the covenant of grace God freely offereth unto sinners life and salvation by Jesus Christ, requiring of them faith in Him that they may be saved, and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto eternal life His Holy Spirit, to make them willing and able to believe. Notice: God offers. God requires faith. And God gives the Spirit to produce that faith. The covenant is, from first to last, a work of divine initiative.

John Owen wrote that the covenant of grace is the foundation of all the Christian's assurance. If your salvation depended on your performance — on the quality of your faith, the consistency of your obedience, the depth of your repentance — you could never be sure of it. But because your salvation rests on God's covenant promise, secured by Christ's blood and sealed by the Holy Spirit, it is as certain as God Himself.

The practical implications of covenant theology are immense. First, it means that God's relationship with you is not arbitrary or unpredictable. It is covenantal — structured by promises, sealed by an oath, and guaranteed by the blood of Christ. God will not change His mind. He will not revoke His promise. He will not abandon His covenant people.

Second, it means that the entire Bible is one unified story. The Old Testament is not a failed experiment that God replaced with the New Testament. It is the earlier administration of the same covenant of grace that reaches its fulfillment in Christ. The Puritans read the whole Bible as one covenant story with one covenant Lord.

Third, it means that you belong to a covenant community. You are not an isolated individual. You are part of a people — a covenant people — that stretches back to Abraham and forward to the end of the age. Your faith connects you to every believer who has ever lived and every believer who will ever live. You are part of something much larger than yourself.

--- Closing ---

You are a covenant child. God has bound Himself to you by promise, by oath, and by blood. Nothing can sever that bond. Lord, I thank You for the covenant of grace — the unbreakable promise that secures my salvation. When I doubt, remind me of the covenant. When I fear, point me to the blood that sealed it. I am Yours, and You are mine. Amen.
