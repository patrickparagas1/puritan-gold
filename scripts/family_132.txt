PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 1
"Leaving the City of Destruction"
Pilgrim Families — Week 1, Day 1

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to tonight's family devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for gathering together around God's Word. Let us quiet our hearts and prepare to listen to what the Lord has for us.

Let us begin with prayer. Heavenly Father, we thank You for this family. We thank You for bringing us safely through another day. Now we ask that You would open our eyes to see wonderful things in Your Word. Speak to each heart gathered here, from the oldest to the youngest. In Jesus' name we pray. Amen.

Tonight we open our Bibles to Luke 13:24; Matthew 7:13-14. Listen carefully as I read our passage:

"Strive to enter in at the strait gate: for many, I say unto you, will seek to enter in, and shall not be able. Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it."

That is Luke 13:24; Matthew 7:13-14. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Today we open one of the most beloved books ever written by a Christian — The Pilgrim's Progress by John Bunyan. Bunyan wrote this masterpiece while sitting in a cold, dark prison cell in Bedford, England. He was jailed for the crime of preaching the Gospel without a license. But God used those prison walls to produce a story that has touched millions of souls across four centuries.

Our Scripture today comes from the lips of our Lord Jesus Christ Himself. In Luke 13:24, Jesus commands us: "Strive to enter in at the strait gate: for many, I say unto you, will seek to enter in, and shall not be able." And in Matthew 7:13-14, He expands this teaching: "Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it."

These are solemn and searching words. Jesus tells us plainly that there are two paths before every person — the broad way that leads to destruction, and the narrow way that leads to life. The broad way is easy, popular, and crowded. The narrow way is difficult, unpopular, and traveled by few. Yet only the narrow way leads home to God.

This is exactly where Bunyan's story begins. His hero, a man named Christian, lives in a place called the City of Destruction. One day, Christian reads a book — the Bible — and discovers that his city is doomed. A terrible fire is coming. He runs through the streets crying, "What shall I do to be saved?" But his neighbors laugh at him. Even his own wife and children think he has lost his mind.

Have you ever felt like that? Have you ever tried to share something important from God's Word, and the people around you did not want to listen? Christian felt alone, but he knew what the Bible said was true. So when a man named Evangelist pointed him toward a distant light and told him to run toward the Wicket Gate, Christian put his fingers in his ears, ran from the city, and cried, "Life! Life! Eternal Life!"

What a picture of repentance! Repentance means turning away from sin and turning toward God. It means leaving behind the things that are destroying us — even when others do not understand. Christian did not wait until everyone agreed with him. He did not wait until it was convenient. He ran.

Jesus said the gate is strait — that means narrow, tight, difficult to pass through. It requires effort. It requires leaving things behind. You cannot carry the world's baggage through a narrow gate. Christian had a great burden on his back — the burden of his sin — and he could not remove it himself. But he knew he had to start moving toward the one who could remove it.

As families, we must ask ourselves: Are we on the narrow way? Have we left the City of Destruction? It is not enough to live in a nice neighborhood, attend church on Sunday, and be generally good people. Jesus said many will seek to enter and will not be able. The question is not whether we are religious, but whether we have truly repented and placed our faith in Christ alone.

Bunyan understood this because he lived it. Before his conversion, Bunyan was a profane man — he cursed, he broke the Sabbath, he had no fear of God. But when the Holy Spirit opened his eyes to the truth of Scripture, everything changed. He became a new creation. And from that transformation came one of the greatest allegories of the Christian life ever written.

Tonight, as your family gathers, talk about what it means to be on the narrow way. Talk about the courage it takes to follow Jesus when others do not understand. And thank God that He has shown you the path of life, even though it is narrow, even though it is hard — because at the end of that path is the Celestial City, and the welcoming arms of our Savior.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

Now let us hear from one of the great Puritan teachers of the faith.

John Bunyan, in his work "The Pilgrim's Progress," wrote these words:

"As I walked through the wilderness of this world, I lighted on a certain place where was a den, and I laid me down in that place to sleep; and as I slept, I dreamed a dream."

What John Bunyan is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Luke 13:24; Matthew 7:13-14 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Now it is time for our family discussion. Here are some questions to talk about together. Take your time with each one.

Question 1: Why do you think most people choose the broad way instead of the narrow way?

Question 2: Christian's family did not want to leave the City of Destruction. Have you ever felt alone in wanting to follow God?

Question 3: What are some things in our world today that might be part of the 'City of Destruction' — things we need to turn away from?

Question 4: What does it mean for our family to be on the narrow way together?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat." -- Matthew 7:13

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Here is a family activity you can do this week to help these truths take root:

Draw a picture of two roads — one broad and one narrow. On the broad road, write things that pull us away from God. On the narrow road, write things that draw us closer to Him. Post it somewhere your family can see it this week.

--- Closing ---

Let us close in prayer. Heavenly Father, thank You for gathering our family together in Your Word tonight. Thank You for the truth we have heard from Luke 13:24; Matthew 7:13-14. Help us to live out what we have learned. Guard our hearts, guide our steps, and fill our home with Your love and peace. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

Goodnight, dear ones. Walk in wisdom tomorrow.
