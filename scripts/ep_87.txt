PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"He Healeth the Broken in Heart"
May 26, 2026

---

"Praise ye the Lord: for it is good to sing praises unto our God; for it is pleasant; and praise is comely. The Lord doth build up Jerusalem: he gathereth together the outcasts of Israel. He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds. He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names. Great is our Lord, and of great power: his understanding is infinite." -- Psalm 147:1-5

There is a juxtaposition in this psalm that ought to take our breath away. The God who numbers the stars, who calls each one by name, who sustains the vast machinery of the cosmos with nothing more than the word of His power, this same God stoops down to heal the broken in heart and to bind up their wounds. The One whose understanding is infinite, whose power is unlimited, whose greatness is beyond all calculation, concerns Himself with the tears of individual men and women, with the sorrows of particular souls, with the broken pieces of specific human hearts. This is the gospel in miniature, and it is the theme that the psalmist sets before us with such beauty and such power.

"He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds." A broken heart is not merely a sad heart or a disappointed heart. It is a heart that has been shattered, as a vessel of pottery is shattered when it is dashed upon the ground. The pieces lie scattered. The shape is destroyed. The usefulness is gone. And no human hand can put them back together again. Only the God who made the heart in the first place has the skill and the power to repair it when it is broken.

And what breaks the human heart? Sin breaks it, for sin is the great destroyer of all that is good and whole and beautiful in the human soul. Grief breaks it, for the loss of those we love leaves a wound that time alone cannot heal. Failure breaks it, for the discovery of our own weakness and inadequacy crushes the pride upon which we had built our self-esteem. Injustice breaks it, for the experience of being wronged by those who should have cared for us produces a hurt that goes deeper than any physical wound. And the world is full of broken hearts, beloved. They are in every pew on Sunday morning. They are in every home on every street. They walk past us on the pavement and sit beside us on the train. And most of them suffer in silence, because they have been told that brokenness is weakness, and weakness is something to be ashamed of.

But the God of this psalm does not despise brokenness. He heals it. He does not pass by the broken heart with a contemptuous glance, as the priest and the Levite passed by the wounded man on the road to Jericho. He stops. He kneels. He binds up the wounds. He pours in the oil and the wine of His grace. He lifts the broken one upon His own shoulders and carries him to a place of safety and rest. This is the character of our God, and it is the message that a broken world desperately needs to hear.

Richard Sibbes, whom his contemporaries called "the heavenly Doctor Sibbes," wrote an entire book on this theme, which he titled "The Bruised Reed." In it, he demonstrated from Scripture that Christ Jesus, the great Shepherd of the sheep, has a special tenderness for the weak, the wounded, and the broken. He does not break the bruised reed. He does not quench the smoking flax. He tends the feeble flame until it burns bright. He supports the bending reed until it stands straight. And He heals the broken heart with a gentleness that surpasses the skill of the most compassionate physician.

But now see the wonder of it. This same God who heals the broken heart also numbers the stars. "He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names." Modern astronomy tells us that there are more stars in the observable universe than there are grains of sand upon all the shores of all the seas of the earth. The number is so vast that it defies comprehension. And yet God knows each one by name. He does not merely count them. He names them. Each burning sun, each distant galaxy, each faintly glowing ember in the furthest reaches of space has a name that God has given it, and a place that God has assigned it, and a purpose that God has ordained for it.

And the point of this juxtaposition is simply this: the God who is great enough to name every star is also gentle enough to heal every broken heart. His greatness does not make Him distant. His power does not make Him cold. His infinity does not make Him impersonal. On the contrary, it is precisely because He is so great, so powerful, so infinite, that He is able to attend to the smallest details of the smallest life. A lesser god might be too busy with the stars to bother with the broken hearts. But the God of the Bible is not a lesser god. He is the great God, and His greatness encompasses both the cosmos and the individual soul.

"Great is our Lord, and of great power: his understanding is infinite." Here is the foundation upon which the broken heart may rest with absolute confidence. The God who heals is great. His power is great. His understanding is infinite. He knows what you are going through. He knows the precise nature of your brokenness. He understands the tangled web of circumstances and emotions and memories that have brought you to this point. And He has the wisdom to know exactly what you need and the power to provide it.

"The Lord lifteth up the meek: he casteth the wicked down to the ground." The economy of God is the reverse of the economy of the world. The world lifts up the proud and casts down the meek. But God lifts up the meek and casts down the proud. In His kingdom, brokenness is not a disqualification but a qualification. The broken heart is the heart that God delights to heal. The contrite spirit is the spirit in which God delights to dwell. And the wounded soul that brings its pieces to the great Physician shall find, to its everlasting joy, that He is able to make all things new. Amen.
