--- Lesson ---

The apostle Paul wrote, "If Christ be not raised, your faith is vain; ye are yet in your sins" (1 Corinthians 15:17). Everything in Christianity stands or falls with the resurrection. If Jesus rose from the dead, then He is who He claimed to be — the Son of God, the Savior of the world, the Lord of all. If He did not rise, then Christianity is a well-intentioned fraud. The resurrection is the linchpin. And the evidence for it is remarkably strong.

Historians — including many who are not Christians — generally accept several facts about the events surrounding the death of Jesus. These facts form the evidential foundation for the resurrection.

Fact one: Jesus died by crucifixion under Pontius Pilate. This is one of the best-attested facts of ancient history. It is affirmed not only by all four Gospels but by non-Christian sources, including the Roman historian Tacitus, the Jewish historian Josephus, and the Babylonian Talmud. No serious historian doubts that Jesus was crucified and died.

Fact two: The tomb was found empty. The earliest Christian proclamation was that Jesus had risen from the dead, and this proclamation was made in Jerusalem — the very city where Jesus had been buried. If the tomb had still contained the body, the authorities could have produced it and silenced Christianity in its cradle. They never did. Furthermore, the empty tomb is reported in all four Gospels, and the earliest report identifies women as the first witnesses. In the first-century world, women's testimony was not highly regarded in legal proceedings. If the early Christians were inventing the story, they would have chosen more culturally credible witnesses. The fact that they reported women as the first witnesses strongly suggests they were recording what actually happened.

Fact three: Multiple individuals and groups reported seeing Jesus alive after His death. Paul, in 1 Corinthians 15:3-8, provides a list of resurrection appearances that dates to within a few years of the crucifixion itself. He reports that Jesus appeared to Peter, then to the twelve, then to more than five hundred people at once (most of whom were still alive and could be questioned when Paul wrote), then to James, then to all the apostles, and finally to Paul himself. The diversity, number, and independence of these witnesses make hallucination or fabrication extremely unlikely.

Fact four: The disciples were transformed. Before the resurrection, the disciples were a terrified, demoralized group hiding behind locked doors. After the resurrection, they became bold, fearless proclaimers of the risen Christ — willing to suffer persecution, imprisonment, and death rather than recant their testimony. Something happened that transformed these ordinary men into extraordinary witnesses. The most natural explanation is that they actually saw what they claimed to have seen: the risen Jesus.

Fact five: The early church exploded in growth. Within decades of the crucifixion, Christianity had spread across the Roman Empire. Thousands of Jews — people who had been raised to worship on the Sabbath, to offer animal sacrifices, and to expect a conquering Messiah — suddenly began worshipping on Sunday, celebrating the Lord's Supper, and proclaiming that the crucified Jesus was the risen Lord. Something unprecedented had happened to produce this dramatic shift. The resurrection is the only adequate explanation.

Alternative explanations have been proposed throughout history, but none of them adequately account for all the evidence. The swoon theory — that Jesus did not actually die but merely fainted on the cross — fails because Roman crucifixion was an extremely effective method of execution, and the soldiers verified His death. The theft theory — that the disciples stole the body — fails because it cannot explain why they would willingly die for a lie they had fabricated. The hallucination theory fails because hallucinations are individual experiences, not shared group events, and they do not explain the empty tomb. The legend theory fails because the resurrection was proclaimed too early and too publicly for legend to develop.

The evidence points in one direction: Jesus of Nazareth died on a Roman cross, was buried in a tomb, and three days later rose from the dead. He appeared to His followers, commissioned them to spread the gospel, and ascended to the right hand of God. This is the heart of the Christian faith, and the evidence for it is solid.

--- Review Questions ---

Review Question 1: What are the five historical facts that form the evidential foundation for the resurrection?

Review Question 2: Why is the testimony of women as the first witnesses significant for the historicity of the empty tomb?

Review Question 3: Choose one alternative explanation for the resurrection (swoon theory, theft theory, hallucination theory, or legend theory) and explain why it fails to account for the evidence.

--- Activity ---

Prepare a five-minute oral presentation defending the resurrection of Jesus. Structure your presentation around the five facts discussed in today's lesson. For each fact, state the evidence clearly and explain why it supports the resurrection. Practice delivering your presentation to a family member or friend, and have them ask questions when you are finished. Refine your responses based on their questions.

--- Closing ---

The resurrection of Jesus Christ is the most well-evidenced miracle in human history. Because He lives, our faith is not in vain. Because He lives, we too shall live. Let this truth give you confidence as you share your faith with others. Until next time, students.
