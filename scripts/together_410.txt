PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 18
"Managing the Household Together"
William Gouge — On Shared Stewardship


Welcome, dear couple. The Puritans were refreshingly practical, and tonight we address a topic that many devotional guides avoid but that occupies an enormous portion of married life: the management of the household.

Our Scripture tonight is Proverbs 31:27:

"She looketh well to the ways of her household, and eateth not the bread of idleness."

And Proverbs 27:23:

"Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds."

Both husband and wife are called to diligent stewardship of the home God has given them.


William Gouge devoted a significant portion of "Domestical Duties" to the practical management of the household — finances, meals, children's education, hospitality, and the ordering of daily life. He recognized that many marital conflicts arise not from deep theological disagreements but from the mundane stresses of running a home.

Gouge's first principle is that household management is a shared responsibility. While he acknowledged that cultural norms in his day assigned certain tasks to husband and wife respectively, he insisted that both partners are stewards of everything God has given them. The husband who says "the house is her responsibility" and the wife who says "the finances are his problem" are both abdicating their shared calling.

His second principle is communication. Many households suffer because husband and wife operate in separate spheres without coordination. The husband makes financial decisions without consulting his wife. The wife makes decisions about the children's schedule without informing her husband. The result is chaos, misunderstanding, and resentment. Gouge counseled regular, honest conversations about the state of the household — the budget, the calendar, the children's needs, the condition of the home, the plans for the future.

Richard Baxter added that financial management is one of the most common sources of marital conflict, and that the remedy is transparency and agreement. He counseled couples to develop a shared understanding of their finances — what comes in, what goes out, what is saved, what is given. He warned against secret spending and hidden debts, which erode trust. And he urged generosity — both to those in need and to each other.

Thomas Gataker observed that the Proverbs 31 woman is not a superwoman who does everything alone. She is a wise manager who orders her household with skill, initiative, and industry. Her husband trusts her, and she trusts him. Together they create a home that is productive, generous, and God-honoring.

The Puritans also taught that the management of the household should be marked by contentment rather than anxiety. "Having food and raiment let us be therewith content" (1 Timothy 6:8). A couple that is always striving for more — a bigger house, a nicer car, a more impressive lifestyle — will never find peace. But a couple that gratefully stewards what God has given them, however much or little, will discover a deep and settled joy.

Tonight, consider the practical rhythms of your household. Not with anxiety, but with the calm wisdom of stewards who know that everything they have belongs to God.


Discuss together:

Question 1: How well do you communicate about the practical management of your household — finances, schedules, responsibilities? Where could you improve?

Question 2: Are there areas of household management where one of you carries a disproportionate burden? How could you redistribute the load more equitably?

Question 3: Is your household marked more by contentment or by striving? What would it look like to cultivate greater gratitude for what God has already given you?


Pray together for wisdom in managing your household. Ask God for the grace of contentment, the discipline of good stewardship, and the joy of working together as a team. Pray for any specific financial or practical challenges you are currently facing.


May the Lord who provides for the birds of the air and the lilies of the field provide abundantly for your household. May your home be a place of order, generosity, and contentment. Goodnight.
