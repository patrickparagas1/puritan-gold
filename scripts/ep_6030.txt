PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"Give the King Thy Judgments, O God"
June 30, 2026

---

"Give the king thy judgments, O God, and thy righteousness unto the king's son. He shall judge thy people with righteousness, and thy poor with judgment. The mountains shall bring peace to the people, and the little hills, by righteousness. He shall judge the poor of the people, he shall save the children of the needy, and shall break in pieces the oppressor." -- Psalm 72:1-4

This psalm, attributed to Solomon, rises so far above the reign of any earthly monarch that it can only be fully understood as a prophecy of the kingdom of Christ. No human king has ever ruled with perfect righteousness. No earthly government has ever saved all the children of the needy or broken in pieces every oppressor. But the kingdom of Christ, which is an everlasting kingdom, shall accomplish all that this psalm declares, and infinitely more besides. And so we read it not merely as a prayer for good government in the present age, but as a vision of that coming day when the knowledge of the Lord shall cover the earth as the waters cover the sea.

"Give the king thy judgments, O God." The prayer begins with a recognition that righteous government is a gift from God, not an achievement of man. The wisest legislator, without the wisdom of God, will produce laws that are defective and unjust. The most powerful ruler, without the righteousness of God, will exercise his power for selfish and oppressive ends. And therefore the first duty of every governor, and indeed of every Christian who prays for those in authority, is to ask God for the gift of His own judgments -- His standards of right and wrong, His principles of justice and equity, His wisdom in the administration of power.

Spurgeon applied this prayer to the kingdom of Christ and found in it a description of the most perfect government the universe will ever know. When Christ reigns, He reigns with the judgments of God, for He is God. His righteousness is not borrowed or derived or partial; it is the very righteousness of the Almighty, exercised with infinite wisdom and infinite love. He does not need advisors to tell Him what is just, for He is the fountain of all justice. He does not need counsellors to guide His decisions, for He is the source of all wisdom. He judges His people with a righteousness that is absolute, and His poor with a judgment that is perfectly compassionate.

"He shall judge the poor of the people, he shall save the children of the needy, and shall break in pieces the oppressor." Here is the heart of Christ's kingdom: justice for the powerless and judgment upon the powerful who abuse their power. The poor and the needy, who are trampled by the mighty in every age and in every society, shall find in Christ a champion who will never fail them. He will hear their cry. He will take up their cause. He will defend them against every oppressor and will break in pieces every tyrant who grinds the face of the poor.

Thomas Manton preached a series of sermons upon the kingship of Christ, in which he demonstrated that the rule of Christ is the only rule that perfectly combines justice with mercy, power with compassion, authority with tenderness. Every human government fails at some point in this balance. Some governments are strong but unjust. Others are just but weak. Some are merciful to the rich but harsh to the poor. Others are generous with public funds but corrupt in their administration. But the government of Christ, which rests upon His shoulders, is the government of the Wonderful Counsellor, the Mighty God, the Everlasting Father, the Prince of Peace, and of the increase of His government and peace there shall be no end.

Richard Sibbes meditated upon the phrase "the mountains shall bring peace to the people" and found in it a picture of universal blessing flowing down from the heights of Christ's throne to the lowest valleys of human need. When Christ reigns, peace comes to every corner of His dominion. Not merely the absence of war, but the positive presence of well-being, of harmony, of shalom -- that rich and comprehensive Hebrew word that describes a state of total flourishing in which every relationship is rightly ordered, every need is met, every wrong is made right, and every soul is at rest in the love of God.

John Owen looked forward to the consummation of Christ's kingdom with eager longing, teaching his congregation that the present state of the world, with all its injustice and suffering and oppression, is not the final state. Christ is even now ruling from the right hand of the Father, and His kingdom is even now advancing, and the day is coming when every knee shall bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father. And in that day, the prayer of this psalm shall be perfectly and eternally fulfilled. The king shall judge with righteousness. The poor shall be saved. The oppressor shall be broken in pieces. And the whole earth shall be filled with the glory of the Lord.

Let us therefore pray this ancient prayer with fresh urgency. Give the King Thy judgments, O God. Let His righteousness cover the earth. Let His justice reach to the poorest and the most oppressed. And let His kingdom come in all its fulness, that the nations may rejoice and the earth may be glad, and the name of the Lord may be praised from the rising of the sun to the going down of the same. Amen.
