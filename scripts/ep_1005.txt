PURITAN GOLD -- Study Topic: The Book of Sirach, Part 5 of 10
"A Faithful Friend Is a Strong Defence"
Sirach Chapter 6 — The Value and Testing of True Friendship

---

Good evening, and welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we come to one of the most beloved and frequently quoted passages in all of the Book of Sirach — chapter 6, on the nature of true friendship. Of all the subjects Ben Sira addresses, this one speaks with particular immediacy to the human heart, for we are all made for fellowship, and the quality of our friendships shapes the quality of our souls.

Let us read from Sirach chapter 6, verses 5 through 17:

"Sweet language will multiply friends: and a fairspeaking tongue will increase kind greetings. Be in peace with many: nevertheless have but one counsellor of a thousand. If thou wouldest get a friend, prove him first, and be not hasty to credit him. For some man is a friend for his own occasion, and will not abide in the day of thy trouble. And there is a friend, who being turned to enmity and strife will discover thy reproach. Again, some friend is a companion at the table, and will not continue in the day of thy affliction. But in thy prosperity he will be as thyself, and will be bold over thy servants: If thou be brought low, he will be against thee, and will hide himself from thy face. Separate thyself from thine enemies, and take heed of thy friends. A faithful friend is a strong defence: and he that hath found such an one hath found a treasure. Nothing doth countervail a faithful friend, and his excellency is invaluable. A faithful friend is the medicine of life; and they that fear the Lord shall find him."

--- Teaching ---

There are few passages in wisdom literature that match the penetrating insight of Sirach 6 on the subject of friendship. Ben Sira was a keen observer of human nature. He had seen friendships forged and friendships shattered. He had watched fair-weather companions melt away in adversity and faithful friends stand firm when all else fell. Out of that long observation, he distills counsel that is as relevant in our age as it was in ancient Jerusalem.

Let us begin with his first and most fundamental distinction: not all who call themselves friends are friends in truth. "Some man is a friend for his own occasion, and will not abide in the day of thy trouble." This is the friend of convenience — the person who delights in your company when your table is full, your purse is heavy, and your reputation is high, but who vanishes like morning mist when trouble descends. Ben Sira calls this out with clear-eyed realism. Do not be deceived by pleasant words and easy laughter. The test of friendship is not the feast but the furnace.

This theme echoes powerfully through the canonical Scriptures. Proverbs 17:17 declares: "A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity." Proverbs 18:24 adds: "A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly: and there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother." And Proverbs 19:4 observes with sobering honesty: "Wealth maketh many friends; but the poor is separated from his neighbour." The Bible is not naive about human nature. It knows that prosperity attracts companions the way honey attracts flies. The question is always: who remains when the honey is gone?

Ben Sira counsels a deliberate testing of friendship before extending full trust: "If thou wouldest get a friend, prove him first, and be not hasty to credit him." This is not cynicism. It is prudence. The Puritans distinguished carefully between charity — which thinks the best of all people — and prudence, which tests character before entrusting the heart. John Owen wrote that "the best friendships are those that have been proved by time and trial, not those formed in the heat of first enthusiasm." We live in an age of instant connection, where social media creates the illusion of deep relationship without the substance. Ben Sira's counsel to prove a friend before crediting him is a corrective our generation desperately needs.

But the heart of this passage is the glorious declaration in verses 14 through 16: "A faithful friend is a strong defence: and he that hath found such an one hath found a treasure. Nothing doth countervail a faithful friend, and his excellency is invaluable. A faithful friend is the medicine of life."

Consider these three images. A faithful friend is a strong defence — a fortress, a shield, a wall of protection. In the ancient world, where dangers were physical and immediate, the loyal companion who stood beside you in battle was quite literally the difference between life and death. David and Jonathan are the supreme biblical example of this. Jonathan loved David as his own soul (1 Samuel 18:1) and risked his father's wrath to protect him. He was David's strong defence in the darkest chapter of his life.

A faithful friend is a treasure — something of surpassing worth that, once found, should be guarded and cherished. The world offers many pleasures that money can buy. But a true friend is not among them. Friendship cannot be purchased, bribed, or manufactured. It is a gift of providence, and Ben Sira says its value is beyond calculation.

A faithful friend is the medicine of life. This is perhaps the most tender of the three images. We all know the healing power of a friend who listens, who speaks truth in love, who weeps with those who weep and rejoices with those who rejoice. The right word from a trusted friend can heal wounds that no physician can reach. Proverbs 27:6 says, "Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful." Even the painful truth, spoken by a genuine friend, is medicinal. It heals because it comes from love.

The Puritans placed an extraordinarily high value on what they called "spiritual friendship" — the bond between believers who spur one another on toward holiness. Jonathan Edwards and David Brainerd shared such a friendship. George Whitefield and John Wesley, despite their theological disagreements, maintained a deep mutual love. Cotton Mather wrote about the importance of having a "faithful monitor" — a friend brave enough to tell you the truth about your sins and failings. This is exactly what Ben Sira envisions: friendship rooted not merely in shared interests or temperamental compatibility, but in the shared fear of God.

And here is the key that unlocks the entire passage: "They that fear the Lord shall find him." The faithful friend is found among those who fear God. This does not mean that unbelievers cannot be kind or loyal. Common grace produces genuine acts of friendship among all people. But Ben Sira is speaking of the deepest, most enduring form of friendship — the bond that is forged in shared worship, shared repentance, shared devotion to the living God. When two souls fear the Lord together, their friendship has a foundation that no storm can shake.

The New Testament takes this principle even further. The Apostle Paul's letters are filled with references to his beloved companions in the faith — Timothy, whom he calls "my own son in the faith"; Luke, "the beloved physician"; Epaphroditus, "my brother, and companion in labour, and fellowsoldier." These were friendships tested in persecution, imprisonment, and the daily rigors of missionary life. They exemplify everything Ben Sira describes.

Let me also note what Ben Sira warns against: the false friend who, "being turned to enmity and strife, will discover thy reproach." The betrayal of a friend is one of the deepest wounds a human being can suffer. It was a friend's betrayal that pierced the heart of the Psalmist: "For it was not an enemy that reproached me; then I could have borne it... But it was thou, a man mine equal, my guide, and mine acquaintance. We took sweet counsel together, and walked unto the house of God in company" (Psalm 55:12-14). And it was a friend's kiss that betrayed our Lord in the Garden of Gethsemane. The pain of Judas's treachery was sharpened immeasurably by the fact that he had been a companion at Christ's table for three years.

If you have experienced such a betrayal, Ben Sira's counsel offers both warning and comfort. The warning: be prudent in whom you trust. The comfort: a faithful friend does exist, and the Lord will grant him to those who fear Him. Do not let the pain of one betrayal close your heart to all friendship. That would be to let the enemy rob you of one of life's greatest gifts.

--- Application ---

1. Take inventory of your friendships. Who in your life is a fair-weather friend, and who has proved faithful in adversity? Do not despise the companions of the table — enjoy fellowship freely. But recognize which friendships have been tested and proved, and invest most deeply there.

2. Be the faithful friend you seek. Friendship is reciprocal. If you want a friend who is a strong defence, a treasure, and a medicine, you must be that kind of friend to others. Stand by your friends in their day of trouble. Speak truth to them in love. Keep their confidences sacred.

3. Root your closest friendships in the fear of the Lord. Seek out companions who will sharpen your faith, challenge your sin, and walk with you toward holiness. As Proverbs 27:17 says, "Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend."

4. If you have been wounded by a friend's betrayal, bring that pain to the Lord in prayer. Ask Him to heal your heart and to guard you from bitterness. And ask Him, in His providence, to bring into your life the faithful friend who is the medicine of life.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, who called Thy disciples not servants but friends, and who showed the greatest love by laying down Thy life for us, we thank Thee for the gift of friendship. Forgive us when we have been false friends — fickle in adversity, careless with trust, selfish in our affections. Teach us to love as Thou hast loved, faithfully and sacrificially. Grant us, in Thy mercy, at least one friend who is a strong defence, a treasure, and the medicine of life. And may our deepest friendships be rooted in the fear of Thy holy name. Amen.

Go in peace, dear friend. In our next session, we turn to Sirach chapters 18 and 19 and the vital subject of mastering the tongue and the passions. Until then, cherish the faithful friends God has given you, and be a faithful friend to others.
