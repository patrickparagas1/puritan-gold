PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 26
"The Love of Christ Constrains Us"
Pilgrim Families — Week 4, Day 5

---

Hello and welcome to our family devotional. Come, young and old alike. God's Word has something for every member of this family tonight. Let us draw near to Him.

Shall we pray? Our Father, we gather tonight hungry for Your truth. We know that man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from Your mouth. Feed us now, Lord. Bless our family time together. In the name of Christ we pray. Amen.

God has preserved His Word for thousands of years so that families like ours could hear it tonight. Let us read from 2 Corinthians 5:14; Romans 8:35-39:

"For the love of Christ constraineth us; because we thus judge, that if one died for all, then were all dead. Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword? Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us. For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

That is 2 Corinthians 5:14; Romans 8:35-39. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Paul uses a remarkable word in 2 Corinthians 5:14: the love of Christ constrains us. The word "constraineth" means to press together, to compel, to leave no other option. Christ's love does not merely invite us — it presses upon us so powerfully that we cannot resist. It is not a gentle suggestion but an overwhelming force that redirects the entire course of our lives.

Why does Christ's love constrain us? "Because we thus judge, that if one died for all, then were all dead." The logic of the cross compels us. If Christ died for us, then we are obligated — not by law but by love — to live for Him. A person who truly understands the cross cannot go on living for themselves. The love that sent Jesus to die demands a response, and the only adequate response is a life poured out in return.

Romans 8:35-39 then asks the most important question a family will ever answer: "Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?" Paul lists the worst things life can throw at a human being: tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword. These are not hypothetical — Paul experienced every single one. And his triumphant conclusion? "Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us."

Not just conquerors — more than conquerors. A conqueror barely wins the battle. More than a conqueror wins so decisively that the victory is never in doubt. Through Christ's love, we do not merely survive our trials — we triumph through them. Every enemy that comes against us becomes a stepping stone rather than a stumbling block.

Then Paul extends the list to include cosmic forces: death, life, angels, principalities, powers, things present, things to come, height, depth, and any other creature. He exhausts every category of existence. Nothing in all creation — nothing that has ever existed, exists now, or will ever exist — can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord. This is the most comprehensive promise of security in all of Scripture.

Rutherford understood this love personally and wrote about it with unmatched fervor. He described Christ's love as an ocean — you can see where it begins at the cross, but you can never see where it ends. It stretches into eternity, deeper than our deepest sin, wider than our widest failure, higher than our highest aspiration. We will spend eternity exploring the love of Christ and never reach the bottom.

For families, this truth is the foundation of all security. Children need to know that they are loved with a love that will never let them go. Spouses need to know that Christ's love for them does not fluctuate with their performance. Parents need to know that even when they fail — and they will fail — God's love does not waver.

The love of Christ should also constrain how family members love each other. If Christ loved us when we were unlovable (Romans 5:8), then we are called to love each other when we are unlovable. When your spouse irritates you — love. When your child disobeys — love. When your sibling offends you — love. Not because they deserve it, but because Christ loved you when you did not deserve it.

Rutherford wrote from exile, surrounded by enemies, separated from everything he loved — yet he was overflowing with the love of Christ. His circumstances could not separate him from that love. Neither can yours. Whatever your family faces tonight, face it with this confidence: nothing — absolutely nothing — can separate you from the love of God which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

There is much we can learn from those who walked with God before us. Consider these words.

Samuel Rutherford, in his work "Letters of Samuel Rutherford," wrote these words:

"The love of Christ is like an ocean: you may see where it begins but never where it ends."

What Samuel Rutherford is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in 2 Corinthians 5:14; Romans 8:35-39 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Family, let us talk about what we have learned. Consider these questions together.

Question 1: What does it mean that Christ's love 'constrains' us? How does His love change the way we live?

Question 2: Paul lists many things that cannot separate us from God's love. Which one is most meaningful to you tonight?

Question 3: How should the unbreakable love of Christ affect the way we love each other in our family?

Question 4: Rutherford compared Christ's love to an ocean. What other images come to mind when you think about how big God's love is?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord." -- Romans 8:38-39

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Learning sticks better when we do something with it. Try this activity as a family:

Write Romans 8:38-39 on a large piece of paper. As a family, try to add anything to the list that Paul might have missed. You will find that you cannot — Paul has covered everything. Nothing can be added because nothing can separate us. Hang the paper somewhere visible as a daily reminder.

--- Closing ---

Lord, as we close tonight's devotional, we place our family in Your hands. Watch over us as we sleep. Guard our dreams. Prepare our hearts for tomorrow. May the truths from 2 Corinthians 5:14; Romans 8:35-39 follow us into the new day. We love You, Lord. Amen.

Goodnight. Remember your memory verse, and carry it with you tomorrow.
