PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Pastoral Counsel, Day 19
"Serving in the Local Church"
Using Your Gifts for the Body

---

Good evening, friend. Tonight we turn our attention to a subject that is intensely practical and deeply biblical: the call to serve in the local church. The Puritans did not regard church membership as passive spectating. They believed that every believer is gifted by the Holy Spirit and called to active service in the body of Christ. Let us recover that vision tonight.

--- Scripture ---

Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is 1 Peter 4:10-11:

"As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God. If any man speak, let him speak as the oracles of God; if any man minister, let him do it as of the ability which God giveth: that God in all things may be glorified through Jesus Christ, to whom be praise and dominion for ever and ever. Amen."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.

--- Teaching ---

Peter's words are striking in their universality. He does not say "as some men have received a gift" or "as the pastors and elders have received gifts." He says "every man." Every believer has received a gift from God, and every believer is called to minister that gift in service to others. This is the Puritan vision of the church: not a gathering of spectators watching a few performers, but a body of servants, each contributing something essential to the health and growth of the whole.

The apostle Paul develops this same truth in 1 Corinthians 12, where he compares the church to a human body. Every member has a function. The eye cannot say to the hand, "I have no need of thee." The head cannot say to the feet, "I have no need of you." Each member is necessary, and each member has been placed in the body by God's sovereign design. "But now hath God set the members every one of them in the body, as it hath pleased him" (1 Corinthians 12:18). Your place in the body is not accidental. God put you there on purpose.

Richard Baxter, in "The Reformed Pastor," argues that the health of a congregation depends not merely on the faithfulness of its ministers but on the active service of every member. Baxter laments churches where the majority of the work falls upon the shoulders of the pastor while the congregation sits idle. This is a distortion of the biblical pattern. The pastor's role is not to do all the ministry but to equip the saints for the work of ministry (Ephesians 4:11-12). When every member is serving, the church flourishes. When only the pastor is serving, the church grows weary and stagnant.

What are the gifts the Spirit distributes? Scripture identifies a wide variety: teaching, exhortation, giving, mercy, administration, helps, hospitality, encouragement, and many others. The Puritans were careful to point out that spiritual gifts are not identical to natural talents, though God often works through natural abilities. A spiritual gift is a grace-empowered capacity given by the Holy Spirit for the building up of the body of Christ. You may not have a gift of preaching, but you may have a gift of mercy that brings comfort to the afflicted. You may not have a gift of teaching, but you may have a gift of hospitality that makes the stranger feel at home in the household of God.

How do you discover your gifts? The Puritans offered practical counsel. First, pray. Ask God to reveal how He has equipped you. He knows, and He is willing to show you. Second, serve. Do not wait until you have identified your gift before you begin working. Start where there is need, and your gifts will become evident through experience. Thomas Watson writes that the Spirit often reveals our calling through the things that burden our hearts most deeply. If your heart breaks for the lost, you may have a gift for evangelism. If your heart aches for the confused, you may have a gift for teaching. If your heart is drawn to the suffering, you may have a gift for mercy.

Third, listen to the body. Often, other believers can see your gifts more clearly than you can see them yourself. When multiple people in your congregation tell you that you have a gift for encouragement, or for practical helps, or for prayer, pay attention. God frequently confirms our gifts through the observation and testimony of the church.

The Puritans also warned against two errors. The first is pride — the temptation to regard your gift as superior to the gifts of others. Paul addresses this directly: "For who maketh thee to differ from another? and what hast thou that thou didst not receive?" (1 Corinthians 4:7). Every gift is a grace, not an achievement. The second error is false humility — the refusal to use your gift because you do not consider it important enough. The gift of helps is no less necessary than the gift of teaching. The gift of mercy is no less valuable than the gift of preaching. In the body of Christ, there are no insignificant members.

--- Application ---

First, if you are not currently serving in your local church, commit this week to approaching your pastor or an elder and asking where you are needed. Do not wait for a formal invitation. The need is already there.

Second, examine your heart for the twin dangers of pride and false humility. Are you using your gifts with a servant's heart, or are you seeking recognition? Are you withholding your gifts out of fear or laziness?

Third, remember that service is not optional. Peter calls us stewards of God's grace. A steward who buries his master's goods will be called to account. Use what you have been given, and trust God to multiply it.

--- Closing ---

Lord, Thou hast given every member of Thy body a gift for the building up of the whole. Forgive us for our idleness and our excuses. Show us where we are needed. Give us the courage to step forward and the humility to serve without recognition. Let our churches become places where every member is a minister. Through Christ, the Head of the body, Amen.

Goodnight, friend. Find your place in the body, and serve with all your heart.
