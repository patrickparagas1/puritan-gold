PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 21
"Worshiping Together at God's Wisdom"
Romans for Marriage — Day 21


Welcome, husband and wife. There is something powerful about two hearts turning toward God at the same time. When you both face the same direction, toward Christ, everything in your marriage finds its proper alignment. Let us do that now.

Every marriage needs a foundation that does not shift. Let us hear from that foundation now, from Romans 11:33-36:

"O the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out! For who hath known the mind of the Lord? or who hath been his counsellor? Or who hath first given to him, and it shall be recompensed unto him again? For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever. Amen."

That is Romans 11:33-36. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


The Puritans treasured marriage as a covenant made before God. Stephen Charnock wrote beautifully about how Scripture speaks to the hearts of married couples.

Paul's doxology in Romans 11:33-36 erupts from the depths of theological wonder. After eleven chapters of dense, systematic theology — covering sin, justification, sanctification, election, and the mystery of Israel — Paul can contain himself no longer. He breaks into worship: 'O the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God!'

Stephen Charnock, who wrote the most extensive Puritan treatise on God's attributes, argued that worship is the highest activity of the human soul and the truest expression of the marriage of hearts. When husband and wife worship God together, they are participating in the activity that most fully unites their souls. Worship is not merely a Sunday activity — it is the atmosphere in which Christian marriages thrive.

Charnock taught that the contemplation of God's wisdom should produce humility, wonder, and adoration. God's judgments are 'unsearchable' — we cannot trace them to their source. His ways are 'past finding out' — we cannot follow them to their end. We do not sit as God's counselors; He does not consult us before acting. This should produce profound rest in marriage. When we do not understand what God is doing, we can still trust His wisdom. When circumstances make no sense, we can worship instead of worry.

The culmination of the doxology provides the theological framework for everything: 'For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things.' Every good thing in your marriage comes from God. Every sustaining grace flows through God. And the ultimate purpose of your marriage is directed to God — for His glory. Your marriage exists not primarily for your happiness but for God's glory. And paradoxically, when you make God's glory the aim, your deepest happiness follows.

Charnock urged couples to make worship the centerpiece of their home. Not merely attending church together, but actively worshiping in the private sanctuary of their own home — through prayer, Scripture reading, singing, and the reverent contemplation of God's perfections. A couple that worships together builds a bond that transcends every earthly connection.


When was the last time you and your spouse worshiped together — not just sat in church together, but truly worshiped? When was the last time you marveled together at the greatness of God?

Tonight, read Romans 11:33-36 aloud together as an act of worship. Let the words wash over you. Then spend a few moments simply praising God for His wisdom — in your lives, in your marriage, in His perfect plan that you cannot always understand.

Worship together regularly. It does not have to be elaborate. Sing a hymn together. Read a psalm aloud. Pray a prayer of pure adoration with no requests attached. These simple acts of worship knit your hearts to God and to each other in ways that nothing else can.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Now turn to each other. Here are tonight's reflection questions. Answer from the heart, not from the head.

Question 1: How often do you and your spouse worship together outside of church? What holds you back?

Question 2: Is there a situation in your marriage where you need to trust God's unsearchable wisdom rather than demanding answers?

Question 3: What would change in your marriage if you truly embraced that 'of Him, and through Him, and to Him are all things'?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


The Puritans ended every evening with prayer. Let us follow their example and bring our marriage before the Lord.

Tonight's prayer focus: Turn your prayer time tonight into pure worship. Do not bring requests — bring praise. Marvel together at God's wisdom, knowledge, and glory. Let worship bind your hearts to each other and to Him.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the God of infinite wisdom fill your marriage with wonder. May you worship together in the depths of His riches and find your deepest joy in His eternal glory.

May the words you have heard tonight bear fruit in your marriage for years to come. The Lord be with you both. Until next time.
