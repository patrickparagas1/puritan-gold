PURITAN GOLD -- Study Devotional, Day 16
Series: Romans
"More Than Conquerors"
Present sufferings, future glory, and the invincible love of God


Welcome, friend. Tonight we open our Bibles again for another session of deep, prayerful study. The Puritans believed that the richest treasures of Scripture are found not on the surface but beneath it, by those willing to dig.

We are studying the book of Romans together. Tonight's lesson is titled "More Than Conquerors." Each session builds on what came before, so if you have been following along, you will see how tonight's passage connects to the larger argument of the book.


Let us read our passage for tonight. It comes from Romans 8:18-39:

"For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us... And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose. For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren. Moreover whom he did predestinate, them he also called: and whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified... What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us? He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?... Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us. For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

Read that passage again to yourself. Mark any words or phrases that stand out. The Puritans read with a pen in hand, always ready to note what God was showing them.


Within tonight's passage, one verse stands out as a key that unlocks the rest.

"For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord." -- Romans 8:38-39

This verse is the lens through which we will examine the rest of tonight's passage. Keep it in the front of your mind as we proceed.


With our key verse before us, let us examine the broader teaching.

Thomas Goodwin offers valuable commentary on this passage. The Puritans were expositors of the highest order, and their insights remain remarkably fresh.

Thomas Goodwin's exposition of the latter half of Romans 8 is among the most rapturous in all Puritan literature. He considered verses 28-30 — the golden chain of redemption — to be the most comforting passage in all of Scripture. Goodwin argued that the chain is unbreakable because every link is forged by God alone. Foreknowledge, predestination, calling, justification, glorification — each is an act of God, not of man. And the chain is stated in the past tense, including glorification, even though it is still future in our experience. God sees it as already accomplished because His purposes cannot fail.

Goodwin was deeply moved by the question of verse 31: 'If God be for us, who can be against us?' He argued that this is not hypothetical — the 'if' means 'since.' Since God is for us (and the preceding verses have proven it beyond all doubt), it does not matter who stands against us. Not Satan, not the world, not our own accusing conscience, not death itself. Goodwin saw in verse 32 the definitive proof of God's commitment to His people: 'He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all.' If God was willing to give the greatest gift conceivable — His own beloved Son — He will certainly not withhold any lesser gift. The cross is the guarantee that every need of God's children will be met.

Goodwin gave perhaps his most ecstatic commentary on verses 35-39. He argued that the list of potential separators — tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword, death, life, angels, principalities, powers, things present, things to come, height, depth, any other creature — is intentionally comprehensive. Paul has searched the entire created order, from the highest heaven to the deepest abyss, from the present moment to the end of time, and has found nothing capable of separating the believer from the love of God in Christ Jesus. Goodwin concluded that if nothing in all creation can sever this bond, then the love of God is as eternal and unshakeable as God Himself.

Romans 8:18-39 is the climax of the first eight chapters of Romans and one of the most magnificent passages ever written. It begins with suffering and ends with glory, moving through creation's groaning, the Spirit's intercession, God's sovereign purpose, and the invincible love of Christ.

Verse 18 sets the perspective: 'For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us.' Paul, who had been beaten, stoned, shipwrecked, imprisoned, and left for dead, makes a calculated assessment: the sufferings are real and intense, but they are temporary and light compared to the eternal weight of glory (2 Corinthians 4:17). The glory will not merely be revealed to us but in us — we will participate in it, be transformed by it, be saturated with it.

Verses 19-22 expand the vision beyond humanity to the entire created order. 'For the earnest expectation of the creature waiteth for the manifestation of the sons of God.' Creation itself was subjected to futility when Adam fell — thorns, decay, death, natural disaster — all are consequences of the curse (Genesis 3:17-19). But creation does not groan without hope. It waits for the day when God's children will be fully revealed in glory, because on that day creation itself 'shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God.'

Verses 23-25 bring the groaning closer to home. Even believers, who have 'the firstfruits of the Spirit,' groan within ourselves, 'waiting for the adoption, to wit, the redemption of our body.' We already have the Spirit as a down payment, but we await the full inheritance — the resurrection body, the complete transformation, the final eradication of sin and suffering. This is the hope that sustains us: 'For we are saved by hope: but hope that is seen is not hope: for what a man seeth, why doth he yet hope for it?'

Verses 26-27 reveal the Spirit's ministry of intercession. 'Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered.' When we do not know how to pray — when words fail, when burdens are too heavy for expression, when confusion clouds our petitions — the Spirit intercedes for us with groans too deep for words. And 'he that searcheth the hearts knoweth what is the mind of the Spirit, because he maketh intercession for the saints according to the will of God.' The Father always answers the Spirit's prayers because the Spirit always prays according to the Father's will. We can never pray perfectly, but the Spirit within us can.

Verse 28 is one of the most beloved promises in Scripture: 'And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.' Not some things but all things. Not all things are good in themselves, but all things work together for good — like the individual ingredients of a recipe, some bitter, some sharp, but combined by the Master Chef into something wonderful. And this promise has conditions: it is for those who love God, those who are called according to His purpose.

Verses 29-30 unveil the golden chain of redemption: foreknowledge, predestination, calling, justification, glorification. 'For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son.' God's foreknowledge is not mere advance awareness but intimate, covenantal, electing love (as in 'Adam knew Eve his wife'). Those whom God foreknew in this way, He predestined — appointed beforehand — to be conformed to the image of His Son. The goal of salvation is Christlikeness. 'Moreover whom he did predestinate, them he also called' — effectively summoned from death to life. 'And whom he called, them he also justified' — declared righteous. 'And whom he justified, them he also glorified' — this final link is stated in the past tense (aorist) even though glorification is still future. In God's purpose, it is as good as done. Not one link can break. Not one person foreknown will fail to be glorified.

Verses 31-34 ask five rhetorical questions that demolish every possible threat to the believer's security. 'If God be for us, who can be against us?' Answer: no one of any consequence. 'He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?' If God gave the greatest gift, He will not withhold lesser ones. 'Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect?' No one — because God has justified them. 'Who is he that condemneth?' No one — because Christ has died, risen, and intercedes at God's right hand. 'Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?' Nothing and no one.

Verses 35-37 list the most extreme threats: 'tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword.' These are not hypothetical — Paul experienced them all. 'As it is written, For thy sake we are killed all the day long; we are accounted as sheep for the slaughter.' The world treats Christians as expendable. Yet 'in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us.' Not merely conquerors but hyper-conquerors (hupernikomen). We do not merely survive — we triumph overwhelmingly.

Verses 38-39 deliver the final, comprehensive, unshakeable assurance: 'For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.' Paul has searched the entire universe — every possible threat from every possible direction, every conceivable power in every conceivable realm — and his conclusion is certain: nothing can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus. This love is as eternal as God, as unchangeable as His character, and as secure as the throne of heaven.


Application is where truth meets life. Let us consider what this passage demands of us.

Romans 8:18-39 is meant to carry you through the darkest valleys of life. Here are specific applications.

First, recalibrate your perspective on suffering. Whatever you are enduring right now — health problems, relational pain, financial pressure, spiritual warfare — weigh it against the coming glory. This is not denial of pain but a higher calculation. 'The sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us.' Read 2 Corinthians 4:16-18 alongside this passage and let the reality of eternal glory dwarf your temporary afflictions.

Second, trust God's providence in all things. Romans 8:28 does not promise that all things feel good or make sense in the moment. It promises that God is weaving every circumstance — including suffering, loss, and apparent disaster — into a pattern for your good and His glory. When you cannot see the purpose, trust the Purposer. Journal your unanswered questions and commit them to the God who makes all things work together for good.

Third, cling to the golden chain. When doubt assails your assurance, walk through Romans 8:29-30 link by link. If God foreknew you, He predestined you. If He predestined you, He called you. If He called you, He justified you. If He justified you, He will glorify you. Every link is held by God's hand, not yours. Your salvation is as secure as God's purpose.

Fourth, let Romans 8:38-39 be your anchor in every storm. Memorize these verses. When death threatens, declare: death cannot separate me. When life overwhelms, declare: life cannot separate me. When spiritual forces attack, declare: neither angels nor principalities can separate me. Nothing in all creation — absolutely nothing — can sever you from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord. Let this truth be the bedrock of your soul.

Write down one concrete action you will take this week in response to tonight's study. Do not let this truth remain theoretical. The Puritans measured the success of a sermon not by how much the hearer learned but by how much the hearer changed.


Almighty God, we are overwhelmed by the love You have poured out upon us in Christ. We thank You that nothing in all creation — not death, not life, not angels, not demons, not the present, not the future, not any power, not height, not depth — can separate us from Your love. We thank You for the golden chain that stretches from eternity past to eternity future, every link forged by Your sovereign hand. In our sufferings, give us the perspective of glory. In our confusion, give us the assurance of Your providence. In our weakness, give us the intercession of Your Spirit. Make us more than conquerors through Him that loved us. In Christ Jesus our Lord. Amen.

Goodnight. May the truths you have studied tonight take deep root in your heart.
