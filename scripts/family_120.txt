PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 20
"The Narrow Way"
William Secker — The Nonsuch Professor


Good evening, family. There is no place God loves to meet His people more than when families gather in His name. Welcome to Puritan Gold. Let us open our Bibles and our hearts.

Let us begin in prayer. Lord Jesus, You said that where two or three are gathered in Your name, there You are in the midst of them. We claim that promise tonight as a family. Be here with us. Teach us. Shape us. We pray in Your holy name. Amen.

Every word of Scripture is breathed out by God. Tonight we receive His breath from Matthew 7:13-14:

"Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it."

That is Matthew 7:13-14. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Today Jesus puts before us a choice — perhaps the most important choice any person will ever make. In Matthew 7:13-14, He says, 'Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it.'

Jesus describes two gates, two roads, two destinations, and two crowds. Let us look at each.

The wide gate and the broad way. This is the easy path — the path of least resistance. It requires no commitment, no sacrifice, no self-denial. You can bring whatever you want through this gate — your sins, your pride, your selfishness. There are no demands, no standards, no accountability. The crowd on this road is large. 'Many there be which go in thereat.' In every generation, the majority of people choose the easy path.

But notice where it leads: destruction. Not comfort. Not happiness. Not paradise. Destruction. The wide road that promises freedom and pleasure ends in ruin. The Bible is honest about this — sin is often enjoyable in the short term (Hebrews 11:25 speaks of 'the pleasures of sin for a season'), but its wages are death (Romans 6:23).

The strait gate and the narrow way. The word 'strait' (not 'straight') means narrow, tight, constricted — like a narrow mountain pass. This gate is not easy to enter. It requires repentance — turning from sin. It requires faith — trusting Christ alone for salvation. It requires surrender — giving up your own way to follow God's way. You cannot bring your old life through this gate. You must leave it behind.

The road beyond is narrow. Following Jesus involves discipline, obedience, and sometimes suffering. It means going against the crowd. It means saying no to things the world says yes to. And 'few there be that find it.' The narrow way is not the popular way.

But notice where it leads: life. Not just survival — real, abundant, eternal life. Jesus said in John 10:10, 'I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.' The narrow road leads to the most satisfying, joyful, meaningful existence possible.

Luke 13:24 adds urgency: 'Strive to enter in at the strait gate: for many, I say unto you, will seek to enter in, and shall not be able.' The word 'strive' is 'agonizomai' in Greek — from which we get our word 'agonize.' Entering the narrow gate requires earnest effort. Not because we earn salvation — it is always by grace — but because genuine faith requires a real decision, a real turning, a real commitment.

William Secker wrote about the narrow way with great passion and encouragement. He said that the way to heaven is narrow and uphill, and the pilgrim must expect difficulty. But every step brings the pilgrim nearer to glory, and the end of the journey will make amends for the roughness of the road.

Secker also offered a beautiful contrast. The broad way begins easy and ends in misery. The narrow way begins with difficulty but ends in eternal joy. He compared the broad road to a river that begins wide and ends in a waterfall. The narrow road is like climbing a mountain — hard at times, but the view from the top is glorious.

Secker urged his readers not to be discouraged by the narrowness of the way or the smallness of the crowd. He reminded them that Christ Himself walked this road before us. We are not alone on the narrow path. The Savior walks with us, and He has already reached the destination and is calling us home.

For our youngest: Jesus says there are two paths — a wide, easy one and a narrow, harder one. The wide one looks fun but leads to a bad place. The narrow one is harder but leads to life with God forever. We want to walk the narrow one! For older children: have you ever felt pressure to go along with the crowd even when you knew it was wrong? That is the pull of the broad road. It takes courage to choose the narrow way. For the whole family: following Jesus is not always easy, but it is always worth it. Let us encourage each other to stay on the narrow path, even when it is hard.


One of the great blessings of the Christian tradition is that we stand on the shoulders of faithful saints who came before us.

William Secker, in his work "The Nonsuch Professor in His Meridian Splendour," wrote these words:

"The way to heaven is narrow and uphill, and the pilgrim must expect difficulty. But every step brings him nearer to glory, and the end of the journey will make amends for the roughness of the road."

What William Secker is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Matthew 7:13-14 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Now we get to the best part: talking about God's Word together as a family. Here are tonight's questions.

Question 1: Jesus talks about two roads. What is different about them?

Question 2: Why do you think more people choose the wide road?

Question 3: What does it mean to enter the 'strait gate'? What do we have to leave behind?

Question 4: How can our family help each other stay on the narrow way, especially when it gets hard?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it." -- Matthew 7:14

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a practical way to live out tonight's devotional:

Set up two paths in your yard or living room using tape, rope, or cushions. Make one very wide and easy, and the other narrow and a bit tricky to walk through (stepping over pillows, balancing on a line of tape). Have the family walk both. Discuss: which was easier? Which leads to 'life' according to Jesus?


Gracious God, thank You for meeting with our family tonight. Your Word never returns void, and we trust that what we have heard will bear fruit in each of our lives. Strengthen our family bonds, deepen our faith, and make us a light in this world. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

May the peace of God guard your hearts and minds tonight. Goodnight, family.
