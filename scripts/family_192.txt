PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 31
"The Household of Faith"
Synthesis — Building a Puritan Home Today, Day 3

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to our final Puritan Gold family devotion for May. What a month this has been. We have walked with four great teachers through the richest tradition of household theology in the history of the church. Tonight we close with gratitude, with resolve, and with hope. Let us come before the Lord one last time this month.

Let us pray. Lord God, we thank You for this month of learning, growing, and drawing nearer to You and to one another. As we close this chapter, seal its lessons in our hearts. Let the truths we have learned bear fruit in our lives — fruit that endures to eternity. We offer this month to You, with all its imperfections, and we trust You to complete the good work You have begun in our family. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Psalm 127:1 and Psalm 128:1-4. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Except the Lord build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the Lord keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain. Blessed is every one that feareth the Lord; that walketh in his ways. For thou shalt eat the labour of thine hands: happy shalt thou be, and it shall be well with thee. Thy wife shall be as a fruitful vine by the sides of thine house: thy children like olive plants round about thy table. Behold, that thus shall the man be blessed that feareth the Lord."

That is Psalm 127:1 and Psalm 128:1-4. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

We come to the end of our month with one great truth ringing in our ears: "Except the Lord build the house, they labour in vain that build it." This is the foundation beneath the foundation — the truth that undergirds everything Gouge, James, Henry, and Baxter taught us. We can practice every duty, follow every principle, and adopt every habit we have studied this month, but if the Lord does not build the house, our labors are in vain. The Puritan home is not built by human effort alone. It is built by the grace of God, working through human faithfulness.

This is both humbling and liberating. It is humbling because it reminds us that we are utterly dependent on God. We cannot save our own children. We cannot sanctify our own marriages. We cannot produce godliness in our own hearts by sheer willpower. We need God. We need His Word, His Spirit, His grace, and His providence every single day.

But it is liberating because it takes the unbearable weight of responsibility off our shoulders and places it on His. We are called to be faithful — not to be perfect. We are called to plant and water — God gives the increase. We are called to teach and pray and love and serve — and then to trust that the God who is building our house is more than sufficient to complete what He has begun.

Let us review the great harvest of this month. From William Gouge, we learned that the family is a sacred institution, a little church, where every member has God-given duties and where mutual love, submission, and worship should characterize daily life. We learned about the husband's sacrificial love, the wife's noble calling, the training of children, the words we speak, and the worship we offer together.

From John Angell James, we learned about the power and beauty of godly womanhood — the influence of a pious woman on her husband, her children, her church, and her world. We learned about the mother's irreplaceable role in shaping souls, the sanctification of daily duties, the importance of guarding the heart, and the eternal glory that awaits the faithful.

From Matthew Henry, we learned about the centrality of the Bible in the home — how to read it together, how to study it carefully, how to pray over it fervently, how to teach it catechetically, and how to build a legacy of faith that passes from generation to generation.

From Richard Baxter, we learned about the atmosphere of the home, the art of winning children's hearts, the practice of redemptive discipline, the stewardship of time, the ministry of peacemaking, the preparation of children for the world, and the eternal perspective that transforms everything.

Psalm 128 paints the picture of the household that fears the Lord: a husband who walks in God's ways, a wife who is like a fruitful vine, children who are like olive plants around the table. This is the vision. Not a perfect family — there is no such thing this side of heaven. But a faithful family. A family that fears the Lord, walks in His ways, and trusts in His grace. A family that reads, prays, worships, loves, forgives, teaches, and serves — not perfectly, but persistently. A family that is being built by God.

This is the Puritan vision for the home. It is our inheritance. It is our calling. And by the grace of God, it is within our reach.

Thank you for walking through this month together. Thank you for opening the Word of God as a family. Thank you for discussing, questioning, praying, and growing together. The work you have done this month is not in vain. God sees it, God honors it, and God will use it in ways you cannot yet imagine. Go forward in faith, dear family. The Lord is building your house, and He will not stop until it is complete.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: "Except the Lord build the house, they labour in vain." How does this truth change the way we approach family life? Is it humbling, liberating, or both?

Question 2: Looking back over the entire month, what is the single most important lesson our family has learned? How will we carry it forward?

Question 3: What is one thing each family member wants to say to the others tonight — a word of gratitude, encouragement, or commitment for the journey ahead?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Except the Lord build the house, they labour in vain that build it." -- Psalm 127:1

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for this month. We thank You for Gouge, James, Henry, and Baxter — for their faithfulness, their wisdom, and the legacy they left for us. We thank You for Your Word, which has been our guide. We thank You for one another — for the gift of family, the gift of fellowship, the gift of growing together in grace.

Build our house, Lord. Build it on the rock of Your Word. Build it with the mortar of Your grace. Build it for the glory of Your name. And when the storms come — as they will — let this house stand, because it was built by You.

We commit our family to You — every member, every relationship, every hope, every struggle. You are our God. We are Your people. And as for us and our house, we will serve the Lord.

In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
