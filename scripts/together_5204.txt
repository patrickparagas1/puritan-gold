PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 4
"Nourish and Cherish"
Ephesians 5:28-30 — Mutual Submission and Love


Good evening, dear husband and wife. We continue our study through Ephesians 5 tonight with a passage that reveals the intimate tenderness God intends for the marriage bond. After the high calling of sacrificial love, Paul now brings us to the daily, practical expression of that love: nourishing and cherishing.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 5:28-30 (KJV):

"So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself. For no man ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church: For we are members of his body, of his flesh, and of his bones."


Paul here makes an argument so simple and so profound that it should reshape how every husband thinks about his wife. He appeals to the most basic instinct of human nature: self-preservation. No man hates his own body. When he is hungry, he feeds himself. When he is cold, he covers himself. When he is hurt, he tends to his wounds. Paul says: treat your wife with the same instinctive care. Nourish her. Cherish her. Attend to her needs as naturally and as urgently as you attend to your own.

The word "nourish" in the original language carries the idea of feeding, sustaining, and bringing to maturity. It is the same word used for a parent nurturing a child. The husband is to nourish his wife, not merely with food and provision, but with attention, with encouragement, with spiritual sustenance, and with emotional care. He is to be attentive to what she needs in order to grow and flourish.

The word "cherish" is equally rich. It means to warm, to comfort, to hold close. It is the word used in 1 Thessalonians 2:7 where Paul describes his care for the Thessalonians: "even as a nurse cherisheth her children." To cherish is to create an atmosphere of warmth and safety. It is to make your wife feel that she is treasured, valued, and protected.

William Gouge noted that many husbands fail not in the dramatic moments of sacrifice but in the daily moments of nourishing and cherishing. They would lay down their lives in a crisis, but they neglect to offer a kind word at breakfast. They would defend their wife from a threat, but they do not defend her from their own sharp tongue. Gouge urged husbands to examine the small details of their daily conduct. Do you greet your wife warmly when you come home? Do you ask about her day and listen to her answer? Do you notice when she is tired, discouraged, or burdened, and offer your help?

Richard Baxter observed that the husband who nourishes and cherishes his wife is, in truth, nourishing and cherishing himself. Paul says it plainly: "He that loveth his wife loveth himself." This is not a selfish motive but a statement of profound truth. In marriage, the two have become one flesh. What blesses her blesses him. What wounds her wounds him. The husband who starves his wife of affection and care is starving himself. The husband who cherishes his wife is building his own happiness.

Notice that Paul grounds this instruction in the example of Christ: "even as the Lord the church." Christ nourishes the church with His Word, with the sacraments, with the ministry of the Spirit, and with His constant intercession. Christ cherishes the church, holding her close, warming her with His love, comforting her in affliction, and rejoicing over her with singing. The husband who follows Christ's example will find that his marriage becomes a place of deep mutual delight.

Dear wife, you are called to receive this nourishing and cherishing with gratitude and to communicate clearly when you are in need. Do not expect your husband to read your mind. Tell him what you need. And dear husband, do not wait to be told. Be observant. Be attentive. Study your wife as you would study a precious and irreplaceable text. Learn what makes her feel loved, secure, and valued, and then do those things with consistency and joy.

The Puritan home was meant to be a haven of nourishing love. In a world that is harsh and demanding, your marriage should be the one place where both of you can find warmth, safety, and renewal. Nourish and cherish one another, even as the Lord nourishes and cherishes His church.


Question 1: Husband, in what practical ways can you better nourish and cherish your wife in the routines of daily life?

Question 2: Wife, do you feel nourished and cherished in this marriage? What would help you feel more so?

Question 3: How can we make our home a warmer, safer, more nurturing place for both of us?


Gracious Father, Thou who dost nourish and cherish Thy church with unfailing love, teach us to nourish and cherish one another in this marriage. Help the husband to attend to his wife's needs with the same care he gives to his own body. Help the wife to receive his love with gratitude and to express her needs with honesty. May our home be a sanctuary of warmth and tenderness, where both of us are fed, sustained, and brought to maturity in Christ. We ask it in the name of our Lord Jesus, who nourishes us with His own body and blood, Amen.
