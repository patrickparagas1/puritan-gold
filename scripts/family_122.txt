PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 22
"The Godly Person Prays"
Thomas Watson — The Godly Man's Picture

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to tonight's family devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for gathering together around God's Word. Let us quiet our hearts and prepare to listen to what the Lord has for us.

Let us begin with prayer. Heavenly Father, we thank You for this family. We thank You for bringing us safely through another day. Now we ask that You would open our eyes to see wonderful things in Your Word. Speak to each heart gathered here, from the oldest to the youngest. In Jesus' name we pray. Amen.

Tonight we open our Bibles to Philippians 4:6. Listen carefully as I read our passage:

"Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God."

That is Philippians 4:6. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Welcome to Week 4, where we draw from one of the greatest Puritan writers of all time — Thomas Watson. Watson was a London preacher who was ejected from his pulpit in 1662 for his nonconformist beliefs. Rather than abandon his calling, he continued to write and preach wherever he could. His book The Godly Man's Picture paints a portrait of what a genuine Christian looks like, drawn stroke by stroke from Scripture. Each chapter describes one characteristic of the godly person.

We begin with prayer. Watson wrote that the first mark of the godly person is that he is a praying person. And our Scripture today confirms this. Paul writes in Philippians 4:6, 'Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.'

The command begins with 'be careful for nothing.' The word 'careful' here does not mean careless or irresponsible. It means anxious — do not be consumed with worry. The Greek word is 'merimnao,' which means to be pulled apart, divided, distracted by anxiety. Paul is saying: stop being torn apart by worry.

But Paul does not just say 'stop worrying' and leave it at that. He gives us the antidote: prayer. 'But in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.' Notice the scope — 'in every thing.' Not just the big crises but every thing. The small worries, the daily concerns, the nagging fears — bring them all to God.

Paul uses three words for prayer. 'Prayer' (proseuche) — the general word for worship-oriented prayer. 'Supplication' (deesis) — specific, earnest requests for particular needs. 'Thanksgiving' (eucharistia) — gratitude woven into our asking. When we pray with thanksgiving, we remember that the God we are asking for help is the same God who has already helped us countless times.

And what is the result? Verse 7: 'And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.' The peace of God — not peace from God like a package in the mail, but the very peace that God Himself possesses — will guard (the Greek word is a military term for standing guard) your hearts and minds. This peace surpasses understanding because it does not depend on circumstances. You can have it in the middle of a storm.

Jesus modeled this perfectly. In the Garden of Gethsemane, facing the cross, He prayed, 'O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt' (Matthew 26:39). He brought His deepest anguish to the Father in prayer.

The Psalmist writes in Psalm 62:8, 'Trust in him at all times; ye people, pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us.' Pour out your heart. Do not hold back. Do not filter. Do not perform. Just pour it all out before God.

Thomas Watson wrote that prayer is the soul's breathing. A godly person cannot live without prayer — it is not merely his task but his nature. He prays as naturally as he breathes. Watson saw prayer as the defining habit of the genuine Christian. Just as a living person breathes, a spiritually alive person prays. If there is no prayer, Watson suggested, there may be no spiritual life.

Watson described several qualities of genuine prayer. First, it is reverent — we come to the King of the universe. Second, it is sincere — we mean what we say. Third, it is humble — we come as needy sinners, not proud achievers. Fourth, it is persistent — we do not give up after one prayer. Fifth, it is believing — we pray expecting God to hear and answer.

Watson also gave comfort to those who struggle with prayer. He wrote that God does not judge our prayers by their eloquence but by their earnestness. A stammering, broken prayer from a sincere heart is more pleasing to God than the most polished prayer spoken without feeling. God hears the groan that words cannot express (Romans 8:26).

For our youngest family members: talking to God is easy. You can tell Him anything — what makes you happy, what scares you, what you need. He always listens! For older children: when you feel worried, what is your first response? Is it prayer or panic? Try making prayer your first reaction this week. For the whole family: let us be a praying family — not just at mealtimes and bedtime but in every thing, bringing our joys and burdens to the God who cares for us.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

Now let us hear from one of the great Puritan teachers of the faith.

Thomas Watson, in his work "The Godly Man's Picture," wrote these words:

"Prayer is the soul's breathing. A godly man cannot live without prayer. It is not his task merely; it is his nature. He prays as naturally as he breathes."

What Thomas Watson is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Philippians 4:6 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Now it is time for our family discussion. Here are some questions to talk about together. Take your time with each one.

Question 1: What does Paul say we should do instead of worrying?

Question 2: What does it mean to pray 'in every thing'? Can you think of small things you could pray about?

Question 3: Watson says prayer is like breathing for a Christian. What do you think he means?

Question 4: What is the difference between praying because you have to and praying because you want to?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God." -- Philippians 4:6

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Here is a family activity you can do this week to help these truths take root:

Create a family prayer jar. Write prayer requests on slips of paper and put them in a jar. Each day, draw one out and pray for it together. When a prayer is answered, move it to an 'Answered Prayers' jar and celebrate God's faithfulness.

--- Closing ---

Let us close in prayer. Heavenly Father, thank You for gathering our family together in Your Word tonight. Thank You for the truth we have heard from Philippians 4:6. Help us to live out what we have learned. Guard our hearts, guide our steps, and fill our home with Your love and peace. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

Goodnight, dear ones. Walk in wisdom tomorrow.
