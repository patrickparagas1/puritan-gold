PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 9
"The Doctrine of Divine Providence"
Theological Day — God's Sovereign Government of All Things


Welcome. Today is a Theological day. We examine the doctrine of divine providence — God's active, ongoing governance of all creatures, actions, and things, from the greatest to the least.

Let us read Daniel 4:35 (KJV):

"And all the inhabitants of the earth are reputed as nothing: and he doeth according to his will in the army of heaven, and among the inhabitants of the earth: and none can stay his hand, or say unto him, What doest thou?"


Providence is the doctrine that God not only created all things but actively governs all things — directing, sustaining, and ordering every event in the universe according to His eternal purposes. The Westminster Confession states that God the great Creator of all things doth uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all creatures, actions, and things, from the greatest even to the least, by His most wise and holy providence.

The Puritans distinguished three aspects of providence. First, preservation — God sustains all things in existence moment by moment. Without His sustaining power, the universe would collapse into nothingness. As Paul writes in Colossians 1:17, by Him all things consist — hold together. You do not exist because you were created once and then left to run on your own. You exist because God is actively sustaining you right now.

Second, concurrence — God works in and through secondary causes. He does not typically bypass the natural order. He works through natural laws, human decisions, historical events, and the ordinary processes of the created world. But He is never absent from these processes. John Flavel wrote in The Mystery of Providence that God's hand is always upon the helm, even when we cannot see it.

Third, government — God directs all things toward His appointed ends. Nothing is random. Nothing is meaningless. Nothing is outside His sovereign plan. Flavel urged Christians to keep what he called a providence journal — a record of the ways God had ordered their circumstances. Over time, patterns emerge that would be invisible in the moment but become unmistakable in retrospect.

The doctrine of providence raises the question of evil. If God governs all things, how do we account for sin and suffering? The Puritans addressed this with great care. They affirmed that God is never the author of sin. He does not tempt anyone. However, He permits evil for wise purposes that are often hidden from our sight. He uses evil — without approving of it — to accomplish His good ends.

Joseph's story is the classic illustration. His brothers sold him into slavery — a wicked act. But God used that wickedness to bring Joseph to Egypt, where he would save his family and surrounding nations from famine. Joseph summarized it: "But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good" (Genesis 50:20).

Stephen Charnock argued that providence is one of the strongest evidences for the existence of God. The intricate ordering of events, the remarkable timing of divine interventions, the way suffering is transformed into blessing — these patterns cannot be explained by chance. They are the fingerprints of an infinitely wise God.

The practical effect of believing in providence is twofold. First, it produces trust. If God governs all things, then nothing that happens to you is outside His control. You may not understand the purpose. But you can trust that the One who holds the universe is holding your life with the same care. Second, it produces patience. If God is working all things according to His eternal counsel, you do not need to panic when circumstances go wrong. You can wait for the pattern to emerge. The story is not over yet.


God governs all things. Your circumstances, your challenges, your joys, your sorrows — all are under His sovereign direction. Rest in that today. Lord, I confess that I often live as if my life were governed by chance. Teach me to see Your hand in all things. Give me trust when I cannot understand and patience when I cannot see the pattern. You are on the throne. I rest in that. Amen.
