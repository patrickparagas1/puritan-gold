PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 28
"Christ's Crown and Covenant"
Pilgrim Families — Week 4, Day 7

---

Welcome, dear family. Another day has passed, and God has been faithful through it all. Now let us close this day together by sitting at the feet of Jesus and hearing His Word.

Let us pray. Almighty God, You are the giver of every good gift, and one of the greatest gifts You have given us is this family. We dedicate this time to You. Help us to listen well and to grow in wisdom together. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

Our passage tonight is Revelation 2:10; 2 Timothy 4:8. Listen with expectant hearts:

"Fear none of those things which thou shalt suffer: behold, the devil shall cast some of you into prison, that ye may be tried; and ye shall have tribulation ten days: be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life. Henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give me at that day: and not to me only, but unto all them also that love his appearing."

That is Revelation 2:10; 2 Timothy 4:8. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

We close our week with Rutherford at the intersection of faithfulness and reward. Jesus speaks to the suffering church in Smyrna in Revelation 2:10: "Be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life." The call is to faithfulness — not to success, not to comfort, not to popularity, but to faithfulness. And the reward for faithfulness is a crown of life. Not just life, but a crown — a symbol of honor, victory, and royal dignity.

Paul, writing near the end of his life and facing execution, declares with confident joy: "Henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give me at that day." Paul does not say he hopes for a crown or wishes for a crown. He says it is "laid up" — already prepared, already reserved, already waiting. All that remains is for the righteous Judge to present it on the appointed day.

And Paul extends this promise beyond himself: "Not to me only, but unto all them also that love his appearing." Every believer who loves the return of Christ, who longs for His appearing, who lives in faithful expectation of that day — to them also a crown is laid up. This is not a reward for the super-spiritual elite. It is for all who love His appearing.

Rutherford's dying words are among the most famous in church history. When told that he had been summoned to appear before the Scottish Parliament on charges of high treason, Rutherford — lying on his deathbed — replied: "Tell them I have got a summons already before a superior Judge and judicatory, and I behove to answer my first summons; and ere your day arrive, I will be where few kings and great folks come." He died on March 29, 1661, and his last words reportedly included: "Glory, glory dwelleth in Immanuel's land!"

Rutherford often spoke of Christ's crown and covenant — the crown that Christ earned through His obedience and suffering, and the covenant of grace that guarantees our share in that crown. The cross is warranted by our sin. The glory is guaranteed by God's grace. One is for time; the other is for eternity. The suffering passes; the crown endures.

For families, faithfulness is the calling. Not perfection — faithfulness. God does not ask us to be flawless parents, flawless children, or flawless Christians. He asks us to be faithful. Faithful in prayer even when we do not feel like it. Faithful in church even when it is inconvenient. Faithful in obedience even when it costs us. Faithful in love even when love is hard. Faithful unto death — and then the crown.

The Puritans understood that the Christian life is a marathon, not a sprint. There are no shortcuts to the crown. It is won through years of quiet faithfulness — through thousands of mornings opening the Bible when no one is watching, thousands of prayers offered in the silence of the night, thousands of small acts of obedience that the world never sees. But God sees. And God rewards.

Tonight, as you close this week with Rutherford, consider the kind of family you want to be. A family that is faithful to the end. A family that holds fast to Christ's covenant. A family that endures hardship with grace and faces death with hope. A family that will one day hear the Savior say, "Well done, good and faithful servant... enter thou into the joy of thy Lord" (Matthew 25:23).

The crown is sure. The covenant is unbreakable. The glory is eternal. Be faithful, pilgrim family. Your King is coming, and He brings your reward with Him.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher and learn something that will deepen our understanding.

Samuel Rutherford, in his work "Letters of Samuel Rutherford," wrote these words:

"The cross is warranted; the glory is sure. The one is for time; the other is for eternity."

What Samuel Rutherford is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Revelation 2:10; 2 Timothy 4:8 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Let us discuss what we have heard. Parents, encourage even the youngest to share what they are thinking.

Question 1: What does it mean to be 'faithful unto death'? Does this mean we have to be perfect?

Question 2: Paul said the crown is for 'all them that love his appearing.' Do you love the thought of Jesus returning? Why or why not?

Question 3: Rutherford was summoned before Parliament but said he had a higher summons. What does this teach us about our ultimate allegiance?

Question 4: Looking back at this week with Rutherford, what has been the most meaningful lesson for you?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life." -- Revelation 2:10

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Before we close, here is a family challenge for the days ahead:

Make simple paper crowns together as a family. On each crown, write 'Faithful unto Death — Revelation 2:10.' Talk about the crowns that await those who follow Jesus faithfully. Place the crowns somewhere visible as a reminder that the race is worth running and the prize is worth pursuing.

--- Closing ---

Heavenly Father, we end where we began, in gratitude. Thank You for Your Word, for this family, and for the hope we have in Christ. May tonight's lesson from Revelation 2:10; 2 Timothy 4:8 shape the way we live, the way we speak, and the way we love one another. Amen and amen.

Until next time, dear family. The Lord goes before you. Walk with Him.
