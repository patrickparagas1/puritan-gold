PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 5
"The Great Mystery"
Ephesians 5:31-33 — Mutual Submission and Love


Good evening, dear couple. Tonight we arrive at the summit of Paul's teaching on marriage in Ephesians 5. All that has come before has been leading to this moment, to a revelation so stunning that Paul himself calls it a great mystery. Let us approach this passage with reverence and wonder.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 5:31-33 (KJV):

"For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and they two shall be one flesh. This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church. Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband."


Paul here reaches back to the very beginning, to the garden of Eden, to the first words God spoke over the first marriage. He quotes Genesis 2:24, the foundational text of all marriage. A man shall leave his father and mother. He shall be joined to his wife. The two shall become one flesh. This is the architecture of marriage as God designed it.

But then Paul says something that changes everything. "This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church." In other words, the marriage of Adam and Eve, and every marriage since, is a living picture of something far greater: the union between Christ and His church. Your marriage, dear couple, is not merely a human arrangement for companionship and procreation. It is an earthly portrait of a heavenly reality. Every act of love between husband and wife points to Christ's love for His people. Every act of reverent trust points to the church's glad submission to her Lord.

William Gouge meditated deeply on this mystery. He wrote that when we understand marriage as a reflection of Christ and the church, it transforms every aspect of married life. The mundane becomes sacred. The ordinary becomes extraordinary. The daily acts of service, patience, forgiveness, and affection are no longer mere human kindnesses. They are echoes of the gospel. They proclaim to the watching world that Christ loves His church and that the church finds her joy in Christ.

This is why Paul summarizes the entire passage with two simple commands: let the husband love his wife as himself, and let the wife reverence her husband. Love and reverence. These two words capture the essence of the Christian marriage. The husband's primary calling is to love, actively, sacrificially, consistently, as we have seen throughout this week. The wife's primary calling is to reverence, to honor, respect, and esteem her husband, not because he is perfect, but because he bears a role that points to Christ.

Richard Baxter urged couples to keep this mystery always before their eyes. When love grows cold, remember that your marriage is a picture of Christ and the church. When conflict arises, remember that the way you treat each other is a testimony to the gospel. When weariness sets in, remember that every sacrifice you make for each other is a sacrifice made in the service of the King.

The Puritans took this mystery seriously. They viewed marriage not as a distraction from spiritual life but as one of the primary means through which God sanctifies His people. Marriage teaches us patience as nothing else can. Marriage humbles our pride and exposes our selfishness. Marriage drives us to our knees in prayer. And marriage, when it reflects the love of Christ, becomes one of the most powerful witnesses to the truth of the gospel that the world will ever see.

Dear husband and wife, your marriage is a great mystery. It is far more than a legal contract or a social arrangement. It is a divine institution that reflects the deepest reality in the universe: the love of God for His people in Christ Jesus. Honor this mystery. Protect this mystery. Let every day of your life together be a fresh declaration that Christ loves the church and gave Himself for her.

As we close this first week of devotionals, carry these truths with you. Mutual submission. Sacrificial love. Nourishing tenderness. Reverent honor. And beneath it all, the great mystery of Christ and the church, played out in the theater of your daily life together.


Question 1: How does knowing that our marriage reflects Christ and the church change the way we think about our daily interactions?

Question 2: Husband, do you feel reverenced and respected by your wife? Wife, do you feel loved and cherished by your husband? Share honestly.

Question 3: What is one way we can more intentionally let our marriage be a witness to the gospel this month?


Almighty God, we stand in awe of the great mystery Thou hast revealed. Our marriage, frail and imperfect as it is, is a picture of the love between Christ and His church. We are humbled by this honor and sobered by this responsibility. Help us, O Lord, to love and reverence one another in a manner worthy of this mystery. May our union bring glory to Thy name and bear witness to the saving love of Jesus Christ. Forgive us for the times we have treated our marriage as small or ordinary. Open our eyes to see the sacred in the everyday. In the name of our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ, Amen.
