PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Theological Reflection, Day 30
"Doxology: To God Be the Glory"
A Closing Hymn of Praise — The End of All Theology Is Worship


Good evening, friend. We have come to the final evening of June, and there is only one fitting way to end this month of growth: with praise. All of our study, all of our reflection, all of our application — if it has not led us to worship, it has failed. The Puritans understood that theology is not an end in itself. It is a road, and the road leads to the throne of God. Tonight, let us arrive there together.


Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Romans 11:33-36:

"O the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out! For who hath known the mind of the Lord? or who hath been his counsellor? Or who hath first given to him, and it shall be recompensed unto him again? For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever. Amen."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.


Paul's great doxology in Romans 11 does not arrive out of nowhere. It is the eruption of praise that follows eleven chapters of the most rigorous theological argument ever written. Paul has expounded sin, justification, sanctification, election, the place of Israel, and the purposes of God in history. And when he has exhausted the limits of his reasoning — when he has gone as deep as human language can carry him — he breaks into song. The theology does not end in a conclusion. It ends in a doxology. And this is exactly as it should be.

The Puritans understood this principle at the deepest level. For all their reputation as rigorous thinkers and precise theologians, they were first and foremost worshipers. Stephen Charnock, whose "Existence and Attributes of God" is perhaps the most exhaustive treatment of the divine nature ever written, did not write that massive work as a philosophical exercise. He wrote it to lead people to adoration. Every attribute of God that Charnock examines — His power, His wisdom, His holiness, His goodness, His sovereignty — becomes in his hands not merely a doctrine to be understood but a perfection to be praised.

Thomas Watson captured this perfectly when he wrote that the chief end of man is to glorify God and to enjoy Him forever. This answer from the Westminster Shorter Catechism is not merely an intellectual proposition. It is a declaration of purpose. We exist to glorify God. We were made for worship. And all the activities of the Christian life — prayer, study, service, fellowship, suffering — find their ultimate meaning in this one great end: the glory of God.

Consider what it means to say, as Paul does, that all things are "of him, and through him, and to him." All things originate in God. He is the fountain from which every good thing flows. All things are sustained by God. He is the power by which every moment is upheld. And all things are directed toward God. He is the end, the goal, the destination of every created thing. When we worship, we are not adding something to the universe that was not there before. We are joining the chorus that has been singing since the foundation of the world. We are finding our place in the great purpose of all creation.

John Owen reflected that the highest activity of the human soul is the contemplation of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ. Owen argued that this is not merely one spiritual discipline among many but the supreme exercise for which we were created and redeemed. In his treatise "Meditations on the Glory of Christ," Owen wrote that the believer who regularly gazes upon the glory of Christ will be gradually transformed into His likeness. Worship is not passive. It is the most powerful agent of change in the Christian life.

And what shall we praise God for as we close this month? For His holiness, which sets the standard for all goodness. For His sovereignty, which governs all things according to His perfect will. For His mercy, which pardons sinners who deserve only judgment. For the cross of Christ, where justice and love met in a single, saving act. For the gift of the Spirit, who dwells within us and makes us new. For the Word of God, which has fed us and guided us through these weeks of growth. For the communion of saints, who encourage us along the way. For the hope of glory, which sustains us in every trial. For the promise of the new creation, where every tear will be wiped away and every sorrow will give way to everlasting joy.

Richard Baxter, whose "Saints' Everlasting Rest" we studied earlier this month, wrote that the proper response to every truth we learn about God is not merely to understand it but to praise Him for it. If God is sovereign, praise Him for His sovereignty. If God is good, praise Him for His goodness. If God is faithful, praise Him for His faithfulness. Do not let a single attribute of God remain merely a doctrine in your head. Let it become a song in your heart.

The doxology is not the end. It is the beginning of everything. When this life is over and all our theological questions have been answered, we will discover that the only appropriate response to infinite glory is infinite praise. And we will spend eternity doing what we were made to do: worshiping the God who made us, redeemed us, and brought us safely home.


First, close this month with deliberate, unhurried worship. Set aside time tonight — even if only ten minutes — to praise God for what He has taught you this month. Do not ask Him for anything. Simply tell Him who He is and thank Him for what He has done.

Second, carry the habit of doxology into the months ahead. Let your study of God always end in worship. When you learn a new truth about God, turn it into a prayer of praise before you turn it into a point of application.

Third, remember that your entire life is meant to be a doxology. Every act of obedience, every word of kindness, every moment of faithfulness is an offering of praise to the God who is worthy of all glory. Live tomorrow as an act of worship.


O Lord our God, how excellent is Thy name in all the earth! Of Thee, and through Thee, and to Thee are all things. To Thee be glory forever. We praise Thee for Thy holiness, Thy sovereignty, Thy mercy, and Thy love. We praise Thee for the cross of Christ, the gift of the Spirit, and the hope of glory. We praise Thee for this month of growth, for every truth we have received, and for every grace Thou hast bestowed. Now unto the King eternal, immortal, invisible, the only wise God, be honour and glory for ever and ever. Amen.

Goodnight, dear friend. The month is ended, but the song has only begun. To God be the glory, forever and ever.
