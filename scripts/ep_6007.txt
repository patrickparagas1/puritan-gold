PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"The Fool Hath Said in His Heart"
June 7, 2026

---

"The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God. They are corrupt, they have done abominable works, there is none that doeth good. The Lord looked down from heaven upon the children of men, to see if there were any that did understand, and seek God. They are all gone aside, they are all together become filthy: there is none that doeth good, no, not one." -- Psalm 14:1-3

The Holy Spirit does not mince His words when He describes the character of the atheist. He calls him a fool. Not because he lacks intellectual ability -- for some of the most brilliant minds the world has ever known have professed unbelief -- but because he has made the most catastrophic miscalculation that any rational creature can make. He has weighed the evidence of creation, of conscience, and of providence, and he has arrived at the conclusion that there is no God. And this is folly of the highest order, for it is the denial of the most self-evident truth in the universe.

Thomas Watson, with his usual penetrating wit, observed that the atheist does not arrive at his denial of God through rational inquiry but through moral corruption. "The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God." Note well: he says it in his heart, not in his head. His atheism is not the product of philosophical reasoning but of sinful desire. He does not want there to be a God, because a God would mean a judge, and a judge would mean an accounting, and an accounting would mean condemnation. And so he suppresses the truth in unrighteousness, as the apostle Paul tells us in the epistle to the Romans, and persuades himself that the Being whose existence is written upon every star and proclaimed by every sunrise simply does not exist.

"They are corrupt, they have done abominable works, there is none that doeth good." Here the Spirit reveals the connection between atheism and moral degradation. The denial of God is not a merely theoretical error that leaves the rest of a man's life untouched. It is a corruption that spreads through the entire fabric of his being, poisoning every thought, every desire, every action. For when a man has removed God from his universe, he has removed the only foundation upon which morality can rest. If there is no God, there is no law above the law of man's own making. If there is no law above man's law, there is no absolute standard of right and wrong. And if there is no absolute standard, then every man becomes a law unto himself, and the only question is who has the power to impose his will upon others.

"The Lord looked down from heaven upon the children of men, to see if there were any that did understand, and seek God." What a solemn picture this is. The Almighty, from the vantage point of His infinite omniscience, surveys the entire race of mankind, searching for even one soul that truly understands, even one heart that genuinely seeks after God. And the verdict is devastating: "They are all gone aside, they are all together become filthy: there is none that doeth good, no, not one." The apostle Paul quotes these very words in the third chapter of Romans to prove the universal guilt of mankind and the absolute necessity of salvation by grace through faith in Jesus Christ. For if the whole race has turned aside, if every heart is corrupted, if no one seeks God of his own natural inclination, then salvation must come from outside the race altogether. It must come from God Himself, descending in mercy to seek and to save those who would never have sought Him.

John Owen, that mighty champion of the doctrines of grace, built much of his theology upon this very truth. The total depravity of human nature is not a pleasant doctrine, but it is a necessary one, for without it we can never understand the glory of redemption. If man is only partially fallen, then he needs only partial saving. If he has merely stumbled, he can perhaps pick himself up. But if he is dead in trespasses and sins, as the Scriptures declare, if there is none that seeketh God, no not one, then nothing less than the sovereign, almighty, irresistible grace of God can raise him from the grave of his spiritual death and bring him into the light of everlasting life.

Spurgeon preached with great earnestness upon this psalm, urging his hearers to see in it not merely a description of the heathen or the openly irreligious, but a mirror of their own natural condition apart from grace. "There is none that doeth good, no, not one." This includes every man and woman born of Adam. This includes the moral man and the religious man, the educated man and the philanthropist. Apart from the regenerating work of the Holy Spirit, every human heart is a fountain of corruption, and every human life, however outwardly respectable, falls infinitely short of the glory of God.

But the psalm does not end in despair. It ends with a cry of longing and hope: "Oh that the salvation of Israel were come out of Zion! When the Lord bringeth back the captivity of his people, Jacob shall rejoice, and Israel shall be glad." Out of the blackest darkness of human depravity shines the brightest star of divine promise. Salvation shall come out of Zion. God Himself shall intervene. The captives shall be set free. And when that great day dawns, when the Saviour comes forth to redeem His people from the bondage of sin and death, then shall God's people rejoice with a joy unspeakable and full of glory. And we who live on this side of Calvary know that this salvation has indeed come. It came in the person of Jesus Christ, who was born in Zion, who lived and died and rose again for our justification, and who shall come again in glory to bring back the captivity of His people and to make all things new. Amen.
