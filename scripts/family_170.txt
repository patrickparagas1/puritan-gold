PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 9
"The Godly Mother's Influence"
John Angell James — Female Piety


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight John Angell James speaks to us about the extraordinary influence of a godly mother — an influence that reaches further than any sermon, any book, or any institution. Let us listen with grateful hearts.

Let us pray. Father, we thank You tonight for mothers. We thank You for the love that wakes before dawn and works after dark, for the prayers whispered over sleeping children, for the hands that comfort and the voices that teach. Bless our time in Your Word tonight. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from 2 Timothy 1:5 and 2 Timothy 3:14-15. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"When I call to remembrance the unfeigned faith that is in thee, which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice; and I am persuaded that in thee also. But continue thou in the things which thou hast learned and hast been assured of, knowing of whom thou hast learned them; And that from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures, which are able to make thee wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus."

That is 2 Timothy 1:5 and 2 Timothy 3:14-15. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


John Angell James devotes some of the most powerful pages of "Female Piety" to the subject of motherhood, and he does so with a conviction born of pastoral observation spanning five decades. Again and again, as he counseled young converts, James would ask them how they came to faith. And again and again, the answer was the same: "My mother taught me." "My mother prayed for me." "My mother showed me what faith looked like."

The example of Timothy is the great biblical model. Paul traces Timothy's "unfeigned faith" — his sincere, genuine, unhypocritical faith — backward through two generations: from Timothy to his mother Eunice, and from Eunice to his grandmother Lois. This is the golden chain of maternal influence. A godly grandmother taught a daughter, and that daughter taught a son, and that son became one of the most effective ministers in the history of the church. The faith did not begin in the pulpit. It began at the hearth.

James writes that the mother's influence is unique for several reasons. First, she is with her children during their most formative years — the years when the mind is most impressionable, the heart most tender, and the habits of a lifetime are being formed. What is planted in a child's soul during those early years will bear fruit — good or ill — for decades to come. The father may be absent for much of the day at his labor, but the mother is present, and her presence is a constant, shaping force.

Second, the mother teaches as much by example as by instruction. Children watch their mothers with an intensity that few adults appreciate. They observe how she responds to difficulty. They notice whether she prays. They listen to the tone of her voice when she speaks of God. They absorb her attitudes toward Scripture, toward worship, toward other people. A mother who lives her faith openly and honestly before her children is teaching them every hour of every day, even when she is not aware of it.

James tells the story of Augustine of Hippo, one of the greatest theologians in the history of the church. Augustine wandered far from God in his youth, pursuing pleasure, philosophy, and heresy. But his mother Monica never stopped praying for him. For over thirty years, she pleaded with God for her son's salvation. She wept so many tears that a bishop once told her, "It is impossible that the child of such tears should perish." And indeed, Augustine was gloriously converted, and the church has been enriched by his writings ever since. James holds Monica up as the model of the praying mother — a woman who refused to give up on her child, who carried him before the throne of grace year after year, and who lived to see the answer to her prayers.

Third, the mother possesses a peculiar tenderness that makes her uniquely suited to communicate the love of God. James observes that children often first understand the love of God through the love of their mother. A mother's forgiveness mirrors God's forgiveness. A mother's patience mirrors God's patience. A mother's unfailing care mirrors the unfailing care of the heavenly Father. This is not to say that fathers are unimportant — they are essential. But the mother's love has a quality of tenderness that often opens the door of a child's heart to receive the greater love of God.

James also warns of the consequences when a mother neglects her spiritual influence. A mother who is indifferent to faith, who never speaks of Christ, who never prays with her children, who prioritizes everything above their spiritual welfare — such a mother may provide for her children's bodies while starving their souls. James writes with evident grief of homes where every material need is met but where God is never mentioned, where the Bible collects dust, and where children grow up without any knowledge of their Creator, their Savior, or their eternal destiny.

To every mother listening tonight, and to every family that loves a mother: take courage. The work of motherhood is sacred work. It is eternal work. It is work that God sees, values, and blesses — even when the world does not. Pray for your children. Teach them the Scriptures. Show them what faith looks like. And trust that the God who heard Monica's prayers for Augustine hears your prayers as well.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Paul says Timothy learned the Scriptures "from a child" through his mother and grandmother. What are some of the earliest spiritual lessons you remember learning? Who taught them to you?

Question 2: James says children learn as much from a mother's example as from her words. What are some ways a parent teaches without speaking?

Question 3: Monica prayed for Augustine for over thirty years before he was converted. What does her example teach us about persevering in prayer for our loved ones?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"From a child thou hast known the holy scriptures, which are able to make thee wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus." -- 2 Timothy 3:15

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for the mothers and grandmothers who have planted the seeds of faith in our lives. Bless every mother in our family. Give her strength for weary days, patience for difficult moments, and the joy of seeing her children walk in truth. Hear the prayers she lifts to You on their behalf, and answer them according to Your great mercy. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
