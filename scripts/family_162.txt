PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 1
"The Sacred Household"
William Gouge — Domestical Duties

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to a new month of Puritan Gold family devotions. This May, we turn our attention to the Puritan vision for the Christian household. We begin with one of the greatest Puritan works ever written on family life — William Gouge's "Of Domesticall Duties." Let us draw near to God together.

Let us pray. Heavenly Father, we gather as a family under Your roof and under Your care. We thank You for the gift of home, for one another, and for the wisdom You have preserved through Your faithful servants across the centuries. Open our hearts tonight to receive instruction on how to build our household according to Your Word. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Ephesians 5:21-25. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God. Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord. For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body. Therefore as the church is subject unto Christ, so let the wives be to their own husbands in every thing. Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it."

That is Ephesians 5:21-25. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Tonight we begin a journey into the heart of the Puritan home. And there is no better guide than William Gouge, pastor of Blackfriars Church in London for over thirty years, from 1608 until his death in 1653. Gouge was not a man who spoke of family life from a distance. He was a husband and father of thirteen children. He knew the joys and sorrows, the triumphs and trials of household life from intimate, daily experience.

In 1622, Gouge published "Of Domesticall Duties," a massive and meticulous treatment of family life drawn from his exposition of Ephesians 5 and 6. The work is remarkable for its thoroughness. Gouge addresses every relationship within the household — husbands and wives, parents and children, masters and servants — and brings each one under the searching light of Scripture. His great conviction was that the family is not merely a social institution but a sacred one, ordained by God as the foundational unit of both church and society.

Gouge begins with a principle that must frame everything else we study this month: the family is a little church. He writes that what the pastor is to the congregation, the father ought to be to his household. The home is not merely a place where we eat and sleep and go about our private affairs. It is a place of worship, instruction, prayer, and spiritual formation. Every Christian household, however humble, is a sanctuary where God's name is to be honored and His Word is to be taught.

This idea — the family as a little church — was one of the most powerful and distinctive convictions of the Puritan movement. The Puritans believed that if the family was right, the church would be right, and if the church was right, the nation would be right. But if the family was disordered, no amount of public religion could make up for the spiritual neglect that occurred behind closed doors.

Paul's letter to the Ephesians provides the theological foundation. In chapter 5, he draws a stunning parallel between marriage and the relationship between Christ and His church. The husband is to love his wife "even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it." This is not a call to domination but to sacrifice. Christ's headship was expressed not in demanding service from His bride but in laying down His life for her. The husband who leads his family in the pattern of Christ leads through self-giving love, through patient instruction, through prayerful intercession, and through personal holiness.

And notice that Paul begins the entire passage not with a command to one party or another, but with a call to mutual submission: "Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God." The fear of God is the atmosphere in which all household duties are to be performed. When every member of the family lives in reverent awareness of God's presence, the home becomes a place of order, peace, and mutual care.

Gouge was careful to insist that every member of the household has sacred duties. The husband has duties to his wife. The wife has duties to her husband. Parents have duties to their children. Children have duties to their parents. No one is exempt, and no one's duties are less important than another's. The household is a web of mutual obligation, held together by love and governed by the Word of God.

As we begin this month, let us ask ourselves: Is our home a little church? Do we worship together? Do we pray together? Do we read Scripture together? Do we speak of Christ and His ways around our table and in our daily conversations? If we do, we are building on the foundation that Gouge and the Puritans commended. If we do not, this month is an invitation to begin.

The Puritan household was not perfect. Gouge himself acknowledged that his own home fell short of the ideal he preached. But the ideal was always there — shining, beckoning, calling families upward toward the pattern God has revealed in His Word. Let us pursue that ideal together, not in our own strength, but in the strength that God supplies through His Spirit and His grace.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: What does it mean to call the family "a little church"? In what ways does our family already function like a church, and in what ways could we grow?

Question 2: Paul tells husbands to love their wives "as Christ loved the church." What are some practical ways this self-sacrificing love can be shown in daily family life?

Question 3: Paul begins with "submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God." How does the fear of God change the way family members treat each other?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God." -- Ephesians 5:21

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for this time as a family. Thank You for the wisdom of Your servant William Gouge, and for the vision of the Christian household that You have preserved for us in Your Word. Help us to build our home on the foundation of mutual love, mutual submission, and the fear of Your holy name. Make our family a little church where Your presence is known and Your glory is displayed. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
