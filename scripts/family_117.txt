PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 17
"The Heart God Sees"
William Secker — The Nonsuch Professor

---

Welcome, family. Whether you are gathered on the couch, around the dinner table, or riding in the car, God is here with you. Let us turn our attention to Him and see what treasure He has stored up for us tonight.

Let us bow our heads. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family in Your presence. We need Your wisdom tonight. We need Your guidance. Open our ears to hear, our minds to understand, and our hearts to receive. We pray in the precious name of Jesus. Amen.

Let us turn together to 1 Samuel 16:7. Follow along in your Bible if you have one:

"But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart."

That is 1 Samuel 16:7. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Today we explore one of the most important truths in all of Scripture: God looks at the heart. In 1 Samuel 16:7, the prophet Samuel is sent by God to anoint a new king over Israel. Samuel arrives at the house of Jesse in Bethlehem, and when he sees Jesse's oldest son Eliab — tall, handsome, impressive — he thinks, 'Surely this is the Lord's anointed.'

But God stops Samuel with these words: 'Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.'

One by one, seven of Jesse's sons pass before Samuel, and God rejects each one. Finally, they send for the youngest — David, the shepherd boy, out in the fields tending sheep. And God says, 'Arise, anoint him: for this is he.' God chose the one nobody expected. The one who was overlooked. The one who was young, small, and insignificant by human standards. Because God was not looking at the outside. He was looking at the heart.

This principle runs throughout Scripture. In Proverbs 4:23, Solomon writes, 'Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.' The heart — your inner life, your thoughts, your motives, your affections — is the source from which everything else flows. A good heart produces good fruit. A corrupt heart produces corruption, no matter how polished the outside looks.

Jesus made this crystal clear in Matthew 23:25-26, when He said to the Pharisees, 'Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye make clean the outside of the cup and of the platter, but within they are full of extortion and excess. Thou blind Pharisee, cleanse first that which is within the cup and platter, that the outside of them may be clean also.' The Pharisees were obsessed with outward religion — proper rituals, visible piety, impressive prayers — but their hearts were far from God.

In Jeremiah 17:9-10, we read the honest diagnosis: 'The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it? I the LORD search the heart, I try the reins, even to give every man according to his ways, and according to the fruit of his doings.' Only God can truly know the human heart. And only God can change it. That is why Psalm 51:10 is such an important prayer: 'Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.'

William Secker wrote about this with great penetration. He said that God regards not the pomp of our words but the power of our hearts. He values not the shell of profession but the kernel of sincerity. A clean heart, Secker said, is worth more than a loud tongue.

Secker observed that many people spend enormous energy polishing their outward appearance — their reputation, their public behavior, their religious performance — while neglecting the condition of their hearts. He compared this to a person who paints the outside of a house while the inside is full of rot. Eventually, the rot will show through, no matter how many coats of paint you apply.

Secker also made a comforting observation. Just as God sees through the false piety of hypocrites, He also sees the genuine faith of humble believers who may not look impressive on the outside. The quiet grandmother who prays faithfully every morning may be a far greater saint in God's eyes than the famous preacher who draws crowds. The child who sincerely whispers, 'Jesus, I love You,' at bedtime may offer a more pleasing prayer than the theologian who crafts elaborate petitions. God sees the heart.

This truth is both sobering and liberating. Sobering, because we cannot hide anything from God. He sees our secret thoughts, our hidden motives, our private sins. But liberating, because we do not have to pretend with God. We can come to Him exactly as we are — messy, imperfect, struggling — and He receives us because He looks at our hearts, not our performance.

For our youngest family members: God can see inside your heart, and He loves what is real — not what is fake. Even if nobody else notices when you do something kind, God sees it! For older children: are there areas of your life where you look good on the outside but know something is not right on the inside? God invites you to be honest with Him. For the whole family: let us be a family that values sincerity over appearance — where it is safe to be honest, to struggle, and to grow.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

For centuries, faithful Christians have reflected on passages like this one. Let us hear from a trusted voice of the past.

William Secker, in his work "The Nonsuch Professor in His Meridian Splendour," wrote these words:

"God regards not the pomp of our words but the power of our hearts. He values not the shell of profession but the kernel of sincerity. A clean heart is worth more than a loud tongue."

What William Secker is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in 1 Samuel 16:7 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Discussion time. These questions are for every member of the family, young and old alike.

Question 1: Why did God choose David and not Eliab? What was different about them?

Question 2: What does it mean that God looks at the heart? Can we fool God by looking good on the outside?

Question 3: Why do you think people spend so much time on how they look on the outside instead of their hearts?

Question 4: How does it make you feel to know that God sees the real you — and still loves you?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"For man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart." -- 1 Samuel 16:7b

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

The Puritans believed that truth must be practiced, not just heard. Here is something you can do together:

Get two boxes or bags. Decorate one to look beautiful on the outside but put something unpleasant inside (crumpled paper, empty wrappers). Leave the other one plain on the outside but put a small treat or treasure inside. Let the children choose one. Discuss: things are not always what they seem on the outside. God cares about what is inside.

--- Closing ---

Let us bow our heads and pray. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family around Your Word. We ask You to write these truths on our hearts. Bless our family, protect our home, and lead us in the path of righteousness for Your name's sake. In Christ we pray. Amen.

Until tomorrow, family. May the Lord bless you and keep you.
