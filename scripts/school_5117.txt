PURITAN GOLD -- Homeschool, Lesson 17
"Is Jesus the Only Way?"
Apologetics for Young Christians — Lesson 17

---

Welcome back to Puritan Gold's homeschool program. Today we address one of the most culturally sensitive questions in apologetics: Is Jesus really the only way to God? We will examine the biblical teaching and the reasoning behind the Christian claim of exclusivity. Let us pray and begin.

Lord Jesus, You said, "I am the way, the truth, and the life." Give us courage to affirm this truth and wisdom to share it with gentleness and respect. In Christ's name, Amen.

--- Lesson ---

In a culture that celebrates diversity and tolerance, the Christian claim that Jesus is the only way to God sounds arrogant, narrow-minded, and even offensive to many people. "How can you say that your religion is the only right one? Aren't all religions basically the same? Isn't it presumptuous to claim that billions of non-Christians are wrong?" These are common objections, and they deserve thoughtful, careful responses.

Let us begin with what the Bible actually teaches. Jesus made the exclusive claim Himself in John 14:6: "I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me." This is not one interpretation among many. It is Jesus's own unambiguous statement. He did not say, "I am a way" or "I am one of many paths." He said, "I am the way" — the definite article indicating uniqueness and exclusivity.

The apostles echoed this claim. Peter declared before the Jewish leaders, "Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved" (Acts 4:12). Paul wrote, "There is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus" (1 Timothy 2:5). The New Testament is unanimous: salvation is found in Christ alone.

But why? Why would God provide only one way of salvation? The answer lies in the nature of the problem that salvation addresses. The human predicament is not merely ignorance (which could be solved by better teaching) or moral imperfection (which could be addressed by a moral example). The human predicament is guilt before a holy God — a guilt so profound and so pervasive that no human effort, no religious ritual, and no amount of moral improvement can remove it.

The Ten Commandments, which we studied earlier this month, reveal the depth of this problem. God's standard is perfection. "Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect" (Matthew 5:48). No human being has ever met this standard. We have all fallen short. And the penalty for falling short is not a slap on the wrist — it is death. "The wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23).

Given the gravity of this problem, only a solution that addresses it at its root can be effective. And that solution is the cross of Christ. On the cross, Jesus — the sinless Son of God — bore the penalty for human sin in His own body. He died in our place, satisfying the demands of divine justice so that we could be forgiven. No other religious figure in history has made this claim or accomplished this work. Muhammad did not die for sins. Buddha did not atone for guilt. No human moral teacher, however wise, has the ability to bear the penalty for another person's sin before a holy God. Only Christ — who is both God and man — could bridge the infinite gap between a holy God and sinful humanity.

Now, are all religions basically the same? This is one of the most common claims in our culture, and it sounds open-minded and tolerant. But is it true? A brief examination shows that it is not. Hinduism teaches that ultimate reality is impersonal. Christianity teaches that ultimate reality is a personal God. Buddhism teaches that the self is an illusion to be extinguished. Christianity teaches that the self is a creation of God with eternal significance. Islam teaches that salvation comes through submission and good works. Christianity teaches that salvation comes through grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone. These are not minor differences. They are fundamental, irreconcilable contradictions. All religions cannot be basically the same, because they make mutually exclusive truth claims. They may all be wrong, but they cannot all be right.

Is the claim of Christian exclusivity arrogant? It would be if Christians were saying, "We are better than everyone else." But that is not the Christian claim. The Christian claim is that all of us — Christians included — are sinners in need of a Savior. The exclusivity of Christ is not about the superiority of Christians. It is about the uniqueness of Christ. He is the only one who died for our sins and rose again. That is not a claim of arrogance. It is a recognition of reality.

How should Christians communicate this truth? With the meekness and reverence that Peter commended. With genuine love for the people we are speaking to. With a recognition that the exclusivity of Christ is not a weapon to be wielded but a gift to be offered. Jesus is the only way, and that is extraordinarily good news — because the way is open to everyone.

--- Review Questions ---

Review Question 1: What did Jesus claim about Himself in John 14:6? Why is this claim significant for the question of religious exclusivity?

Review Question 2: Why can't all religions be "basically the same"? Give at least two examples of fundamental contradictions between major world religions.

Review Question 3: How can Christians communicate the exclusivity of Christ without being arrogant or dismissive?

--- Activity ---

Create a comparison chart of four major world religions: Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, and Buddhism. For each religion, summarize its teaching on three key topics: the nature of God (or ultimate reality), the human problem, and the solution. Identify specific points where these teachings directly contradict one another. Then write a paragraph explaining how these contradictions demonstrate that the claim "all religions are basically the same" is inaccurate.

--- Closing ---

The claim that Jesus is the only way to God is not arrogance — it is the gospel. And the gospel is the best news the world has ever heard. Share it with confidence and love. Until next time, students.
