PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 30
"The Family Covenant"
Synthesis — Building a Puritan Home Today, Day 2


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we take the wisdom we have gathered this month and forge it into something practical — a family covenant. The Puritans were great believers in covenant-making, and tonight we follow their example. Let us come before the Lord.

Let us pray. Lord God, the Puritans understood that the Christian life is a life of covenants — promises made before You and kept by Your grace. Help our family tonight to make promises that are rooted in Your Word, empowered by Your Spirit, and aimed at Your glory. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Joshua 24:14-15, 24. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Now therefore fear the Lord, and serve him in sincerity and in truth: and put away the gods which your fathers served on the other side of the flood, and in Egypt; and serve ye the Lord. And if it seem evil unto you to serve the Lord, choose you this day whom ye will serve; whether the gods which your fathers served that were on the other side of the flood, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye dwell: but as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord. And the people said unto the Lord, The Lord our God will we serve, and his voice will we obey."

That is Joshua 24:14-15, 24. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


The Puritans were covenant people. They believed that the entire relationship between God and His people was structured by covenants — solemn promises, sealed by God's faithfulness and received by human faith. The covenant of grace, the covenant of redemption, the covenant of baptism, the covenant of church membership — these were the frameworks within which the Puritans understood their lives.

And they extended this covenantal thinking to the family. Many Puritan households drew up family covenants — written statements of the commitments that every member of the household made before God and before one another. These were not legal contracts but sacred pledges — expressions of the family's determination to live together according to the Word of God and to hold one another accountable for faithfulness.

Joshua modeled this practice at Shechem. After reviewing all that God had done for Israel, he called the people to a decision: "Choose you this day whom ye will serve." And then he made his own declaration: "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." This was a household covenant — a public commitment, made by the head of the family on behalf of the entire household, to serve the living God.

Tonight, drawing from the wisdom of Gouge, James, Henry, and Baxter, let us consider what a family covenant might include.

From William Gouge, we learn that we should commit to treating our home as a little church — a place of worship, instruction, and mutual care. We commit to fulfilling the duties of our particular roles — husbands loving sacrificially, wives partnering faithfully, children honoring gladly, and all members speaking words that build up rather than tear down.

From John Angell James, we learn that we should commit to cultivating inner piety — the hidden life of the heart. We commit to pursuing godliness not only in public but in private, to guarding our hearts against worldliness and complacency, and to serving others with the practical compassion of Dorcas.

From Matthew Henry, we learn that we should commit to the daily practice of family worship — reading the Scriptures, praying together, and singing praises to God. We commit to making the Bible the central book of our home and the Lord's Day the central day of our week.

From Richard Baxter, we learn that we should commit to building a household characterized by love, patience, encouragement, and peace. We commit to resolving conflicts quickly, disciplining with grace, stewarding our time wisely, and keeping our eyes fixed on eternity.

A family covenant might be written down and signed by every member of the household. It might be displayed in a prominent place in the home — on the wall, on the refrigerator, by the family Bible. It might be read aloud together at the beginning of each month or on special occasions. The form matters less than the substance. What matters is that the family makes a deliberate, conscious, prayerful commitment to live together under the authority of God's Word.

Tonight, I invite each family to begin drafting your own family covenant. It need not be long or elaborate. A few simple sentences, drawn from the truths we have studied this month, will serve as a powerful reminder of who you are and how you have committed to live. And the God who honors every sincere commitment made in His name will honor yours.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Joshua made a family covenant before God: "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." What would it mean for our family to make a similar commitment?

Question 2: From this month's four teachers, what are the most important commitments our family should include in a family covenant?

Question 3: Would our family like to write a family covenant together? What should it say? Let us begin drafting it tonight.

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"And the people said unto the Lord, The Lord our God will we serve, and his voice will we obey." -- Joshua 24:24

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord God, we stand before You tonight as a family and say with Joshua: as for us and our house, we will serve the Lord. We make this commitment not in our own strength but in Yours. Seal our promises with Your grace. Hold us accountable by Your Spirit. And when we fail, restore us by Your mercy. We are Yours, Lord — every member of this household. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
