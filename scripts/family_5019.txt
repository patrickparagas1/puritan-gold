PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 19
"Gracious Speech and Loving Words"
Jonathan Edwards — Religious Affections Applied to the Family

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Edwards shows us that genuine affections transform our speech, and we explore how gracious words can create a home atmosphere filled with love and encouragement. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, set a watch over our mouths tonight. Let the words of our lips and the meditations of our hearts be acceptable in Your sight. Fill our home with gracious speech that builds up and never tears down. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Ephesians 4:29-32. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers. And grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption. Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice: And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you."

That is Ephesians 4:29-32. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Jonathan Edwards taught that one of the most reliable indicators of genuine spiritual affection is the transformation of speech. The mouth speaks what the heart is full of, and when the heart is full of true love for God and others, the speech that flows from it is gracious, edifying, and kind. Conversely, when the heart is full of self-interest, bitterness, or pride, the speech that flows from it is harsh, critical, and destructive.

For families, this principle is inescapable. The words spoken in a home have enormous power. They can build a child's confidence or crush it. They can strengthen a marriage or erode it. They can create an atmosphere of warmth and safety or an atmosphere of tension and fear. Edwards would say that if you want to know the true spiritual condition of a family, listen to how its members speak to one another — especially when they are tired, stressed, or in conflict.

Paul's instruction in Ephesians 4 provides a comprehensive guide for family speech. First, the negative: "Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth." Corrupt communication includes not only profanity and vulgarity but also gossip, sarcasm, cutting remarks, constant criticism, and passive-aggressive comments. These forms of speech are like poison in the family's water supply — they contaminate everything they touch.

Edwards would apply this with pastoral specificity. Parents, do you use sarcasm with your children? It may seem harmless or even humorous, but sarcasm is veiled contempt, and children — who are remarkably perceptive — feel the sting even when they cannot articulate what hurt them. Do you compare your children to one another? "Why can't you be more like your brother?" These words create rivalry and resentment that can last a lifetime. Do you express frustration through harsh tone and volume rather than through calm, clear instruction? The volume of your voice communicates just as much as the content of your words.

Children, do you speak to your parents with respect, or do you respond with eye-rolls, sighs, and dismissive tones? Do you speak to your siblings with kindness, or do you resort to name-calling and mockery? The habits of speech you form now will follow you into adulthood and shape every relationship you ever have.

Now the positive: Paul says our words should be "good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers." This is a stunning standard. Every word that leaves our mouths should be measured by this question: Does it build up? Does it minister grace? Grace means undeserved kindness, and a home where words minister grace is a home where people feel valued, encouraged, and loved — not because they have earned it, but because grace overflows from hearts that are full of God's goodness.

Edwards connected this transformation of speech to the work of the Holy Spirit. We cannot produce gracious speech by willpower alone. We need the Spirit to change our hearts, because the heart is the wellspring of all speech. When the Spirit fills a person with genuine love for God and others, the speech that flows out is naturally more gracious, more patient, more kind. This is why family devotion is so important — it is one of the primary means by which the Spirit does His transforming work in our hearts.

Paul concludes with the ultimate motivation: "Forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you." The words we speak to our family members should be shaped by the staggering reality that God has forgiven us. If God — who has every right to condemn us — speaks to us with grace, how dare we speak to one another with contempt?

Tonight, let your family commit to a new standard of speech. Let every word be measured by grace.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Edwards said that speech reveals the condition of the heart. What does the way we speak to one another at home reveal about our hearts?

Question 2: Paul said our words should "minister grace" to the hearer. What would it look like for each family member to speak one grace-filled sentence to another family member right now?

Question 3: What is one pattern of speech in our home that we would like to change this week?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers." -- Ephesians 4:29

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord, forgive us for the careless and hurtful words we have spoken to one another. Transform our speech by transforming our hearts. Fill our home with words that build up, encourage, and minister grace. Let our family be known as people who speak with kindness and love. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, blessed by the gracious words of a God who speaks life over you.
