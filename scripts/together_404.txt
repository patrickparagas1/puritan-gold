PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 12
"Redeeming the Time Together"
Ephesians 5:15-16 — Walking Wisely as a Couple

---

Welcome, dear couple. Tonight we consider how the stewardship of time shapes the health of a marriage.

Our Scripture tonight is Ephesians 5:15-16:

"See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, Redeeming the time, because the days are evil."

The Puritans applied this verse not only to individual believers but to married couples — and their insights are remarkably relevant to our busy modern lives.

--- Teaching ---

William Gouge observed that one of the surest signs of a healthy marriage is the way a couple uses their time. "Many a marriage," he wrote, "has died not from hatred but from hurry." The demands of work, children, church, and community can crowd out the very relationship that all of these things are meant to serve.

The phrase "redeeming the time" means buying it back — rescuing it from waste and using it wisely. For married couples, this means being intentional about carving out time for each other. Not leftover time. Not the scraps at the end of an exhausting day. But deliberate, protected, prioritized time for your marriage.

Richard Baxter counseled couples to establish daily rhythms of connection. He suggested morning prayers together, even if brief. Evening conversation about the day's mercies and struggles. Weekly time set apart for deeper discussion of spiritual matters and the state of the marriage. And periodic seasons of retreat — getting away from the routine to remember why you chose each other and to dream about the future together.

The Puritans were not legalistic about these practices. They recognized that every couple's circumstances are different and that the specific rhythms will vary. But they were unanimous in their conviction that a marriage left on autopilot will drift. Intentionality is the rudder that keeps a marriage on course.

Thomas Gataker noted that "the days are evil" applies particularly to married life. The world constantly conspires against your marriage. The culture devalues commitment. Work demands expand to fill every available hour. Technology creates the illusion of connection while fostering actual isolation. Entertainment offers easy pleasure that requires no investment. Against all of these forces, a couple must be vigilant — walking circumspectly, making deliberate choices about how they spend their limited time.

The Puritans also taught that quality time together need not be elaborate or expensive. Reading the Bible together — as you are doing right now — is one of the most powerful uses of time a couple can invest in. Praying together. Walking together. Sitting in companionable silence. Cooking a meal and eating it without screens. These simple practices build intimacy more effectively than any vacation or grand gesture.

But the Puritans were also advocates of fun. Baxter wrote that recreation and laughter are essential to a healthy marriage. A couple that never plays together, never laughs together, never enjoys the simple pleasures of life together, is missing an important dimension of God's gift. Redeeming the time does not mean filling every moment with serious spiritual activity. It means using time wisely — and wisdom includes joy, rest, and play.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Discuss together:

Question 1: How would you honestly describe the way you currently spend your time together? Is it intentional or accidental? Nourishing or depleting?

Question 2: What is one daily rhythm of connection you would like to establish or restore this summer? What is standing in the way?

Question 3: When was the last time you had fun together — genuine, lighthearted fun? What would you enjoy doing together this week?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Pray together for wisdom in the stewardship of your time. Ask God to reveal the time-wasters that are stealing from your marriage and to give you the courage to eliminate them. Pray for the discipline to prioritize each other and the creativity to enjoy each other.

--- Closing ---

May the Lord who gives us every moment help you redeem the time you have been given as a couple. May your days be filled with purpose, your evenings with connection, and your hearts with the joy of a marriage well-tended. Goodnight.
