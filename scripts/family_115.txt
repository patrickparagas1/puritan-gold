PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 15
"What Makes a True Christian"
William Secker — The Nonsuch Professor

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to tonight's family devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for gathering together around God's Word. Let us quiet our hearts and prepare to listen to what the Lord has for us.

Let us begin with prayer. Heavenly Father, we thank You for this family. We thank You for bringing us safely through another day. Now we ask that You would open our eyes to see wonderful things in Your Word. Speak to each heart gathered here, from the oldest to the youngest. In Jesus' name we pray. Amen.

Tonight we open our Bibles to Matthew 7:21-23. Listen carefully as I read our passage:

"Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven. Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works? And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity."

That is Matthew 7:21-23. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Welcome to Week 3, where we will learn from the pen of William Secker, a Puritan preacher from the 1600s. His most famous work, The Nonsuch Professor, is a sparkling collection of observations about what it means to be a genuine, true-hearted Christian. 'Nonsuch' means 'without equal' — Secker was describing the Christian who lives with authentic, genuine, matchless faith.

We begin with a sobering passage from Jesus Himself. In Matthew 7:21-23, near the end of the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus delivers what may be the most frightening words in the entire Bible. He says, 'Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven.'

This is startling. Jesus says that there will be people who call Him 'Lord' — who use all the right religious language — but who will not enter heaven. Not because they did not know the right words, but because they did not do the will of the Father.

Then Jesus paints an even more alarming picture. He says, 'Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works?' These people are not small-time religious pretenders. They prophesied, cast out demons, and did wonderful works — all in Jesus' name! By any outward measure, they look like powerful, successful Christians.

But Jesus' response is devastating: 'I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.' Not 'I used to know you' — 'I never knew you.' Despite all their impressive religious activity, they had no genuine relationship with Christ. Their religion was outward performance without inward reality.

James the brother of Jesus addresses this same issue in James 2:17, 'Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone.' And in 1 John 2:4, the apostle John writes, 'He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.' The whole New Testament insists that genuine faith produces genuine change.

This does not mean we earn our salvation by good works. Ephesians 2:8-9 is clear: 'For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.' But the very next verse says, 'For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.' Salvation is by grace alone, but saving grace always produces a changed life.

William Secker understood this perfectly. He wrote that the true Christian does not only profess the faith of Christ but possesses the life of Christ. He does not wear religion as a garment that can be put on and taken off but has it as his very nature.

Secker was a master of vivid, memorable phrases. He compared the hypocrite to a person who wears a crown but has no kingdom — who has the title without the reality. He compared the true Christian to a tree that bears fruit naturally, not because it is forced to but because fruit is what healthy trees produce.

Secker wrote, 'A painted fire is no fire; a painted feast will not satisfy a hungry man.' In other words, a picture of a warm fire will not keep you warm on a cold night, and a painting of a banquet will not fill an empty stomach. In the same way, a pretended faith will not save your soul. Only the real thing will do.

This is not meant to frighten children — or adults — into anxious self-examination. It is meant to encourage us to seek the real thing. God is not looking for perfection. He is looking for genuineness. A child who sincerely loves Jesus but struggles with obedience is in a very different place than someone who goes through religious motions with no real heart for God.

For our youngest ones: God does not just want us to say we love Him — He wants us to show it by how we live. If you love someone, you want to make them happy, right? That is what God wants from us. For older children: think about this — is your faith your own, or are you just going along with what your parents believe? It is okay to ask that question. God wants you to have your own genuine relationship with Him. For the whole family: let us not settle for a painted fire. Let us seek the real, living, burning faith that comes from truly knowing Jesus Christ.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

Now let us hear from one of the great Puritan teachers of the faith.

William Secker, in his work "The Nonsuch Professor in His Meridian Splendour," wrote these words:

"The Christian is one who does not only profess the faith of Christ but who possesses the life of Christ. He does not have religion as his garment but as his nature."

What William Secker is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Matthew 7:21-23 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Now it is time for our family discussion. Here are some questions to talk about together. Take your time with each one.

Question 1: What does Jesus say about people who just say 'Lord, Lord' but do not obey God?

Question 2: What is the difference between knowing about Jesus and actually knowing Him?

Question 3: What did Secker mean when he said religion should be our nature, not just our garment?

Question 4: How can we tell if our faith is real — not just going through the motions?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven." -- Matthew 7:21

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Here is a family activity you can do this week to help these truths take root:

Do a 'Real vs. Fake' test. Gather some real and fake items — a real fruit and a plastic one, a real flower and an artificial one, a real coin and a toy one. Examine them together. Discuss: what makes the real one different from the fake? How does this apply to real vs. pretend faith?

--- Closing ---

Let us close in prayer. Heavenly Father, thank You for gathering our family together in Your Word tonight. Thank You for the truth we have heard from Matthew 7:21-23. Help us to live out what we have learned. Guard our hearts, guide our steps, and fill our home with Your love and peace. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

Goodnight, dear ones. Walk in wisdom tomorrow.
