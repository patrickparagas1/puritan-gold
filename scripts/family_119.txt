PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 19
"Bearing Fruit for God's Glory"
William Secker — The Nonsuch Professor

---

Hello and welcome to our family devotional. Come, young and old alike. God's Word has something for every member of this family tonight. Let us draw near to Him.

Shall we pray? Our Father, we gather tonight hungry for Your truth. We know that man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from Your mouth. Feed us now, Lord. Bless our family time together. In the name of Christ we pray. Amen.

God has preserved His Word for thousands of years so that families like ours could hear it tonight. Let us read from John 15:1-5:

"I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman. Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away: and every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit. Now ye are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you. Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me. I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing."

That is John 15:1-5. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Today we come to one of the most beautiful images in all of Scripture — Jesus as the vine and His followers as the branches. In John 15, Jesus speaks these words on the night before His crucifixion, during His final intimate conversation with His disciples.

'I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.' Jesus uses a picture that every person in Israel would have understood. Vineyards were everywhere. The grapevine was a symbol of Israel itself. Now Jesus declares that He is the true vine — the real one, the one all others pointed to.

The Father is the 'husbandman' — the gardener, the vinedresser. He tends the vine. He prunes the branches. He watches over the vineyard.

Then Jesus explains the relationship between Himself and His followers: 'I am the vine, ye are the branches.' We are not independent trees standing on our own. We are branches, connected to Christ the vine. Everything we are, everything we produce, comes from our connection to Him.

The key command in this passage is 'abide.' Jesus says it again and again: 'Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me.' To abide means to remain, to stay connected, to dwell, to make your home. A branch that is broken off from the vine withers and dies. It cannot produce a single grape on its own. But a branch that stays connected to the vine receives the vine's life, nutrients, and strength — and it produces fruit naturally and abundantly.

Then comes a profound statement: 'For without me ye can do nothing.' Nothing. Not 'without me you can do less.' Nothing. Apart from Christ, we cannot produce anything of eternal value. This is humbling. But it is also liberating. It means the pressure is not on us to produce spiritual fruit by our own effort. Our job is to stay connected to Christ — through prayer, His Word, worship, and obedience. He produces the fruit through us.

Galatians 5:22-23 describes this fruit: 'But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.' Notice it is called 'the fruit of the Spirit,' not 'the fruit of human effort.' These qualities grow in us as we abide in Christ and His Spirit works within us.

Jesus also mentions pruning: 'Every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit.' Pruning involves cutting away — and that can be painful. In our lives, pruning may look like difficulty, discipline, loss, or the removal of something we loved. But the gardener prunes not to harm but to help. He cuts away what hinders growth so that even more fruit can flourish.

William Secker wrote that the Christian's life is not barren but fruitful. He does not carry his religion as a burden but bears it as a tree bears fruit — naturally, abundantly, and for the nourishment of others. Secker loved this image because it captures the effortless quality of genuine faith. A healthy apple tree does not strain and sweat to produce apples. It simply stays rooted, receives nourishment, and fruit appears.

Secker also warned against fruitlessness. He said that a tree in an orchard that bears no fruit is worse than useless — it takes up space and resources that a fruitful tree could use. In the same way, a professing Christian who bears no fruit is a contradiction in terms.

For our little ones: if you stay close to Jesus — talking to Him, listening to His Word, obeying Him — good things will grow in your life, like love, kindness, and joy. Just like a branch grows grapes when it stays on the vine! For older children: what does 'abiding' in Christ look like practically? It means daily time with Him, honest prayer, reading His Word, and choosing to obey. For the whole family: we cannot manufacture spiritual fruit by trying harder. We grow fruit by staying connected to Jesus. Let us make sure our family stays rooted in Him.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

There is much we can learn from those who walked with God before us. Consider these words.

William Secker, in his work "The Nonsuch Professor in His Meridian Splendour," wrote these words:

"The Christian's life is not barren but fruitful. He does not carry his religion as a burden but bears it as a tree bears fruit — naturally, abundantly, and for the nourishment of others."

What William Secker is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in John 15:1-5 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Family, let us talk about what we have learned. Consider these questions together.

Question 1: What happens to a branch that is cut off from a tree? Can it grow fruit on its own?

Question 2: What does Jesus mean when He says 'abide in me'? How do we stay connected to Jesus?

Question 3: What kind of 'fruit' does God want to grow in our lives? (Love, joy, peace, etc.)

Question 4: Why does the gardener prune branches? How might God 'prune' things in our lives to help us grow?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing." -- John 15:5

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Learning sticks better when we do something with it. Try this activity as a family:

If possible, find a real vine or branch — even a small twig from a tree. Show how it is green and alive when connected to the tree, and how it becomes dry and brittle when broken off. Alternatively, draw a large vine on poster paper, and have each family member draw a branch with 'fruits' labeled with the fruit of the Spirit from Galatians 5:22-23.

--- Closing ---

Lord, as we close tonight's devotional, we place our family in Your hands. Watch over us as we sleep. Guard our dreams. Prepare our hearts for tomorrow. May the truths from John 15:1-5 follow us into the new day. We love You, Lord. Amen.

Goodnight. Remember your memory verse, and carry it with you tomorrow.
