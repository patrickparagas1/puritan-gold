PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"The Lord Is in His Holy Temple"
June 4, 2026

---

"In the Lord put I my trust: how say ye to my soul, Flee as a bird to your mountain? For, lo, the wicked bend their bow, they make ready their arrow upon the string, that they may privily shoot at the upright in heart. If the foundations be destroyed, what can the righteous do? The Lord is in his holy temple, the Lord's throne is in heaven: his eyes behold, his eyelids try, the children of men." -- Psalm 11:1-4

The counsel of fear is always near at hand when danger threatens, and it speaks with a voice that sounds remarkably like wisdom. "Flee as a bird to your mountain," they said to David. Escape while you can. The situation is hopeless. The wicked have bent their bows, the arrows are on the string, and you are the target. What can you possibly do against such overwhelming odds? Run. Hide. Save yourself. And indeed, from a merely human perspective, this advice seemed perfectly reasonable. But David, by the grace of God, saw through the disguise of faithless counsel and planted his feet upon the rock of divine trust. "In the Lord put I my trust," he declared. And that settled the matter.

Spurgeon, with his characteristic insight, noted that this is one of the shortest and sweetest confessions of faith in all the Psalter. Seven words that contain a world of theology. "In the Lord put I my trust." Not in the strength of his own arm, not in the swiftness of his feet, not in the height of his mountain fortress, but in the Lord Himself. This is the fundamental difference between the man of faith and the man of the world. When trouble comes, the worldling looks to his own resources. He calculates his chances. He measures his strength against the strength of his adversary. And if the odds are against him, he runs. But the believer looks upward. He measures the power of his adversary against the power of his God. And finding that comparison absurdly unequal, he stands his ground.

"If the foundations be destroyed, what can the righteous do?" This is one of the most searching questions in all of Scripture, and it is as relevant in our day as it was in David's. The foundations of which the psalmist speaks are the foundations of law, of order, of justice, of truth -- those pillars upon which any godly society must rest. When these are undermined, when truth is despised, when justice is perverted, when the wicked are exalted and the righteous are cast down, what recourse is left to the people of God?

Thomas Manton, that methodical and thorough Puritan expositor, addressed this question with great pastoral care. He taught that when earthly foundations crumble, the believer must fall back upon the only foundation that can never be shaken: the eternal throne of God in heaven. "The Lord is in his holy temple, the Lord's throne is in heaven." Whatever happens upon earth, however completely the structures of human society may collapse, the government of heaven remains unchanged. God is still on His throne. His purposes still stand. His decrees still move forward with irresistible certainty toward their appointed end. And the man who has built his hope upon this foundation shall never be moved, though the earth be removed and the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea.

"His eyes behold, his eyelids try, the children of men." Here is a truth of immeasurable comfort to the righteous and of terrible solemnity to the wicked. God sees everything. Nothing escapes His notice. The arrow that the wicked man prepares in secret is as visible to God as if it were shot at noonday in the marketplace. The plot that is hatched in the darkness, the conspiracy that is whispered behind closed doors, the malice that lurks in the hidden chambers of the heart -- all these are naked and open before the eyes of Him with whom we have to do. He does not merely glance at the affairs of men; He tries them. His eyelids, as it were, narrow with the intensity of His scrutiny, examining every motive, weighing every action, piercing through every disguise to the naked truth beneath.

John Flavel wrote beautifully upon the doctrine of divine providence, teaching that not a sparrow falls to the ground without our Father's knowledge, and that the hairs of our heads are all numbered. If God exercises such minute and particular care over the smallest creatures and the most insignificant details of the natural world, how much more does He watch over the lives and destinies of His own children? The wicked may bend their bows and make ready their arrows, but they can do nothing without the permission of the One whose eyes behold and whose eyelids try all the sons of men. Every arrow must pass through the hand of divine providence before it can reach the heart of a saint, and no arrow shall strike that God has not weighed and measured and determined to use for His own wise and holy purposes.

Let us therefore refuse the counsel of cowardly flight and cling with all our might to the confession of David: "In the Lord put I my trust." The foundations of this world may shake and crumble, the institutions of men may fail, the wicked may seem to hold every advantage, but the throne of heaven stands fast, and the eyes of the Lord are upon the righteous. He sees our affliction. He knows our cause. He measures the strength of our adversaries. And in His own perfect time and in His own perfect way, He will arise for our deliverance and will make it known to all the earth that the righteous have not trusted in vain. Amen.
