PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 17
"Satan's Device: The Secrecy of Sin"
Pilgrim Families — Week 3, Day 3


Welcome, family. Whether you are gathered on the couch, around the dinner table, or riding in the car, God is here with you. Let us turn our attention to Him and see what treasure He has stored up for us tonight.

Let us bow our heads. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family in Your presence. We need Your wisdom tonight. We need Your guidance. Open our ears to hear, our minds to understand, and our hearts to receive. We pray in the precious name of Jesus. Amen.

Let us turn together to Numbers 32:23; Hebrews 4:13. Follow along in your Bible if you have one:

"But if ye will not do so, behold, ye have sinned against the LORD: and be sure your sin will find you out. Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do."

That is Numbers 32:23; Hebrews 4:13. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Satan's third device is to convince us that secret sin is safe sin. "No one will ever know," he whispers. "It is just between you and yourself. What harm can it do if no one finds out?" This is one of the most effective lies in the enemy's arsenal, because it exploits our natural desire for privacy and our fear of exposure.

Moses warned the tribes of Israel in Numbers 32:23: "Be sure your sin will find you out." This is not a threat — it is a statement of fact, as certain as the law of gravity. Sin cannot stay hidden forever. It may be concealed for a time, but eventually it surfaces. It comes out in our words, in our behavior, in our relationships, in our health, in our conscience. And even if no human eye ever sees it, there is One who sees everything.

Hebrews 4:13 states this with startling clarity: "Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do." Every thought, every motive, every secret act is fully visible to God. We are completely naked before His gaze. There is no shadow dark enough to hide from the God who made both light and darkness.

This truth should produce both fear and comfort. Fear, because it means we can never truly get away with sin. God sees the anger we hide behind a smile. He sees the jealousy we disguise as concern. He sees the lustful thought we suppress before it reaches our lips. He sees the dishonesty we coat in half-truths. Nothing escapes His notice.

But also comfort, because it means we have nothing left to hide. The exhausting work of keeping up appearances, of maintaining a false image, of covering our tracks — all of it is pointless. God already knows. And the amazing grace of the Gospel is that He knows everything about us and loves us still. He sent His Son to die not for an idealized version of us, but for the real us — the us that sins in secret.

Brooks warned that secret sins are the most dangerous because they grow in the dark. A sin that is confessed and brought into the light can be dealt with. But a sin that is hidden in the dark grows like mold in a closed room. It spreads, it deepens, it strengthens its hold on the heart. Secret sin leads to a hardened conscience, which leads to more sin, which leads to spiritual death.

The Puritans were profoundly serious about the danger of hidden sin. They taught the practice of daily self-examination — carefully reviewing the thoughts, motives, and actions of each day before God. Not to earn God's favor, but to maintain a clean conscience and a close walk with Him. David prayed, "Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting" (Psalm 139:23-24).

For families, the remedy for secret sin is a culture of openness and confession. Not a culture of shame, where sins are paraded and punished, but a culture of grace, where family members feel safe to admit their struggles and receive help. James 5:16 instructs us: "Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed."

Parents, you set the tone. If you are willing to admit your own failures to your children — age-appropriately — you create an environment where honesty is valued more than image. If you respond to your children's confessions with grace and help rather than rage and punishment, they will keep coming to you. But if they learn that honesty leads to condemnation, they will learn to hide — and hidden sin will do its destructive work.

Teach your children that there is no such thing as a truly secret sin. God sees everything. But also teach them that there is a God who forgives everything when we come to Him honestly. "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9). The safest place for a sinner is not the shadows — it is the light of God's grace.


For centuries, faithful Christians have reflected on passages like this one. Let us hear from a trusted voice of the past.

Thomas Brooks, in his work "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices," wrote these words:

"Secret sins commonly lie most heavy upon the conscience when God comes to deal with the soul. One secret sin harboured in the heart will undo thee forever."

What Thomas Brooks is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Numbers 32:23; Hebrews 4:13 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Discussion time. These questions are for every member of the family, young and old alike.

Question 1: Why does Satan want us to think secret sin is safe? What makes hidden sin more dangerous than open sin?

Question 2: What does it mean that 'your sin will find you out'? Can you think of examples from the Bible or from life?

Question 3: How does knowing that God sees everything make you feel? Is it frightening or comforting — or both?

Question 4: How can our family create a safe environment where we can be honest about our struggles?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do." -- Hebrews 4:13

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


The Puritans believed that truth must be practiced, not just heard. Here is something you can do together:

In a dark room, hide several objects. Try to find them with the lights off. Then turn on the lights and see how easy they are to spot. Discuss how sin hides in darkness but is always visible to God. Talk about why bringing sin into the light is the path to freedom.


Let us bow our heads and pray. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family around Your Word. We ask You to write these truths on our hearts. Bless our family, protect our home, and lead us in the path of righteousness for Your name's sake. In Christ we pray. Amen.

Until tomorrow, family. May the Lord bless you and keep you.
