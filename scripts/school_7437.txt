From "Keeping the Heart" by John Flavel:

PURITAN GOLD -- Keeping the Heart: Complete Summary A Comprehensive Overview of John Flavel's Classic Treatise Keeping the Heart. By John Flavel. A Complete Summary. Welcome to this summary of John Flavel's Keeping the Heart, one of the most practical and searching works in all Puritan literature. Based on Proverbs 4:23 — Keep thy heart with all diligence, for out of it are the issues of life — Flavel demonstrates that the diligent keeping of the heart is the great business of the Christian life. Flavel defines heart-keeping as the diligent and constant use of all holy means to preserve the soul from sin and maintain its sweet communion with God. The heart is the seat of principles, the fountain of actions, and the center of the spiritual life. As the heart goes, so goes the whole person. The genius of Flavel's work lies in his application of this duty to every season and circumstance of life. He shows how to keep the heart in the time of prosperity, when success and comfort tempt us to pride, worldliness, and forgetfulness of God. He shows how to keep it in adversity, when affliction tempts us to despondency and unbelief. He addresses public troubles and national calamities, showing how to maintain peace and faith when Zion is in distress. He treats the seasons of want and poverty, the demands of religious duty, the provocations that stir up anger and revenge, and the crosses and losses that test our patience. Special attention is given to keeping the heart in temptation, in spiritual darkness and doubt, in times of persecution for righteousness, and when sickness warns us that death approaches. Each season brings its own peculiar dangers, and Flavel provides specific counsel for each. The work concludes with solemn warnings to hypocrites and formal professors, tender encouragement to sincere believers, and ten powerful motives for engaging heartily in this great duty. Keeping the Heart remains essential reading because it addresses the one thing that affects everything else in the Christian life. External religion without internal watchfulness is mere hypocrisy; but the soul that diligently guards its heart will find that all other duties follow naturally.
