PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 14
"Building a Family Legacy of Trust"
John Flavel — Providence and Family Trust

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we close our week with John Flavel by considering how a family can build a lasting legacy of trust in God's providence — a legacy that will bless generations to come. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Faithful God, You have been faithful to our family this week, and You will be faithful forever. As we close this week with Flavel, help us to build a family legacy of trust — a heritage of faith that will outlast us and bless our children's children. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Psalm 78:1-7. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Give ear, O my people, to my law: incline your ears to the words of my mouth. I will open my mouth in a parable: I will utter dark sayings of old: Which we have heard and known, and our fathers have told us. We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the LORD, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done. For he established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children: That the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to their children: That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments."

That is Psalm 78:1-7. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

John Flavel was deeply concerned not only with the present faith of families but with their future faithfulness. He understood that faith is not automatically inherited. Each generation must be taught, trained, and nurtured in the knowledge of God. And the primary means by which this happens is the faithful testimony of parents who tell their children what God has done.

Psalm 78 lays out a magnificent vision of multigenerational faithfulness. The psalmist declares that the works of God — His providential acts of power, justice, and mercy — are to be passed down from parents to children, from generation to generation, so that each new generation will set its hope in God. This is the family legacy that Flavel wanted every Christian household to build: not a legacy of wealth or status, but a legacy of trust.

How do you build such a legacy? Flavel offered several principles that are as relevant today as they were in the seventeenth century.

First, tell the stories. Your family has a history with God, and those stories must be told and retold. Remember the time God provided when the budget was tight? Tell that story. Remember the answered prayer that came at just the right moment? Tell that story. Remember the trial that seemed unbearable but produced growth you never expected? Tell that story. Children who grow up hearing their parents recount God's faithfulness develop a confidence in God that no textbook can provide. These stories become part of the family's identity — the living memory of a God who has never failed.

Second, keep a record. Flavel encouraged believers to maintain what he called a "providence journal" — a written account of God's dealings with the family. Recording God's providences serves two purposes: it strengthens the faith of those who write it, and it provides a testimony for future generations. Imagine your great-grandchildren opening a journal and reading, in your own words, how God sustained your family through a season of difficulty. What a treasure that would be. Tonight, consider starting such a journal if you do not already have one.

Third, teach your children to interpret life through the lens of providence. This is perhaps the most important legacy a parent can leave. When something good happens, teach your children to say, "God provided that." When something difficult happens, teach them to say, "God has a purpose in this." When the future is uncertain, teach them to say, "God is in control." Over time, this way of thinking becomes second nature, and your children will carry it with them long after they leave your home.

Fourth, pray together as a family — regularly, honestly, and expectantly. Family prayer is not merely a religious exercise. It is a declaration of dependence on God. When your children hear you praying about the family's needs, concerns, and hopes, they learn that the God of providence is not a distant concept but a present reality. And when they see those prayers answered — sometimes in surprising ways — their faith grows roots that will sustain them through every storm.

Flavel closed his teaching on providence with a vision that should inspire every family. He pictured the final day when all of God's providences will be fully revealed — when the tangled threads of this life will be turned over to reveal the beautiful design, when every question will be answered, every mystery explained, every tear justified by the weight of glory it produced. On that day, families who trusted God through the darkness will stand in the light and say, "He was faithful. Every moment, every trial, every providence — He was faithful."

Build that legacy tonight. Tell the stories. Keep the record. Teach your children. Pray together. And trust the God who has been faithful to every generation and will be faithful to yours.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Psalm 78 says we should tell our children about God's works so that they will "set their hope in God." What are two or three stories from our family's history that we want to be sure to pass down?

Question 2: Flavel suggested keeping a "providence journal." Would our family like to start one? What would we write in it first?

Question 3: What is one lesson from this week with John Flavel that you want to remember and carry into the weeks ahead?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the LORD, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done." -- Psalm 78:4

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord, we want to build a legacy of trust. Help us to tell our children what You have done, to record Your faithfulness, and to live in such a way that the next generation sets their hope in You. Thank You for this week with John Flavel and the glorious doctrine of Your providence. May we never stop trusting the God who has never stopped being faithful. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that the faithful God who blessed your ancestors will bless your descendants.
