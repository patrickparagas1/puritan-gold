PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 24
"Overcoming Evil with Good"
Romans for Marriage — Day 24


Welcome, dear ones. Marriage is a gift from God, and so is this time together in His presence. Whether this has been an easy day or a hard one, you are here, together, and that matters more than you know.

Our Scripture reading this evening is from Romans 12:14-21. Let us receive it together:

"Bless them which persecute you: bless, and curse not. Rejoice with them that do rejoice, and weep with them that weep. Be of the same mind one toward another. Mind not high things, but condescend to men of low estate. Be not wise in your own conceits. Recompense to no man evil for evil. Provide things honest in the sight of all men. If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men. Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord. Therefore if thine enemy hunger, feed him; if he thirst, give him drink: for in so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head. Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good."

That is Romans 12:14-21. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


The Puritans understood marriage in a way that the modern world has largely forgotten. Let us hear from Richard Baxter, who wrote with great wisdom on this subject.

Paul's instruction for handling conflict reaches its climax in verse 21: 'Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.' This is not passive surrender. It is aggressive love. It is the strategy of the cross — where the greatest evil in human history was overcome by the greatest act of goodness.

Richard Baxter applied these principles to marriage with remarkable insight. He understood that conflict in marriage is inevitable — two sinners living in close proximity will hurt each other regularly. The question is not whether conflict will occur but how it will be handled. Paul offers a revolutionary approach: do not return evil for evil. Instead, return good for evil.

Baxter identified the natural responses to marital conflict: retaliation ('you hurt me, so I will hurt you'), withdrawal ('you hurt me, so I will shut you out'), and scorekeeping ('I will remember this and use it later'). All three are forms of evil repaying evil. All three escalate conflict rather than resolving it. All three damage trust and deepen wounds.

The Gospel alternative is stunning: 'If thine enemy hunger, feed him.' In marriage, this means: when your spouse has hurt you, serve them. When they have been cold toward you, show them warmth. When they have spoken harshly, respond with gentleness. This feels counterintuitive — even foolish. But it is the way of Christ, who returned love for hatred and forgiveness for crucifixion.

Baxter cautioned that overcoming evil with good does not mean pretending nothing happened. It means refusing to let the evil dictate your response. You acknowledge the hurt. You may need to address the behavior. But your overall posture is one of goodness, not retaliation. You refuse to be dragged down to the level of the offense. You rise above it — not by your own strength, but by the power of the Holy Spirit who lives within you.

'As much as lieth in you, live peaceably.' Paul acknowledges that peace requires two parties. You cannot control your spouse's responses. But you can control yours. Do everything in your power to be a peacemaker. And when you have done all you can, leave the rest to God: 'Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.'


Think about your most recent conflict. How did you respond? Did you retaliate, withdraw, or keep score? Or did you overcome evil with good?

Tonight, commit to a new pattern: the next time your spouse hurts you, instead of retaliating, do something kind for them. Make their coffee. Write them a note. Offer a genuine compliment. This is not weakness — it is the most powerful strategy in the universe. It is the way God defeated sin: not by retaliating against sinners but by dying for them.

If there is an unresolved conflict between you, take a step toward peace tonight. Not by demanding an apology, but by extending grace. Be the first to soften. Be the first to serve. Overcome evil with good.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


The Puritans practiced what they called holy conference, speaking honestly about spiritual things with those they loved most. Do that now with these questions.

Question 1: What is your default response when your spouse hurts you: retaliation, withdrawal, or scorekeeping? How can you change?

Question 2: What would it look like to 'overcome evil with good' in your specific marriage conflicts?

Question 3: Is there an unresolved conflict between you that needs a fresh infusion of grace tonight?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


It is time to bring what we have heard and discussed before the Lord in prayer. Pray out loud if you can. Let your spouse hear your heart.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray for the supernatural ability to overcome evil with good in your marriage. Ask God to break the cycle of retaliation and replace it with the cycle of grace. Pray for peace — true, lasting peace — in your home.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May you never be overcome by evil but always overcome it with good. May your marriage be a place where grace wins every battle and love conquers every offense.

May the God who brought you together continue to bind you together in love, in faith, and in purpose. Sleep well tonight. He watches over your marriage.
