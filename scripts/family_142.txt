PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 11
"The Sin of Murmuring"
Pilgrim Families — Week 2, Day 4


Good evening, beloved family. The Puritans believed that the family altar was the most important altar in all the land. Tonight we gather at that altar together. Welcome to Puritan Gold.

Let us come before the Lord. Gracious God, thank You for the gift of family. Thank You that we can come to You together. We pray that You would teach us from Your Word this evening. Let every word sink deep into our hearts. We love You, Lord. In Jesus' name, Amen.

The Word of God is a lamp to our feet and a light to our path. Tonight that light shines from Numbers 11:1-6; Philippians 2:14-15:

"And when the people complained, it displeased the LORD: and the LORD heard it; and his anger was kindled; and the fire of the LORD burnt among them, and consumed them that were in the uttermost parts of the camp. And the mixt multitude that was among them fell a lusting: and the children of Israel also wept again, and said, Who shall give us flesh to eat? We remember the fish, which we did eat in Egypt freely; the cucumbers, and the melons, and the leeks, and the onions, and the garlick: But now our soul is dried away: there is nothing at all, beside this manna, before our eyes. Do all things without murmurings and disputings: That ye may be blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom ye shine as lights in the world."

That is Numbers 11:1-6; Philippians 2:14-15. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


If contentment is the rare jewel, then murmuring — complaining — is the thief that steals it. And few passages in Scripture illustrate the danger of complaining more powerfully than Numbers 11.

The children of Israel had been miraculously delivered from slavery in Egypt. God had parted the Red Sea, drowned their enemies, led them with a pillar of cloud and fire, and fed them with manna from heaven. Manna was miraculous bread that appeared fresh every morning on the ground — a daily provision from the hand of God Himself. Yet instead of gratitude, the people complained: "Who shall give us flesh to eat? We remember the fish, which we did eat in Egypt freely; the cucumbers, and the melons, and the leeks, and the onions, and the garlick: But now our soul is dried away: there is nothing at all, beside this manna."

Read that again carefully. They were romanticizing slavery. They were longing for Egypt — the land of their bondage, the land where their babies were murdered, the land where they were beaten and worked to death — because they missed the food. They had miraculous bread from heaven, and they were bored with it. They wanted cucumbers and garlic instead of God's daily provision.

The Scripture tells us plainly: "When the people complained, it displeased the LORD." God's response was severe — fire broke out and consumed those on the outskirts of the camp. This was not an overreaction. This was a holy God responding to a profound insult. Complaining against God's provision is really complaining against God Himself. It says, "What you have given me is not good enough. You have failed to meet my needs. I know better than you what I need."

Paul addresses this in Philippians 2:14-15: "Do all things without murmurings and disputings: That ye may be blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom ye shine as lights in the world." Do ALL things without murmuring. Not some things. All things. Homework without murmuring. Chores without murmuring. Going to church without murmuring. Eating dinner without murmuring. Everything.

And notice the result: when we stop complaining, we shine as lights in the world. In a world where everyone complains about everything — the weather, the traffic, the government, their jobs, their families — a person who does not complain stands out like a lighthouse in a storm. A family that practices gratitude instead of grumbling becomes a powerful testimony to the goodness of God.

Burroughs taught that murmuring is one of the most destructive sins a family can practice. It spreads like a disease. One person starts complaining, and soon the whole family is infected. Burroughs compared murmuring to rust on wheat and moths in a garment — it eats away quietly at first but eventually destroys everything. A family that complains together will eventually be miserable together. But a family that gives thanks together will be joyful together.

The Puritans identified several things that make murmuring particularly sinful. First, it is ungrateful. God has given us breath, life, food, shelter, family, and above all, salvation through His Son. To complain is to spit on His gifts. Second, it is faithless. Complaining says, "God will not provide. God does not care. God has forgotten me." Third, it is contagious. Israel's murmuring started with the "mixt multitude" — the non-Israelites who had tagged along — and spread to the whole camp. One complainer can poison an entire family.

The remedy for murmuring is not simply biting your tongue. It is replacing complaint with thanksgiving. When you are tempted to complain about what is on your plate, thank God that you have a plate. When you are tempted to complain about your house, thank God that you have a roof. When you are tempted to complain about your family members, thank God that you have a family.

Tonight, challenge your family to go 24 hours without a single complaint. Every time someone catches themselves starting to murmur, replace it with a specific "thank you" to God instead. You may be surprised how deeply this simple exercise changes the atmosphere in your home.


The wisdom of the Puritans remains as fresh today as when it was first written. Listen to this insight.

Jeremiah Burroughs, in his work "The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment," wrote these words:

"Murmuring is as the rust to the wheat, as the moth to the garment; it eats out the very heart of contentment."

What Jeremiah Burroughs is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Numbers 11:1-6; Philippians 2:14-15 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Here are some questions for us to think about and discuss together. There are no wrong answers here, just honest hearts.

Question 1: Why was God so angry at the Israelites' complaining? What were they really saying about God when they murmured?

Question 2: The Israelites remembered Egypt fondly even though it was a place of slavery. Do we ever romanticize things from our past that were not actually good for us?

Question 3: How does complaining spread in a family? Can you think of times when one person's grumbling affected everyone?

Question 4: What are three specific things you tend to complain about that you could replace with thanksgiving?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Do all things without murmurings and disputings." -- Philippians 2:14

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a hands-on way to remember what we have learned tonight:

Start a 'Gratitude Jar.' Every time a family member catches themselves about to complain, they must write something they are thankful for instead and put it in the jar. At the end of the week, read all the gratitude notes together.


Father, we have heard Your Word tonight, and we are grateful. We confess that we cannot live it out in our own strength. Fill us with Your Spirit. Give us wisdom for tomorrow, patience with one another, and a deep love for You. Keep our family close to You always. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Rest well tonight. God is faithful, and His wisdom is sure.
