PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 31
"Preparing for Eternity"
Richard Baxter — The Christian Directory


Welcome, family. Whether you are gathered on the couch, around the dinner table, or riding in the car, God is here with you. Let us turn our attention to Him and see what treasure He has stored up for us tonight.

Let us bow our heads. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family in Your presence. We need Your wisdom tonight. We need Your guidance. Open our ears to hear, our minds to understand, and our hearts to receive. We pray in the precious name of Jesus. Amen.

Let us turn together to 2 Corinthians 5:1-5. Follow along in your Bible if you have one:

"For we know that if our earthly house of this tabernacle were dissolved, we have a building of God, an house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens. For in this we groan, earnestly desiring to be clothed upon with our house which is from heaven: If so be that being clothed we shall not be found naked. For we that are in this tabernacle do groan, being burdened: not for that we would be unclothed, but clothed upon, that mortality might be swallowed up of life. Now he that hath wrought us for the selfsame thing is God, who also hath given unto us the earnest of the Spirit."

That is 2 Corinthians 5:1-5. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


We have come to the final day of our month-long journey through Puritan Household Treasures. It is fitting that we end where all of Christian faith ultimately points — toward eternity. Paul writes in 2 Corinthians 5:1-5 with calm confidence about what lies beyond this life.

'For we know that if our earthly house of this tabernacle were dissolved, we have a building of God, an house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens.' Paul uses the image of a tent — a tabernacle — to describe our physical bodies. Tents are temporary. They wear out. They are fragile. One day, our earthly tent will be taken down — our bodies will die. But Paul says, 'we know' — not we hope, not we guess — we know that we have something better waiting. A building of God. Not a handmade structure but something God Himself has prepared. Not temporary but eternal. Not on earth but in the heavens.

'For in this we groan, earnestly desiring to be clothed upon with our house which is from heaven.' Paul is honest about the present — we groan. Life in this fallen world involves suffering, loss, limitation, and frustration. Our bodies grow weary. Our hearts get broken. We face sickness, aging, and ultimately death. But this groaning is not despair. It is longing. We groan because we desire something better — and we know it is coming.

Paul says we do not wish to be 'unclothed' — he is not eager for death itself. Rather, he desires to be 'clothed upon' — to receive the new, glorified, resurrected body that God has prepared. He wants 'mortality to be swallowed up of life.' Death is not the end. It is swallowed up — consumed, defeated, overwhelmed — by life.

And then comes the guarantee: 'Now he that hath wrought us for the selfsame thing is God, who also hath given unto us the earnest of the Spirit.' God made us for eternal life. This is not a human invention. God Himself designed us for eternity. And to prove His intention, He has given us 'the earnest of the Spirit' — a down payment, a deposit, a guarantee. The Holy Spirit living in every believer is God's pledge that the full inheritance is coming.

Jesus Himself spoke about eternity with certainty and tenderness. In John 14:2-3, He told His disciples, 'In my Father's house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also.' Jesus is preparing a place for His people. He will come back for them. And they will be with Him forever.

In Revelation 21:4, we are given a breathtaking picture of what eternity holds: 'And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.' No more tears. No more death. No more sorrow. No more pain. Everything that grieves us now will be gone forever.

Richard Baxter is perhaps best known for his magnificent work The Saints' Everlasting Rest, in which he describes the glory that awaits believers in heaven. Baxter wrote this book while gravely ill, believing he was about to die. His personal nearness to death gave the book an urgency and warmth that has moved readers for nearly four centuries.

Baxter wrote that the Christian who keeps eternity in view will find that this life, with all its trials and blessings, is but a brief preface to an everlasting story of glory. He urged believers to live every day as if it were their last — not in fear but in readiness. If you are prepared to meet God today, you are free to live with joy and purpose.

Baxter gave a beautiful practice for families: spend a little time each week meditating on heaven. Think about what it will be like to see Christ face to face. Imagine a world with no sin, no suffering, no separation. Let the glory of the future fill your heart with joy and give you strength for the present.

Baxter also addressed children with tenderness. He reminded them that God loves young people and that heaven is not just for old saints. Jesus said, 'Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God' (Mark 10:14). Children have a place in God's eternal kingdom, and the faith they develop now is preparing them for an eternity of joy.

As we close this month, let us remember what we have learned. From John Angell James, we learned that the family is a little church, the home a training ground for eternity. From Matthew Henry, we learned the sweetness of daily devotion, prayer, and gratitude. From William Secker, we learned the importance of genuine, heart-level faith that walks the talk. From Thomas Watson, we learned the marks of the godly person — prayer, love of Scripture, faith, repentance, charity, holiness, and heavenly-mindedness. And from Richard Baxter, we are learning to use our time wisely, to be content, and to keep our eyes fixed on the eternal glory that awaits.

For our youngest ones: heaven is a real, beautiful place where we will be with God forever. There will be no sadness, no crying, and no boo-boos. God is getting it ready for us right now! For older children: how does knowing about heaven change the way you feel about hard days? If the best is yet to come, we can face anything with hope. For the whole family: this month has been a journey of growing closer to God and one another. Let us carry these lessons forward — not just for March but for the rest of our lives, as we walk together toward our eternal home.


For centuries, faithful Christians have reflected on passages like this one. Let us hear from a trusted voice of the past.

Richard Baxter, in his work "The Saints' Everlasting Rest," wrote these words:

"Live every day as if it were your last, and you will never be unprepared for death. The Christian who keeps eternity in view will find that this life, with all its trials and blessings, is but a brief preface to an everlasting story of glory."

What Richard Baxter is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in 2 Corinthians 5:1-5 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Discussion time. These questions are for every member of the family, young and old alike.

Question 1: What does Paul say our bodies are like? (A tent!) What is the 'building from God' that is waiting for us?

Question 2: What does heaven sound like based on Revelation 21:4? What are you most excited about?

Question 3: Baxter says living with eternity in view helps us live well today. How does thinking about heaven change the way you face hard days?

Question 4: As we finish this month of devotionals, what is the most important thing you have learned? How will you carry it forward?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"For we know that if our earthly house of this tabernacle were dissolved, we have a building of God, an house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens." -- 2 Corinthians 5:1

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


The Puritans believed that truth must be practiced, not just heard. Here is something you can do together:

Create a 'Month in Review' poster. Each family member writes or draws their favorite lesson, verse, or moment from this month's devotionals. Hang it somewhere visible as a reminder of all God has taught your family. End with a special family prayer time, thanking God for the journey and committing to continue growing together.


Let us bow our heads and pray. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family around Your Word. We ask You to write these truths on our hearts. Bless our family, protect our home, and lead us in the path of righteousness for Your name's sake. In Christ we pray. Amen.

Until tomorrow, family. May the Lord bless you and keep you.
