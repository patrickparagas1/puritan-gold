PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 25
"Contentment in Seasons of Plenty"
Jeremiah Burroughs — The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment for Families


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Burroughs addresses a counterintuitive truth: contentment is needed not only in seasons of want but also in seasons of plenty. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, when You bless us with abundance, help us to receive it with grateful hearts and open hands. Keep us from the spiritual dangers of prosperity, and teach us to be as content in plenty as in need. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Deuteronomy 8:10-14. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"When thou hast eaten and art full, then thou shalt bless the LORD thy God for the good land which he hath given thee. Beware that thou forget not the LORD thy God, in not keeping his commandments, and his judgments, and his statutes, which I command thee this day: Lest when thou hast eaten and art full, and hast built goodly houses, and dwelt therein; And when thy herds and thy flocks multiply, and thy silver and thy gold is multiplied, and all that thou hast is multiplied; Then thine heart be lifted up, and thou forget the LORD thy God, which brought thee forth out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage."

That is Deuteronomy 8:10-14. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


We naturally associate contentment with learning to be satisfied in times of scarcity. But Jeremiah Burroughs made a remarkable observation: contentment is equally necessary — and equally difficult — in times of plenty. Paul himself said, "I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound." Knowing how to abound requires its own kind of spiritual wisdom.

Burroughs taught that prosperity presents unique spiritual dangers to families. When things are going well — when the income is steady, the health is good, the children are thriving — there is a subtle but powerful temptation to forget God. Moses warned Israel about this very danger. After years of dependence on God in the wilderness, Israel was about to enter a land of abundance. And Moses told them: Beware. When you have eaten and are full, when your houses are built and your flocks have multiplied, do not forget the Lord your God.

The danger of prosperity is not that it is sinful — God is the giver of every good gift. The danger is that it becomes a substitute for God. When everything is comfortable, we stop praying with desperation. When the bank account is full, we stop depending on God for daily bread. When relationships are smooth, we stop seeking God for wisdom and grace. Prosperity can anesthetize the soul, putting us into a spiritual slumber from which we may not awaken until a crisis forces us to our knees.

For families, the danger is especially acute. In a prosperous season, families may gradually shift their center of gravity from God to stuff. Activities multiply. Purchases increase. Entertainment fills every spare moment. Family devotion is crowded out by a calendar that is bursting with opportunities. The family becomes so busy enjoying God's gifts that it forgets God Himself.

Burroughs taught that true contentment in prosperity involves several disciplines. First, gratitude: the content family in a season of plenty remembers that every good thing is a gift from God. They do not take credit for their success or assume they have earned their blessings. They say grace at every meal not as a formality but as a genuine expression of thanksgiving.

Second, generosity: the content family holds its prosperity with open hands. They recognize that God has blessed them not merely for their own enjoyment but for the good of others. They give generously to the church, to those in need, and to causes that advance God's kingdom. Generosity is one of the surest antidotes to the spiritual dangers of wealth.

Third, simplicity: the content family in a season of plenty does not allow its lifestyle to expand to fill every available dollar. They live below their means, resist the cultural pressure to accumulate, and maintain a simplicity of life that keeps their hearts free from the entanglements of materialism.

Fourth, vigilance: the content family watches its own heart carefully. They ask honest questions: Are we as devoted to God as we were when times were harder? Are we as faithful in prayer, in worship, in service? Has our prosperity drawn us closer to God or further from Him? This kind of honest self-examination is essential for spiritual health in prosperous times.

Burroughs would say that the truly content family is content in plenty precisely because it holds its plenty loosely. They enjoy God's gifts, but they do not cling to them. They know that what God gives, He can also take away, and that their joy does not depend on the gifts but on the Giver.

Tonight, if your family is in a season of relative prosperity, thank God — and then ask Him to keep your hearts from the dangers that prosperity brings.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Why might contentment be harder in prosperity than in scarcity? What spiritual dangers does abundance bring?

Question 2: Burroughs said that generosity is an antidote to the spiritual dangers of wealth. How is our family doing in the area of generosity? What could we give this week?

Question 3: Moses told Israel, "Beware that thou forget not the Lord." What practices help our family remember God and stay close to Him when times are good?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"When thou hast eaten and art full, then thou shalt bless the LORD thy God for the good land which he hath given thee." -- Deuteronomy 8:10

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord, we thank You for every good gift You have given our family. Keep our hearts from forgetting You in our prosperity. Teach us gratitude, generosity, and simplicity. May we hold Your gifts with open hands and Your presence as our greatest treasure. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, grateful for a God whose generosity knows no bounds.
