From "The Reformed Pastor" by Richard Baxter:

PURITAN GOLD -- The Reformed Pastor: Complete Summary A Comprehensive Overview of Richard Baxter's Classic Work on Pastoral Ministry The Reformed Pastor. By Richard Baxter. A Complete Summary. Welcome to this summary of Richard Baxter's The Reformed Pastor, one of the most challenging and convicting works ever addressed to Christian ministers. Published in 1656, it calls pastors to take seriously the oversight of their own souls and the souls of their people. THE AUTHOR AND HIS PURPOSE. Richard Baxter served as minister in Kidderminster, England, where he saw an entire town transformed by faithful pastoral labor. The Reformed Pastor grew out of an agreement among ministers in Worcestershire to catechize and personally instruct every family in their parishes. Baxter wrote to motivate his fellow pastors to undertake this demanding work. THE OVERSIGHT OF OURSELVES. Baxter begins with the pastor's own soul. He insists that ministers must first take heed to themselves before they can take heed to their flocks. An unconverted minister is the most miserable and dangerous of creatures. Baxter warns that many who preach against sin are strangers to saving grace. He then provides powerful motives for self-examination, arguing that the minister who neglects his own soul will inevitably neglect his people's. THE OVERSIGHT OF THE FLOCK. Baxter next addresses the nature, manner, and motives of pastoral oversight. He argues for personal, individual attention to every soul in the congregation — not merely public preaching but private instruction, counsel, and admonition. He insists that pastors must know their people personally, visit them in their homes, and teach them one by one. APPLICATION. The largest section of the book applies these principles practically. Baxter calls for deep humiliation among ministers who have neglected their duty. He then specifically recommends the practice of personal catechizing — meeting with families to go through the fundamentals of the faith. He answers every objection that ministers raise against this work and provides detailed practical directions for carrying it out. LASTING SIGNIFICANCE. The Reformed Pastor has convicted and transformed countless ministers over nearly four centuries. Its power lies in Baxter's own burning zeal and his refusal to let pastors rest in mere professionalism. The book remains essential reading for every person in Christian ministry.
