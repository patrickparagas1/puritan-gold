PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 5
"Honor Thy Father and Mother"
Thomas Watson — The Ten Commandments


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we reach the fifth commandment — the great commandment with promise — and Thomas Watson's rich teaching on the honor that children owe their parents and the responsibilities that parents bear before God. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Gracious Father, You are the original Father from whom every family takes its name. Teach us tonight what it means to honor the authorities You have placed in our lives, and help parents to lead with the wisdom and love that reflect Your own fatherly care. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Exodus 20:12 and Ephesians 6:1-4. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee. Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right. Honour thy father and mother; which is the first commandment with promise; That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth. And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord."

That is Exodus 20:12 and Ephesians 6:1-4. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


The fifth commandment stands at the hinge point of the Decalogue. The first four commandments govern our relationship with God. The last six govern our relationships with one another. And the very first relationship God addresses in the second table of the law is the relationship between parents and children. Thomas Watson saw profound significance in this ordering. The family is the first institution God created, and the family is the foundational relationship upon which all other social relationships are built. If the family is disordered, society will be disordered. If children do not learn to honor their parents, they will not learn to honor anyone — not their teachers, not their neighbors, not their rulers, and ultimately not their God.

Watson taught that the fifth commandment is about much more than children being polite to their parents. It encompasses the entire structure of God-ordained authority. We are to honor all those whom God has placed over us — parents, teachers, pastors, magistrates — recognizing that their authority is derived from God's authority. But the family is the first and most intimate arena where this principle is learned.

For children, Watson outlined several specific duties. First, reverence: children should hold their parents in high esteem, speaking of them and to them with respect, not with contempt or mockery. Second, obedience: children should obey their parents' lawful commands promptly and cheerfully, not grudgingly or with a spirit of resistance. Third, gratitude: children should be thankful for the sacrifices their parents make on their behalf — the sleepless nights, the financial provision, the prayers poured out daily on their behalf. Fourth, care in old age: the honor due to parents does not expire when children grow up. Adult children have a sacred obligation to care for aging parents, just as those parents cared for them in their helpless infancy.

But Watson was equally insistent on the duties of parents. He read the fifth commandment in light of Paul's instruction in Ephesians 6:4: "Ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord." Parents have a solemn responsibility to lead their children with both tenderness and truth. Watson warned against two extremes. On one hand, parents must not be so harsh and demanding that they break their children's spirits and drive them to resentment. On the other hand, parents must not be so permissive and indulgent that they fail to provide the discipline and instruction that children desperately need.

Watson believed that the primary duty of parents is to bring their children to Christ. All the education, provision, and protection in the world amounts to nothing if the child's soul is neglected. He urged parents to pray earnestly for their children's conversion, to teach them the Scriptures from the earliest age, to set a consistent example of godliness, and to create a home atmosphere where faith is not merely professed but practiced.

The promise attached to this commandment — "that thy days may be long upon the land" — is not merely about individual longevity. It is about societal stability. When families are healthy, when children honor their parents and parents nurture their children in the faith, communities flourish. Civilization itself depends on the proper functioning of the family. Watson would say that every evening of family devotion, every act of parental instruction, every moment of filial obedience is a contribution to the common good that extends far beyond the walls of the home.

Tonight, whether you are a parent or a child, the fifth commandment speaks to you. Children, honor your parents — not because they are perfect, but because God has placed them over you for your good. Parents, lead your children — not with harshness or indifference, but with the nurture and admonition of the Lord. And together, give thanks for the gift of family, which is God's first and most enduring institution.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Why do you think God placed the commandment about family right at the hinge point between our duties to God and our duties to others? What does this tell us about the importance of family?

Question 2: Watson said that honoring parents means more than obedience — it also includes reverence, gratitude, and care. Which of these do you find easiest? Which is hardest?

Question 3: Parents, how can you lead your family with both tenderness and truth, avoiding the extremes of harshness and permissiveness?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee." -- Exodus 20:12

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Father, we thank You for our family. We thank You for the gift of parents and children. Help the children in our home to honor their parents with reverence and obedience. Help the parents to lead with wisdom, patience, and love. Bind us together in the bond of Christ, and let our home be a place where Your love is known and practiced. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that you belong to a family that is held in the hands of God.
