PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 2
"Not Ashamed of the Gospel"
Romans for Marriage — Day 2


Hello, beloved couple. It is a beautiful thing when husband and wife sit down together in God's Word. The Puritans called marriage a little church within the church. Tonight, your little church is in session. Welcome.

Tonight we turn to Romans 1:16-17. Listen with your hearts, not just your ears:

"For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek. For therein is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith: as it is written, The just shall live by faith."

That is Romans 1:16-17. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


William Gouge had deep insight into the truths we have just read. The Puritans did not separate theology from daily life, and their reflections on marriage are remarkably practical.

Paul's bold declaration — 'I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ' — rings across the centuries with undimmed power. He is writing to believers in Rome, the capital of the mightiest empire on earth, where the Gospel was considered foolishness by the philosophers and a stumbling block by the religious. Yet Paul is not ashamed, because the Gospel is 'the power of God unto salvation.'

William Gouge, the great Puritan pastor of Blackfriars, London, taught that the Gospel must be the animating center of every Christian home. In his work Of Domestical Duties, Gouge argued that a marriage without the Gospel at its center is like a body without a soul — it may have outward form, but it lacks the life-giving power that makes it truly function.

The Gospel is not merely the message that saves us initially — it is the power that sustains us daily. In marriage, we need the Gospel every single day. We need it when we sin against each other and require forgiveness. We need it when we feel inadequate and require grace. We need it when we face trials and require hope. The Gospel is not just how we enter the Christian life; it is how we live the Christian life, and it is certainly how we sustain a Christian marriage.

Paul says this Gospel reveals 'the righteousness of God from faith to faith.' The Christian life is a journey of deepening faith. We begin by faith, we continue by faith, and we will finish by faith. Marriage is the same. You entered your covenant by faith — trusting God's design even when you could not see the future. You sustain your covenant by faith — trusting God's provision even when resources are scarce and patience runs thin. And you will complete your covenant by faith — trusting that the God who joined you together will carry you to the finish line.

'The just shall live by faith.' Not by sight, not by feeling, not by circumstances. By faith. This is the foundation upon which your marriage stands or falls. When you cannot see how things will work out, live by faith. When your feelings are unreliable, live by faith. When the world pressures you to abandon biblical principles, live by faith.


Are you ashamed of the Gospel in your home? This may seem like a strange question, but consider: Is your marriage visibly shaped by the good news of Jesus Christ? Do your children see the Gospel lived out in the way you forgive each other, serve each other, and sacrifice for each other?

Many Christian couples live functionally secular marriages — marriages where faith is an add-on rather than the foundation. The Gospel sits on the shelf while worldly wisdom runs the household. But Paul declares that the Gospel is the power of God. If you want power in your marriage — power to forgive, power to persevere, power to love unconditionally — it comes from the Gospel.

This week, make a deliberate effort to bring the Gospel into your daily interactions. When you are offended, remember: Christ forgave you a greater debt. When you are exhausted, remember: Christ's grace is sufficient. When you are tempted to give up, remember: the just shall live by faith, not by feeling.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


It is time for reflection. Turn to each other and consider these questions together. There is no rush. Let the conversation go wherever the Spirit leads.

Question 1: In what ways might we be living a 'functionally secular' marriage — where faith is an add-on rather than the foundation?

Question 2: How does the Gospel specifically help you forgive your spouse when they have hurt you?

Question 3: What would it look like for our home to be a place where the Gospel is not just believed but visibly lived?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Now let us pray together. The Puritans said that a couple who prays together is bound by cords that cannot be broken. Let us strengthen those cords tonight.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray that the Gospel would move from a Sunday truth to a daily reality in your marriage. Ask God to make you bold and unashamed in living out your faith together, and to give you power through the Gospel for every challenge you face.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the power of the Gospel transform your marriage from the inside out. May you never be ashamed of Christ in your home, and may your love for each other declare His goodness to the world.

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you both, tonight and always. Goodnight.
