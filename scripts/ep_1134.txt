The Pilgrim's Progress from This World to That Which Is to Come. By John Bunyan. A Complete Summary.

Welcome to this summary of John Bunyan's The Pilgrim's Progress, the most widely read Christian allegory ever written. First published in 1678, it tells the story of Christian's journey from the City of Destruction to the Celestial City.

THE AUTHOR AND HIS PURPOSE.

John Bunyan was a tinker and lay preacher who wrote The Pilgrim's Progress while imprisoned in Bedford jail for unlicensed preaching. The book came to him, he said, like a flood, and he wrote it to show the way of salvation in the form of a dream narrative. It has been translated into over two hundred languages and has never been out of print.

THE JOURNEY BEGINS.

Christian, burdened with a great weight on his back representing his sin, flees the City of Destruction after reading a book that warns of coming judgment. He falls into the Slough of Despond, is deceived by Mr. Worldly Wiseman, but finally arrives at the Wicket Gate and enters the narrow way.

THE INTERPRETER'S HOUSE AND THE CROSS.

At the Interpreter's House, Christian is shown vivid pictures of spiritual truth. He then comes to the Cross, where his burden falls from his back and rolls into an open sepulchre. He is clothed in new garments and receives a sealed roll — his assurance of salvation.

TRIALS AND COMPANIONS.

Christian faces the Hill Difficulty, rests at the Palace Beautiful, battles the fiend Apollyon in the Valley of Humiliation, and passes through the terrifying Valley of the Shadow of Death. He meets Faithful, who becomes his companion. Together they enter Vanity Fair, where Faithful is martyred for refusing to buy the world's merchandise.

After Faithful's death, Hopeful joins Christian. They are led astray into By-Path Meadow and captured by Giant Despair in Doubting Castle. They escape using the Key of Promise and reach the Delectable Mountains, where the Shepherds show them glimpses of the Celestial City.

THE CELESTIAL CITY.

After passing through the Enchanted Ground and the Country of Beulah, Christian and Hopeful must cross the deep River of Death. Christian nearly drowns in despair, but Hopeful sustains him. On the far shore, they are met by angels and conducted through the gates of the Celestial City with trumpets and singing.

LASTING SIGNIFICANCE.

The Pilgrim's Progress endures because it tells the truth about the Christian life in the language of vivid imagination. Every trial, temptation, and triumph that Bunyan describes is recognizable to every genuine believer. The book is at once a gripping story, a profound theology, and a faithful guide for the journey from this world to the next.
