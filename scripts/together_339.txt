PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 8
"Fully Persuaded Together"
Romans for Marriage — Day 8


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to tonight's couples devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for setting aside this time to draw near to God and to each other. The moments you invest in your marriage before the Lord are among the most important moments of your day.

Let us open God's Word together. Our passage tonight comes from Romans 4:20-21:

"He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief; but was strong in faith, giving glory to God; And being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform."

That is Romans 4:20-21. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Now let us hear from Thomas Manton, one of the great Puritan voices on this subject. The Puritans wrote extensively about marriage because they saw it as one of God's most profound gifts.

We linger on this text because its implications for marriage are so profound. To be 'fully persuaded' is to have settled the matter in your heart before the evidence arrives. Abraham did not wait to see the baby before believing. He believed first, and the baby came later. Faith precedes sight.

Manton distinguished between a wavering faith and a settled faith. A wavering faith believes on good days and doubts on bad days. It is tossed about by circumstances like a ship without an anchor. A settled faith has made its decision: God has promised, and God is able. Period. The circumstances may scream otherwise, but the settled heart does not move.

In marriage, this looks like two people who have made a settled decision: our marriage will glorify God, and God is able to make it so. This is not naive optimism. It is not denial of real problems. It is a deliberate, faith-filled choice to trust God's ability over our own assessment of the situation.

Manton also noted that Abraham's faith gave glory to God. When we trust God in impossible situations, we honor Him. When we refuse to give up on what God has joined together, we declare that He is powerful and faithful. Conversely, when we surrender to despair, we effectively say that our problems are bigger than God. Faith glorifies God; despair diminishes Him.

Being 'fully persuaded' as a couple means you face every challenge with a shared conviction: God is able. Able to restore trust after betrayal. Able to renew love after years of coldness. Able to bring unity after deep division. Able to heal wounds that have festered for decades. When both husband and wife are fully persuaded, their combined faith creates an atmosphere where God works mightily.


Full persuasion in marriage requires intentional cultivation. It does not happen by accident. You must choose it, speak it, and practice it together.

Tonight, make a declaration together: 'We are fully persuaded that God is able to fulfill every good purpose He has for our marriage.' Say it aloud. Write it down. Return to it when doubt creeps in.

Then identify one area where your faith as a couple has been wavering — where you have been 'staggering at the promise.' It might be a long-standing conflict, a financial burden, a parenting challenge, or a spiritual struggle. Bring it before God together with the declaration: You are able.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Now let us pause for reflection. Here are some questions for you and your spouse to discuss together. Be honest. Be gentle. Listen more than you speak.

Question 1: Would you describe your faith as a couple as 'settled' or 'wavering'? What causes it to waver?

Question 2: How does trusting God together glorify Him in a way that trusting individually cannot?

Question 3: What promise of God do you most need to be 'fully persuaded' about for your marriage right now?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Let us move into a time of prayer together. If you are able, hold hands as you pray. There is something powerful about a husband and wife joining hands before the throne of grace.

Tonight's prayer focus: Declare together that God is able. Pray over the specific areas where your faith has wavered. Ask the Holy Spirit to strengthen your shared conviction and to give glory to God through your trust.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May you be fully persuaded together — not staggering, not wavering, but standing firm on the promises of a faithful God who is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all you ask or think.

May the Lord bless your marriage and keep it strong. May He make His face to shine upon your home, and give you peace. Goodnight, dear couple. Walk together in His love.
