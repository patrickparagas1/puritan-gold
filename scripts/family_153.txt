PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 22
"Christ Is Altogether Lovely"
Pilgrim Families — Week 4, Day 1


Good evening, dear family. Welcome to tonight's family devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for gathering together around God's Word. Let us quiet our hearts and prepare to listen to what the Lord has for us.

Let us begin with prayer. Heavenly Father, we thank You for this family. We thank You for bringing us safely through another day. Now we ask that You would open our eyes to see wonderful things in Your Word. Speak to each heart gathered here, from the oldest to the youngest. In Jesus' name we pray. Amen.

Tonight we open our Bibles to Song of Solomon 5:16; Philippians 3:8. Listen carefully as I read our passage:

"His mouth is most sweet: yea, he is altogether lovely. This is my beloved, and this is my friend, O daughters of Jerusalem. Yea doubtless, and I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord: for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and do count them but dung, that I may win Christ."

That is Song of Solomon 5:16; Philippians 3:8. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


This week we turn to one of the most passionate lovers of Christ in all of church history — Samuel Rutherford. Rutherford was a Scottish pastor who was exiled to the remote town of Aberdeen for his faithful preaching. While in exile, he wrote some of the most beautiful letters ever penned about the loveliness of Jesus Christ. His letters burn with affection for the Savior and have warmed countless cold hearts over the centuries.

The Song of Solomon gives voice to the Bride's adoration: "He is altogether lovely." Not partly lovely. Not mostly lovely. Altogether lovely. Every aspect of Christ is beautiful. His wisdom is lovely. His grace is lovely. His justice is lovely. His mercy is lovely. His patience is lovely. His power is lovely. There is not a single quality in Jesus that is anything less than utterly captivating.

Paul expresses the same devotion in Philippians 3:8: "I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord: for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and do count them but dung, that I may win Christ." Paul was a man of extraordinary accomplishments — educated, respected, zealous, accomplished. Yet he looked at everything he had achieved and counted it as refuse compared to knowing Jesus. The word "dung" is strong and deliberate. Paul is saying that the most impressive human accomplishments are garbage compared to the surpassing worth of Christ.

Rutherford knew this from experience. He was stripped of his pulpit, separated from his beloved congregation, and sent to a cold, lonely exile. Yet from Aberdeen he wrote: "I have got new eyes and a new heart since I came to this prison. I see more of Christ here than ever I saw before." His suffering did not drive him from Christ — it drove him to Christ. And in Christ, he found a sweetness that made exile feel like paradise.

This is the foundation of the Christian life: the loveliness of Jesus. Not rules. Not religion. Not moral improvement. Jesus Himself, in all His beauty, is the treasure at the center of everything. When families lose sight of how lovely Jesus is, Christianity becomes a list of dos and do nots. But when families fall in love with Jesus — when they see Him as altogether lovely — obedience becomes a delight rather than a burden.

Rutherford's letters overflow with affection for Christ. He wrote of Jesus as a bridegroom, a friend, a king, a brother, and a feast. He described the sweetness of prayer, the delight of Scripture, and the joy of communion with Christ in such vivid language that readers feel they are tasting heaven on earth. And all of this was written from exile — from a place of suffering, not comfort.

For your family, the question tonight is simple and searching: Is Jesus lovely to you? Not just useful. Not just believed in. Not just obeyed out of duty. But genuinely lovely. Do you delight in Him? Do you think about Him with affection? Do your children see you loving Jesus, not just serving Jesus?

The Puritans taught that there is an infinite difference between serving Christ out of duty and serving Christ out of delight. Duty can be maintained by willpower for a season, but it eventually runs dry. Delight is a fountain that never stops flowing because it is fed by the loveliness of Christ Himself. When you find Jesus beautiful, you want to pray. You want to read His Word. You want to obey His commands. You want to tell others about Him. Love does what duty cannot.

Rutherford once wrote to a friend: "Put your love upon Christ. He is a worthy, lovely, loving Lord." Tonight, put your love upon Christ. Read the Song of Solomon's description of the Beloved. Read Paul's testimony of counting all things as loss. And ask God to open your eyes to see Jesus as He truly is — not as a distant doctrine or a historical figure, but as the altogether lovely one who gave Himself for you.


Now let us hear from one of the great Puritan teachers of the faith.

Samuel Rutherford, in his work "Letters of Samuel Rutherford," wrote these words:

"I have got new eyes and a new heart since I came to this prison. I see more of Christ here than ever I saw before. O that all Scotland knew my joy!"

What Samuel Rutherford is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Song of Solomon 5:16; Philippians 3:8 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Now it is time for our family discussion. Here are some questions to talk about together. Take your time with each one.

Question 1: What does it mean that Christ is 'altogether lovely'? Can you name specific things about Jesus that are beautiful to you?

Question 2: Paul gave up everything for Jesus and called it all 'dung.' What would it look like for our family to value Jesus above everything else?

Question 3: Rutherford said he saw more of Christ in exile than ever before. Why do we often see Jesus more clearly during hard times?

Question 4: What is the difference between serving Jesus out of duty and serving Him out of love? Which describes our family more?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Yea doubtless, and I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord." -- Philippians 3:8

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a family activity you can do this week to help these truths take root:

Have each family member write a short letter to Jesus, telling Him what they love about Him. Share the letters with each other. This is a beautiful exercise in expressing devotion — something we rarely do but deeply need.


Let us close in prayer. Heavenly Father, thank You for gathering our family together in Your Word tonight. Thank You for the truth we have heard from Song of Solomon 5:16; Philippians 3:8. Help us to live out what we have learned. Guard our hearts, guide our steps, and fill our home with Your love and peace. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

Goodnight, dear ones. Walk in wisdom tomorrow.
