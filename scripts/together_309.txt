PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 9
"The Beginning of Wisdom for Your Home"
Proverbs 9:10

---

Hello, beloved couple. It is a beautiful thing when husband and wife sit down together in God's Word. The Puritans called marriage a little church within the church. Tonight, your little church is in session. Welcome.

Tonight we turn to Proverbs 9:10. Listen with your hearts, not just your ears:

"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding."

That is Proverbs 9:10. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Matthew Henry had deep insight into the truths we have just read. The Puritans did not separate theology from daily life, and their reflections on marriage are remarkably practical.

This verse echoes and deepens the theme of Proverbs 1:7. Where that verse spoke of the fear of the Lord as the beginning of knowledge, here Solomon declares it the beginning of wisdom. Matthew Henry distinguishes the two: knowledge is the accumulation of truth; wisdom is the right application of truth to life. Both begin with the fear of the Lord.

Henry writes: 'The fear of the Lord is not only the beginning of wisdom, as the first stone in the foundation, but as the spring of the stream. It is the root of the tree, from which all the branches of wisdom grow.' This fear is comprehensive: it governs the intellect (what we believe), the affections (what we love), and the will (what we choose). A man may be very learned yet very foolish if he lacks this reverential awe of God.

The second clause adds a further dimension: 'the knowledge of the holy is understanding.' Henry interprets 'the holy' as referring to the Holy One, God Himself. To know God, not merely to know about God, is the essence of understanding. This is relational, experiential knowledge: the kind that comes from walking with God, communing with Him in prayer, sitting under His Word, and observing His works in providence.

Charles Bridges applies this to the home: 'The family that fears God has the beginning of every blessing. The family that knows God has the substance of every blessing.' When husband and wife together cultivate the fear of the Lord and press deeper into the knowledge of God, their home becomes a school of wisdom. Every challenge becomes an opportunity to grow in understanding, every trial becomes a classroom where the Holy One teaches His children how to live.

--- Marriage Application ---

As you return to this foundational theme, consider how far you have come and how far you have yet to go. The fear of the Lord is not a lesson learned once and then set aside; it is a posture maintained throughout life.

In your marriage, the fear of the Lord should be the atmosphere you breathe. It should shape how you speak to one another when you are tired and irritable. It should govern how you handle money when resources are scarce. It should direct how you raise your children when cultural pressures push in another direction.

But notice the second clause: 'the knowledge of the holy is understanding.' This is the deeper invitation. God does not merely want you to fear Him; He wants you to know Him. As a couple, pursue not just obedience but intimacy with your Creator. Read His Word not as a textbook but as a love letter. Pray not as a duty but as a conversation. Worship not as a habit but as a response to His glory.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

It is time for reflection. Turn to each other and consider these questions together. There is no rush. Let the conversation go wherever the Spirit leads.

Question 1: How has our understanding of the fear of the Lord grown since we first married?

Question 2: Are we growing in our knowledge of God Himself, or have we settled for merely knowing about Him?

Question 3: What is one way we can deepen our shared knowledge of the Holy One this month?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Now let us pray together. The Puritans said that a couple who prays together is bound by cords that cannot be broken. Let us strengthen those cords tonight.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to deepen both your fear of Him and your knowledge of Him as a couple. Pray that your home would be a place where wisdom is not merely discussed but lived, where the Holy One is not merely acknowledged but intimately known.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Holy One who is the fountain of all wisdom draw you ever deeper into the knowledge of Himself, that your marriage may overflow with understanding and grace. Amen.

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you both, tonight and always. Goodnight.
