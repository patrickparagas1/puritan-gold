PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"He That Dwelleth in the Secret Place"
May 15, 2026

---

"He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty. I will say of the Lord, He is my refuge and my fortress: my God; in him will I trust. Surely he shall deliver thee from the snare of the fowler, and from the noisome pestilence. He shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his wings shalt thou trust: his truth shall be thy shield and buckler." -- Psalm 91:1-4

The ninety-first psalm has been called the soldier's psalm, for it has been the companion of believers in every kind of danger, from the battlefields of earthly warfare to the far more perilous battlefields of spiritual conflict. It speaks of protection, of shelter, of divine guardianship so thorough and so tender that the believer is depicted as a chick hidden beneath the wings of the Almighty, safe from every hawk and every storm. But we must be careful to understand this psalm rightly, for it has been misused by those who would turn it into a charm or a magic formula, rather than receiving it as what it truly is: a description of the security that belongs to every soul who dwells in habitual communion with the living God.

"He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High." The key word here is "dwelleth." Not "he that visits," not "he that occasionally passes through," but "he that dwelleth." To dwell is to make one's permanent home, to take up residence, to settle down and remain. The secret place of the Most High is not a geographical location that one can find on a map. It is the place of intimate fellowship with God, the inner sanctuary of communion that is accessible to every believer through prayer and faith. It is that hidden life with God that the apostle Paul describes when he says, "Your life is hid with Christ in God."

The man who merely visits this secret place from time to time, who drops in on God at Sunday morning services and then forgets Him for the rest of the week, does not have the same assurance that is promised here. The promise belongs to the one who dwells there, who makes the presence of God his habitual atmosphere, who breathes the air of heaven as naturally as his lungs breathe the air of earth. This is not to say that the believer must be in a state of conscious prayer every moment of the day. But it is to say that the fundamental orientation of his life, the settled direction of his heart, the habitual posture of his soul, is toward God.

"I will say of the Lord, He is my refuge and my fortress: my God; in him will I trust." Here the psalmist makes his personal declaration of faith. He does not merely think these things. He says them. There is power in the verbal confession of faith, not as a magical incantation, but as a deliberate act of the will that reinforces trust in the heart. When a man says aloud, "The Lord is my refuge," he is nailing his colours to the mast. He is committing himself publicly to a position of dependence upon God. And this public commitment strengthens the private faith upon which it is built.

"Surely he shall deliver thee from the snare of the fowler, and from the noisome pestilence." The snare of the fowler represents those hidden traps that the enemy sets for the unwary soul: the subtle temptations, the carefully disguised deceptions, the apparently innocent paths that lead gradually to destruction. The fowler does not chase his prey in the open field. He sets his snare in secret, covers it with leaves, and waits for the unsuspecting bird to walk into it. So the devil does not always come with horns and a pitchfork. He comes as an angel of light, with reasonable arguments and attractive propositions, and many a soul has been caught in his snare before it even knew that danger was near.

But the God who sees all things, who knows the end from the beginning, who discerns the thoughts and intents of the heart, this God shall deliver His people from every snare. He does not merely warn them of the danger. He delivers them from it. He opens their eyes to see the trap before they step into it. He sends His Spirit to awaken their conscience and to sound the alarm in their souls. And when they have already stepped into the snare, He breaks it and sets them free.

"He shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his wings shalt thou trust." Here is one of the most intimate and tender images of divine protection in all of Scripture. The mighty God, the Creator of the ends of the earth, the Lord of hosts before whom the seraphim veil their faces, is compared to a mother bird sheltering her young beneath her wings. It is the same image that our Lord Jesus used when He lamented over Jerusalem: "How often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not!" The wings of God are the wings of omnipotence, and the feathers of God are the tender, detailed, particular provisions of His care. He does not merely protect His people in a general way. He covers them. He wraps them. He enfolds them in the warmth and safety of His own being.

"Thou shalt not be afraid for the terror by night; nor for the arrow that flieth by day; nor for the pestilence that walketh in darkness; nor for the destruction that wasteth at noonday." Four dangers are listed, representing every possible source of harm: night terrors and daylight attacks, unseen plagues and visible destruction. The believer who dwells in the secret place is protected from them all. Not necessarily that he shall never experience them, for many godly saints have suffered disease and persecution and death. But that in the midst of them all, he shall not be afraid. The fear is removed, even when the danger remains. And this freedom from fear is itself one of the greatest of all God's gifts, for a man who is free from fear is free indeed, regardless of his outward circumstances.

As William Gurnall wrote with such wisdom in his great work on spiritual warfare, the Christian's armour is not his own but God's. The shield is God's truth. The fortress is God's presence. The refuge is God's character. And the believer's only responsibility is to dwell there, to remain there, to make his home in the secret place of the Most High, and to trust the Almighty to do what He has promised. For He is faithful, and He cannot deny Himself. Amen.
