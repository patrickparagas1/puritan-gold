PURITAN GOLD -- Study Topic: The Book of Melchizedek, Part 6 of 8
"The Unchangeable Priesthood"
The Book of Melchizedek — Hebrews 7:23-28 — Christ's eternal intercession

---

Beloved friends, we come today to the glorious climax of Hebrews chapter 7 — the passage in which the writer brings his argument about Melchizedek and Christ's priesthood to its summit. Everything we have studied in the previous episodes — the mystery of Genesis 14, the oath of Psalm 110, the superiority over Abraham, the change of law — all of it converges here, in one of the most comforting and triumphant passages in all of Scripture.

Let us read Hebrews 7:23-28:

"And they truly were many priests, because they were not suffered to continue by reason of death: But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood. Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them. For such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens; Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people's: for this he did once, when he offered up himself. For the law maketh men high priests which have infirmity; but the word of the oath, which was since the law, maketh the Son, who is consecrated for evermore."

--- Teaching ---

"And they truly were many priests, because they were not suffered to continue by reason of death." The writer begins with a simple but devastating observation about the Levitical priesthood: the priests kept dying. From Aaron onward, every high priest served for a time and then passed away. The chain was unbroken for over a thousand years, but it was a chain of mortality — each link forged in human frailty and dissolved by death.

Consider the implications. A worshipper under the Levitical system might develop a relationship of trust and confidence with a particular high priest, only to have that priest die and be replaced by another. The new priest might be less faithful, less devout, less sympathetic. There was no guarantee of continuity in quality, only in office. The priesthood endured, but the priests did not.

"But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood." The Greek word translated "unchangeable" is aparabatos — it means "untransferable," "inviolable," "that which cannot pass to another." Christ's priesthood will never be handed off to a successor because it will never need to be. He lives forever, and therefore His priestly ministry continues forever — without interruption, without degradation, without transfer.

John Owen seized upon this word aparabatos with great joy. He wrote that the unchangeableness of Christ's priesthood is the foundation of all the believer's comfort. If Christ's priesthood could end — if He could cease to intercede, if He could grow weary of His office, if death or any other power could remove Him — then the believer's salvation would be uncertain. But because His priesthood is aparabatos — unchangeable, untransferable, inviolable — the believer's salvation is as secure as the throne of heaven itself.

"Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them." Here is the golden verse — the summit toward which the entire argument has been climbing. Because Christ's priesthood is eternal, His saving power is complete. The phrase "to the uttermost" translates the Greek eis to panteles, which means "completely," "perfectly," "to the full extent," "to the final end." Christ does not save partially. He does not save temporarily. He does not save with reservations or conditions. He saves to the uttermost — completely, perfectly, and forever.

And the ground of this complete salvation is His perpetual intercession: "seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them." Christ's saving work did not end at the cross. The cross was the foundation — the once-for-all sacrifice that atoned for sin — but upon that foundation, Christ continues to build through His ongoing ministry of intercession at the Father's right hand. At this very moment, the risen Christ is interceding for His people. He is presenting His finished work before the Father. He is pleading their cause. He is securing their perseverance. He is ensuring that not one of them is lost.

Thomas Goodwin wrote with extraordinary tenderness about Christ's intercession. He compared it to the work of an advocate in a courtroom — not one who argues for the acquittal of the guilty on a technicality, but one who presents irrefutable evidence of satisfaction rendered. Christ does not beg the Father to overlook sin; He presents His own blood as the full and final payment for it. The Father does not grudgingly grant what Christ requests; He joyfully acknowledges the sufficiency of the sacrifice and freely bestows all that Christ asks on behalf of His people.

Richard Sibbes, the Puritan known as "the heavenly doctor," wrote beautifully about the comfort of Christ's intercession for struggling believers. He noted that Christ intercedes not for the strong but for the weak — not for those who have no need of help but for those who are battered by temptation, weighed down by guilt, and struggling to persevere. It is precisely when you feel least worthy of Christ's attention that His intercession is most active on your behalf. He prayed for Peter before Peter even knew he needed prayer (Luke 22:32). He intercedes for you in the same way — anticipating your needs, covering your failures, and securing your final perseverance.

"For such an high priest became us." The word "became" here means "was fitting for us" — this is the kind of high priest we needed. And what kind is He? "Holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens." Each adjective builds upon the last, creating a portrait of absolute moral perfection.

"Holy" — consecrated to God, set apart for divine service. "Harmless" — the Greek word akakos means "without guile," "innocent," "free from all malice." "Undefiled" — pure, without any stain of moral corruption. "Separate from sinners" — not in the sense that He avoided their company (He was famously the friend of sinners), but in the sense that He was utterly distinct from them in His nature. He walked among the fallen without falling. He touched the unclean without becoming unclean. He bore the sins of the world without committing a single sin. "Made higher than the heavens" — exalted to the supreme place of honor, above all principalities and powers, at the very right hand of God.

"Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people's: for this he did once, when he offered up himself." The Levitical high priests were themselves sinners. Before they could atone for the sins of the people, they had to atone for their own sins. But Christ had no sin of His own to atone for. His sacrifice was not for Himself but entirely for others. And He offered it "once" — hapax, once for all, never to be repeated. The sacrifice of Christ on Calvary was so complete, so perfect, so infinitely sufficient that it needs no supplement, no repetition, no addition. It is finished.

"For the law maketh men high priests which have infirmity; but the word of the oath, which was since the law, maketh the Son, who is consecrated for evermore." The final contrast: the law appointed weak men as priests; the divine oath appointed the Son of God. The law produced a temporary arrangement; the oath established an eternal one. The law was glorious, but the oath is more glorious still.

--- Application ---

1. Rest in Christ's ability to save to the uttermost. Whatever your sin, whatever your weakness, whatever your failure — it is not beyond the reach of His saving power. He saves completely, and He saves forever.

2. Take daily comfort in Christ's intercession. He is praying for you right now. Not because you are worthy, but because He is faithful. His intercession does not depend upon your performance but upon His own perfect priesthood.

3. Approach God with confidence, not with fear. Your High Priest is holy, harmless, and undefiled. He has opened the way to the Father, and He invites you to come boldly to the throne of grace.

4. Trust in the finality of Christ's sacrifice. Do not look for additional atonement, additional merit, or additional sacrifice. The cross is sufficient. "It is finished" means what it says.

--- Closing ---

Lord Jesus, our great High Priest — holy, harmless, undefiled, and exalted above the heavens — we praise Thee for Thy saving power that reaches to the uttermost. We praise Thee for Thy ceaseless intercession, Thy unchangeable priesthood, and Thy once-for-all sacrifice. We come to the Father through Thee, and in Thee we find all that we need for time and for eternity. To Thee be glory and honor, now and forever. Amen.

Go in peace, beloved. Your High Priest lives, and because He lives, you shall live also. Grace be with you until we gather again.
