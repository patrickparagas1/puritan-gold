PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 1
"The Foundation of a Godly Marriage"
Proverbs 1:7


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to tonight's couples devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for setting aside this time to draw near to God and to each other. The moments you invest in your marriage before the Lord are among the most important moments of your day.

Let us open God's Word together. Our passage tonight comes from Proverbs 1:7:

"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction."

That is Proverbs 1:7. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Now let us hear from Matthew Henry, one of the great Puritan voices on this subject. The Puritans wrote extensively about marriage because they saw it as one of God's most profound gifts.

Solomon here lays the foundation upon which all true wisdom must be built: the fear of the Lord. Matthew Henry observes that this fear is not a slavish dread, but a holy reverence and filial awe of the living God. It is, as Henry writes, 'a grace that is the beginning of wisdom, the head of wisdom, the chief part of wisdom.' Without this reverential posture before our Maker, all other pursuits of knowledge and understanding are built upon sand.

Henry further notes that this fear is called 'the beginning' because it is the foundational principle from which all true knowledge flows. A man may have vast learning, keen intellect, and shrewd judgment in worldly affairs, but if he lacks this holy fear, he is, in the sight of God, a fool. The fear of the Lord is the alphabet of heavenly wisdom; one cannot read the book of divine truth without first learning these letters.

For the married couple, this truth is of surpassing importance. Henry speaks of the household as a little church, a little commonwealth, and if the fear of God does not govern it, all other virtues will prove unstable. The husband who does not fear God will not love his wife as Christ loves the church. The wife who does not fear God will not find in her heart that willing submission which flows from trust in divine order. Both will seek their own way, their own comfort, their own glory, rather than the glory of the One who joined them together.

Charles Bridges adds that this fear 'is not the fear of a slave, but of a child.' It is the tender affection of one who knows the Father's love and trembles at the thought of grieving Him. When both husband and wife share this filial reverence, their home becomes a sanctuary. Disagreements are settled not by who is louder or more stubborn, but by who can best discern the mind of God. Decisions are made not according to worldly expediency, but according to the counsel of Scripture. The fear of the Lord becomes the ballast of the ship, keeping the marriage steady through every storm.


Every marriage is built upon something. Some are built upon romantic feelings, which ebb and flow like the tide. Some are built upon shared interests or financial partnership. But the marriage that will endure and flourish is built upon the fear of the Lord.

What does this look like practically? It means that when you disagree, you do not merely ask 'What do I want?' but 'What does God require?' It means that your shared life is oriented around His Word, His worship, and His purposes. It means that you hold one another accountable not to your own standards, but to the standard of holiness revealed in Scripture.

The fear of the Lord in marriage also means that you recognize your marriage is not ultimately about your happiness, but about God's glory. This does not diminish your joy; rather, it establishes it on an unshakeable foundation. When both husband and wife fear God more than they fear conflict, loneliness, financial hardship, or any other earthly trial, they discover a unity that the world cannot give and the world cannot take away.

Take time today to examine the foundation of your marriage. Is the fear of the Lord truly the beginning of your life together?

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Now let us pause for reflection. Here are some questions for you and your spouse to discuss together. Be honest. Be gentle. Listen more than you speak.

Question 1: In what areas of our marriage have we been building on something other than the fear of the Lord?

Question 2: When we disagree, do we instinctively turn to God's Word, or do we rely on our own reasoning and feelings?

Question 3: How can we cultivate a deeper reverence for God together in our daily routine?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Let us move into a time of prayer together. If you are able, hold hands as you pray. There is something powerful about a husband and wife joining hands before the throne of grace.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray together that the fear of the Lord would be the governing principle of your marriage. Ask God to reveal any areas where you have built on sand rather than on the rock of His Word. Pray that your home would be a place where His name is honored above all.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the Lord who is the fountain of all wisdom fill your marriage with holy reverence, that in fearing Him you may find the beginning of every good thing. Amen.

May the Lord bless your marriage and keep it strong. May He make His face to shine upon your home, and give you peace. Goodnight, dear couple. Walk together in His love.
