PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 12
"Loving Your Neighbor"
Matthew Henry — Commentary on Romans

---

Hello and welcome to our family devotional. Come, young and old alike. God's Word has something for every member of this family tonight. Let us draw near to Him.

Shall we pray? Our Father, we gather tonight hungry for Your truth. We know that man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from Your mouth. Feed us now, Lord. Bless our family time together. In the name of Christ we pray. Amen.

God has preserved His Word for thousands of years so that families like ours could hear it tonight. Let us read from Romans 12:10-13:

"Be kindly affectioned one to another with brotherly love; in honour preferring one another; Not slothful in business; fervent in spirit; serving the Lord; Rejoicing in hope; patient in tribulation; continuing instant in prayer; Distributing to the necessity of saints; given to hospitality."

That is Romans 12:10-13. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Today we turn from the inner life of the family to the outward life — how a Christian family relates to others. Paul writes in Romans 12:10-13 a beautiful string of commands that paint a picture of what genuine Christian love looks like in action.

'Be kindly affectioned one to another with brotherly love.' The word 'kindly affectioned' in Greek is 'philostorgos' — a word that combines 'phileo' (friendship love) and 'storge' (family affection). It describes a warm, tender, natural affection — the kind of love that flows easily between close family members. Paul says this is how all Christians should treat one another.

'In honour preferring one another.' This is the opposite of selfish ambition. Instead of promoting ourselves, we look for ways to honor others, to put them first, to celebrate their achievements and gifts. In a family, this means siblings looking for ways to build each other up instead of tearing each other down.

'Not slothful in business; fervent in spirit; serving the Lord.' We are to be diligent, not lazy. Enthusiastic, not lukewarm. And everything we do — our work, our school, our chores — is service to the Lord. A child who does homework faithfully is serving God. A parent who works hard to provide is serving God.

'Rejoicing in hope; patient in tribulation; continuing instant in prayer.' Three more commands: be joyful because of what God has promised, be patient when things are hard, and keep praying through it all.

'Distributing to the necessity of saints; given to hospitality.' Share what you have with those in need. Open your home to others. Be generous.

This is a comprehensive picture of the Christian life in community. It is not enough to worship God privately. Our faith must overflow into love for others. Jesus said in John 13:35, 'By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another.'

Matthew Henry wrote that true Christianity is not a solitary thing — it shows itself in love for others and in kindness to all. He believed that a Christian family should be like a fountain of love that overflows to bless the neighborhood around it.

Henry gave special attention to hospitality. In his day, traveling preachers and Christians often depended on the hospitality of other believers. Henry saw the open home as one of the most powerful witnesses for Christ. When a family opens its doors — for a meal, for a visit, for a time of fellowship — it demonstrates the generous, welcoming love of God.

Henry also addressed the practical reality that loving others is not always easy. There are difficult neighbors, annoying relatives, and people who are hard to love. But he pointed to Romans 12:21, 'Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.' We do not love others because they deserve it. We love because God first loved us (1 John 4:19), and His love flowing through us has the power to change hearts and relationships.

Henry connected this to family life by noting that the family is the first community where we learn to love. Siblings who learn to prefer one another in honor, to share generously, and to forgive quickly are practicing skills they will use for the rest of their lives. The family is the training ground for all other relationships.

For the youngest: being kind to your brothers, sisters, and friends is one of the best ways to show God's love. For older children: think about who in your life might need encouragement, help, or kindness right now. What could you do this week? For the whole family: how can we open our home or extend generosity to someone in our community?

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

There is much we can learn from those who walked with God before us. Consider these words.

Matthew Henry, in his work "Commentary on the Whole Bible," wrote these words:

"True Christianity is not a solitary thing. It shows itself in love to the brethren and in kindness to all men. A Christian family should be a fountain of love that overflows its banks to bless the neighborhood around it."

What Matthew Henry is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Romans 12:10-13 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Family, let us talk about what we have learned. Consider these questions together.

Question 1: What does it mean to be 'kindly affectioned' to someone?

Question 2: What does 'in honour preferring one another' look like between brothers and sisters?

Question 3: Why does Paul include so many different instructions in just a few verses? What picture does it paint?

Question 4: How can our family be a 'fountain of love' that blesses others around us?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Be kindly affectioned one to another with brotherly love; in honour preferring one another." -- Romans 12:10

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Learning sticks better when we do something with it. Try this activity as a family:

Choose a family 'kindness project' for this week. Ideas: bake cookies for a neighbor, write an encouraging note to someone who is lonely, invite another family for a meal, or collect items to donate to a family in need.

--- Closing ---

Lord, as we close tonight's devotional, we place our family in Your hands. Watch over us as we sleep. Guard our dreams. Prepare our hearts for tomorrow. May the truths from Romans 12:10-13 follow us into the new day. We love You, Lord. Amen.

Goodnight. Remember your memory verse, and carry it with you tomorrow.
