PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 1
"Submit One to Another"
Ephesians 5:21 — Mutual Submission and Love


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to a new month in the Puritan Gold Together devotional. This month, we begin a journey through some of the richest passages in all of Scripture on the subject of marriage. Thank you for choosing to sit together, to open the Word, and to let God speak into your union. These quiet evenings together are among the most important investments you will ever make.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 5:21 (KJV):

"Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God."


There is perhaps no verse in all the marriage passages of Scripture that is more frequently overlooked than this one. We rush past Ephesians 5:21 to get to the instructions about wives and husbands, but the apostle Paul places this verse first for a reason. It is the doorway through which all the household instructions must pass. Before Paul speaks of the wife's submission or the husband's sacrificial love, he establishes the foundational principle: mutual submission in the fear of God.

The Puritan writer William Gouge, in his great work Domestical Duties, observed that this verse governs everything that follows. Gouge wrote that mutual submission does not erase the distinct duties of husband and wife, but it does establish the spirit in which those duties are to be carried out. Neither husband nor wife is to lord it over the other. Neither is to demand service without offering it. The marriage relationship, according to Gouge, is one of mutual deference, mutual honor, and mutual care.

What does it mean to submit to one another? It means to place the needs, the interests, and the well-being of your spouse above your own. The husband submits to his wife by laying down his preferences for her good. The wife submits to her husband by honoring his leadership and supporting him in his calling. Both submit to each other by listening more than speaking, by serving more than demanding, by forgiving more than accusing.

Notice the qualifying phrase: "in the fear of God." This is essential. Our submission to one another is not grounded in mere human affection or romantic sentiment. It is grounded in the fear of God. We submit to each other because we first submit to Him. We serve each other because we first serve Him. When the fear of God governs a marriage, selfishness loses its grip. Pride is dethroned. The question is no longer "What do I deserve?" but "What does God require of me toward my spouse?"

Richard Baxter, in his Christian Directory, spoke of this mutual submission as the great preservative of peace in the home. Where both husband and wife seek to outdo one another in honor and service, strife finds no foothold. Where both are more concerned with their own duties than with their spouse's failures, love flourishes. Baxter counseled married couples to keep short accounts with one another, to be quick to confess and quick to forgive, and to remember always that they stand together before the same Lord who will one day call them to account for how they treated each other.

Consider the example of Christ Himself, to whom Paul will point in the verses that follow. Though He was Lord of all, He took the form of a servant. Though He had every right to demand worship and obedience, He knelt and washed His disciples' feet. Though He was without sin, He bore the sins of His bride, the church, upon the cross. This is the pattern for mutual submission in marriage. It is not weakness; it is the highest expression of strength. It is not loss of dignity; it is the truest dignity of all.

Dear couple, examine your hearts tonight. Is your marriage marked by mutual submission? Do you seek to outdo one another in love and service? Or have you fallen into the pattern of keeping score, of demanding your rights, of insisting on your way? The fear of God calls you to a higher path. It calls you to lay down your weapons, to open your hands, and to serve the one whom God has given you.

The Puritans understood that marriage is a daily discipline of dying to self. It is not a single act of submission performed at the altar on your wedding day. It is a thousand small acts of submission performed in the kitchen, in the bedroom, in the quiet moments when no one is watching. Each act of deference, each moment of patience, each sacrifice of preference is an offering to God and a gift to your spouse.

Let this verse be the motto of your marriage this month: "Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God."


Question 1: In what specific ways can I better submit to you this week, placing your needs above my own?

Question 2: Do we approach our marriage with the fear of God as our governing principle, or have we allowed selfishness to creep in?

Question 3: What does it look like practically for us to outdo one another in honor and service in our daily routines?


Heavenly Father, we come before Thee as husband and wife, acknowledging that apart from Thy grace, we are prone to selfishness and pride. Teach us the holy art of mutual submission. Help us to fear Thee above all, that in fearing Thee we might love one another with the love of Christ. Forgive us for the times we have demanded rather than served, for the moments we have insisted on our own way rather than seeking the good of our spouse. Fill our marriage with the spirit of humble, joyful service. May our home be a place where Thy name is honored and Thy love is made visible. In the name of Jesus Christ our Lord, Amen.
