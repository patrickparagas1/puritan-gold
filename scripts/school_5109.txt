"The Church, Sacraments, and Means of Grace"
Westminster Standards — Lesson 9

---

Welcome back to Puritan Gold's homeschool program. Today we examine the Westminster Standards' teaching on the church, the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's Supper, and the means of grace through which God strengthens His people. Let us pray and begin.

Lord of the church, You have gathered a people for Yourself from every nation and every age. As we study Your design for the church and the means of grace, deepen our love for Your bride and our appreciation for the gifts You have given her. In Christ's name, Amen.

--- Lesson ---

The Westminster Standards devote significant attention to the doctrine of the church, recognizing that Christianity is not merely a private, individual experience but a communal faith lived out in the context of the body of Christ.

The Confession, Chapter 25, describes the church as both invisible and visible. The invisible church "consists of the whole number of the elect, that have been, are, or shall be gathered into one, under Christ the head thereof." This is the church as God sees it — the total number of all true believers throughout all of history, known only to God. The visible church "consists of all those throughout the world that profess the true religion, and of their children." This is the church as it appears to us — local congregations of professing believers and their families, organized for worship, instruction, fellowship, and service.

The Shorter Catechism, Question 88, ties the church to the means of grace: "The outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption are, his ordinances, especially the Word, sacraments, and prayer; all which are made effectual to the elect for salvation." This is a critically important teaching. God does not typically save and sanctify people through dramatic, miraculous interventions. He works through ordinary means — the reading and preaching of the Word, the sacraments, and prayer. These are the channels through which grace flows to God's people.

The Word of God is the primary means of grace. The Shorter Catechism, Question 89, teaches: "The Spirit of God maketh the reading, but especially the preaching, of the Word, an effectual means of convincing and converting sinners, and of building them up in holiness and comfort, through faith, unto salvation." The preaching of the Word is not merely a human lecture about religious topics. It is the living voice of God, spoken through human instruments, made powerful by the Holy Spirit. This is why the Puritans placed the sermon at the center of their worship — not because they loved long speeches, but because they believed that God speaks through the faithful exposition of His Word.

The sacraments are another vital means of grace. The Confession, Chapter 27, defines a sacrament as "a holy ordinance instituted by Christ; wherein, by sensible signs, Christ, and the benefits of the new covenant, are represented, sealed, and applied to believers." A sacrament has three elements: it is instituted by Christ (not by human invention), it uses physical signs (water, bread, wine), and it represents and seals spiritual realities (forgiveness, union with Christ, membership in the covenant community).

The two sacraments recognized by the Westminster Standards are baptism and the Lord's Supper.

Baptism, addressed in Chapters 28 and the relevant catechism questions, is the sign and seal of the covenant of grace. It signifies our union with Christ in His death and resurrection, the washing away of sin, and our incorporation into the visible church. The Confession teaches that baptism is to be administered not only to adult converts but also to "the infants of one or both believing parents," because the children of believers are members of the covenant community. Baptism is administered once and is not to be repeated.

The Lord's Supper, addressed in Chapter 29, is the other sacrament. In the Supper, bread and wine represent the body and blood of Christ, given for us in His atoning death. The Confession affirms that while the bread and wine do not become the actual body and blood of Christ (rejecting the Roman Catholic doctrine of transubstantiation), Christ is nonetheless truly present in the sacrament through His Spirit. The Lord's Supper is not a mere memorial or bare symbol — it is a genuine means of grace through which believers are spiritually nourished, strengthened, and refreshed.

Prayer is the third means of grace. The Shorter Catechism, Question 98, defines prayer as "an offering up of our desires unto God, for things agreeable to his will, in the name of Christ, with confession of our sins, and thankful acknowledgment of his mercies." Prayer is not a technique for getting what we want from God. It is the privileged communion of children with their Father — asking, confessing, thanking, and adoring.

The Westminster Standards present a comprehensive vision of the Christian life: believers gathered in churches, nourished by the Word preached and read, strengthened by the sacraments, and sustained by prayer. These are not optional extras for the spiritually advanced. They are the ordinary, God-appointed means by which every Christian grows in grace and perseveres in faith.

--- Review Questions ---

Review Question 1: What is the difference between the visible and invisible church as taught by the Westminster Confession?

Review Question 2: What are the three "outward and ordinary means" of grace, and why are they called "ordinary"?

Review Question 3: How does the Westminster Confession understand the Lord's Supper? How does it differ from both the Roman Catholic view and a purely symbolic view?

--- Activity ---

Create a "Means of Grace" study guide. Divide a page into three sections: The Word, The Sacraments, and Prayer. For each section, write a summary of the Westminster Standards' teaching, list relevant Shorter Catechism questions and answers, and include three Scripture passages that support the teaching. Then write a personal paragraph describing how you have experienced the means of grace in your own life.

--- Closing ---

The means of grace are God's gifts to His church. Do not neglect them. Hear the Word preached. Receive the sacraments. Pray without ceasing. Through these ordinary means, the extraordinary grace of God will sustain you all the way home. Until next time, students.
