PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"By Grace Through Faith"
July 9, 2026

---

"For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: not of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them." -- Ephesians 2:8-10

Here is the clearest, most concise statement of the Protestant gospel in all of Scripture. Three verses that shook the world when Martin Luther and the Reformers rediscovered them, and that continue to liberate captive souls to this day.

'By grace are ye saved through faith.' Grace is the source; faith is the instrument. Grace is the ocean; faith is the cup that drinks from it. Grace is God's part; faith is our response -- but even that response, Paul hastens to add, is 'not of yourselves: it is the gift of God.' The whole of salvation, from first to last, is a gift. The plan is a gift. The execution is a gift. The application is a gift. Even the hand that receives the gift is itself a gift.

The Puritans labored with great care to maintain the proper relationship between grace and works. They were fierce opponents of both legalism and antinomianism -- both the error that says works contribute to salvation and the error that says salvation has nothing to do with works. Listen to how Paul holds both truths together with perfect balance: 'Not of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works.' We are not saved by good works, but we are saved unto good works. Good works are not the root of salvation but the fruit of salvation. They do not earn our acceptance with God, but they demonstrate it.

Richard Baxter, though sometimes suspected of leaning too far toward the importance of works, actually expressed this truth with great beauty when he wrote, 'Good works are not the way to heaven, but they are the way of heaven. They are not the cause of our salvation, but they are the evidence of it. They do not purchase our inheritance, but they show that we are heirs.'

And then comes that magnificent word: 'workmanship.' In the original Greek, the word is poiema, from which we get our English word 'poem.' We are God's poem, His masterpiece, His work of art. Every believer is a new creation, shaped by the hands of the divine Artist to display the beauty of His grace. And the good works that flow from our new nature are not random or accidental; they were 'before ordained' -- planned in advance by God Himself. The same God who planned your salvation before the foundation of the world also planned the good works you would do after your salvation. Nothing is left to chance. Everything is by design. And the Designer is infinitely wise, infinitely good, and infinitely gracious. Amen.
