PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 3
"The Christian Home as a Little Church"
John Angell James — A Help to Domestic Happiness

---

Welcome, family. Whether you are gathered on the couch, around the dinner table, or riding in the car, God is here with you. Let us turn our attention to Him and see what treasure He has stored up for us tonight.

Let us bow our heads. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family in Your presence. We need Your wisdom tonight. We need Your guidance. Open our ears to hear, our minds to understand, and our hearts to receive. We pray in the precious name of Jesus. Amen.

Let us turn together to Joshua 24:15. Follow along in your Bible if you have one:

"And if it seem evil unto you to serve the LORD, choose you this day whom ye will serve; whether the gods which your fathers served that were on the other side of the flood, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye dwell: but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."

That is Joshua 24:15. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Today we hear one of the boldest declarations in all of Scripture. Joshua, the great leader of Israel, stands before the entire nation and issues a challenge. He says, 'Choose you this day whom ye will serve.' And then he makes his own choice crystal clear: 'But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.'

Let us understand the context. Joshua is now an old man. He has led Israel through the conquest of the Promised Land. He has seen God's faithfulness firsthand — the parting of the Jordan River, the walls of Jericho falling, victory after victory. Now, at the end of his life, he gathers all the tribes together for a final charge.

And what is his message? It is not about military strategy. It is not about political alliances. It is about worship. Joshua knows that the most important decision any family will ever make is this: whom will we serve?

Notice that Joshua does not say, 'As for me, I will serve the LORD.' He says, 'As for me and my house.' Joshua takes responsibility for the spiritual direction of his entire household. He does not leave it to chance. He does not say, 'Well, everyone can decide for themselves.' He leads. He sets the course.

This does not mean Joshua forced his family to believe. You cannot force faith. But Joshua created a home where God was honored, worshipped, and obeyed. He set the tone. He established the priorities. He made the choice that would shape his household's identity.

The Psalms reinforce this picture of the godly home. Psalm 128:1-4 says, 'Blessed is every one that feareth the LORD; that walketh in his ways. For thou shalt eat the labour of thine hands: happy shalt thou be, and it shall be well with thee. Thy wife shall be as a fruitful vine by the sides of thine house: thy children like olive plants round about thy table. Behold, that thus shall the man be blessed that feareth the LORD.'

God paints a picture here of a home blessed by His presence — a table surrounded by children who are growing like olive plants, which are symbols of peace and fruitfulness. This is not a picture of a perfect family with no problems. It is a picture of a family rooted in the fear of the Lord, and because of that, blessed.

The early Puritans had a beautiful phrase for this. They called the Christian home 'a little church.' John Angell James picked up this same language. He wrote that every house should be a little church, and every head of a family should be a kind of minister in it. He believed that family religion is the root of all religion.

What did James mean by calling the home a 'little church'? He meant that the same things that happen in a church gathering — reading Scripture, prayer, singing praise, teaching, encouraging one another — should also happen in the home. The church gathers on Sunday, but the family gathers every day. And it is in the daily rhythm of family worship that faith becomes real and personal.

James also observed that children who grow up in homes where God is genuinely worshipped are far more likely to embrace the faith for themselves. He did not mean empty rituals or forced religion. He meant living, breathing, joyful faith — where parents pray with sincerity, read Scripture with delight, and speak of God's goodness naturally throughout the day.

He warned, however, against the danger of making family worship a cold, mechanical duty. If children see their parents treating worship as a chore, they will learn to see it that way too. But if they see genuine love for God — imperfect, honest, but real — it creates an atmosphere where faith can flourish.

For our youngest family members: your home is like a little church! Every time we pray together or read the Bible, we are worshipping God right here in our house. For those who are older: think about Joshua's bold choice. He did not wait to see what everyone else would do. He decided first. That takes courage. And for the whole family: what does it look like for our home to be a 'little church'? It does not require perfection. It requires intentionality — choosing, as Joshua did, to serve the Lord together.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

For centuries, faithful Christians have reflected on passages like this one. Let us hear from a trusted voice of the past.

John Angell James, in his work "A Help to Domestic Happiness," wrote these words:

"Every house should be a little church, and every head of a family should be a kind of minister in it. Family religion is the root of all religion."

What John Angell James is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Joshua 24:15 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Discussion time. These questions are for every member of the family, young and old alike.

Question 1: What did Joshua tell his family they would do? (Serve the Lord!)

Question 2: What does it mean to call our home a 'little church'?

Question 3: Why did Joshua say 'me and my house' instead of just 'me'? What does this teach us about family leadership?

Question 4: How can we make sure our family worship is joyful and real, not just going through the motions?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD." -- Joshua 24:15b

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

The Puritans believed that truth must be practiced, not just heard. Here is something you can do together:

Create a 'Family Mission Statement' together. On a large piece of paper, write your family's version of Joshua 24:15 — something like 'The [Your Last Name] Family Will Serve the Lord.' Have everyone sign it and hang it in a prominent place.

--- Closing ---

Let us bow our heads and pray. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family around Your Word. We ask You to write these truths on our hearts. Bless our family, protect our home, and lead us in the path of righteousness for Your name's sake. In Christ we pray. Amen.

Until tomorrow, family. May the Lord bless you and keep you.
