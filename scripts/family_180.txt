PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 19
"The Lord's Day in the Home"
Matthew Henry — Family Worship and Bible Study

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Matthew Henry speaks to us about the Christian Sabbath — the Lord's Day — and how the family can make this day the crown jewel of the week. Let us listen with open hearts.

Let us pray. Lord of the Sabbath, we thank You for the gift of rest, worship, and holy time set apart for You. Teach us to treasure this day as You intend it — not as a burden but as a blessing, not as a restriction but as a gift. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Isaiah 58:13-14 and Mark 2:27-28. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"If thou turn away thy foot from the sabbath, from doing thy pleasure on my holy day; and call the sabbath a delight, the holy of the Lord, honourable; and shalt honour him, not doing thine own ways, nor finding thine own pleasure, nor speaking thine own words: Then shalt thou delight thyself in the Lord; and I will cause thee to ride upon the high places of the earth, and feed thee with the heritage of Jacob thy father: for the mouth of the Lord hath spoken it. And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath: Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath."

That is Isaiah 58:13-14 and Mark 2:27-28. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

For Matthew Henry, the Lord's Day was the pinnacle of the week — the day around which all other days were organized. He believed, as did the Puritans generally, that God had set apart one day in seven for special worship, rest, and spiritual refreshment. This was not a burden imposed on weary souls but a gift offered to them — a day when the ordinary demands of work and commerce were set aside so that the family could give undivided attention to God, to worship, and to one another.

Henry saw the Lord's Day as having three great purposes for the family. The first was public worship. The family dressed, gathered, and traveled to the meeting house together. They sang psalms together. They heard the Word preached together. They received the sacraments together. The Lord's Day was the day when the household joined the larger household of God in corporate worship. Henry regarded this as one of the most important acts a family could perform, because it taught children that the faith was not merely a private, individual matter but a communal one — that they belonged to a body larger than themselves.

The second purpose was rest. God created the Sabbath because He knew His creatures needed rest — not just physical rest from labor, but spiritual rest from the relentless demands of the world. Henry counseled families to protect the Lord's Day from the encroachment of work, commerce, and needless activity. This was not legalism but love — love for the body that needs rest, love for the soul that needs refreshment, and love for the family that needs time together without the pressures of the ordinary week.

The third purpose was spiritual growth. Henry saw the Lord's Day as the family's weekly opportunity for extended Bible study, catechetical instruction, spiritual conversation, and prayer. After returning from public worship, the family would gather to discuss the sermon — what was the text? What was the main point? What did each family member learn? How will we apply it this week? This practice of sermon review, Henry wrote, multiplied the value of the preaching many times over, because it forced every member of the family to listen attentively, to think carefully, and to engage with the Word at a personal level.

Henry's vision of the Lord's Day was not gloomy or severe. He delighted in the day. Isaiah says, "Call the sabbath a delight" — and Henry took this literally. The Lord's Day should be the happiest day of the week. The best meal, the warmest fellowship, the richest conversation, the most joyful singing — all should mark this day as special, set apart, and beloved.

Henry also addressed the challenge of making the Lord's Day meaningful for children. He recommended that parents prepare children for worship by discussing the Scripture readings in advance. He suggested that after the service, parents ask questions suited to the child's age and understanding. He encouraged families to read devotional works together on Sunday afternoons. And he urged parents to model joy and delight in the day — because children will adopt their parents' attitude toward worship. If the parents treat the Lord's Day as a drudgery, the children will too. If the parents treat it as a delight, the children will learn to delight in it as well.

Jesus said, "The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath." The Lord's Day is a gift tailored to our deepest needs — the need for God, the need for rest, and the need for one another. Let us receive this gift with gratitude and make the most of it as a family.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Isaiah says to "call the sabbath a delight." What would it look like for our family to truly delight in the Lord's Day? What could we do differently?

Question 2: Henry recommends discussing the Sunday sermon together as a family. How could we begin this practice? What questions could we ask?

Question 3: Jesus says the sabbath was made for man. How is the Lord's Day a gift rather than a burden? What blessings does it bring to our family?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"If thou call the sabbath a delight, the holy of the Lord, honourable; then shalt thou delight thyself in the Lord." -- Isaiah 58:13-14

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord of the Sabbath, thank You for the gift of the Lord's Day. Help our family to treasure it, to protect it, and to use it for worship, rest, and spiritual growth. Make it the brightest day of our week — the day when we draw nearest to You and to one another. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
