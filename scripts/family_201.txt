PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 9
"When God's Plan Surprises Us"
John Flavel — Providence and Family Trust

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight John Flavel teaches us about the surprising turns of God's providence and how families can respond with faith when God's plan looks different from what we expected. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, we confess that we often try to write the script of our own lives. Tonight, teach us to surrender our plans to Yours and to trust that Your surprises are always better than our expectations. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Proverbs 16:9 and Isaiah 55:8-9. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps. For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, saith the LORD. For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts."

That is Proverbs 16:9 and Isaiah 55:8-9. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

One of the most remarkable features of God's providence, according to John Flavel, is its unpredictability. God delights in working in ways that surprise us. He uses unexpected means, unexpected timing, and unexpected people to accomplish His purposes. And nowhere is this more evident than in the life of the family.

Every family has experienced moments when God's plan looked dramatically different from their own. Perhaps you planned to live in one city, but God moved you to another. Perhaps you expected a season of ease, but God sent a season of difficulty. Perhaps a door you desperately wanted to walk through was closed, while a door you never noticed was opened wide. Flavel would say that these surprises are not signs of God's absence but of His active, personal involvement in your family's story.

Flavel drew attention to the story of Joseph in the book of Genesis as one of the most powerful illustrations of surprising providence. Joseph's brothers sold him into slavery. He was falsely accused and thrown into prison. Year after year, his circumstances seemed to go from bad to worse. And yet, behind every apparent setback, God was positioning Joseph for the role that would save his entire family from famine. At the end of the story, Joseph could say to his brothers: "Ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive" (Genesis 50:20).

This is the pattern of divine providence: what appears to be disaster is often preparation. What appears to be loss is often the clearing of ground for something better. What appears to be the end of the road is often a turn toward a destination more glorious than anything we could have imagined.

Flavel taught that families face a particular temptation when God's plan surprises them: the temptation to anxiety. When things do not go according to plan, our natural response is worry. Will we be okay? Will the children be affected? Will we recover from this setback? Flavel answered these questions not with shallow optimism but with robust theology. God is sovereign. God is wise. God is good. If He has allowed this surprise into your family's life, He has a purpose for it, and that purpose is your ultimate good.

But Flavel also acknowledged that trusting God in the midst of surprise is not easy. It requires what the Puritans called "the exercise of faith" — the active, deliberate choice to believe God's promises even when our circumstances seem to contradict them. Faith is not a feeling. It is a decision to stand on the Word of God when everything else is shifting beneath our feet.

Flavel counseled families to develop what we might call a "providence perspective." This means training ourselves to look at every event — good or bad, expected or unexpected — through the lens of God's sovereign care. It means asking, "What might God be doing through this?" rather than "Why did God let this happen to me?" It means trusting that the Author of our family's story knows the ending even when we are lost in a confusing chapter.

Tonight, share with your family a time when God surprised you with His providence. Tell the story. Trace His hand. And let your children hear you say, "I did not understand at the time, but now I can see that God was at work." These testimonies are among the most powerful tools God gives parents for building faith in their children's hearts.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Can you think of a time when God's plan for our family was different from what we expected? How did it turn out?

Question 2: Joseph told his brothers, "Ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good." How does this help us when something painful happens in our family?

Question 3: Flavel said that faith is not a feeling but a decision to trust God's Word. What makes this decision difficult, and what helps us make it?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps." -- Proverbs 16:9

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Father, we surrender our plans to You tonight. We confess that Your ways are higher than ours and Your thoughts greater than ours. Help our family to embrace the surprises of Your providence with faith rather than fear. Thank You for writing a story for our family that is far better than anything we could have written ourselves. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, trusting the Author of your family's story.
