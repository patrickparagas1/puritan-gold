PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 28
"Pursuing Peace and Building Up"
Romans for Marriage — Day 28

---

Welcome, husband and wife. There is something powerful about two hearts turning toward God at the same time. When you both face the same direction, toward Christ, everything in your marriage finds its proper alignment. Let us do that now.

Every marriage needs a foundation that does not shift. Let us hear from that foundation now, from Romans 14:19:

"Let us therefore follow after the things which make for peace, and things wherewith one may edify another."

That is Romans 14:19. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

The Puritans treasured marriage as a covenant made before God. Matthew Henry wrote beautifully about how Scripture speaks to the hearts of married couples.

Paul gives a beautiful dual command: pursue peace and pursue edification. These are not passive goals but active pursuits — things we must 'follow after' with intention and energy. Peace does not happen by default in a fallen world. Edification does not occur naturally between sinful people. Both require deliberate, sustained effort.

Matthew Henry commented that this verse provides the two-fold mission of every Christian relationship, and especially marriage: make peace and build up. A marriage that is merely peaceful but not edifying is stagnant. A marriage that is growing but not peaceful is turbulent. God calls us to both — a home where peace reigns and where both partners are being built up in faith, character, and love.

Henry defined peace not as the absence of conflict but as the presence of harmony. Peaceful marriages still disagree. They still have difficult conversations. They still navigate real differences. But they do so with a shared commitment to resolve rather than win. The goal is not to defeat your spouse in an argument but to reach a place of mutual understanding and unity. Pursuing peace means being willing to compromise on nonessentials, to listen before speaking, and to value the relationship over the issue.

Henry defined edification as the intentional building up of another person. To edify your spouse means to actively contribute to their spiritual, emotional, and personal growth. It means speaking words that strengthen rather than weaken. It means noticing their efforts and affirming them. It means challenging them to grow while supporting them in the process.

Henry observed that many marriages suffer from a deficit of edification. Spouses are quick to point out what is wrong but slow to affirm what is right. They are expert critics but amateur encouragers. This slowly erodes confidence, joy, and intimacy. A marriage where both spouses are committed to building each other up is a marriage that grows stronger with every passing year.

'Follow after' implies pursuit — running after these qualities with the same energy you would use to chase something you desperately wanted. Peace and edification are worth pursuing with everything you have.

--- Marriage Application ---

Tonight, practice both halves of this command. First, pursue peace. If there is any unresolved tension between you, address it gently. Listen more than you speak. Seek understanding before being understood. Let go of the need to be right.

Second, pursue edification. Each of you speak three specific words of encouragement to the other. Not vague compliments but specific affirmations: 'I noticed how patient you were with the children today.' 'I admire the way you handled that difficult situation at work.' 'You are growing in your faith, and it inspires me.'

Make this a daily practice: pursue peace every morning, practice edification every evening. Watch what happens to your marriage in a month.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Now turn to each other. Here are tonight's reflection questions. Answer from the heart, not from the head.

Question 1: Is your marriage characterized more by peace or by tension? What is one step you can take toward greater peace?

Question 2: When was the last time you intentionally edified your spouse with specific encouragement? What holds you back?

Question 3: What would it look like to 'follow after' peace and edification with the same energy you put into other pursuits?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

The Puritans ended every evening with prayer. Let us follow their example and bring our marriage before the Lord.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray for a marriage marked by both peace and edification. Ask God to make you peacemakers and encouragers — quick to resolve conflict and quick to build each other up.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May peace rule your home and edification fill your words. May you build each other up daily, growing stronger together in the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ.

May the words you have heard tonight bear fruit in your marriage for years to come. The Lord be with you both. Until next time.
