PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 17
"A Friend Who Loves at All Times"
Proverbs 17:17


Welcome, dear ones. Marriage is a gift from God, and so is this time together in His presence. Whether this has been an easy day or a hard one, you are here, together, and that matters more than you know.

Our Scripture reading this evening is from Proverbs 17:17. Let us receive it together:

"A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity."

That is Proverbs 17:17. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


The Puritans understood marriage in a way that the modern world has largely forgotten. Let us hear from Charles Bridges, who wrote with great wisdom on this subject.

Charles Bridges opens his comments on this verse by noting that true friendship is one of God's greatest temporal gifts. But he quickly distinguishes true friendship from its counterfeits. The world is full of fair-weather friends: those who love when circumstances are pleasant, when association brings advantage, when the relationship is easy and comfortable. But Solomon describes a friend who 'loveth at all times' — in prosperity and adversity, in health and sickness, in agreement and disagreement.

Bridges writes: 'The constancy of a true friend is one of his most distinguishing features. He does not love for a season, or with conditions, or until it becomes costly. He loves at all times.' This is the love that Paul describes in 1 Corinthians 13: patient, kind, enduring all things, hoping all things.

The second clause adds a further dimension: 'a brother is born for adversity.' Matthew Henry explains that this means the closest bonds are revealed and strengthened in the hardest times. Henry writes: 'Nothing tries friendship like adversity. Many will love a man when he is in prosperity, and they may share in his comforts; but if he be in adversity, they will forsake him.' The brother born for adversity is the one who draws closer when others withdraw.

Bridges applies this directly to marriage: 'Your spouse should be your truest friend, the one who loves at all times and stands closest in adversity.' If your marriage does not have this quality of friendship — steadfast, unconditional, and deepened by difficulty — then something fundamental is missing. The romantic dimension of marriage is important, but it is the friendship dimension that sustains the marriage through the longest seasons.


Your spouse should be your best friend. This is not a sentimental cliche; it is a biblical expectation. The friend who loves at all times is exactly what a husband and wife are called to be for one another.

Loving at all times means loving when your spouse is at their worst: when they are sick, when they have failed, when they are difficult to live with, when they have hurt you. It means choosing to be a brother or sister born for adversity: drawing closer in the hard seasons rather than pulling away.

How is the friendship in your marriage? Do you enjoy one another's company? Do you confide in one another? Do you laugh together? Do you bear one another's burdens? Invest in the friendship dimension of your marriage. It is the soil in which every other aspect of your relationship grows.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


The Puritans practiced what they called holy conference, speaking honestly about spiritual things with those they loved most. Do that now with these questions.

Question 1: Am I truly a friend who loves at all times to my spouse, or does my love fluctuate with circumstances?

Question 2: In what recent adversity did our friendship as a couple grow stronger or weaker?

Question 3: How can we invest in the friendship dimension of our marriage this week?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


It is time to bring what we have heard and discussed before the Lord in prayer. Pray out loud if you can. Let your spouse hear your heart.

Tonight's prayer focus: Thank God for the gift of friendship in your marriage, and ask Him to deepen it. Pray for the grace to love at all times, especially in the hard times. Ask that you would be to each other a brother or sister born for adversity.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the Lord who is the friend of sinners make your marriage a friendship that loves at all times and grows ever stronger through every trial. Amen.

May the God who brought you together continue to bind you together in love, in faith, and in purpose. Sleep well tonight. He watches over your marriage.
