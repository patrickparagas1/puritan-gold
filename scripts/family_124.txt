PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 24
"The Godly Person Lives by Faith"
Thomas Watson — The Godly Man's Picture


Welcome, family. Whether you are gathered on the couch, around the dinner table, or riding in the car, God is here with you. Let us turn our attention to Him and see what treasure He has stored up for us tonight.

Let us bow our heads. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family in Your presence. We need Your wisdom tonight. We need Your guidance. Open our ears to hear, our minds to understand, and our hearts to receive. We pray in the precious name of Jesus. Amen.

Let us turn together to Hebrews 11:1-6. Follow along in your Bible if you have one:

"Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen. For by it the elders obtained a good report. Through faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear. By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain, by which he obtained witness that he was righteous, God testifying of his gifts: and by it he being dead yet speaketh. By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death; and was not found, because God had translated him: for before his translation he had this testimony, that he pleased God. But without faith it is impossible to please him: for he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him."

That is Hebrews 11:1-6. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Today Thomas Watson shows us the third mark of the godly person: living by faith. Hebrews chapter 11 is often called the 'Hall of Faith' because it tells the stories of men and women who trusted God against all odds. But before listing those heroes, the author gives us a definition of faith that is one of the most profound statements in all of Scripture.

'Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.' This is a packed sentence. Let us unfold it.

'Faith is the substance of things hoped for.' The word 'substance' in Greek is 'hypostasis,' which means foundation, ground, or assurance. Faith gives substance — reality, solidity — to the things we hope for. We hope for heaven, for resurrection, for the fulfillment of all God's promises. Faith makes these future realities present and real to us now. It is not wishful thinking. It is confident assurance based on the character and promises of God.

'The evidence of things not seen.' Faith is proof — convincing evidence — of realities that our physical eyes cannot see. We cannot see God with our eyes, but faith gives us certainty that He is real. We cannot see heaven, but faith assures us it exists. We cannot see the future, but faith trusts that God holds it in His hands.

Then comes one of the most important statements in the Bible about faith: 'But without faith it is impossible to please him.' Not difficult — impossible. No amount of good works, religious activity, moral behavior, or intellectual knowledge can please God apart from faith. 'For he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him.' Two beliefs are required: God exists, and He rewards those who genuinely seek Him.

The chapter then unfolds the stories of faith. Abel offered a better sacrifice by faith. Enoch walked with God by faith. Noah built an ark by faith when there was no sign of rain. Abraham left his homeland by faith, not knowing where he was going. Sarah conceived a child by faith when she was far past the age of childbearing. Moses chose to suffer with God's people by faith, rather than enjoy the pleasures of Egypt.

What ties all these stories together? These people trusted God's word over their own experience. They obeyed when it did not make sense. They held on when they could not see the outcome. That is faith.

In Romans 1:17, Paul writes, 'The just shall live by faith.' This is quoted from Habakkuk 2:4 and is repeated three times in the New Testament (Romans, Galatians, and Hebrews). The righteous person does not live by sight, by feelings, by popularity, or by convenience. He lives by faith — trusting God's Word and God's character in every situation.

Thomas Watson wrote that faith is the eye of the soul that looks to Christ, the hand of the soul that receives Christ, and the foot of the soul that walks with Christ. The godly person lives by faith as the body lives by breathing — constantly, naturally, dependently.

Watson distinguished between different kinds of faith. There is historical faith — believing that the facts of Christianity are true. Even demons have this kind of faith (James 2:19). There is temporary faith — emotional excitement about spiritual things that fades when difficulty comes, like the seed that fell on rocky ground in Jesus' parable (Matthew 13:20-21). And there is saving faith — a deep, personal trust in Christ that transforms the whole life. Watson urged his readers to examine themselves to make sure their faith was genuine, not merely intellectual or emotional.

Watson also offered encouragement to those whose faith feels small or weak. He wrote that God does not despise a weak faith. A trembling hand can receive a gift as well as a strong one. Jesus said that faith even as small as a mustard seed can move mountains (Matthew 17:20). The question is not how big our faith is but how big the God is in whom we place it.

For our little ones: faith means trusting God even when you cannot see Him. Just like you trust that the sun is still there even when clouds cover it, we trust that God is always there, always loving us, always keeping His promises. For older children: is your faith based on feelings or on the promises of God? Feelings change; God's Word does not. For the whole family: faith is not a leap in the dark. It is a step into the light of God's trustworthy Word. Let us walk by faith today.


For centuries, faithful Christians have reflected on passages like this one. Let us hear from a trusted voice of the past.

Thomas Watson, in his work "The Godly Man's Picture," wrote these words:

"Faith is the eye of the soul that looks to Christ, the hand of the soul that receives Christ, and the foot of the soul that walks with Christ. A godly man lives by faith as the body lives by breathing."

What Thomas Watson is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Hebrews 11:1-6 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Discussion time. These questions are for every member of the family, young and old alike.

Question 1: Can you think of something you believe in even though you cannot see it? (Air, love, gravity?) How is faith like that?

Question 2: Why does the Bible say it is impossible to please God without faith?

Question 3: Which hero of faith from Hebrews 11 do you find most inspiring, and why?

Question 4: Watson says even small, weak faith is accepted by God. How does that encourage you if your faith sometimes feels small?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen." -- Hebrews 11:1

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


The Puritans believed that truth must be practiced, not just heard. Here is something you can do together:

Do a 'trust fall' exercise (with safety precautions!). One person falls backward, trusting that another family member will catch them. Start from a low height. Discuss: how did it feel to trust someone you could not see behind you? How is this like trusting God when we cannot see Him?


Let us bow our heads and pray. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family around Your Word. We ask You to write these truths on our hearts. Bless our family, protect our home, and lead us in the path of righteousness for Your name's sake. In Christ we pray. Amen.

Until tomorrow, family. May the Lord bless you and keep you.
