PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 12
"The Crown of a Virtuous Spouse"
Proverbs 12:4


Hello and welcome to your couples devotional. Whether you are sitting side by side on the couch or listening together at the end of a long day, God is here with you both. He meets married couples in a special way when they turn their hearts to Him together.

Let me read our passage for this evening. It comes from Proverbs 12:4:

"A virtuous woman is a crown to her husband: but she that maketh ashamed is as rottenness in his bones."

That is Proverbs 12:4. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Charles Bridges was a faithful pastor and theologian whose words still carry weight centuries later. Listen to what the Puritans had to say about the truths in tonight's passage.

Charles Bridges draws out the striking contrast in this proverb with pastoral sensitivity. A 'virtuous woman' — the Hebrew word is chayil, meaning strength, capability, and moral excellence — is not merely a helper but a crown. Bridges notes that 'a crown is the highest ornament; it is worn on the head, in full view.' A virtuous spouse is a public honor, a visible testimony of God's grace.

Bridges is careful to note that this virtue is not merely moral propriety but a robust, active excellence. The virtuous wife of Proverbs is strong, industrious, wise, and generous. She brings honor to her husband not by being invisible but by being visibly excellent. And the husband is crowned by her: her virtue reflects upon him, not as though he deserves credit for her goodness, but because God has joined them as one.

Matthew Henry adds the sobering contrast: 'she that maketh ashamed is as rottenness in his bones.' Henry writes: 'A cross, ill-tempered, or wicked wife, makes a man miserable. It is an incurable grievance, like a disease in the bones, which there is no remedy for.' This is not merely about outward behavior but about character. A spouse who is dishonest, contentious, unfaithful, or foolish brings a slow decay to the other's soul.

Bridges applies this in both directions, noting that while Solomon writes here of the wife, the principle extends to both partners. A husband who is lazy, harsh, unfaithful, or ungodly brings rottenness to his wife's bones just as surely. Both husband and wife should aspire to be a crown, not a cancer, to their spouse.


This verse invites honest self-examination. Are you a crown to your spouse or a source of shame? Do you bring honor, strength, and joy to your marriage, or do you bring anxiety, embarrassment, and discouragement?

Being a crown means cultivating virtue: growing in character, pursuing holiness, exercising your gifts, and bringing your best to the marriage rather than reserving your best for the world and giving your spouse the leftovers.

It also means building your spouse up in the eyes of others. Speak well of one another. Celebrate one another's strengths publicly. Never mock or belittle your spouse, whether in their presence or their absence. The virtuous spouse is one whose partner can say with genuine gratitude: 'You are my crown.'

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Let us reflect together. These questions are designed to help you go deeper, both with God and with each other.

Question 1: In what specific ways am I being a crown to my spouse, and where might I be causing shame or discouragement?

Question 2: How do I speak about my spouse to others — do my words honor them as a crown?

Question 3: What is one area of personal virtue I can cultivate that would bless my marriage?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


As we near the end of tonight's devotional, let us bring our hearts before God in prayer. Pray for each other. Pray for your marriage. Pray for your home.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray that God would make you a crown to your spouse: a source of honor, joy, and strength. Ask Him to reveal any ways you have been causing shame or discouragement, and give you grace to change.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the Lord make each of you a crown to the other, adorning your marriage with virtue, honor, and the beauty of holiness. Amen.

The Lord bless you and keep you. The Lord make His face shine upon you and be gracious to you. The Lord lift up His countenance upon you both, and give you peace. Amen.
