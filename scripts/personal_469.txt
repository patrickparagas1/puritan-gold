PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 8
"The Whole Armour of God"
Pastoral Day — Practical Counsel on Spiritual Warfare


Welcome. Today is a Pastoral day. Our subject is spiritual warfare — the real, daily battle that every Christian faces against principalities and powers. The Puritans took this battle seriously, and their counsel remains indispensable.

Let us read Ephesians 6:11-13 (KJV):

"Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil. For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places. Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand."


The Puritans took spiritual warfare seriously. William Gurnall's massive work The Christian in Complete Armour runs to over a thousand pages and remains the fullest Puritan treatment of this subject. Today we draw from Gurnall and his fellow Puritans practical counsel for the battle.

First, know your enemy. Paul says we wrestle not against flesh and blood but against principalities and powers. The devil is real, intelligent, experienced, and relentless. He has been studying human nature for millennia. He knows your particular weaknesses better than you do. Gurnall wrote that the Christian who does not know his enemy is already at a disadvantage.

The devil's primary tactic is deception. Jesus called him the father of lies. He distorts Scripture — quoting the Word of God but twisting its meaning. He disguises sin — making evil look attractive and holiness look burdensome. He accuses the conscience — whispering that you are too sinful to be forgiven, too weak to persevere. And he distracts the mind — filling it with anxieties and trivialities to keep it from meditating on truth.

Second, put on the armour. Paul lists six pieces: the belt of truth, the breastplate of righteousness, the shoes of the gospel of peace, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit. The essential point is this: the armour is not your own. It is God's armour. You do not manufacture it; you receive it. The armour represents the truths of the Gospel applied to the specific attacks of the enemy.

When the devil attacks with lies, you put on the belt of truth. When he attacks with guilt, you put on the breastplate of righteousness — not your own but Christ's imputed righteousness. When he attacks with fear, you take up the shield of faith. When he attacks with despair, you put on the helmet of salvation — remembering that your salvation is secured by God's sovereign grace.

Third, use the sword. The sword of the Spirit is the only offensive weapon in the list. It is the Word of God. The Puritans were emphatic about the necessity of knowing Scripture deeply. Gurnall wrote that a Christian without the Word is a soldier without a sword. When Jesus was tempted in the wilderness, He responded to every assault with "It is written." The Word is your weapon. Know it. Memorize it. Meditate on it. And wield it with confidence.

Fourth, pray always. Paul adds: "Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit." Prayer is not a separate piece of armour; it is the atmosphere in which the armour is worn. John Bunyan said that prayer is a shield to the soul, a sacrifice to God, and a scourge to the devil.

Fifth, stand. Paul's command is not advance or attack but stand. The Christian's primary posture is defensive. Christ has already won the decisive victory at the cross. The devil is a defeated enemy. Your task is not to win the war — that is already won — but to hold the ground that Christ has taken. Stand firm. Do not give up territory. Do not retreat from truths you know. Do not abandon disciplines you have established.

The Puritans found great comfort in the assurance that the battle belongs to the Lord. You do not fight alone. The Spirit fights within you. The Word fights beside you. And Christ intercedes for you at the right hand of the Father. The outcome is not in doubt.


The battle is real, but the victory is certain. Put on the armour. Wield the sword. Pray without ceasing. And stand. Lord God of hosts, clothe me in Your armour. Arm me with Your Word. Grant me the strength to stand firm until the day when every enemy is put beneath the feet of Christ. Amen.
