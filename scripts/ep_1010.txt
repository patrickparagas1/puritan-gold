PURITAN GOLD -- Study Topic: The Book of Sirach, Part 10 of 10
"A Sage's Farewell Prayer"
Sirach Chapter 51 — Ben Sira's Personal Thanksgiving and Wisdom Hymn

---

Good evening, and welcome to this final session on the Book of Sirach. Tonight we arrive at chapter 51 — the conclusion of this remarkable work. Here Ben Sira steps forward not as a teacher addressing students, but as a man standing before God. This closing chapter is intensely personal: a prayer of thanksgiving for God's deliverance, followed by an alphabetic hymn describing the sage's lifelong pursuit of wisdom, and ending with a final invitation to all who would learn. It is a fitting farewell — tender, grateful, and deeply devout.

Let us read from Sirach chapter 51, verses 1 through 12, and then verses 23 through 30:

"I will thank thee, O Lord and King, and praise thee, O God my Saviour: I do give praise unto thy name: For thou art my defender and helper, and hast preserved my body from destruction, and from the snare of the slanderous tongue, and from the lips that forge lies, and hast been mine helper against mine adversaries: And hast delivered me, according to the multitude of thy mercies and greatness of thy name, from the teeth of them that were ready to devour me, and out of the hands of such as sought after my life, and from the manifold afflictions which I had; From the choking of fire on every side, and from the midst of the fire which I kindled not; From the depth of the belly of hell, and from an unclean tongue, and from lying words."

"Come unto me, ye unlearned, and dwell in the house of learning. Wherefore are ye slow, and what say ye to these things, seeing your souls are very thirsty? I opened my mouth, and said, Buy her for yourselves without money. Put your neck under the yoke, and let your soul receive instruction: she is hard at hand to find. Behold with your eyes, how that I have had but little labour, and have gotten unto me much rest. Get learning with a great sum of money, and get much gold by her. Let your soul rejoice in his mercy, and be not ashamed of his praise. Work your work betimes, and in his time he will give you your reward."

--- Teaching ---

We began our study of Sirach with a question: who was this man, and why should we listen to him? Now, at the end, we discover the most important thing about him — he was a man who feared God, experienced God's deliverance, and devoted his entire life to the pursuit of divine wisdom. Chapter 51 opens the door to Ben Sira's soul, and what we find there is a heart overflowing with gratitude.

The thanksgiving prayer of verses 1 through 12 is one of the most moving prayers in all of ancient literature. Ben Sira had known real danger — enemies who sought his life, slanderers who tried to destroy his reputation, fires that threatened to consume him. We do not know the specific historical circumstances, but the language is vivid and urgent. He speaks of "the teeth of them that were ready to devour me" and "the depth of the belly of hell." These are not metaphors lightly chosen. Ben Sira had walked through the valley of the shadow of death, and the Lord had brought him safely through.

This is the testimony of every true believer in every age. David sang: "I sought the Lord, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears" (Psalm 34:4). Paul testified: "The Lord stood with me, and strengthened me... and I was delivered out of the mouth of the lion" (2 Timothy 4:17). John Bunyan wrote his entire autobiography under the title Grace Abounding to the Chief of Sinners — a book-length prayer of thanksgiving for God's merciful deliverance. The Christian life is, at its core, a life of grateful remembrance. We look back at the dangers God has brought us through, and we praise Him.

The second portion of chapter 51 is Ben Sira's personal account of his pursuit of wisdom. In an alphabetic hymn — each line beginning with a successive letter of the Hebrew alphabet — he describes how he sought wisdom from his youth. "When I was yet young, or ever I went abroad, I desired wisdom openly in my prayer. I prayed for her before the temple, and will seek her out even to the end" (51:13-14). Wisdom was not something he stumbled upon by accident. It was the deliberate, lifelong pursuit of his heart.

The Puritans understood this principle deeply. They called it "the improvement of time" — the deliberate use of every season of life for the acquisition of godly knowledge. Cotton Mather began his studies at age twelve. Jonathan Edwards wrote his famous resolutions at age nineteen, including: "Resolved, never to lose one moment of time; but improve it the most profitable way I possibly can." John Owen spent years in the systematic study of Scripture, producing works of theological depth that are still studied four centuries later. All of them would have resonated with Ben Sira's testimony: the pursuit of wisdom is the work of a lifetime, and it requires diligence, prayer, and surrender.

The final section of chapter 51 is Ben Sira's invitation — his last words to the world. "Come unto me, ye unlearned, and dwell in the house of learning." This is not merely a teacher's appeal for students. It is a spiritual invitation, echoing what Jesus Himself would later say: "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls" (Matthew 11:28-29). The parallels are striking. Ben Sira says "put your neck under the yoke" and "let your soul receive instruction." Jesus says "take my yoke upon you and learn of me." Both are invitations to discipleship — the willing submission of the self to the wisdom of God.

"Buy her for yourselves without money." Wisdom is free. She cannot be purchased, but neither is she cheap — she costs us our pride, our self-sufficiency, our illusion that we can navigate life without God. Isaiah would later echo this same invitation: "Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money; come ye, buy, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price" (Isaiah 55:1). The greatest gifts of God — wisdom, salvation, grace — are freely given to all who seek them.

Ben Sira's final words are a benediction and a promise: "Work your work betimes, and in his time he will give you your reward." Be faithful now. Do the work that God has placed before you. And trust that in His perfect timing, the reward will come. This is the final counsel of a sage who has walked with God through a long and faithful life. It is the counsel of a man who has tasted both suffering and deliverance, both the sweetness of wisdom and the bitterness of opposition, and who has found — at the end of all things — that the fear of the Lord truly is the beginning, the middle, and the end of wisdom.

--- Application ---

1. Write your own prayer of thanksgiving. Look back over your life and name the specific dangers, trials, and difficulties from which God has delivered you. Offer Him praise for each one.

2. Recommit yourself to the lifelong pursuit of wisdom. What book will you read next? What portion of Scripture will you study? What teacher will you learn from? Wisdom does not come by accident — it comes by diligent, prayerful pursuit.

3. Accept Ben Sira's invitation: "Put your neck under the yoke." Submit to God's instruction, even when it is difficult. The yoke of wisdom — like the yoke of Christ — is easy, and its burden is light.

4. Trust the timing of God's reward. "In his time he will give you your reward." Do not grow weary in well doing. Be faithful in the small things, and leave the results to God.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray. O Lord our God, we have walked through the Book of Sirach and heard the voice of a sage who feared Thee with all his heart. We thank Thee for the wisdom of Ben Sira — for his counsel on the fear of the Lord, the duties owed to parents, the value of friendship, the governance of the tongue, the grace of the table, the worship that pleases Thee, and the heroes of faith who have gone before us. Now, as we close this study, we echo his prayer: we thank Thee, O Lord and King, for Thy mercies, Thy deliverances, and Thy patient instruction. We come to Thy house of learning, willing to put our necks under the yoke, ready to receive wisdom without money and without price. Work Thy work in us, O God, and in Thy time, give us the reward that fadeth not away. Through Jesus Christ our Lord, in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. Amen.

And so we bid farewell to the Book of Sirach — but not to its wisdom. Go in peace, dear friend. Let the fear of the Lord be the crown upon your head. Let wisdom be the companion of your days. And let the God of Ben Sira — who is the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ — keep you in His perfect peace, now and forevermore.
