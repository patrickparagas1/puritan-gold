PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 6
"This Is a Great Mystery"
Ephesians 5:32 — Marriage and the Gospel


Welcome, dear couple. Tonight we arrive at the verse that unlocks the entire passage — the verse that reveals why Paul has been talking about marriage at all. This is the theological summit of Ephesians 5.

Our Scripture tonight is Ephesians 5:32:

"This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church."

There it is. The great mystery. Paul has not been giving mere household advice. He has been unveiling one of the deepest truths in all of Scripture.


The word Paul uses here — "mystery" — does not mean something puzzling or incomprehensible. In the New Testament, a mystery is a truth that was once hidden but has now been revealed by God. And the mystery Paul is unveiling is this: marriage was designed from the very beginning to be a picture of the relationship between Christ and His Church.

This means that when God created Adam and Eve in the Garden, when He brought the woman to the man and established the first marriage, He was not merely solving the problem of loneliness. He was creating a living parable that would one day point to the greatest love story in the universe — the love of Christ for His people.

The Puritans were captivated by this truth. John Owen wrote that the union between Christ and the Church is the original, and marriage is the copy. We do not understand Christ's love by looking at marriage; rather, we understand marriage by looking at Christ's love. Christ and the Church are the primary reality; husband and wife are the reflection.

This elevates marriage to an almost sacramental dignity. Your marriage is not merely a social arrangement, a legal contract, or a romantic partnership. It is a living, breathing testimony to the Gospel. Every time you love each other well, you proclaim the Good News. Every time you forgive each other, you demonstrate the grace of Christ. Every time you remain faithful in difficulty, you testify to the steadfast love of God.

William Gouge saw in this verse a profound motivation for marital faithfulness. If your marriage represents Christ and the Church, then unfaithfulness is not merely a betrayal of your spouse — it is a distortion of the Gospel message. And conversely, a marriage marked by sacrificial love, mutual respect, and enduring commitment is one of the most powerful evangelistic tools the world has ever seen.

Thomas Gataker observed that this mystery should fill married couples with both humility and joy. Humility, because you have been entrusted with a sacred responsibility — to display the Gospel in your daily life together. And joy, because the God who gave you this calling also gives you the grace to fulfill it. You are not left to your own resources. The same Christ whose love you are called to reflect is present in your marriage, working through you, sustaining you, perfecting you.

The Puritans also saw in this mystery a source of comfort during marital difficulty. When your marriage is hard — when communication breaks down, when wounds are fresh, when the road ahead seems long — remember that your marriage is caught up in a story far bigger than yourselves. God is at work in your struggles, not just despite them. The same Gospel that your marriage is meant to proclaim is also the power that holds your marriage together.

Tonight, let this truth redefine the way you see your marriage. You are not merely husband and wife. You are living illustrations of the love between Christ and His Church. This is a great mystery indeed.


Consider together:

Question 1: How does it change your view of marriage to know that it was designed from creation to be a picture of Christ and the Church?

Question 2: In what specific ways does your marriage currently proclaim the Gospel to those around you — your children, your neighbors, your church community?

Question 3: Is there an area of your marriage that, if strengthened, would make your Gospel witness more powerful? What would it take to begin that work?


Pray tonight in wonder at the great mystery God has entrusted to you. Ask Him to help you see your marriage through the lens of the Gospel — not merely as a human relationship, but as a sacred testimony. Pray for the grace to love each other in a way that makes the invisible love of Christ visible.


May the God who reveals mysteries and who entrusted you with this great one fill you with awe, gratitude, and renewed purpose. May your marriage be a beacon of the Gospel in a world that desperately needs to see it. Grace, mercy, and peace be with you both. Goodnight.
