PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 23
"Hope Instead of Envy"
Proverbs 23:17-18

---

Hello, beloved couple. It is a beautiful thing when husband and wife sit down together in God's Word. The Puritans called marriage a little church within the church. Tonight, your little church is in session. Welcome.

Tonight we turn to Proverbs 23:17-18. Listen with your hearts, not just your ears:

"Let not thine heart envy sinners: but be thou in the fear of the LORD all the day long. For surely there is an end; and thine expectation shall not be cut off."

That is Proverbs 23:17-18. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Matthew Henry had deep insight into the truths we have just read. The Puritans did not separate theology from daily life, and their reflections on marriage are remarkably practical.

Matthew Henry addresses a temptation that touches every believing couple at some point: the envy of sinners. 'When we see the wicked prosper in the world,' Henry writes, 'we are tempted to think they have chosen the better part.' The couple across the street who ignores God seems to have the nicer house, the easier life, the happier children. The temptation is to envy them and to question whether faithfulness to God is truly worthwhile.

Henry responds with the text: 'Be thou in the fear of the LORD all the day long.' This is not merely a command to endure; it is a command to maintain a posture of holy reverence throughout the entirety of each day. All the day long — not just on Sundays, not just in crisis moments, but from morning to evening, in every circumstance. This constant fear of the Lord is the antidote to envy because it keeps eternal realities before the eyes.

The promise in verse 18 is the anchor: 'Surely there is an end; and thine expectation shall not be cut off.' Henry explains that 'the end' refers to the final outcome, the eschatological conclusion of all things. The prosperity of the wicked has an end; the suffering of the righteous has an end. But the expectation of the godly — their hope of eternal life, of vindication, of being received into glory — that expectation shall never be cut off.

Charles Bridges adds: 'Envy is the poison of contentment. The couple who envies what others have cannot enjoy what God has given them. But the couple who lives in the fear of the Lord all the day long finds that their expectation grows brighter even as their earthly circumstances grow harder.'

--- Marriage Application ---

Envy is a subtle destroyer of marriages. It breeds discontentment: 'Why do they have a bigger house? Why is their marriage easier? Why do their children behave better?' Envy shifts your gaze from God's provision to others' possessions, and in doing so, it robs you of gratitude.

As a couple, commit to fighting envy with the fear of the Lord and the hope of His promises. When you are tempted to compare your marriage to others, remind one another: 'Surely there is an end, and our expectation shall not be cut off.' God is faithful. His promises are sure. The life of faithfulness is not always the life of ease, but it is the life of hope.

Practice gratitude together. Name your blessings. Recall God's faithfulness in the past. Fix your eyes on the eternal weight of glory that far outweighs the temporary ease of those who do not know the Lord.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

It is time for reflection. Turn to each other and consider these questions together. There is no rush. Let the conversation go wherever the Spirit leads.

Question 1: Are we envying any other couple or family, and how is that envy affecting our contentment?

Question 2: How can we practice being 'in the fear of the LORD all the day long' in our daily married life?

Question 3: What specific expectations and hopes do we hold onto together as believers?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Now let us pray together. The Puritans said that a couple who prays together is bound by cords that cannot be broken. Let us strengthen those cords tonight.

Tonight's prayer focus: Confess any envy that has crept into your hearts and ask God to replace it with contentment and hope. Pray that the fear of the Lord would govern every hour of your day, and that your expectation in Christ would be the anchor of your marriage.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the God of all hope fill your marriage with expectation that cannot be cut off, that in fearing Him all the day long, you may find joy that no envy can diminish. Amen.

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you both, tonight and always. Goodnight.
