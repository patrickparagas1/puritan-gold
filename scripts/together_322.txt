PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 22
"Training Children in the Way"
Proverbs 22:6


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to tonight's couples devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for setting aside this time to draw near to God and to each other. The moments you invest in your marriage before the Lord are among the most important moments of your day.

Let us open God's Word together. Our passage tonight comes from Proverbs 22:6:

"Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it."

That is Proverbs 22:6. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Now let us hear from Charles Bridges, one of the great Puritan voices on this subject. The Puritans wrote extensively about marriage because they saw it as one of God's most profound gifts.

This is perhaps the most frequently cited verse in Proverbs regarding parenting, and Charles Bridges handles it with great care. He notes that the word 'train' (Hebrew: chanak) originally referred to the act of a midwife rubbing the palate of a newborn to stimulate the instinct to nurse. From this root, it came to mean 'to dedicate, to initiate, to instruct from the beginning.' Bridges writes: 'Training is more than teaching. Teaching gives knowledge; training shapes character.'

The phrase 'in the way he should go' has been variously interpreted. Bridges notes that it can mean both 'according to his nature and capacity' and 'according to the way of God.' Both readings are valid and complementary. The wise parent understands the unique temperament, gifts, and weaknesses of each child, and applies the unchanging truth of God's Word in a way suited to that particular child. One-size-fits-all parenting is not what Solomon commends.

Matthew Henry adds that the promise — 'when he is old, he will not depart from it' — is a general principle, not an absolute guarantee. Henry writes: 'Good impressions made upon young people are abiding, and will ordinarily hold. Grace is most likely to take root in those who are seasoned with it betimes.' This is a promise of hope, not a blank check. Faithful parents may still grieve over wayward children, but they can trust that the seed planted in youth has a divine tendency to bear fruit.

Bridges emphasizes that this training must be a shared endeavor: 'Father and mother must be united in the training of their children. Divided counsel produces confused children. United, consistent, loving discipline, grounded in the Word of God, is the greatest gift parents can bestow.'


If you are parents, this verse calls you to a shared and sacred task. Training children in the way they should go requires both unity between you and sensitivity to each child's unique design.

Discuss together: what is 'the way' you are training your children to walk? Is it the way of academic achievement? The way of cultural respectability? Or the way of the Lord: the path of faith, repentance, obedience, and delight in God?

Training also requires consistency. Children learn not from what you say once but from what you repeat daily. Family worship, Scripture memory, consistent discipline, prayer together, modeling repentance and forgiveness — these are the tools of training. And they require a united front from both parents, working together as partners in the most important project of your lives.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Now let us pause for reflection. Here are some questions for you and your spouse to discuss together. Be honest. Be gentle. Listen more than you speak.

Question 1: Are we united in our approach to training our children, or do we contradict each other?

Question 2: What is the 'way' we are actually training our children to walk, as revealed by our daily practices?

Question 3: How well do we understand each child's unique temperament and needs, and how does that shape our training?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Let us move into a time of prayer together. If you are able, hold hands as you pray. There is something powerful about a husband and wife joining hands before the throne of grace.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray for wisdom and unity in training your children. Ask God to help you understand each child's unique design and to apply His truth faithfully to their lives. Pray for the long-term fruit of your training, trusting God with the results.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the Lord who entrusted these children to your care equip you to train them in His ways, that when they are old they may walk in the paths of righteousness. Amen.

May the Lord bless your marriage and keep it strong. May He make His face to shine upon your home, and give you peace. Goodnight, dear couple. Walk together in His love.
