PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 8
"By Night on My Bed"
Song of Solomon 3:1-5 — Seeking the One Your Soul Loves

---

Welcome, beloved couple. We enter the third chapter of the Song tonight, and the tone shifts dramatically. The bride lies awake in the night, searching for her beloved who has somehow become absent. This is a passage about the ache of disconnection and the urgency of pursuit. Every couple knows this experience. Listen carefully.

Song of Solomon 3:1-5:

"By night on my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not. I will rise now, and go about the city in the streets, and in the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not. The watchmen that go about the city found me: to whom I said, Saw ye him whom my soul loveth? Scarcely was I past from them, but I found him whom my soul loveth: I held him, and would not let him go, until I had brought him into my mother's house, and into the chamber of her that conceived me. I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, and by the hinds of the field, that ye stir not up, nor awake my love, till he please."

--- Teaching ---

"By night on my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not." There are few more painful experiences in marriage than lying next to someone who feels miles away. The bride is in bed, but the beloved is absent — not just physically, perhaps, but emotionally, spiritually. There is a distance between them that the darkness amplifies. She reaches for him and finds emptiness.

Every marriage knows nights like this. Seasons when you are together in the same house, the same room, the same bed, but separated by silence, misunderstanding, busyness, or unresolved conflict. The Puritans were remarkably honest about these seasons. Jeremiah Burroughs wrote that even the best marriages have "their winters and their nights" — periods when the warmth of connection fades and the cold of distance sets in. The question is not whether these nights will come, but what you do when they arrive.

The bride does something courageous. She does not stay in bed. "I will rise now, and go about the city in the streets, and in the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth." She gets up. She pursues. She searches. This is the picture of a spouse who refuses to accept emotional distance as the new normal. She will not simply roll over and go to sleep. She will not wait passively for things to improve. She rises, she goes out, and she seeks.

William Gouge commended this kind of active pursuit within marriage. He wrote that when distance develops between husband and wife, both have a duty to close it — but the one who first becomes aware of the distance bears the greater responsibility to act. Do not wait for your spouse to take the first step. Rise. Go. Seek.

"I sought him, but I found him not." The first attempt fails. This is important. Pursuit does not always succeed immediately. You may reach out to your spouse and be met with a cold shoulder, a deflection, a non-response. The temptation is to give up — to say, "I tried, and they did not respond, so it is their problem now." But the bride does not give up. She keeps searching.

She even asks the city watchmen for help. In practical terms, this may mean seeking counsel from wise friends, a pastor, or a counselor when you cannot bridge the gap on your own. The Puritans were not individualists when it came to marriage. Richard Baxter wrote that a couple in difficulty should seek the counsel of "godly, experienced, faithful friends" who can help them find their way back to each other. There is no shame in asking for help. The shame is in giving up the search.

"Scarcely was I past from them, but I found him whom my soul loveth." She finds him. After the long, dark night of searching, reunion comes. And notice what she does: "I held him, and would not let him go." She does not play it cool. She does not punish him for the distance with cold indifference. She grabs him and holds on. This is the posture of a heart that values connection above pride. Pride says, "Make them come to me." Love says, "I found you, and I am never letting go."

"Until I had brought him into my mother's house, and into the chamber of her that conceived me." She brings him home. She restores the intimacy. She does not leave things in the street, in the public sphere of acquaintances and watchmen. She brings the reconciliation into the most private, most intimate space she knows. Reconciliation in marriage must ultimately happen in private — in the quiet, sacred space that belongs only to the two of you.

Thomas Gataker wrote that the greatest art in marriage is the art of reconciliation. Not the art of never fighting — for that is unrealistic. Not the art of keeping the peace at any cost — for that leads to suppression. But the art of finding each other again after distance, of holding on after the night of searching, of bringing each other home again into the chamber of intimacy. This art must be practiced and perfected over a lifetime.

Tonight, if there is any distance between you — any unresolved tension, any emotional gap, any sense of separation — let this passage give you courage. Rise. Seek. Do not give up until you have found each other. And when you do, hold on and do not let go.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Turn to each other and consider these questions.

Question 1: Is there any distance between you right now — emotional, spiritual, or physical? Can you name it honestly without blame?

Question 2: When you feel disconnected from your spouse, do you tend to pursue or withdraw? What would it look like to "rise and seek" the next time distance develops?

Question 3: The bride held him and would not let him go. Is there anything keeping you from holding onto each other fully right now — pride, hurt, fear? Can you let it go tonight?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray for the courage to pursue each other through seasons of distance. Ask God to shorten the nights of searching and to make your reunions sweet. Pray that pride would never keep you from rising, seeking, and holding on.

--- Closing ---

After every dark night, there is a finding. After every search, there is a reunion. Hold onto each other tonight, dear couple, and do not let go. The God who brought you together will keep you together. Goodnight.
