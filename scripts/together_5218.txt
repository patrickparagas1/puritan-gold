PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 18
"Financial Stewardship Together"
Domestical Duties — Puritan Household Duties


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we turn to a subject that is the source of more marital conflict than perhaps any other: money. William Gouge addressed the management of household finances with great wisdom, and his counsel, though spoken four centuries ago, remains remarkably relevant.

Let us read our passage. Proverbs 3:9-10 (KJV):

"Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase: So shall thy barns be filled with plenty, and thy presses shall burst out with new wine."


William Gouge taught that the financial resources of the household belong to neither the husband alone nor the wife alone, but to the marriage. When two become one flesh, their possessions also become one. This is a radical principle that cuts against the grain of both ancient and modern customs. In Gouge's day, laws gave husbands virtually absolute control over marital finances. In our own day, many couples maintain entirely separate financial lives, treating their money as "mine and yours" rather than "ours." Gouge rejected both extremes. He believed that the husband and wife should manage their finances together, with transparency, mutual consultation, and shared purpose.

The first principle Gouge established was stewardship. Everything a couple possesses belongs to God. They are not owners but stewards. This truth, when embraced, transforms the way a couple thinks about every financial decision. The question is no longer "Can we afford this?" but "Does God want us to use His resources in this way?" The question is no longer "What do I want?" but "What is wise, what is generous, and what is faithful?"

The second principle was priority. Gouge taught, in line with Proverbs 3:9, that the first portion of every increase belongs to God. Before paying bills, before buying necessities, before setting aside savings, the godly couple gives to the Lord. This act of faith honors God and declares that He, not money, is the true source of security. Gouge observed that couples who honored God with their firstfruits consistently experienced His provision, while those who gave God the leftovers often found themselves in chronic anxiety about finances.

The third principle was transparency. Gouge strongly discouraged financial secrets between husband and wife. Hidden debts, secret purchases, undisclosed income, all of these erode trust and create division. The godly couple has open books. Both know what comes in and what goes out. Both participate in decisions about spending, saving, and giving. This does not mean that every small purchase requires a committee meeting. But it does mean that the overall financial direction of the household is a shared responsibility and a shared conversation.

The fourth principle was contentment. Gouge warned that the love of money is one of the most destructive forces in a marriage. When a couple is driven by the desire for more, they become anxious, competitive, and dissatisfied. When a couple learns contentment, finding their security in God rather than in their bank account, they experience a freedom that no amount of wealth can provide. Paul wrote to Timothy: "Godliness with contentment is great gain" (1 Timothy 6:6). This is as true in marriage as anywhere.

Richard Baxter added that financial disagreements often reveal deeper issues. The husband who spends recklessly may be seeking comfort or identity in material things. The wife who hoards anxiously may be struggling with fear and insecurity. The couple that fights about money may actually be fighting about control, trust, or values. Baxter counseled couples to look beneath the surface of their financial conflicts and address the heart issues that drive them.

Dear couple, how are your finances? Not merely in terms of dollars and cents, but in terms of stewardship, generosity, transparency, and contentment. Are you honoring the Lord with your substance? Are you managing His resources wisely and together? Tonight is a good night to have an honest conversation about money, not with anxiety, but with faith and mutual trust.


Question 1: Are we honoring the Lord with the firstfruits of our income, or are we giving Him what is left over?

Question 2: Is there full financial transparency in our marriage, or are there areas we need to bring into the open?

Question 3: Where do we fall on the spectrum between contentment and the desire for more? How can we cultivate greater contentment together?


Lord God, every good gift comes from Thee, and all that we have belongs to Thee. Teach us to be faithful stewards of the resources Thou hast entrusted to us. Help us to honor Thee with our firstfruits, to manage our finances with wisdom and transparency, and to find our contentment in Thee rather than in the abundance of our possessions. Deliver us from the love of money and from the anxiety it produces. Unite us in our financial decisions, and let our giving be generous and our spending be wise. In the name of Jesus, who though He was rich, for our sakes became poor, Amen.
