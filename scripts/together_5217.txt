PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 17
"The Wife's Wisdom and Strength"
Domestical Duties — Puritan Household Duties


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we turn to William Gouge's teaching on the wife's role in the household. If last night we saw the husband called to authority tempered by gentleness, tonight we see the wife called to a wisdom and strength that are every bit as noble and every bit as necessary.

Let us read our passage. Proverbs 14:1 (KJV):

"Every wise woman buildeth her house: but the foolish plucketh it down with her hands."


William Gouge held the wife's role in the highest esteem. Far from diminishing women, Gouge saw the wife as the very builder of the home. Drawing on Proverbs 14:1, he taught that the wise wife constructs something magnificent with her daily faithfulness, her practical wisdom, her relational skill, and her spiritual influence. The foolish wife, by contrast, tears down with her own hands what God is trying to build.

What did Gouge mean by the wife's wisdom? He meant several things. First, the wise wife knows her own worth in the eyes of God. She does not derive her identity from the world's standards of beauty, success, or status. She knows that she is a daughter of the King, created in the image of God, gifted with abilities and graces that the household desperately needs. This knowledge gives her a quiet confidence that does not need to compete with her husband but can work alongside him as a true partner.

Second, the wise wife exercises discernment in the daily management of the home. Gouge recognized that in most households, the wife bears the primary responsibility for the ordering of domestic life. She manages resources, oversees the upbringing of children, tends to the health and comfort of the family, and sets the emotional tone of the home. These are not small responsibilities. They require intelligence, creativity, endurance, and a deep dependence on God. The wife who fulfills these duties faithfully is doing a work of enormous significance, though the world may never notice.

Third, the wise wife is a counselor to her husband. Gouge was careful to distinguish between counsel and usurpation. The wise wife does not seek to take over her husband's leadership, but she does contribute her insight, her perspective, and her judgment to the decisions they make together. Gouge noted that many husbands have been saved from foolish decisions by the wisdom of their wives. The husband who refuses to listen to his wife's counsel is not exercising strong leadership; he is exercising foolish pride.

Gouge also spoke of the wife's strength. The strength he had in mind was not physical but moral and spiritual. The wife who stands firm in her faith when circumstances are difficult, who maintains her composure when the household is in chaos, who continues to love and serve when she receives little in return, is a woman of extraordinary strength. Proverbs 31:25 says of the virtuous woman: "Strength and honour are her clothing; and she shall rejoice in time to come." This is the strength that Gouge commended.

But Gouge was equally honest about the ways in which the wife can tear down her house. A nagging tongue, a discontented spirit, a habit of criticizing her husband in public, a refusal to trust God's provision, all of these, Gouge warned, are like hands that pull apart the very structure of the home. The foolish wife does not need an enemy from outside to destroy her household; she does it herself with her own attitudes, words, and choices.

Dear wife, receive Gouge's teaching as an encouragement and a challenge. You are a builder. Every day, with every word and every choice, you are constructing something that will outlast you. Build wisely. Build with faith, with patience, with love, and with the strength that comes from the Lord. And dear husband, honor the wisdom and strength of your wife. She is your greatest ally, your most trusted counselor, and the builder of your home. Treat her accordingly.


Question 1: Wife, in what areas do you feel most confident in your role as the builder of your home? Where do you feel you need to grow?

Question 2: Husband, do you actively seek and honor your wife's wisdom and counsel? How can you do so more intentionally?

Question 3: Are there any attitudes, words, or habits in our marriage that are tearing down rather than building up? How can we address them together?


Heavenly Father, we thank Thee for the wisdom and strength Thou hast given to the wife. We praise Thee for her faithfulness in building the home, in managing its affairs, and in setting its emotional and spiritual tone. Strengthen her for this holy work. Give her wisdom for every decision, patience for every trial, and joy in every season. Help the husband to honor and support her in this calling. May our home be built upon the rock of Thy Word, and may the hands that build it be guided by Thy Spirit. In Christ's name, Amen.
