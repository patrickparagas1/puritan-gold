PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"Unto Thee I Lift Up My Soul"
May 5, 2026

---

"Unto thee, O Lord, do I lift up my soul. O my God, I trust in thee: let me not be ashamed, let not mine enemies triumph over me. Yea, let none that wait on thee be ashamed: let them be ashamed which transgress without cause. Shew me thy ways, O Lord; teach me thy paths. Lead me in thy truth, and teach me: for thou art the God of my salvation; on thee do I wait all the day." -- Psalm 25:1-5

In this psalm we find David in a posture that every believer must learn if he is to walk safely through the perils of this present world. He is on his knees, lifting up his soul to God. Not merely his voice, not merely his hands, but his soul, the whole inward man, with all its desires, all its fears, all its hopes, and all its affections. This is no formal or mechanical prayer. It is the outpouring of a heart that knows its own weakness and casts itself entirely upon the strength of God.

"Unto thee, O Lord, do I lift up my soul." The natural tendency of the fallen heart is to sink downward. Like water, it flows to the lowest place. The cares of this world, the deceitfulness of riches, the lusts of other things, all conspire to drag the soul earthward, to fix its attention upon things below, to make it forget the heavenly country toward which it is bound. And therefore the act of lifting up the soul to God is an act that requires deliberate effort, enabled by the Holy Spirit. It is a conscious turning away from the gravitational pull of sin and self, and a conscious turning toward the face of God in humble dependence.

David continues, "O my God, I trust in thee: let me not be ashamed." To trust in God is to rest the entire weight of one's life upon His character and His promises. It is to say, in the face of every contrary appearance, "God is faithful. God is good. God is wise. God is powerful. And He will not suffer me to be put to shame." The Hebrew word for trust carries the sense of taking refuge, as a man fleeing from a storm takes refuge in a strong tower. David was a man who knew what it meant to flee. He had fled from Saul through the wilderness of Judea. He had hidden in caves and among the rocks. He had known the terror of being hunted by a powerful enemy. And in all these experiences, he had learned that there is no refuge so strong, no hiding place so secure, as the name of the Lord.

"Shew me thy ways, O Lord; teach me thy paths." Here is the prayer of a teachable heart, and it is a prayer that the Lord delights to answer. The man who comes to God with a fixed determination to go his own way will receive no guidance from heaven. But the man who comes with a genuine willingness to be taught, to be corrected, to be led wherever the Lord sees fit to lead him, that man shall never lack direction. The ways of God are not our ways. They often run contrary to human wisdom. They frequently lead through valleys that our flesh would never choose to enter. But they always, without exception, lead to blessing.

John Flavel, that beloved Puritan minister who laboured so faithfully in the seaport town of Dartmouth, had a saying that has comforted many a perplexed believer: "The providence of God is like Hebrew words; it can be read only backwards." How many times have we looked back upon some trial, some disappointment, some painful turn in the road, and seen at last the wisdom and the goodness of God in leading us by that very path! At the time, it seemed cruel and meaningless. But in retrospect, we can see that the Lord was teaching us lessons we could have learned in no other school, and bringing us to places of blessing we could have reached by no other road.

"Lead me in thy truth, and teach me: for thou art the God of my salvation; on thee do I wait all the day." Here David identifies the substance of the guidance he seeks. It is not merely practical direction, though that is included. It is truth. Lead me in thy truth. The soul that is led by God's truth is a soul that walks upon solid ground. It is not tossed about by every wind of doctrine. It is not deceived by the plausible lies of the world, the flesh, and the devil. It has a fixed point of reference, an infallible standard by which to judge every situation and every decision.

And then David adds a phrase of extraordinary significance: "On thee do I wait all the day." Not merely in the morning, when the soul is fresh and the world has not yet pressed in with its distractions. Not merely in the evening, when the day's work is done and there is leisure for reflection. But all the day. Every hour. Every moment. The waiting upon God is not a seasonal exercise but a continual posture of the soul. It is the habitual looking upward, the constant dependence, the unbroken communion with the Almighty that characterizes the mature believer.

Later in this psalm, David writes, "The secret of the Lord is with them that fear him; and he will shew them his covenant." What a remarkable promise this is! The secret of the Lord. There is an intimacy between God and those who fear Him that the world can neither understand nor experience. God takes His children into His confidence. He reveals to them the deep things of His Word that remain hidden from the wise and prudent of this world. He shows them His covenant, not merely as a legal document, but as a living relationship, as the bond of love between a faithful God and His redeemed people.

Thomas Manton, that great expositor of the Puritan era, observed that the fear of the Lord is not a cringing, servile terror, but a holy reverence, a filial awe, that draws the soul nearer to God rather than driving it away. The child who truly reverences his father does not hide from him but runs to him. And the believer who truly fears the Lord does not shrink from His presence but longs for it above all things.

Let this psalm teach us the blessed art of waiting upon God. In a world that prizes speed and self-reliance, the call to wait upon the Lord sounds like foolishness. But those who have practised it know that it is the highest wisdom. For the soul that waits upon God is never disappointed. The promise is sure: "Let none that wait on thee be ashamed." The Lord may delay, but He does not deny. He may try our patience, but He never mocks our faith. And in His own time, which is always the right time, He answers the soul that lifts itself to Him with all the riches of His grace. Amen.
