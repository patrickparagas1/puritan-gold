PURITAN GOLD — Episode 25
"The Doctrine of Election"
Week 4, Day 4 (Thursday) — Theological Reflection from A.W. Pink

---

Let me ask you something — and I want you to sit with this question honestly before you answer it. Why are you a Christian? I don't mean what church you attend, or what theological tradition you identify with. I mean... why are you a believer? What made the difference between you and someone who heard the same gospel and walked away unmoved?

Was it your intelligence? Your moral superiority? Your spiritual sensitivity? Was it because you were somehow better, wiser, more open-minded than the person sitting next to you who never believed?

If you're honest — truly, deeply honest — the answer is no. A thousand times no. And the Bible tells us why. The reason you believe is because God chose you. Before you were born. Before the world was made. Before time itself began, the living God set His love upon you and determined that you would be His.

That's the doctrine of election. And today on Puritan Gold, we're going to explore it — carefully, reverently, and with the help of one of the twentieth century's most uncompromising Reformed writers: Arthur Walkington Pink.

---

A.W. Pink was born in Nottingham, England in 1886 and died in relative obscurity in 1952 on the Isle of Lewis, off the coast of Scotland. During his lifetime, his work was largely ignored. His monthly publication, Studies in the Scriptures, had only a small circulation — at times fewer than a thousand subscribers. He pastored churches in America and Australia, but never achieved the fame or influence of a Spurgeon or a Lloyd-Jones.

And yet... after his death, his writings were discovered by a new generation. Books like The Sovereignty of God, The Attributes of God, and his massive exposition of the Gospel of John found audiences that Pink himself never lived to see. Today he's recognized as one of the most important Reformed writers of the twentieth century.

And there's a quiet providence in that, isn't there? A man who wrote about God's sovereign purposes was himself an example of them. God's timing is not ours.

---

Now, Pink wrote extensively about election — the doctrine that God, from before the foundation of the world, chose certain individuals to be saved. Not because of anything in them. Not because He foresaw their faith. But simply because it pleased Him to do so, according to the good pleasure of His will.

Let's look at the biblical foundation. Ephesians 1:4 says: "He chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and blameless before him."

Before the foundation of the world. Before you took your first breath. Before the stars were flung into space. God chose you. In Christ. For holiness. Not because you were holy — but so that you might become holy.

And Romans 8:29-30 gives us what theologians call the "golden chain of salvation": "For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, in order that he might be the firstborn among many brothers. And those whom he predestined he also called, and those whom he called he also justified, and those whom he justified he also glorified."

Look at that chain. Foreknown. Predestined. Called. Justified. Glorified. Every link is forged by God. Not one of them depends on human effort or human decision. It's all of grace. From beginning to end.

Pink, in The Sovereignty of God, wrote: "God is sovereign in the exercise of His love. God does not love everybody. If He did, then He would love the Devil. But God does not love the Devil; rather, He hates him with a perfect hatred." Now, that's strong language. And Pink was not afraid of strong language. But his point is important: God's love is not a sentimental, undiscriminating feeling. It's a purposeful, electing love. It's a love that chooses. And the wonder is not that God chooses some — the wonder is that He chooses any.

---

Now, I want to anchor this in the Proverbs passage we're studying this week. Proverbs 16:4 says: "The Lord has made everything for its purpose, even the wicked for the day of trouble."

This is a verse that many people try to soften or explain away. But Solomon states it plainly: God has made everything for its purpose. Everything. He is not a God who reacts to events. He is a God who ordains them. His sovereignty extends not just to the pleasant things — but to all things. Even the difficult. Even the mysterious. Even the things we cannot fully understand.

Pink commented on this kind of sovereign purposefulness when he wrote: "To say that God the Father has decreed all things that come to pass is to state a truth which few really believe, for the majority of professing Christians would have us believe that God's will is thwarted by the creature." And he's right. We pay lip service to sovereignty, but when it comes to the hard doctrines — election, predestination, God's absolute control over all events — we quietly retreat to a god who merely observes and responds. But that's not the God of the Bible. The God of the Bible reigns.

---

Now... I know what some of you may be thinking. This sounds harsh. This sounds unfair. How can God choose some and not others? How is that just?

And I want to address that honestly, because it's a question that every honest Christian has wrestled with. Paul anticipated this objection in Romans 9. After laying out the doctrine of election, he writes: "You will say to me then, 'Why does he still find fault? For who can resist his will?' But who are you, O man, to answer back to God? Will what is molded say to its molder, 'Why have you made me like this?'"

Paul doesn't give a philosophical answer. He gives a doxological one. He points us to the majesty of God. Who are we to question the Potter? Not because our questions are sinful — but because our minds are too small to contain the fullness of God's wisdom.

And here's the thing that Pink and the Puritans and the Reformed tradition have always emphasized: the real question isn't "Why didn't God choose everyone?" The real question is "Why did God choose anyone?" Because none of us deserve it. Not one. We are all — every last one of us — rebels against a holy God. We have all sinned and fallen short of His glory. If God gave us what we deserve, we would all perish.

So when God, in His sovereign mercy, reaches down and saves a sinner... that's not injustice. That's grace. Pure, unmerited, astonishing grace.

---

And this is where the doctrine of election becomes deeply practical. Because if you understand it rightly, it doesn't produce pride. It produces humility.

Pink himself addressed this. He wrote that the doctrine of election, rightly understood, "lays the axe at the root of all human boasting." You can't boast in your own faith, because your faith was a gift. You can't boast in your own decision, because God decided first. You can't look down on the unbeliever, because apart from grace, you would be exactly where they are.

The Puritan Thomas Goodwin put it memorably when he said that election is "the highest act of God's free grace, and the deepest humiliation of the creature." The higher you go in understanding God's sovereignty, the lower you go in your estimation of yourself. And that's exactly where God wants you — low before Him, empty of self, and full of wonder.

And election produces gratitude. If you understand that your salvation was planned before the world began... if you understand that God set His love on you when there was nothing in you to attract that love... then the only appropriate response is overwhelmed, speechless, tearful gratitude. "Why me, Lord? Why me?"

You'll never exhaust that question. And you'll never exhaust the gratitude that flows from it.

---

Let me say a word here about how election relates to evangelism and the free offer of the gospel — because this is a place where many people stumble.

Some say, "If God has already chosen who will be saved, why bother preaching? Why share the gospel at all?"

Pink answered this, and so did Spurgeon, and so did Calvin, and so did every faithful Reformed theologian in history: election does not eliminate the need for gospel preaching. It guarantees its success.

Think about it. If salvation depended entirely on human persuasion and human willpower, we'd have reason to despair. Hearts are hard. Minds are darkened. People are dead in sin. What chance does a preacher have against all that?

But because God has elected a people — because He has determined to save them — the gospel goes forth with power. The preacher sows the seed, and God gives the growth. The evangelist casts the net, and God fills it. Election doesn't make evangelism pointless. It makes evangelism hopeful.

Spurgeon said it best: "If God had painted a yellow stripe on the backs of the elect, I'd go around lifting up coattails. But since He hasn't, I must preach the gospel to every creature." The free offer goes to all. The effectual call comes to those whom God has chosen. And the preacher's job is to be faithful — and to leave the results to God.

---

So... where does this leave you today?

If you're a believer — if you've trusted in Christ, if you love His Word, if you long for His presence — then hear this: you were chosen. Before the foundation of the world. Not because you deserved it. Not because you earned it. But because God, in His infinite and mysterious love, set His heart on you.

Let that sink in. The Creator of the universe... the God who spoke galaxies into existence... looked across the vast expanse of eternity and said, "That one. That one is mine."

And if you're listening to this and you're not sure where you stand with God... if you're uncertain, if you're seeking, if you're drawn but haven't yet fully come — then hear this: the very fact that you're drawn is evidence of grace at work. Dead hearts don't seek God. The fact that you want to know Him is a sign that He is already at work in you. Come to Christ. Come today. You will not be turned away. John 6:37 — "All that the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never cast out."

The door is open. The table is set. And the Shepherd is calling.

Let's pray.

Father, we are undone by the doctrine of election. Not because it is harsh — but because it is kind. Not because it diminishes us — but because it magnifies You. We did not choose You. You chose us. And we are staggered by that grace. Humble us, Lord. Take away every trace of pride and self-reliance. Fill us with the kind of gratitude that can only come from knowing we were loved before we existed. And give us boldness to proclaim this gospel to every creature — trusting that Your sovereign purposes will not fail. In the name of Christ, our chosen and beloved Savior, we pray. Amen.
