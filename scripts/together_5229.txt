PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 29
"Summer Love: Keeping Romance Alive"
Song of Solomon — The Beauty of Marital Affection

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. As June draws to a close and summer stretches before us, tonight we turn to a book of the Bible that many Christians neglect but that the Puritans cherished: the Song of Solomon. Contrary to popular misconception, the Puritans were not cold or passionless about romantic love within marriage. They celebrated it as a gift of God, and they encouraged married couples to cultivate affection, delight, and even playful love throughout every season of their life together.

Let us read our passage. Song of Solomon 2:10-13 (KJV):

"My beloved spake, and said unto me, Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away. For, lo, the winter is past, the rain is over and gone; The flowers appear on the earth; the time of the singing of birds is come, and the voice of the turtle is heard in our land; The fig tree putteth forth her green figs, and the vines with the tender grape give a good smell. Arise, my love, my fair one, and come away."

--- Teaching ---

What a magnificent invitation. The beloved calls to his bride: Come away with me. Winter is past. Spring has arrived. The earth is alive with beauty and fragrance, and I want to share it with you. This is not the language of mere duty or obligation. This is the language of delight, of desire, of a love that finds its joy not in solitude but in the presence of the beloved.

The Puritans understood that romantic love within marriage is not a worldly indulgence but a sacred gift. Richard Baxter wrote with surprising warmth on this subject, urging husbands and wives to maintain their affection for each other with deliberate intentionality. He warned that the pressures of daily life, the demands of work, the cares of raising children, and the weariness of the body conspire to drain the romance from marriage if it is not actively cultivated. Love that is not tended grows cold, he cautioned, not because the love was never real but because it was never nourished.

Thomas Watson spoke of the love between husband and wife as a fire that must be continually fed. He observed that many couples who began their marriage with great warmth and tenderness allowed the fire to diminish over the years until nothing remained but cold ashes. Watson urged married couples to add fuel to the fire regularly, through kind words, through thoughtful gestures, through time spent together without distraction, and through the physical affection that God designed to strengthen the marriage bond.

William Gouge, in his treatment of the common duties of husband and wife, listed the cultivation of mutual delight as a genuine obligation, not merely a recommendation. He argued that God intends for husband and wife to find pleasure in each other and that the neglect of this pleasure is a form of unfaithfulness. This is a remarkable statement from a Puritan divine, and it reminds us that the Puritans had a far richer and more joyful view of marriage than they are often credited with.

The Song of Solomon teaches us that romance is not merely for the young. It is for every season. The invitation to "arise" and "come away" is an invitation that should be renewed throughout the years of marriage. Summer, with its longer days and warmer evenings, offers natural opportunities for couples to step away from routine and rediscover the delight they once found in each other's company. Take a walk together at sunset. Sit on the porch and talk as you did when you were courting. Laugh together. Pray together. Remember why you fell in love, and let those memories kindle fresh affection.

Dear husband, be the one who issues the invitation tonight. Call your wife away from the demands and distractions of daily life. Let her know that she is still your beloved, that your desire for her company has not diminished with the passing of years.

Dear wife, respond to the invitation with gladness. Do not let weariness or resentment close your heart to the love your husband offers. The winter may have been long, but spring is here. Let the flowers of renewed affection bloom between you.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: When was the last time we deliberately set aside time for romance, for being together without distraction or agenda? How can we create more of those moments this summer?

Question 2: What does each of you find most romantic? Has this changed over the years? Share honestly what makes you feel loved and desired.

Question 3: Are there any barriers to affection in our marriage right now, unresolved conflicts, weariness, or emotional distance? How can we address them together?

--- Prayer ---

O God of love, who hast given us the gift of marital affection and delight, forgive us for the times we have neglected this precious gift. Rekindle the fire of love between us. Teach us to delight in one another as Thou dost delight in Thy people. Give us the courage to be vulnerable, the generosity to be attentive, and the wisdom to tend the garden of our romance with fresh intentionality. As summer unfolds before us, may our love also unfold in new and beautiful ways. Through Christ, who loved His Bride and gave Himself for her, Amen.
