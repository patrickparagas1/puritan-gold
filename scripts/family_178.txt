PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 17
"Searching the Scriptures Together"
Matthew Henry — Family Worship and Bible Study

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Matthew Henry teaches us how to read the Bible together in a way that transforms hearts and minds — not merely as information, but as the living voice of God. Let us come before the Lord.

Let us pray. Lord, open our eyes that we may behold wondrous things out of Your law. Give us not only the discipline to read Your Word but the delight to treasure it. Make the Scriptures come alive in our home tonight. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Acts 17:10-11. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"And the brethren immediately sent away Paul and Silas by night unto Berea: who coming thither went into the synagogue of the Jews. These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so."

That is Acts 17:10-11. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

The Bereans hold a special place in the hearts of all who love the Bible. Luke praises them as "more noble" than the Thessalonians — and the reason for their nobility was not their wealth, their education, or their social standing, but their attitude toward Scripture. They "received the word with all readiness of mind" — they were eager, open, and hungry for truth. And they "searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so" — they tested everything they heard against the standard of God's Word.

Matthew Henry loved the Bereans. He saw in them the model for every Christian family. A family that reads the Bible together is good. But a family that searches the Bible together — that digs into its depths, asks questions, compares passages, and seeks to understand not just the words but the meaning — that family is building on the surest possible foundation.

Henry offers practical counsel for searching the Scriptures as a family. His first recommendation is to read with attention. Do not rush through the passage as though checking an item off a list. Read slowly. Read aloud. Pause at words and phrases that strike you. Let the text breathe. Henry observed that many Christians read the Bible the way they read a newspaper — quickly, superficially, looking for the main headline and then moving on. But the Bible is not a newspaper. It is the Word of the living God, and every sentence repays careful, thoughtful, attentive reading.

His second recommendation is to read with questions. When you come to a passage, ask: Who is speaking? To whom? In what circumstances? What is the main point? Are there any commands to obey? Any promises to claim? Any warnings to heed? Any examples to follow? These questions turn passive reading into active engagement. They draw the reader into dialogue with the text, and they make the reading a family conversation rather than a monologue.

Henry's third recommendation is to read with comparison. Scripture interprets Scripture. When you encounter a difficult passage, look for other passages that address the same theme. When you find a promise in one place, look for its fulfillment in another. When you meet a character in one book, trace their story through others. This practice of comparing Scripture with Scripture is one of the most fruitful habits a Bible reader can develop, and it is especially rewarding when practiced as a family, with different members contributing their observations and discoveries.

His fourth recommendation is to read with prayer. Henry repeats this counsel so often that it clearly lay at the very center of his convictions about Bible study. Before you open the Bible, pray for the Holy Spirit to illuminate the text. As you read, pray for understanding. After you close the Bible, pray for the grace to obey what you have learned. The same Spirit who inspired the Scriptures must illuminate the Scriptures, or they will remain a closed book no matter how often we read them.

Henry also commends the practice of memorizing Scripture. He writes that the Word of God hidden in the heart is a treasury that can never be stolen, a weapon that can never be lost, and a comfort that can never be taken away. He encouraged families to memorize passages together — beginning with simple verses and gradually working up to longer passages. Children especially, he noted, have a remarkable capacity for memorization, and verses learned in childhood often remain with a person for the rest of their life.

What would it look like for our family to become Bereans? It would mean reading our Bibles not just dutifully but eagerly. It would mean asking questions, digging deeper, comparing passages, and refusing to be satisfied with a surface understanding. It would mean praying before, during, and after every reading. And it would mean hiding God's Word in our hearts so that it becomes a part of us — shaping our thoughts, guiding our decisions, and sustaining our faith in every season of life.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: The Bereans were called "more noble" because they searched the Scriptures daily. What makes daily Bible reading so important? What happens when we neglect it?

Question 2: Henry suggests reading with questions: Who is speaking? What is the main point? Are there commands or promises? Let us try this with tonight's passage from Acts 17.

Question 3: What is one passage of Scripture our family could memorize together this month?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee." -- Psalm 119:11

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Holy Spirit, You who inspired the Scriptures, illuminate them for us. Give us the mind of the Bereans — eager, attentive, searching, faithful. Help us to read Your Word daily, to study it carefully, to memorize it diligently, and to obey it wholeheartedly. Make the Bible the dearest book in our home. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
