PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"In Thee, O Lord, Do I Put My Trust"
June 14, 2026

---

"In thee, O Lord, do I put my trust; let me never be ashamed: deliver me in thy righteousness. Bow down thine ear to me; deliver me speedily: be thou my strong rock, for an house of defence to save me. For thou art my rock and my fortress; therefore for thy name's sake lead me, and guide me. Pull me out of the net that they have laid privily for me: for thou art my strength." -- Psalm 31:1-4

The language of this psalm is the language of a man who has been driven to the end of his own resources and has discovered that the end of self is the beginning of God. David is hemmed in on every side. His enemies have laid nets for him in secret, conspiring to catch him as a hunter catches a wild animal. And in this extremity he does the only thing that a believing soul can do: he flings himself upon the mercy and the might of his covenant God. "In thee, O Lord, do I put my trust."

Trust is the hinge upon which the whole of the Christian life turns. Without trust, prayer is a meaningless ritual, worship is an empty form, and obedience is a grudging submission. But with trust, prayer becomes the confident petition of a child to a loving Father, worship becomes the adoring response of a heart that has tasted and seen that the Lord is good, and obedience becomes the willing service of a soul that knows its Master is wise and kind and faithful. And this trust is not a vague, general optimism about the way things will turn out. It is a specific, personal reliance upon a specific, personal God who has revealed Himself in His Word and who has bound Himself by covenant to be the rock and fortress of His people.

"Let me never be ashamed." This is a petition that touches the honour of God Himself. For if those who trust in the Lord are put to shame, then the trustworthiness of God is called into question. Every time a believer is vindicated, God is glorified. Every time a prayer is answered, the faithfulness of God is demonstrated. And every time a saint is delivered from his enemies, the world is given fresh evidence that the God of Israel is not a deaf idol or an absent landlord, but a living, active, powerful Redeemer who keeps His promises.

Thomas Watson, in his exposition of the Lord's Prayer, taught that the plea "for thy name's sake" is the most powerful argument that a believer can bring before the throne of grace. When David says, "For thy name's sake lead me, and guide me," he is not merely asking for a personal favour. He is reminding God -- if we may speak with reverence -- that His own reputation is at stake in the outcome. If David perishes, the enemies of God will say that the Lord was unable to protect His servant. If David is led astray, the scoffers will say that the God of Israel is no better than the gods of the nations. And therefore David appeals not to his own worthiness but to the honour of God's name, knowing that God will never suffer His own glory to be tarnished.

"Be thou my strong rock, for an house of defence to save me." There is a multiplying of metaphors here that reveals the intensity of David's need. God is his rock -- solid, immovable, unshakeable. God is his house of defence -- a fortified dwelling where the enemy cannot reach him. God is his fortress -- a military stronghold built to withstand the fiercest assault. Each metaphor adds another layer of security, another dimension of protection, until the picture is one of absolute, impregnable safety in the arms of the Almighty.

John Flavel delighted in tracing the various names and titles by which God is described in Scripture, and he found in each one a fresh vein of comfort for the believing heart. When God is called a rock, the believer knows that his foundation is sure. When God is called a fortress, he knows that his defence is impenetrable. When God is called a shield, he knows that the fiery darts of the enemy cannot touch him. And when all these titles are gathered together and laid upon the heart of the afflicted saint, they produce a confidence that no earthly power can shake.

"Pull me out of the net that they have laid privily for me." This is a vivid image of the secret machinations of the wicked. They do not attack openly, where David might see them and prepare his defence. They work in the shadows, laying their traps in hidden places, weaving their conspiracies in the darkness, hoping to ensnare the righteous before he is aware of the danger. But David knows that the God who sees in secret can deliver from secret snares. The net that is invisible to human eyes is as plain as day to the eyes of the Almighty, and He can pull His servant out of it as easily as a man pulls a thread from his garment.

Stephen Charnock, in his monumental work upon the attributes of God, demonstrated that the omniscience of God is the terror of the wicked and the comfort of the righteous. The wicked lay their nets in secret, but God sees them. They whisper their plots in the darkness, but God hears them. They imagine that because no human eye has witnessed their treachery, they are safe. But there is an Eye that never closes and an Ear that never sleeps, and the day will come when every secret thing shall be brought to light.

Let us therefore put our trust in the Lord with renewed confidence. He is our rock, our fortress, our house of defence. For His name's sake He will lead us and guide us. He will pull us out of every net that the enemy has laid for us, and He will never suffer those who trust in Him to be put to shame. Amen.
