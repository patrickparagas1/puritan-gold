PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 20
"Speaking Truth in Your Home"
Romans for Marriage — Day 20

---

Good evening, dear couple. The Puritans believed that marriage was one of the greatest means of grace God provides. Tonight we enter into that grace together. Let us open our hearts and our ears to what the Lord has for us.

As we settle in together, let us hear from Romans 10:9-10:

"That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved. For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation."

That is Romans 10:9-10. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

One of the gifts of the Christian tradition is that we do not study alone. We have the wisdom of the saints who came before us. Tonight we hear from William Gouge.

Paul links two essential realities: heart belief and mouth confession. Faith is not merely internal — it must be expressed. What you believe in your heart must be spoken with your mouth. Salvation requires both dimensions: inward conviction and outward declaration. You cannot have one without the other.

William Gouge, whose Of Domestical Duties remains one of the most comprehensive Puritan works on marriage and family, taught that the principle of heart-and-mouth integrity applies directly to the Christian home. What is believed must be spoken. What is felt must be expressed. What is true must be declared.

Many marriages suffer not from a lack of love but from a lack of verbal expression. The husband loves his wife deeply but never says it. The wife respects her husband profoundly but never tells him. Both carry affection in their hearts but fail to confess it with their mouths. This disconnect between heart and mouth creates an emotional vacuum that breeds doubt, insecurity, and loneliness.

Gouge counseled couples to practice regular verbal affirmation — not as a technique but as an overflow of genuine heart conviction. If you believe your spouse is a gift from God, say it. If you are thankful for their faithfulness, declare it. If you love them, confess it with your mouth. Daily. Specifically. Repeatedly.

But the principle extends beyond affirmation. Confession also means honesty. Speaking truth in your home means being honest about your struggles, your failures, and your needs. It means creating an atmosphere where truth is welcomed rather than feared. A home where truth is spoken in love is a home where trust deepens and intimacy grows. A home where truth is suppressed is a home where distance grows and walls build.

Gouge also taught that confession includes the regular practice of confessing sin to each other. When you have wronged your spouse, confess it. Do not wait to be caught. Do not minimize it. Confess it with your mouth, as freely as you confess Christ. A marriage where both spouses practice regular confession is a marriage that stays clean, current, and close.

--- Marriage Application ---

How well do you speak truth in your home? Do you regularly express love, gratitude, and affirmation? Do you honestly share your struggles and needs? Do you quickly confess when you have done wrong?

Tonight, practice heart-and-mouth alignment. Each of you share something you genuinely believe about your spouse but have not said recently. It might be: 'I admire your patience with the children.' Or: 'I am grateful that you work so hard for our family.' Or simply: 'I love you, and I do not say it enough.'

Then ask: Is there anything I need to confess — any wrong I have done, any truth I have been withholding? Speak it gently, receive it graciously, and let truth clean the air between you.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Discussion time. Husband, listen to your wife. Wife, listen to your husband. Let this be a safe and sacred space.

Question 1: Are you better at believing in your heart or confessing with your mouth? Where do you need to grow?

Question 2: What truth about your spouse have you been carrying in your heart but not expressing?

Question 3: Is your home a place where truth is welcomed or feared? What can you do to make it safer for honesty?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Let us close our eyes and open our hearts to the Lord in prayer. Husband, pray for your wife. Wife, pray for your husband. There is no greater gift you can give each other.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray for the courage to speak truth in your home — truth of affirmation, truth of confession, and truth of honesty. Ask God to align your hearts and your mouths so that what you believe is freely spoken.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May your home be filled with spoken love, honest confession, and gentle truth. May what you believe in your hearts flow freely from your lips, building a marriage rooted in authenticity and grace.

Go in peace, husband and wife. The God who ordained your marriage is faithful to sustain it. Trust Him. Love each other. And come back tomorrow for more. Goodnight.
