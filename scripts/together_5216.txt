PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 16
"The Husband's Authority and Gentleness"
Domestical Duties — Puritan Household Duties


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we continue with William Gouge's teaching on household duties, and we come to a subject that requires great care: the husband's authority. Gouge treated this subject with remarkable balance, insisting that authority without gentleness is a perversion of God's design.

Let us read our passage. Colossians 3:19 (KJV):

"Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter against them."


William Gouge devoted substantial attention to the husband's role as head of the household. He affirmed, on the basis of Scripture, that God has given the husband a position of authority in the home. But Gouge was equally emphatic that this authority is not a license for tyranny. It is a calling to servant leadership, patterned after the Lord Jesus Christ, who led by washing feet and by laying down His life.

Gouge identified a grave error common among husbands of his day, and one equally common in ours: the confusion of authority with harshness. Some men, when they hear that God has given them headship, take it as permission to rule with an iron fist. They issue commands without consultation. They make decisions without considering their wife's wisdom. They use their physical or positional strength to dominate rather than to serve. Gouge called this a grievous sin against the very nature of the authority God has bestowed.

True authority, Gouge taught, is always accompanied by gentleness. The apostle Paul makes this explicit in Colossians 3:19: "Be not bitter against them." The word "bitter" speaks of harshness, sourness, a sharp and cutting spirit. It is the opposite of the nourishing and cherishing love that Ephesians 5 requires. The husband who is bitter against his wife poisons his own authority. His wife may obey outwardly, but she will not follow gladly. She will not open her heart to a man whose leadership is marked by roughness and resentment.

Gouge counseled husbands to exercise their authority with what he called "mildness." This does not mean weakness. Mildness is strength under control. It is the strength of the man who could raise his voice but chooses to speak softly. It is the strength of the man who could make a unilateral decision but chooses to seek his wife's counsel. It is the strength of the man who could demand submission but instead earns it through consistent love, wisdom, and integrity.

Gouge also emphasized that the husband's authority is not absolute. It is authority under authority. The husband is head of the wife, but Christ is head of the husband. When a husband exercises his authority in a way that contradicts the Word of God, he has exceeded his bounds. The wife is never required to follow her husband into sin. And the husband who leads his wife away from God rather than toward Him will answer to a higher Authority.

Richard Baxter added that the gentle husband is the strong husband. The world tells men that gentleness is weakness, that real men are tough, unyielding, and dominant. But the strongest man who ever lived, the Lord Jesus Christ, described Himself as meek and lowly in heart. He led with tenderness, with patience, with an unfailing concern for the well-being of those under His care. The husband who follows Christ's example will find that his wife follows him willingly, and that his home becomes a place of peace rather than tension.

Dear husband, examine yourself in the light of Gouge's teaching. Have you confused authority with harshness? Do you lead with gentleness or with bitterness? Does your wife feel safe under your leadership, or does she walk on eggshells? The authority God has given you is a sacred trust. Use it to build up, not to tear down. Use it to serve, not to be served. Use it to draw your wife closer to Christ, not to drive her further away.

Dear wife, pray for your husband tonight. His calling is weighty. He needs your support, your encouragement, and your honest feedback. Help him to see where his leadership is strong and where it needs to grow. And when he leads well, tell him. Affirmation from a wife is one of the most powerful forces in a husband's life.


Question 1: Husband, in what areas do you think you lead with gentleness, and where might you be prone to harshness or bitterness?

Question 2: Wife, do you feel safe and valued under your husband's leadership? Can you share honestly what helps you and what hurts you?

Question 3: How can we work together to make the husband's leadership in our home more Christlike?


Lord Jesus, Thou who art meek and lowly in heart, teach the husband in this marriage to lead with Thy gentleness. Forgive him for every moment of harshness, bitterness, or domination. Give him the strength that is found in mildness and the authority that is earned through love. Help the wife to support his leadership with honesty and encouragement. May our home be governed not by the harshness of fallen human nature but by the gentle authority of Christ. In Thy name we pray, Amen.
