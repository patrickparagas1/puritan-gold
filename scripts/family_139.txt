PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 8
"What Is Contentment?"
Pilgrim Families — Week 2, Day 1

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to tonight's family devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for gathering together around God's Word. Let us quiet our hearts and prepare to listen to what the Lord has for us.

Let us begin with prayer. Heavenly Father, we thank You for this family. We thank You for bringing us safely through another day. Now we ask that You would open our eyes to see wonderful things in Your Word. Speak to each heart gathered here, from the oldest to the youngest. In Jesus' name we pray. Amen.

Tonight we open our Bibles to Philippians 4:11-13; 1 Timothy 6:6-8. Listen carefully as I read our passage:

"Not that I speak in respect of want: for I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content. I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound: every where and in all things I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me. But godliness with contentment is great gain. For we brought nothing into this world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out. And having food and raiment let us be therewith content."

That is Philippians 4:11-13; 1 Timothy 6:6-8. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

This week we turn to one of the most practical books the Puritans ever wrote — The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment by Jeremiah Burroughs. In a world obsessed with getting more, having more, and being more, Burroughs teaches us the forgotten art of being satisfied with what God has given.

Our Scripture tonight comes from the Apostle Paul, who wrote these words from a prison cell: "I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content." Notice that Paul says he learned contentment. It did not come naturally. It was not something he was born with. Contentment is a skill, a discipline, a grace that must be cultivated over time.

Paul goes on to list the extremes he has experienced: being abased and abounding, being full and being hungry, having plenty and suffering need. In every condition — not just the pleasant ones — Paul found contentment. And the secret? "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me." Contentment is not produced by our circumstances changing; it is produced by Christ strengthening us within our circumstances.

Paul's companion passage in 1 Timothy 6:6-8 gives us a foundational principle: "Godliness with contentment is great gain." Not great wealth, not great fame, not great comfort — great gain. True profit in life is not measured by the size of your house or the number of possessions you own. It is measured by the depth of your godliness combined with the peace of your contentment. A poor man who is content in God is richer than a billionaire who is restless and dissatisfied.

And then the sober reminder: "We brought nothing into this world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out." Every toy, every gadget, every outfit, every car — we will leave it all behind. The only things we take with us into eternity are our faith, our character, and our relationship with God. "Having food and raiment let us be therewith content." Food and clothing. The basics. Paul sets the bar astonishingly low compared to our modern expectations.

Jeremiah Burroughs defined Christian contentment as "that sweet, inward, quiet, gracious frame of spirit, which freely submits to and delights in God's wise and fatherly disposal in every condition." Let us unpack this definition for our families.

Sweet — contentment is not bitter resignation. It is not gritting your teeth and saying, "I guess I have to accept this." It is sweet. It tastes good to the soul. The contented person genuinely enjoys what God has given.

Inward — contentment does not depend on outward circumstances. It comes from within, from a heart that is right with God. You can be content in a mansion or in a prison cell, because contentment lives inside you, not around you.

Quiet — the contented heart is not noisy with complaints, demands, and restless ambition. It is quiet, still, at peace. Like a child resting in its mother's arms, the contented soul rests in God.

Gracious — contentment is a work of God's grace. We cannot produce it by willpower alone. It is a fruit of the Holy Spirit working in our hearts.

Freely submits to and delights in God's wise and fatherly disposal — this is the key. Contentment means trusting that God, who is both wise and fatherly, has arranged your circumstances for your good. He is not random. He is not cruel. He is a wise Father who knows exactly what His children need.

In every condition — not just the easy ones. This is what makes Christian contentment rare. Anyone can be happy when things go well. But the Christian who is content when things go badly — that person has found the rare jewel.

For our families, contentment begins at home. Do our children hear us complaining about what we do not have, or thanking God for what we do? Do we compare our lives to others on social media, or do we count our blessings? Contentment is caught as much as taught. When children see parents who are genuinely grateful and at peace, they learn that happiness does not come from things — it comes from God.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

Now let us hear from one of the great Puritan teachers of the faith.

Jeremiah Burroughs, in his work "The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment," wrote these words:

"Christian contentment is that sweet, inward, quiet, gracious frame of spirit, which freely submits to and delights in God's wise and fatherly disposal in every condition."

What Jeremiah Burroughs is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Philippians 4:11-13; 1 Timothy 6:6-8 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Now it is time for our family discussion. Here are some questions to talk about together. Take your time with each one.

Question 1: Paul said he 'learned' to be content. Why do you think contentment has to be learned rather than coming naturally?

Question 2: What is the difference between contentment and laziness? Can you be content and still work hard?

Question 3: What are some things our family tends to complain about? How might we view those things differently?

Question 4: Burroughs calls contentment 'sweet.' Have you ever experienced a moment of genuine contentment? What was it like?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"But godliness with contentment is great gain." -- 1 Timothy 6:6

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Here is a family activity you can do this week to help these truths take root:

Have each family member write down five things they are grateful for that money cannot buy. Share your lists together and talk about how the best things in life are free gifts from God.

--- Closing ---

Let us close in prayer. Heavenly Father, thank You for gathering our family together in Your Word tonight. Thank You for the truth we have heard from Philippians 4:11-13; 1 Timothy 6:6-8. Help us to live out what we have learned. Guard our hearts, guide our steps, and fill our home with Your love and peace. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

Goodnight, dear ones. Walk in wisdom tomorrow.
