SEGMENT 1: MORNING DEVOTION
----------------------------------------

Good morning, students. Welcome to the Puritan Academy. Let us begin this day as the Puritans did, by acknowledging God as the source of all knowledge and wisdom. Before we open our textbooks, let us open our hearts.

Almighty God, the fountain of all wisdom, we ask You to bless our studies today. Open our minds to understand, our hearts to receive, and our wills to obey the truth. Grant us diligence, humility, and a spirit of inquiry that honors You. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

Let us meditate on a portion of Scripture before we begin our main lesson.

Our Scripture for today is John 4:13-14:

"Jesus answered and said unto her, Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life."

Come to the fountain this morning. Do not try to face this day in the strength of yesterday's grace. Come fresh to Christ. Draw deeply from the well of salvation. Drink of the water that will never run dry. Whatever you need today — cleansing from sin, refreshment for weariness, sustenance for the journey, satisfaction for the longings of your heart — it is all found in Christ. He is the fountain of life. With Him is fullness of joy. At His right hand are pleasures forevermore. Do not go to broken cisterns that can hold no water. Come to the living fountain. Drink freely. Drink deeply. And you shall never, ever thirst again.

As we prepare for today's lesson, remember that everything we study flows from this one fountain: the Word of God. Our lesson today is part of The Work of Christ, and it is titled: The Fountain of Life — Daily Dependence. Let us proceed with minds engaged and hearts open.

SEGMENT 2: MAIN LESSON PART 1 -- BIBLICAL FOUNDATION
----------------------------------------

Now let us turn to our main lesson for today.

The first part of our lesson establishes the biblical foundation for today's topic. We will examine what God's Word teaches us about the themes in John 4:13-14.

The image of Christ as the fountain of life runs throughout Scripture like a crystal stream, nourishing every part of the biblical landscape. From the rivers of Eden to the river of life in the New Jerusalem, the Bible consistently presents God — and specifically Christ — as the source of all spiritual life, refreshment, and sustenance. John 4:13-14 records Jesus' conversation with the Samaritan woman at Jacob's well, where He reveals Himself as the fountain that satisfies the deepest thirst of the human soul.

"Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again." Jesus begins with a statement of the inadequacy of all earthly sources of satisfaction. The water in Jacob's well was real water — good, clean, refreshing. But it could not permanently satisfy. Whoever drank would thirst again within hours. This is a parable of all earthly pleasures, possessions, and achievements. They provide temporary refreshment but never permanent satisfaction. The pursuit of happiness through earthly means is like drinking salt water — the more you drink, the thirstier you become. Augustine expressed this truth memorably: "Thou hast made us for Thyself, O Lord, and our hearts are restless until they rest in Thee."

"But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst." The contrast is absolute. Earthly water provides temporary relief; Christ's water provides permanent satisfaction. The word "never" in the Greek is emphatic — "shall by no means, not ever, under any circumstances thirst." This does not mean that the believer never desires more of Christ (indeed, the more we drink, the more we desire), but that the deepest thirst of the soul — the longing for meaning, purpose, acceptance, love, and God Himself — is permanently and completely satisfied in Christ.

"But the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life." The water Christ gives is not merely a drink that satisfies momentarily but an internal spring — a living fountain — that wells up continuously from within the believer. This is the indwelling presence of the Holy Spirit (John 7:38-39), who is the living water that Christ gives to all who believe in Him. This spring is inexhaustible, unfailing, and eternal — it springs up "into everlasting life."

Psalm 36:7-9 celebrates God as the fountain of all life and goodness: "How excellent is thy lovingkindness, O God! therefore the children of men put their trust under the shadow of thy wings. They shall be abundantly satisfied with the fatness of thy house; and thou shalt make them drink of the river of thy pleasures. For with thee is the fountain of life: in thy light shall we see light." The phrase "fountain of life" expresses the truth that all life — physical and spiritual — originates in God and flows from Him. Apart from God, there is no life, no joy, no satisfaction. With God, there is abundance, pleasure, and light. The "river of thy pleasures" is not a metaphor for sensual indulgence but for the deep, pure, overflowing joy that comes from knowing and fellowshipping with God.

Revelation 22:1-2 completes the biblical arc of the fountain motif: "And he shewed me a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb. In the midst of the street of it, and on either side of the river, was there the tree of life, which bare twelve manner of fruits, and yielded her fruit every month: and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations." In the eternal city, the river of life flows from the throne of God and the Lamb. There is no temple, no mediating institution, no barrier between God and His people. The fountain flows freely and openly, and all who dwell in the city drink from it forever.

Isaiah 12:3 captures the joyful experience of drawing from this fountain: "Therefore with joy shall ye draw water out of the wells of salvation." There is joy in this drawing — not drudgery, not duty, but delight. The wells of salvation are not dry or distant; they are full, accessible, and life-giving. Every act of prayer, every reading of Scripture, every moment of worship is a drawing from these wells. And the water never runs out.

The practical message of these passages is daily dependence upon Christ. Just as the physical body needs daily water to survive, the spiritual life needs daily refreshment from the fountain of Christ. We cannot live on yesterday's grace. We cannot sustain ourselves on past spiritual experiences. We need fresh water every day — fresh communion with Christ, fresh reading of His Word, fresh fellowship with His people, fresh surrender to His will. Jeremiah 2:13 records God's lament over Israel's departure from Him: "For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water." The tragedy of the human condition is not merely that we sin but that we abandon the inexhaustible fountain of living water for broken cisterns of our own making — cisterns that crack and leak and leave us empty.

This biblical foundation is essential. Without it, everything else we study today would be like a house built on sand. But with it, we stand on solid rock.

SEGMENT 3: MAIN LESSON PART 2 -- PURITAN TEACHING
----------------------------------------

Now we turn to the Puritan perspective. John Flavel wrote extensively on this subject in "The Fountain of Life." The Puritans were not merely theologians in ivory towers. They were pastors, teachers, and parents who applied God's truth to every dimension of life.

John Flavel titled his masterwork on the person and work of Christ "The Fountain of Life," drawing from the rich biblical imagery of Christ as the source of all spiritual life and refreshment. For Flavel, this was not merely a convenient title but a theological statement of the highest order. Christ is the fountain — the origin, the source, the spring — from which all the blessings of salvation flow to the souls of the redeemed.

Flavel opened his work with a meditation on the inexhaustible sufficiency of Christ. He observed that earthly fountains, no matter how abundant, can eventually be drained or polluted. But the fountain of Christ's grace is infinite and eternal. It cannot be exhausted by the demands of millions of thirsty souls, nor can it be contaminated by the sins of those who come to drink. Flavel wrote: "Christ is an ever-flowing, ever-full, ever-free fountain of grace. All the saints that have ever drawn from this fountain have not diminished it by a single drop. And all the sins that have been washed in this fountain have not polluted it by a single stain."

Flavel explored the richness of the fountain metaphor with great pastoral skill. He observed that a fountain provides four essential services. First, it cleanses. Just as water washes away dirt and impurity, so the blood of Christ washes away sin. Zechariah 13:1 prophesied, "In that day there shall be a fountain opened to the house of David and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem for sin and for uncleanness." This fountain was opened at Calvary, when the blood and water flowed from Christ's pierced side (John 19:34). Every sin — past, present, and future — can be cleansed by this fountain.

Second, a fountain refreshes. The weary traveler who finds a spring of cool water in the desert is revived and strengthened. So the weary believer who draws near to Christ in prayer and worship is refreshed and renewed. Flavel noted that many Christians live in a state of spiritual dehydration — dry, tired, and listless — not because the fountain has run dry but because they have neglected to drink. The remedy for spiritual dryness is not more activity but more communion with Christ.

Third, a fountain sustains. Water is essential for life — without it, every living thing perishes. In the same way, spiritual life depends entirely upon the continuous supply of grace from Christ. The believer who neglects communion with Christ will not lose his salvation (for the fountain never fails), but he will lose his vitality, his joy, and his fruitfulness. Daily dependence upon Christ is not optional but essential.

Fourth, a fountain satisfies. Nothing quenches thirst like pure, fresh water. And nothing satisfies the soul like the grace of Christ. Flavel urged his hearers to stop trying to satisfy their souls with earthly substitutes — wealth, pleasure, fame, relationships — and to come to the only fountain that can truly satisfy. He echoed the invitation of Isaiah 55:1: "Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money; come ye, buy, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price."

Flavel devoted considerable attention to the means by which believers draw from the fountain of Christ. He identified several primary means of grace. Prayer is the bucket with which we draw water from the well. Scripture reading is the channel through which the fountain flows to us. Fellowship with other believers is the communal pool where we drink together. The Lord's Supper is the special feast where the fountain is set before us in visible, tangible form. Preaching is the public announcement that the fountain is open and all are invited to drink.

Flavel was also concerned to address those who felt unworthy to come to the fountain. He observed that many sensitive believers hold back from Christ because they feel their sins are too great, their faith is too weak, or their repentance is too shallow. To these struggling souls, Flavel offered words of tender encouragement. He reminded them that the invitation is to "whosoever" — not to the strong, the worthy, or the righteous, but to anyone who thirsts. The only qualification for coming to the fountain is thirst — a sense of need, a recognition of emptiness, a desire for what only Christ can give. Flavel wrote: "Christ does not invite the full but the thirsty. He does not call the righteous but sinners. If you feel your need of Christ, you are welcome. If you thirst for His grace, you may drink freely. The fountain is open, and no one who comes will be turned away."

The Puritan emphasis on daily dependence upon Christ was not mere theory but lived practice. The Puritans structured their daily lives around communion with Christ. They rose early for morning devotions, spent time in prayer and Scripture before beginning the day's work, paused at midday for reflection, and concluded the day with evening worship and self-examination. This rhythm of daily drawing from the fountain of Christ gave their lives a depth, stability, and joy that was evident to all who knew them.

Flavel's own life illustrated this principle. Despite persecution, poverty, and the loss of his first wife and several children, Flavel maintained a consistent, joyful devotion to Christ that astonished his contemporaries. When asked the secret of his spiritual vitality, Flavel pointed to the fountain: "I know no other source of strength, comfort, or joy than Jesus Christ. He is my fountain, and I drink from Him every day. Without Him, I would be the most miserable of men. With Him, I am the richest.'

The Puritan approach reminds us that theology is never abstract. It is always personal and always practical. What we believe about God shapes everything we do.

SEGMENT 4: DISCUSSION AND APPLICATION
----------------------------------------

Now let us pause and consider what we have learned. Discussion is where understanding deepens.

Discussion Question 1: Jesus said that whoever drinks the water He gives will 'never thirst' (John 4:14). What does this mean in light of the fact that Christians still desire more of Christ?

Discussion Question 2: Jeremiah 2:13 describes the tragedy of forsaking the 'fountain of living waters' for 'broken cisterns.' What are some 'broken cisterns' that people turn to instead of Christ?

Discussion Question 3: Flavel identified four services a fountain provides: cleansing, refreshing, sustaining, and satisfying. Which of these do you most need from Christ today?

Discussion Question 4: How do the means of grace — prayer, Scripture, fellowship, the Lord's Supper, preaching — function as channels through which we draw from the fountain of Christ?

Discussion Question 5: Why do many Christians live in a state of spiritual dryness? What practical steps can we take to drink more deeply from the fountain of life?

Take time with each question. The Puritans valued what they called "holy conference," the practice of discussing spiritual truths together. Do not settle for quick, surface answers. Push deeper. Ask follow-up questions. Listen carefully to one another.

Application is where truth becomes life. It is not enough to know what John 4:13-14 says. We must ask: how does this change the way I live today? How does this shape my thoughts, my words, my actions? The Puritans insisted that a truth that does not transform is a truth that has not been truly understood.

SEGMENT 6: CROSS-SUBJECT CONNECTIONS
----------------------------------------

One of the great insights of the Puritan educational tradition is that all subjects are interconnected under the lordship of Christ. There is no such thing as a purely secular subject. History, literature, and even the natural sciences all reveal the glory and wisdom of God. Let us explore those connections now.

History Connection:

Water has been one of the most important resources in human history. Ancient civilizations arose around water sources — the Nile, the Tigris and Euphrates, the Indus, the Yellow River. Roman aqueducts were engineering marvels that brought water over great distances to sustain cities. Study how the control and distribution of water has shaped human civilization and how the biblical metaphor of God as the 'fountain of living waters' would have resonated powerfully with people in the arid Near East.

Literature Connection:

Water imagery is one of the most universal and powerful symbol systems in world literature. From Homer's wine-dark sea to Coleridge's Ancient Mariner dying of thirst surrounded by water to Eliot's 'The Waste Land' longing for rain, writers have used water to symbolize life, renewal, purification, and hope. Study how the biblical use of water imagery — from the rivers of Eden to the river of life in Revelation — provides the archetypal foundation for these literary treatments.

Science Connection:

Water is uniquely essential to life on earth. Study the remarkable properties of water that make it indispensable for biological processes: its role as a universal solvent, its unusual density behavior (ice floats, allowing aquatic life to survive winter), its high heat capacity (stabilizing climate), and its role in photosynthesis, cellular respiration, and DNA replication. These extraordinary properties of water reflect the design of a Creator who ordained water as the sustainer of physical life, just as He ordained Christ as the sustainer of spiritual life.

As you continue your studies in other subjects, look for connections to what you have learned today. The Puritans saw every branch of knowledge as a thread in the great tapestry of God's truth. Train your eye to see those connections, and your education will be richer for it.

SEGMENT 7: EVENING REVIEW AND MEMORY WORK
----------------------------------------

We have reached the final segment of today's lesson. The Puritans placed great value on review and memorization. They knew that what is not reviewed is soon forgotten. Let us take time now to consolidate what we have learned.

Review Questions:

Review Question 1: In John 4:13-14, how does Jesus contrast the water from Jacob's well with the water He gives?

Review Question 2: What does Psalm 36:9 mean when it says 'with thee is the fountain of life'?

Review Question 3: According to Revelation 22:1-2, what flows from the throne of God in the eternal city?

Review Question 4: What four services of a fountain did Flavel identify as illustrations of Christ's work?

Review Question 5: How did Flavel address believers who felt too unworthy to come to the fountain of Christ?

Write out your answers to these questions. Use complete sentences and support your answers with what you learned in today's lesson. If you are unsure of an answer, go back and review that section of the lesson.

Memory Verse:

"Whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life." -- John 4:14

Read this verse aloud five times. Then close your eyes and try to recite it from memory. Write it out by hand on a card or in your journal. The Puritans memorized vast portions of Scripture, and it was one of the great sources of their spiritual strength. Even one verse, truly hidden in the heart, can sustain you in times of trial.
