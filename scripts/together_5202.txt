PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 2
"Wives, Submit to Your Husbands"
Ephesians 5:22-24 — Mutual Submission and Love


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome back to our journey through Ephesians 5. Tonight we come to a passage that has been greatly misunderstood and greatly abused in our day. Let us approach it with humble hearts and open Bibles, trusting that what God has written is good, wise, and loving.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 5:22-24 (KJV):

"Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord. For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body. Therefore as the church is subject unto Christ, so let the wives be to their own husbands in every thing."


Before we proceed, let us remember the verse we studied last evening. All of tonight's passage flows from the prior command of mutual submission in the fear of God. The wife's submission is not an isolated command dropped upon women with no context. It is the particular expression of a universal principle that governs the entire household of faith.

William Gouge, who preached through Ephesians 5 to his London congregation for years before publishing his Domestical Duties, was careful to note that the wife's submission is neither slavish obedience nor the erasure of her personhood. Gouge taught that the wife submits to her husband "as unto the Lord," which means her submission is ultimately an act of worship. She is not submitting because her husband is inherently superior, but because God has established an order in the home that reflects the relationship between Christ and His church.

This distinction is vital. The wife who submits "as unto the Lord" is free. She is not submitting because she is weak or because she has no will of her own. She is submitting because she trusts the God who has established this order. Her submission is a statement of faith, not a statement of inferiority. It declares that she believes God's design for marriage is good, even when it is difficult, even when her husband is imperfect, even when the world mocks the very idea.

Gouge was equally clear that this passage places an enormous burden upon the husband. If the wife is to submit to her husband "as unto the Lord," then the husband must conduct himself in a manner worthy of that trust. A husband who uses this passage to dominate, to control, or to diminish his wife has not understood it at all. He has weaponized what God intended as a blessing. The headship described here is the headship of Christ, who came not to be served but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many.

Richard Baxter offered wise counsel to wives who found submission difficult. He acknowledged that not every husband is easy to follow. Some are harsh. Some are foolish. Some are neglectful. But Baxter reminded wives that their submission is rendered ultimately to Christ, not to a perfect man. When a wife submits in faith, God honors her obedience even when her husband fails to honor his own calling. This does not mean that a wife must submit to sin or to abuse. The Puritans were clear that submission has limits: no wife is called to obey commands that violate the law of God or that endanger her person.

Notice the beautiful analogy Paul uses: "as the church is subject unto Christ." The church submits to Christ not out of fear but out of love and trust. The church knows that Christ's leadership is always for her good, always for her sanctification, always for her glory. When a husband leads as Christ leads, the wife's submission becomes not a burden but a joy. When a wife submits as the church submits to Christ, the husband is freed to lead with confidence and gentleness.

Dear wives, if you are listening tonight, know that your submission is seen by God and honored by Him. It is not weakness. It is one of the strongest expressions of faith in the Christian life. And dear husbands, if your wife finds it difficult to submit, look first at your own leadership. Are you leading as Christ leads? Are you giving yourself for her sanctification and joy?

The Puritans spoke of the wife as the glory of the husband. When she flourishes, he is honored. When she is diminished, he is shamed. The wise husband creates an environment where his wife's submission is a delight, not a drudgery. And the wise wife knows that her willing submission strengthens her husband's ability to lead well.


Question 1: Wife, are there areas where you find it difficult to trust your husband's leadership? Can you share those honestly tonight?

Question 2: Husband, are you leading in a way that makes it natural and safe for your wife to follow? Where might you need to grow?

Question 3: How can we better reflect the relationship between Christ and the church in our marriage this week?


Lord God, we thank Thee for the beautiful order Thou hast established in marriage. We confess that we have not always honored it. Help the wife in this marriage to submit with joy and trust, knowing that her obedience is unto Thee. Help the husband to lead with the gentleness and sacrifice of Christ, making submission a blessing rather than a burden. Guard us from the distortions of the world that would twist Thy design into something harsh or demeaning. Let our marriage reflect the glorious union of Christ and His church. We ask it in Jesus' name, Amen.
