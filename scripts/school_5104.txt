PURITAN GOLD -- Homeschool, Lesson 4
"God's Eternal Decrees and Providence"
Westminster Standards — Lesson 4

---

Welcome back to Puritan Gold's homeschool program. Today we explore one of the most awe-inspiring and sometimes controversial doctrines in the Westminster Standards — God's eternal decrees and His providential governance of all things. Let us pray and begin.

Sovereign Lord, Your decrees are from everlasting, and Your providence governs every atom and every moment. Give us wisdom and humility as we study these deep truths. Help us to trust what we cannot fully comprehend. In Christ's name, Amen.

--- Lesson ---

The Westminster Confession addresses God's eternal decrees in Chapter 3 and His providence in Chapter 5. These two doctrines are closely related. The decrees tell us what God has determined from eternity. Providence tells us how God carries out those decrees in time and history.

Chapter 3, Section 1 of the Confession states: "God from all eternity did by the most wise and holy counsel of his own will, freely and unchangeably ordain whatsoever comes to pass; yet so as thereby neither is God the author of sin; nor is violence offered to the will of the creatures, nor is the liberty or contingency of second causes taken away, but rather established."

This is a carefully worded statement, and every clause matters. Let us examine it.

"God from all eternity did...ordain whatsoever comes to pass." Before the foundation of the world, before time itself began, God determined everything that would happen throughout the entire course of history. Nothing takes God by surprise. Nothing falls outside His sovereign plan. The rise and fall of nations, the details of your daily life, the number of hairs on your head — all of it was ordained by God from eternity.

"By the most wise and holy counsel of his own will." God's decrees are not arbitrary or capricious. They flow from His infinite wisdom and perfect holiness. He always decrees what is wisest and best, and His decrees are always consistent with His holy character.

"Freely and unchangeably." God was under no compulsion to decree as He did. No one advised Him. No one persuaded Him. He decreed freely, according to His own will. And having decreed, He does not change His mind. His decrees are as eternal and unchangeable as He is.

"Yet so as thereby neither is God the author of sin." This is a crucial qualification. While God ordains all that comes to pass, He is not the cause of evil. Sin is real, and sinners are genuinely responsible for their sinful choices. How God can ordain all things without being the author of sin is one of the deep mysteries of theology, but the Confession affirms both truths: God is sovereign, and God is holy.

"Nor is violence offered to the will of the creatures, nor is the liberty or contingency of second causes taken away, but rather established." This addresses the relationship between God's sovereignty and human freedom. God's decrees do not turn human beings into robots. People make real choices, and those choices have real consequences. God's sovereignty does not destroy human responsibility — it undergirds it. God works through secondary causes — human decisions, natural processes, historical events — to accomplish His eternal purposes.

The Shorter Catechism presents these truths more accessibly. Question 7 asks, "What are the decrees of God?" and answers: "The decrees of God are his eternal purpose, according to the counsel of his will, whereby, for his own glory, he hath foreordained whatsoever comes to pass." Note the phrase "for his own glory." The ultimate purpose of all God's decrees is the display of His glory — His wisdom, power, justice, and mercy revealed for the wonder and worship of His creatures.

Chapter 5 of the Confession turns to providence — the execution of God's decrees in time. Section 1 states: "God, the great Creator of all things, doth uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all creatures, actions, and things, from the greatest even to the least, by his most wise and holy providence." Four verbs define God's providential activity: He upholds (sustains in existence), directs (guides toward His purposes), disposes (arranges circumstances), and governs (rules over all). And this governance extends from the greatest events to the least — from the movements of galaxies to the movements of molecules.

The Confession also addresses the difficult question of God's providence in relation to evil. Section 4 acknowledges that God sometimes permits sin to occur, and that He does so for wise and holy reasons — to display His justice in punishing it or His grace in forgiving it. But God never causes sin, approves of sin, or takes pleasure in sin. His relationship to evil is always that of a sovereign ruler who permits what He does not approve and overrules it for good.

These doctrines call for two responses. First, worship. A God who governs all things from the greatest to the least is a God of unfathomable majesty and power. Second, trust. If God has ordained all things and governs all things according to His wise and holy will, then we can trust Him — completely, unreservedly, in every circumstance of our lives.

--- Review Questions ---

Review Question 1: What does the Westminster Confession mean when it says God "ordained whatsoever comes to pass"? How does this relate to human responsibility?

Review Question 2: What is the difference between God's eternal decrees and His providence?

Review Question 3: How does the Confession address the relationship between God's sovereignty and the existence of evil?

--- Activity ---

Write a two-paragraph essay comparing and contrasting God's sovereignty with human responsibility. Use at least two quotations from the Westminster Confession (Chapters 3 and 5) and two supporting Scripture passages. Then write a personal reflection: How does the doctrine of God's sovereignty affect the way you think about difficult events in your own life?

--- Closing ---

The doctrines of God's decrees and providence are among the deepest waters in all of theology. Let them not merely inform your mind but comfort your heart and strengthen your trust in the God who holds all things in His hands. Until next time, students.
