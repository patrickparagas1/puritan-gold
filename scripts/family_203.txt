PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 11
"Providence and Daily Provision"
John Flavel — Providence and Family Trust


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Flavel directs our attention to one of the most practical applications of providence — God's daily provision for your family's material needs. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Generous Father, every good and perfect gift comes from You. Tonight, teach us to see Your providing hand in the food on our table, the roof over our heads, and every blessing we enjoy. Free us from worry and fill us with gratitude. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Matthew 6:25-26, 31-33. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Therefore I say unto you, Take no thought for your life, what ye shall eat, or what ye shall drink; nor yet for your body, what ye shall put on. Is not the life more than meat, and the body than raiment? Behold the fowls of the air: for they sow not, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns; yet your heavenly Father feedeth them. Are ye not much better than they? Therefore take no thought, saying, What shall we eat? or, What shall we drink? or, Wherewithal shall we be clothed? For your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things. But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you."

That is Matthew 6:25-26, 31-33. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


If there is one area where families feel the weight of anxiety most acutely, it is in the realm of daily provision. Will we have enough? Can we pay the bills? What about the children's education? What about retirement? What about unexpected expenses? The questions multiply, and with them, the worry.

John Flavel addressed this anxiety head-on with the doctrine of providence. He taught that the same God who created the universe and sustains every creature in it has pledged Himself to provide for the daily needs of His children. This is not a vague, general promise. It is a specific, personal commitment from the God who knows exactly what your family needs and has the power and the will to provide it.

Flavel drew attention to the way God provides for the creatures of the natural world. The birds do not sow or reap or gather into barns, yet they are fed. The flowers do not labor or spin, yet they are clothed in splendor that surpasses Solomon's royal robes. If God takes such care of creatures that are here today and gone tomorrow, how much more will He care for the families He has created, redeemed, and adopted as His own children?

But Flavel was careful to distinguish between trust in providence and presumption. Trusting God's providence does not mean we sit back and do nothing. It means we work diligently, plan wisely, and steward our resources faithfully — while recognizing that the outcome of our labor is in God's hands, not ours. The farmer plows and plants, but it is God who sends the rain and causes the seed to grow. Parents earn wages and manage budgets, but it is God who provides the job, sustains the health, and opens the doors of opportunity.

Flavel also taught that God sometimes provides in surprising ways. He told stories of believers who were in desperate need and received help from unexpected sources — a stranger's kindness, an opportunity that appeared at the last moment, a provision that came just when all hope seemed lost. These stories were not fairy tales but testimonies of God's faithfulness, and Flavel collected them to encourage families who were struggling.

One of Flavel's most searching observations was about the spiritual damage that anxiety causes in the family. When parents are consumed with worry about money, the anxiety infects the entire household. Children sense their parents' stress and begin to carry burdens that are too heavy for their young shoulders. The atmosphere of the home becomes tense and fearful rather than peaceful and trusting. Flavel would say that parental anxiety is, at its root, a failure to believe that God is who He says He is — a loving Father who cares for His children.

The antidote Flavel prescribed was not simply "stop worrying" — as if anxiety could be banished by willpower alone. The antidote was meditation on the character and promises of God. Flavel urged families to fill their minds with the truth about who God is: He is the Creator who made everything out of nothing. He is the Sustainer who holds every atom in existence. He is the Father who loves His children with an everlasting love. He is the Provider who has promised that His children will never be forsaken or left to beg bread (Psalm 37:25). When these truths are deeply rooted in the heart, anxiety loses its grip.

Tonight, practice gratitude as a family. Go around the room and let each person name three things God has provided for your family this week. No provision is too small — a meal, a kind word, a sunny day, a laugh together. As you name these blessings, you are training your hearts to see what Flavel saw: a God who is at work in every detail, providing for His children with infinite generosity and perfect timing.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Jesus said that God feeds the birds and clothes the flowers. What is He teaching us about His care for our family?

Question 2: Flavel said that anxiety in parents infects the whole household. How can we as a family replace worry with trust in God's provision?

Question 3: What are three specific things God has provided for our family this week that we can thank Him for right now?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you." -- Matthew 6:33

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Father, we thank You for Your faithful provision. Forgive us for the times we have worried instead of trusted. You know what we need before we ask. Help our family to seek Your kingdom first and to rest in Your promise that You will provide all that we need. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, provided for by a Father whose resources are infinite and whose love never fails.
