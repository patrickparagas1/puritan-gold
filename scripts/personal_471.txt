PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 10
"Keeping the Heart — John Flavel"
Reading Day — Flavel on the Inner Life


Welcome. Today is a Reading day. We turn to John Flavel's Keeping the Heart, one of the most practical and searching works on the inner life ever written — a sustained meditation on Proverbs 4:23.

Let us read Proverbs 4:23 (KJV):

"Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."


John Flavel (c. 1627-1691) was a Puritan minister whose works combine intellectual rigor with pastoral warmth. Keeping the Heart is built on a single conviction: the heart is the command center of the whole person, and its condition determines everything else.

Flavel defines the heart not as merely the seat of emotions but as the center of the whole person — mind, will, affections, conscience, desires. It is the spring from which every thought, word, and action flows. He writes that the heart is the spring and original of all our actions, and that the greatest business in the world is to keep the heart.

Why is heart-keeping so important? Because a polluted spring produces polluted water, no matter how clean the channel. A disordered heart produces disordered living, no matter how disciplined the exterior. Many Christians focus on external behavior while neglecting the internal source. This is like polishing the outside of a cup while the inside is filthy.

Flavel identifies several seasons in which the heart is most vulnerable. In prosperity, the heart is in danger of complacency, pride, and self-sufficiency. Prosperity is actually more dangerous to the soul than adversity, because adversity drives us to God while prosperity tempts us to forget Him. The cure is gratitude — constantly reminding yourself that every good thing comes from God's hand.

In adversity, the heart is in danger of bitterness, despair, and anger toward God. Flavel counsels the suffering Christian to remember that God's love has not changed, that His purposes are wise even when hidden, and that this affliction is temporary while His glory is eternal.

In temptation, the heart is in danger of being deceived. Flavel urges the tempted Christian to look beyond the bait to the hook. Sin promises pleasure but delivers misery. Sin promises freedom but produces bondage. The wise heart sees through the disguise.

In spiritual dryness — when God seems distant and prayer feels empty — the heart is in danger of abandoning the disciplines that sustain faith. Flavel counsels persistence. Dryness is not evidence of God's absence but often the means by which God deepens faith. The Christian who prays through the dryness will emerge stronger than before.

In zeal — after a powerful spiritual experience — the heart is in danger of pride and the neglect of ongoing discipline. Flavel writes that many Christians fall hardest immediately after their highest moments, because they let down their guard in the glow of spiritual excitement.

Flavel's counsel is both simple and demanding: keep your heart with ALL diligence. Not some diligence. All diligence. This means daily self-examination, daily repentance, daily realignment of the affections toward God. It means treating the inner life as more important than the outer life — knowing that if the heart is right, the life will follow.

This is lifelong work. Flavel did not promise quick results. But the Christian who diligently tends the inner garden of the heart will, over time, bear the fruit of a life that honors God and blesses others.


Keep your heart. With all diligence. For out of it are the issues of life. Lord, I confess that I often neglect the inner life while polishing the exterior. Search me, O God, and know my heart. Try me and know my thoughts. Keep my heart with Your diligence when my own fails. Amen.
