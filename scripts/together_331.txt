PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 31
"Celebrating the Excellent Wife Together"
Proverbs 31:10-31

---

Welcome, dear ones. Marriage is a gift from God, and so is this time together in His presence. Whether this has been an easy day or a hard one, you are here, together, and that matters more than you know.

Our Scripture reading this evening is from Proverbs 31:10-12, 28-31. Let us receive it together:

"Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies. The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her, so that he shall have no need of spoil. She will do him good and not evil all the days of her life... Her children arise up, and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praiseth her. Many daughters have done virtuously, but thou excellest them all. Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: but a woman that feareth the LORD, she shall be praised. Give her of the fruit of her hands; and let her own works praise her in the gates."

That is Proverbs 31:10-12, 28-31. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

The Puritans understood marriage in a way that the modern world has largely forgotten. Let us hear from Matthew Henry, who wrote with great wisdom on this subject.

Matthew Henry approaches this grand conclusion of Proverbs with reverence. This is not merely a portrait of an ideal wife; it is a celebration of feminine excellence as defined by the fear of the Lord. Henry writes: 'This description of the virtuous woman is not an impossible standard but a glorious vision of what God's grace can produce in a woman who fears Him.'

Henry walks through the qualities with admiration: she is trustworthy (her husband's heart safely trusts in her), she is consistently good (she does him good and not evil all the days of her life), she is industrious, generous, wise in speech, and vigilant over her household. But Henry is careful to locate the source of all these virtues: 'Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: but a woman that feareth the LORD, she shall be praised.' It is the fear of the Lord, the same foundation laid in Proverbs 1:7, that produces this extraordinary character.

Henry also highlights the husband's role: 'Her husband also, and he praiseth her.' The excellent wife is not merely excellent in isolation; she is recognized, celebrated, and praised by her husband. Henry writes: 'He that has such a wife should acknowledge it to her praise and God's glory. He should not take it for granted but should express his appreciation publicly and privately.'

Charles Bridges adds that this passage is ultimately about the marriage partnership. The woman of Proverbs 31 does not exist in a vacuum; she flourishes in the context of a husband who trusts her, children who bless her, and a community that honors her. The passage ends not with her achievements but with her reward: praise. 'Give her of the fruit of her hands' — let her enjoy the results of her labor. 'Let her own works praise her in the gates' — let her character speak for itself.

--- Marriage Application ---

As we come to the end of Proverbs, this passage calls both husband and wife to a posture of celebration. Husband, when was the last time you praised your wife, not for what she does for you but for who she is in Christ? The Proverbs 31 husband does not take his wife for granted; he praises her publicly, vocally, and specifically.

Wife, this passage is not a burden but a vision. It is a portrait of the beauty that the fear of the Lord produces in a woman's life over time. You are not expected to embody all of these qualities perfectly today, but you are invited to grow toward them by the grace of God.

Together, celebrate what God is doing in your marriage. Look back over this month and name the ways you have grown. Name the ways God has been faithful. Name the ways your spouse has blessed you. Let this final day be a day of gratitude, celebration, and renewed commitment to build your marriage on the fear of the Lord.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

The Puritans practiced what they called holy conference, speaking honestly about spiritual things with those they loved most. Do that now with these questions.

Question 1: Husband: What specific qualities in your wife can you praise today, following the example of the Proverbs 31 husband?

Question 2: Wife: Which qualities of the Proverbs 31 woman do you most aspire to grow in, and how can your husband support that growth?

Question 3: As we finish this month in Proverbs, what is the most important lesson we have learned together, and how will we carry it forward?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

It is time to bring what we have heard and discussed before the Lord in prayer. Pray out loud if you can. Let your spouse hear your heart.

Tonight's prayer focus: Spend time praising God together for the gift of your marriage. Thank Him for what He has taught you through Proverbs this month. Ask Him to continue building your home on the fear of the Lord, that your marriage may be a testimony to His grace for generations to come.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord who began a good work in your marriage bring it to completion, crowning your life together with the beauty of holiness and the joy of a love that fears Him above all. Amen.

May the God who brought you together continue to bind you together in love, in faith, and in purpose. Sleep well tonight. He watches over your marriage.
