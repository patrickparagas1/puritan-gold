PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Guided Puritan Reading, Day 1
"The Mortification of Sin, Part 1"
John Owen — The Nature of Indwelling Sin


Welcome, friend. We begin a new month of growth together, and there is no better place to start than with the great Puritan pastor John Owen. His treatise on the mortification of sin has shaped the spiritual lives of countless believers across the centuries. Tonight we take up this work again, not as an academic exercise, but as a guide for the daily battle every Christian must wage.


Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Romans 8:13:

"For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.


John Owen published "The Mortification of Sin in Believers" in 1656, drawing from sermons he had preached to university students at Oxford. These were young men preparing for ministry, and Owen saw in them a dangerous naivety about the power of indwelling sin. He wrote not as a detached theologian but as a pastor alarmed by what he witnessed: gifted men who did not understand the enemy that lived within their own hearts.

Owen begins with the text of Romans 8:13 and draws from it several foundational observations that govern everything that follows. The first is that the duty of mortification belongs to believers alone. Owen is careful to make this distinction. The unregenerate man cannot mortify sin because he does not possess the Spirit. He may reform his outward behavior, he may suppress certain vices through willpower or social pressure, but he cannot touch the root of sin in his heart. Only the man who has been born again, who has received the indwelling Holy Spirit, is capable of true mortification. This is not a work that earns salvation; it is a work that flows from it.

Owen's second observation concerns the nature of indwelling sin itself. He insists that believers must understand what they are fighting. Indwelling sin is not merely a collection of bad habits or a tendency toward particular transgressions. It is a principle, a law, a remaining corruption that dwells in the very faculties of the soul. Owen draws from Romans 7:21, where Paul writes, "I find then a law, that, when I would do good, evil is present with me." This law of sin operates in the mind, darkening the understanding; in the will, inclining it away from God; and in the affections, disordering the loves of the heart.

Owen describes indwelling sin as restless and relentless. It never sleeps. It never takes a holiday. It is always conceiving, always tempting, always seeking to draw the believer away from communion with God. Owen writes that sin is like a living enemy garrisoned within the walls of a city: it knows every street, every weakness, every unguarded gate. The believer who does not understand this will be perpetually surprised by his own failures.

Third, Owen identifies the agent of mortification. The text says, "if ye through the Spirit do mortify." Owen emphasizes that the Spirit of God is the only effectual agent of true mortification. This does not mean that the believer is passive. The command is addressed to the believer: "do mortify." But the power by which he obeys the command is not his own. It is the power of the Holy Spirit working within him. Owen warns against every method of fighting sin that does not depend upon the Spirit. He writes that mortification achieved by human effort alone, carried on by self-invented methods, to the end of self-righteousness, is the very essence of false religion.

Owen proceeds to distinguish true mortification from its counterfeits. A man may cease from a particular sin because the opportunity has been removed, because the natural desire has faded with age, because social consequences have made the sin costly, or because one lust has been exchanged for another. None of these constitutes true mortification. True mortification strikes at the root. It weakens the habit of sin in the soul. It produces not merely outward conformity but inward change: a genuine hatred of sin, a growing love of holiness, and an increasing tenderness of conscience.

Owen also warns against the error of thinking that mortification means sinless perfection. No believer in this life will be entirely free from the motions of sin. The goal is not eradication but progressive weakening. The mortified man still sins, but he sins less frequently, less willingly, and with more grief. The strength of sin is diminished even though its presence remains.

Finally, Owen lays down the great principle that will govern the rest of his treatise: the vigor, power, and comfort of the spiritual life depend upon the mortification of the deeds of the flesh. A man who neglects this duty will find his prayers lifeless, his reading of Scripture barren, his worship cold, and his usefulness to the church diminished. Mortification is not an advanced discipline for the spiritually elite. It is the daily bread of every believer who would walk with God.

As we begin this journey with Owen, let us receive his opening challenge with sober hearts. The fight against sin is not optional. It is the defining work of the Christian life, carried on by the power of the Spirit, sustained by the grace of God, and aimed at the glory of Christ.


Take a moment to sit with these questions before you sleep.

Question 1: Do I understand the difference between true mortification and mere behavioral change? Where in my life might I be relying on willpower rather than the Spirit?

Question 2: Owen says indwelling sin never rests. Am I vigilant against sin's constant activity, or have I grown complacent in certain areas?

Question 3: How does Owen's insistence that mortification is for believers encourage me rather than discourage me?

Do not rush past these questions. Sit with them. Journal about them if you can. The Puritans believed that truth must be digested, not merely tasted.


O Lord, as we begin this study of the mortification of sin, open our eyes to the reality of indwelling corruption and the sufficiency of Thy Spirit to overcome it. Grant us honest hearts and dependent spirits. Through Jesus Christ our Lord, Amen.
