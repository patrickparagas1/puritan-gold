PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 1
"The Foundation of Family Worship"
John Angell James — A Help to Domestic Happiness


Good evening, dear family. Welcome to tonight's family devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for gathering together around God's Word. Let us quiet our hearts and prepare to listen to what the Lord has for us.

Let us begin with prayer. Heavenly Father, we thank You for this family. We thank You for bringing us safely through another day. Now we ask that You would open our eyes to see wonderful things in Your Word. Speak to each heart gathered here, from the oldest to the youngest. In Jesus' name we pray. Amen.

Tonight we open our Bibles to Deuteronomy 6:4-9. Listen carefully as I read our passage:

"Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up. And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes. And thou shalt write them upon the posts of thy house, and on thy gates."

That is Deuteronomy 6:4-9. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Welcome to our very first family devotional in this series on Puritan Household Treasures. Today we begin where God Himself begins — with the family gathered to hear His Word.

Let us read together the great passage from Deuteronomy chapter six. Moses is speaking to the people of Israel just before they enter the Promised Land, and he gives them the most important commandment of all. He says, 'Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.'

Now notice what comes right after this command to love God with everything we have. God does not say, 'Now go build a great temple.' He does not say, 'Now go conquer your enemies.' No — He says, 'And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children.'

This is remarkable. The very first thing God tells His people to do after loving Him is to teach their children about Him. Not the priests. Not the teachers. The parents. God designed the family to be the first and most important place where children learn about Him.

But look carefully at how God says to do this. He does not say, 'Teach them once a week.' He says to talk about God's words 'when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.' That means all the time — at the dinner table, on a walk, at bedtime, and in the morning. God wants His truth woven into the fabric of everyday family life.

The Hebrew word for 'teach them diligently' is a vivid word. It means to sharpen, like sharpening a sword or an arrow. Parents are to sharpen God's truth into the hearts and minds of their children. This is not casual or occasional. It takes effort and intention, like a craftsman carefully shaping a blade.

God also says to bind these words on your hand and between your eyes and to write them on your doorposts. While the Jewish people took this literally with phylacteries and mezuzahs, the deeper meaning is clear — God's Word should be visible in everything we do (our hands), everything we think about (between our eyes), and everywhere we live (our doorposts). Our homes should be saturated with Scripture.

Now let us hear what the Puritan pastor John Angell James said about this very thing. James was a beloved minister in Birmingham, England, in the 1800s, and he wrote a wonderful book called A Help to Domestic Happiness. He said that religion in the soul is the foundation of domestic happiness, and that without it, a family is merely a group of immortal beings living together without God.

Think about that. James saw clearly what the Bible teaches — that a home without God at the center is missing its very foundation. Just as a house built without a foundation will eventually crumble, a family without worship will drift apart.

James urged families to make worship a daily practice, not just a Sunday habit. He believed that the family altar — a time each day when the family gathers to read Scripture, sing, and pray — was the single most important practice for a Christian household. He wrote that family worship brings down God's blessing upon the household and knits the hearts of family members together in love.

So what does this mean for us today? It means that what we are doing right now — gathering as a family to hear God's Word — is exactly what God designed. You are not just having a nice family moment. You are obeying the command of Deuteronomy 6. You are building the foundation that God says will keep your family strong through every season of life.

For the youngest ones listening, the lesson is simple: God wants your family to talk about Him every day, not just on Sundays. For those who are a bit older, consider this: if God made the family the primary place for learning His truth, then every family devotion time is more important than we might realize. And for the oldest among us, here is the challenge: Are we willing to be intentional, to sharpen God's Word into our daily routines, even when life gets busy?

Let us commit together, as a family, to make these next thirty-one days a time of growing closer to God and to one another.


Now let us hear from one of the great Puritan teachers of the faith.

John Angell James, in his work "A Help to Domestic Happiness," wrote these words:

"Religion in the soul is the foundation of domestic happiness. Without this, a family is but a company of immortal beings living together without God, and hastening to eternity without preparation."

What John Angell James is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Deuteronomy 6:4-9 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Now it is time for our family discussion. Here are some questions to talk about together. Take your time with each one.

Question 1: What does God want families to talk about? (Hint: His words!)

Question 2: When does God say we should talk about His truth — only at church, or all the time?

Question 3: Why do you think God chose families, not just churches, as the main place to teach children about Him?

Question 4: What are some practical ways we can 'write God's words on our doorposts' in our modern home?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might." -- Deuteronomy 6:5

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a family activity you can do this week to help these truths take root:

Make a family 'doorpost verse' — write Deuteronomy 6:5 on a piece of card stock or paper, decorate it together, and hang it somewhere everyone will see it daily (like the front door, the refrigerator, or the hallway).


Let us close in prayer. Heavenly Father, thank You for gathering our family together in Your Word tonight. Thank You for the truth we have heard from Deuteronomy 6:4-9. Help us to live out what we have learned. Guard our hearts, guide our steps, and fill our home with Your love and peace. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

Goodnight, dear ones. Walk in wisdom tomorrow.
