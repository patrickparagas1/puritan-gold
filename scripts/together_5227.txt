PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 27
"Charm Is Deceitful, Beauty Is Vain"
Proverbs 31:30

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we arrive at the climactic verse of the Proverbs 31 portrait, the verse that reveals the true foundation upon which all the virtuous woman's excellence rests. After describing her industry, her wisdom, her generosity, and her strength, Solomon strips away every lesser measure of value and points us to the one thing that truly matters.

Let us read our passage. Proverbs 31:30 (KJV):

"Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: but a woman that feareth the LORD, she shall be praised."

--- Teaching ---

Solomon does not say that charm is evil or that beauty is sinful. He says that charm is deceitful and beauty is vain. That is, they are unreliable. They cannot bear the weight that the world places upon them. Charm can mask a corrupt heart. Beauty fades with time. Neither charm nor beauty can sustain a marriage through decades of trial, loss, sickness, and the slow erosion of youth. The husband who chose his wife primarily for her charm will be disappointed when the charm wears thin. The wife who rests her confidence primarily in her beauty will be devastated when age does its inevitable work.

Thomas Watson addressed this subject with characteristic directness. He warned young men against choosing a wife based on outward appearance alone. A beautiful face, Watson wrote, may hide an ungodly heart, and an ungodly heart will make a beautiful face a source of misery rather than delight. Conversely, the woman who fears the Lord may not turn every head in the marketplace, but she will turn her husband's heart toward heaven. Her beauty is the beauty of holiness, and it does not fade with the passing years. It deepens.

Richard Baxter echoed this teaching with a practical observation drawn from years of pastoral ministry. He noted that the couples who sustained deep and lasting love were not the couples who had married on the basis of physical attraction alone. They were the couples who had been drawn together by shared faith, shared values, and a mutual reverence for God. Physical attraction, Baxter acknowledged, has its proper place in marriage. God designed it, and we ought not to despise it. But it was never meant to be the foundation. When attraction is built upon the fear of the Lord, it endures. When it stands alone, it crumbles.

William Gouge offered a searching word to husbands on this point. He counseled men to value their wives not for the qualities that decline with age but for the qualities that increase with age: wisdom, patience, faithfulness, godliness, and depth of character. The husband who praises his wife for her fear of the Lord communicates something profoundly important. He tells her that her value in his eyes is not contingent upon her appearance, her youthfulness, or her ability to attract. It is rooted in something far more enduring, something that cannot be taken from her by time or circumstance.

Dear wife, this verse is both a comfort and a call. It is a comfort because it liberates you from the relentless pressure of a culture that measures your worth by your appearance. You do not need to compete with the impossible standards the world sets before you. Your true beauty is the fear of the Lord, and that beauty will never fade. It is also a call, because the fear of the Lord is not passive. It is an active, daily choice to live under God's authority, to seek His wisdom, to walk in His ways. This is the foundation upon which every other virtue in the Proverbs 31 portrait is built.

Dear husband, examine your heart tonight. What do you truly value in your wife? Do you praise her for qualities that endure, or have you allowed the world's shallow standards to shape what you admire? Learn to see your wife the way God sees her, and praise her accordingly.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: How has our culture's emphasis on outward appearance affected each of you personally? How has it affected your marriage?

Question 2: Husband, what evidence of the fear of the Lord do you see in your wife's life? Tell her specifically tonight.

Question 3: How can we, as a couple, guard against the deceitfulness of charm and the vanity of beauty, both in ourselves and in how we raise our children?

--- Prayer ---

Lord God, Thou who lookest not on the outward appearance but on the heart, forgive us for the times we have valued what is fleeting above what is eternal. Free us from the tyranny of a culture that worships youth and beauty. Teach us to fear Thee with a reverence that shapes every part of our lives. Help the wife to find her confidence not in the mirror but in Thy Word, and help the husband to praise her for the beauty that Thou Thyself dost prize. May our marriage be built not on the shifting sand of outward appearance but on the solid rock of the fear of the Lord. In Christ's holy name, Amen.
