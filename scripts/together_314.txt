PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 14
"Building Your House"
Proverbs 14:1


Welcome, husband and wife. There is something powerful about two hearts turning toward God at the same time. When you both face the same direction, toward Christ, everything in your marriage finds its proper alignment. Let us do that now.

Every marriage needs a foundation that does not shift. Let us hear from that foundation now, from Proverbs 14:1:

"Every wise woman buildeth her house: but the foolish plucketh it down with her hands."

That is Proverbs 14:1. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


The Puritans treasured marriage as a covenant made before God. Charles Bridges wrote beautifully about how Scripture speaks to the hearts of married couples.

Charles Bridges sees in this verse a principle that extends beyond gender to encompass every person who has a hand in shaping a home. While Solomon specifically addresses the woman here, Bridges notes that the husband is by no means exempt from the calling to build or the danger of tearing down. The 'house' in view is not merely a physical structure but the entire household: its spiritual health, its relational harmony, its moral character.

Bridges writes that building a house is a slow, deliberate, constructive process. It requires planning, patience, and persistent effort. The wise wife builds through her godly influence, her careful management, her nurturing of children, her support of her husband, and her cultivation of a household atmosphere that honors God. But Bridges is quick to add that the wise husband builds equally: through spiritual leadership, sacrificial love, diligent provision, and gentle strength.

Matthew Henry highlights the tragedy of the second clause: 'the foolish plucketh it down with her hands.' Henry writes: 'Sin is a great house-plunderer; it undoes families.' The work of destruction can happen quickly — through a single act of unfaithfulness, a pattern of harsh words, a season of neglect, or a refusal to repent. What took years to build can be torn down in a moment of folly.

Henry further observes that this tearing down often happens 'with her own hands,' meaning that the destruction is self-inflicted. The foolish spouse does not need an external enemy; the enemy is within. Laziness, selfishness, a bitter spirit, an uncontrolled tongue — these are the hands that pluck down what wisdom has built.


Your home is being built or demolished every day. There is no neutral ground. Every word you speak, every decision you make, every attitude you carry contributes either to the construction or the destruction of your household.

Ask yourselves: are we building or tearing down? Building looks like speaking words of encouragement instead of criticism. It looks like serving one another when you are tired. It looks like choosing forgiveness over grudges. It looks like investing in family worship, hospitality, and shared experiences that bind you together.

Tearing down looks like constant complaint, emotional withdrawal, neglecting spiritual disciplines together, criticizing your spouse before others, and letting small grievances accumulate into walls of resentment. Be builders together. Let your hands be instruments of construction, not demolition.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Now turn to each other. Here are tonight's reflection questions. Answer from the heart, not from the head.

Question 1: What am I currently building in our home, and is there anything I am tearing down, perhaps without realizing it?

Question 2: What does our 'house' look like right now — is it marked more by construction or demolition?

Question 3: What is one thing each of us can do this week to build up our home?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


The Puritans ended every evening with prayer. Let us follow their example and bring our marriage before the Lord.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray that God would make you wise builders of your home. Ask Him to reveal any areas where you have been tearing down with your own hands, and give you grace to construct a household that glorifies Him and shelters your family.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the Lord who builds His church also build your house, making it a dwelling of peace, righteousness, and love that endures for generations. Amen.

May the words you have heard tonight bear fruit in your marriage for years to come. The Lord be with you both. Until next time.
