PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 16
"The Wife's Duty of Respect"
William Gouge — Domestical Duties, Chapter 2


Welcome, dear couple. Tonight we continue in William Gouge's "Domestical Duties," turning now to his treatment of the wife's calling in marriage.

Our Scripture tonight is 1 Peter 3:1-2:

"Likewise, ye wives, be in subjection to your own husbands; that, if any obey not the word, they also may without the word be won by the conversation of the wives; While they behold your chaste conversation coupled with fear."

Peter presents a vision of the wife's influence that is quiet, powerful, and deeply Christlike.


William Gouge begins his treatment of the wife's duties with an important clarification that many miss. He insists that the wife's calling to honor her husband does not diminish her dignity, her intelligence, or her spiritual equality. He writes that in Christ, "there is neither male nor female" — husband and wife are equally redeemed, equally indwelt by the Spirit, equally heirs of the grace of life. The wife's submission is not a statement about her inferiority but about the order God has established within the marriage — an order designed not to oppress but to bless.

Gouge identifies several expressions of the wife's respect. First, an inward respect — a genuine esteem for her husband's calling and character. This does not mean ignoring his faults or pretending he is perfect. It means choosing to focus on his strengths, to honor his efforts, and to believe the best about his intentions.

Second, a respectful manner of speech. Gouge is remarkably balanced here. He does not counsel wives to be silent or passive. He affirms that wives should share their opinions, express their concerns, and offer counsel — all of which are acts of love. But he urges wives to do so in a way that is respectful rather than contemptuous, that builds up rather than tears down.

Third, obedience in lawful matters. Gouge is clear that a wife is never obligated to obey a command that contradicts God's Word. Her ultimate allegiance is to Christ, not to her husband. But in the ordinary decisions of daily life — where Scripture does not dictate a specific course — Gouge counsels wives to defer to their husbands' leadership, not because the husband is always right, but because the marriage needs a final decision-maker, and God has assigned that role to the husband.

Gouge was ahead of his time in one critical respect: he insisted that a wife's submission must never be used to justify abuse. He wrote that a husband who mistreats his wife has forfeited his right to demand obedience, and that the church has a responsibility to intervene on behalf of mistreated wives. This was a remarkably progressive position for the seventeenth century.

Peter's words in our passage tonight add another dimension. He says that a wife's godly character — her "chaste conversation coupled with fear" — has an evangelistic power that words alone cannot match. A wife who lives with integrity, who treats her husband with genuine respect, who radiates the peace of Christ — such a woman can win her husband's heart without preaching a single sermon.

Richard Baxter added that the wife's influence in the home is incalculable. "A wise wife is the crown of her husband," he wrote, echoing Proverbs 12:4. Her words of encouragement can sustain him through his darkest days. Her prayers can move heaven on his behalf. Her example of faith can inspire him to greater holiness. Never underestimate the power of a godly wife.


Discuss together:

Question 1: Wife, how do you currently express respect for your husband? Are there ways you could grow in this area?

Question 2: Husband, does your wife feel safe expressing her opinions and concerns? Do you receive her counsel with gratitude or defensiveness?

Question 3: Together, how can you navigate disagreements in a way that honors both the wife's wisdom and the husband's calling to lead?


Pray for the wife tonight. Ask God to fill her heart with genuine respect for her husband, to season her speech with wisdom and grace, and to give her influence that is quiet, powerful, and deeply Christlike. Pray that her husband would become the kind of man who makes respect easy.


May the Lord who adorns His people with the beauty of holiness give every wife a gentle and quiet spirit that is precious in His sight. May your marriage be marked by mutual honor and deep admiration. Grace be with you both. Goodnight.
