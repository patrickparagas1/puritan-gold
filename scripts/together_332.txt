PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 1
"Called Together as Saints"
Romans for Marriage — Day 1


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to tonight's couples devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for setting aside this time to draw near to God and to each other. The moments you invest in your marriage before the Lord are among the most important moments of your day.

Let us open God's Word together. Our passage tonight comes from Romans 1:1-7:

"Paul, a servant of Jesus Christ, called to be an apostle, separated unto the gospel of God, (Which he had promised afore by his prophets in the holy scriptures,) Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh; And declared to be the Son of God with power, according to the spirit of holiness, by the resurrection from the dead: By whom we have received grace and apostleship, for obedience to the faith among all nations, for his name: Among whom are ye also the called of Jesus Christ: To all that be in Rome, beloved of God, called to be saints: Grace to you and peace from God our Father, and the Lord Jesus Christ."

That is Romans 1:1-7. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Now let us hear from Richard Baxter, one of the great Puritan voices on this subject. The Puritans wrote extensively about marriage because they saw it as one of God's most profound gifts.

Paul opens his letter to the Romans by identifying himself as a servant and apostle — called, separated, and commissioned for a specific purpose. He then addresses the Roman believers as those who are also called: 'beloved of God, called to be saints.' This language of calling is profoundly important for understanding marriage in its fullest biblical dimension.

Richard Baxter, in his Christian Directory, taught that marriage is not merely a social arrangement or a romantic partnership — it is a divine calling. Just as Paul was called to apostleship, Christian couples are called to a specific mission within the body of Christ. Your marriage exists for a purpose larger than your personal happiness. You have been called together by God, beloved of God, and set apart as saints — holy ones — with a shared vocation.

Baxter emphasized that the word 'saints' does not refer to an elite spiritual class but to all believers who have been set apart by God's grace. When Paul says 'called to be saints,' he speaks of the identity that defines every true Christian. In marriage, both husband and wife share this identity. You are not merely husband and wife — you are fellow saints, co-laborers in the faith, partners in the Gospel.

The greeting Paul gives — 'Grace to you and peace from God our Father, and the Lord Jesus Christ' — is the foundation of every Christian marriage. Grace means unmerited favor: the love that keeps flowing even when it is not deserved. Peace means wholeness, completeness, shalom: the deep rest that comes from being right with God and right with each other. A marriage built on grace and peace from God is a marriage that can weather any storm.

Baxter counseled couples to begin each day by reminding each other of their shared calling. You did not choose each other by accident. God brought you together for His purposes. He has given you grace for the journey and peace for the trials. Your marriage is part of His plan to display the Gospel before a watching world. When you love each other with grace, you show the world how Christ loves His church. When you live together in peace, you demonstrate the reconciliation that the Gospel produces.


Begin your April journey through Romans by anchoring your marriage in the truth of your shared calling. You are not two individuals who happen to live together — you are saints called by God into a covenant partnership with eternal significance.

Discuss together: What is the specific calling of our marriage? What has God placed before us as a couple that we could not accomplish alone? Perhaps it is raising children who love Christ. Perhaps it is serving your church or community. Perhaps it is simply being a beacon of grace and peace in a fractured world.

Make a commitment this month to approach your marriage not as a convenience but as a calling. When conflict arises, remember: we are called to be saints together. When weariness sets in, remember: grace and peace come from God our Father. Your calling does not depend on your strength — it depends on His faithfulness.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Now let us pause for reflection. Here are some questions for you and your spouse to discuss together. Be honest. Be gentle. Listen more than you speak.

Question 1: What do you believe God's specific calling is for your marriage? Have you ever discussed this together?

Question 2: How does knowing you are 'beloved of God' change the way you treat each other daily?

Question 3: In what areas of your marriage do you most need God's grace and peace right now?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Let us move into a time of prayer together. If you are able, hold hands as you pray. There is something powerful about a husband and wife joining hands before the throne of grace.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray together that God would reveal the specific calling He has for your marriage. Ask Him to fill your home with grace and peace. Commit this month of study in Romans to the Lord, asking Him to transform your marriage through His Word.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the God who called you together fill your marriage with His grace and peace. May you walk as saints, beloved and set apart, for His glory and your joy.

May the Lord bless your marriage and keep it strong. May He make His face to shine upon your home, and give you peace. Goodnight, dear couple. Walk together in His love.
