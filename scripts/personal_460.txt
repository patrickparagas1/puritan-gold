PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Pastoral Encouragement, Day 29
"Stewardship of Your Spiritual Gifts"
Thomas Manton — Pastoral Counsel on Serving the Body

---

Good evening, dear brother or sister. Tonight I speak to you not as a lecturer but as a pastor, as a fellow traveler on the road of faith. Wherever you are tonight, whatever burden you carry, you are not alone. God is near, and He has a word for you.

--- Scripture ---

Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is 1 Peter 4:10-11:

"As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God. If any man speak, let him speak as the oracles of God; if any man minister, let him do it as of the ability which God giveth: that God in all things may be glorified through Jesus Christ, to whom be praise and dominion for ever and ever. Amen."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.

--- Pastoral Word ---

Every believer has received a gift — or more precisely, every believer has received a stewardship. The gifts of God are not given for private enjoyment but for public service. Peter's command is clear: 'As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another.' The purpose of spiritual gifts is not self-enrichment but body-building, not personal distinction but corporate edification.

Thomas Manton, one of the most prolific and careful expositors among the Puritans, addresses the stewardship of spiritual gifts with his characteristic blend of theological depth and practical application. Manton was a man who practiced what he preached — he served his congregation at Stoke Newington with tireless devotion, using his exceptional gifts of exposition and pastoral care for the building up of the saints.

Manton begins by emphasizing that every believer has at least one gift. There are no ungifted members of the body of Christ. Paul makes this clear in 1 Corinthians 12: 'The manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal.' The gifts differ in kind and degree, but no one is without one. The eye cannot say to the hand, 'I have no need of you,' nor can the hand say, 'Because I am not the eye, I do not belong to the body.' Every member is necessary, and every member is equipped.

Manton identifies a common and dangerous sin: the burying of spiritual gifts. Like the servant in the parable of the talents who hid his master's money in the ground, many believers receive gifts from God and never deploy them. They sit in church Sunday after Sunday, receiving ministry but never ministering, being built up but never building. Manton warns that this is not humility; it is disobedience. The steward who refuses to use his master's resources will answer to his master for the waste.

Why do believers bury their gifts? Manton identifies several reasons. First, fear. Many believers are afraid of failure, ridicule, or inadequacy. They compare their gifts with those of others and conclude that they have nothing worthwhile to offer. Manton responds that God does not require extraordinary gifts; He requires faithful use of ordinary ones. The woman who teaches a Sunday school class with modest ability but great faithfulness is more pleasing to God than the brilliant preacher who uses his gifts carelessly.

Second, laziness. The exercise of spiritual gifts requires effort. It is easier to sit and receive than to stand and serve. Manton counsels that the Christian life is not a spectator sport. Every member of the body has a function, and the body suffers when any member fails to fulfill it.

Third, ignorance. Some believers genuinely do not know what their gifts are. Manton counsels self-examination, prayer, and the counsel of other believers. Often, your brothers and sisters can see your gifts more clearly than you can. Ask them: where do you see God using me? What needs in the body am I equipped to meet? Their observations may reveal gifts you have never recognized.

Manton also addresses the spirit in which gifts are to be exercised. Peter says that the one who speaks should speak 'as the oracles of God,' and the one who serves should serve 'as of the ability which God giveth.' In other words, gifts are to be exercised with seriousness, humility, and dependence upon God. The man who uses his gifts for self-promotion has corrupted the stewardship. The man who uses his gifts with a sense of divine calling and divine enablement will find that God blesses both the service and the servant.

Patrick, what gifts has God given you, and how are you using them? Are you actively serving the body of Christ, deploying your gifts for the edification of others? Or are you burying them — consuming ministry without producing it, receiving without giving? The body needs you. Your particular gifts fill a particular need that no one else can fill in exactly the way God has equipped you to fill it. Do not let fear, laziness, or ignorance rob the church of what God has given you to give.

--- Reflection ---

Let us pause and reflect. Consider these questions in the quiet of your heart.

Question 1: Can I identify my spiritual gifts, and am I actively using them for the building up of the body of Christ?

Question 2: Is fear, laziness, or ignorance preventing me from serving the church with the gifts God has given me?

Question 3: Have I sought the counsel of other believers to help me identify where God has equipped me to serve?

Do not rush past these questions. Sit with them. Journal about them if you can. The Puritans believed that truth must be digested, not merely tasted.

--- Closing Prayer ---

Lord of the Church, You have given gifts to every member of Your body, and I am no exception. Forgive me for the times I have buried what You gave me to invest. Show me my gifts with clarity. Give me the courage to deploy them and the humility to use them for Your glory rather than my own. Let me be a faithful steward of the manifold grace of God, serving others with the ability that You supply, so that in all things You may be glorified through Jesus Christ. Amen.

Goodnight, friend. Walk with God tomorrow.
