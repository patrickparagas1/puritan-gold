PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 29
"Looking Back at Our Pilgrimage"
Pilgrim Families — Synthesis, Day 1


Good evening, dear family. Welcome to tonight's family devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for gathering together around God's Word. Let us quiet our hearts and prepare to listen to what the Lord has for us.

Let us begin with prayer. Heavenly Father, we thank You for this family. We thank You for bringing us safely through another day. Now we ask that You would open our eyes to see wonderful things in Your Word. Speak to each heart gathered here, from the oldest to the youngest. In Jesus' name we pray. Amen.

Tonight we open our Bibles to Psalm 77:11-12; Deuteronomy 8:2. Listen carefully as I read our passage:

"I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old. I will meditate also of all thy work, and talk of thy doings. And thou shalt remember all the way which the LORD thy God led thee these forty years in the wilderness, to humble thee, and to prove thee, to know what was in thine heart, whether thou wouldest keep his commandments, or no."

That is Psalm 77:11-12; Deuteronomy 8:2. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


As we near the end of our April pilgrimage, it is time to look back and remember. The Psalmist models this practice in Psalm 77:11-12: "I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old. I will meditate also of all thy work, and talk of thy doings." Three actions: remember, meditate, and talk. Looking back is not passive — it is an active discipline that strengthens faith for the road ahead.

God Himself commanded Israel to look back. In Deuteronomy 8:2, Moses instructed the people: "Thou shalt remember all the way which the LORD thy God led thee these forty years in the wilderness." God wanted them to remember the long, difficult journey — not just the miracles, but the humbling, the testing, the revealing of what was in their hearts. The wilderness was hard, but it was purposeful. Every step was led by God.

This month, our family has traveled with four great guides of the faith. In Week 1, John Bunyan showed us the pilgrim's journey — leaving the City of Destruction, struggling through the Slough of Despond, entering through the Wicket Gate, losing our burden at the Cross, climbing the Hill Difficulty, finding rest at the Palace Beautiful, and walking through the Valley of the Shadow of Death. Each scene taught us something vital about following Christ.

In Week 2, Jeremiah Burroughs taught us the rare jewel of contentment — finding satisfaction in God alone, discovering the mystery of joy in all circumstances, resisting the world's false promises, stopping the sin of murmuring, cultivating thankfulness, trusting God in family trials, and seeking Him first above all things.

In Week 3, Thomas Brooks armed us against Satan's devices — how the enemy makes sin look small, paints sin with virtue's colors, and tempts us with the secrecy of sin. And he gave us precious remedies: keeping eternity in view, fleeing temptation, staying in God's Word, and praying without ceasing.

In Week 4, Samuel Rutherford warmed our hearts with the love of Christ — showing us that Christ is altogether lovely, that suffering produces glory, that there is sweetness in trials, that waiting on God is worthwhile, that nothing separates us from His love, that heaven is our home, and that a crown of life awaits the faithful.

Looking back, we can trace the hand of God through every lesson. Like Israel looking back on forty years in the wilderness, we can see that God was leading us even when the path was difficult. He was humbling us through Burroughs' teaching on contentment. He was proving us through Brooks' teaching on spiritual warfare. He was comforting us through Rutherford's letters. He was guiding us through Bunyan's allegory.

For your family, tonight is a night of remembering. What has God taught you this month? Which lesson spoke most deeply to your heart? Which moment of family worship was most meaningful? Where did God meet you in a way you did not expect?

The Puritans kept spiritual journals — written records of God's dealings with their souls. They reviewed them regularly to remind themselves of God's faithfulness. Your family may not keep a journal, but you can practice the same discipline tonight by sharing your memories of this month's journey.

Remember — not as a sentimental exercise, but as an act of faith. When you remember what God has done, you build confidence for what He will do. The God who led you through April will lead you through May and beyond. He is the same yesterday, today, and forever. And His track record of faithfulness is your guarantee that He will never leave you or forsake you.


Now let us hear from one of the great Puritan teachers of the faith.

John Bunyan, in his work "The Pilgrim's Progress," wrote these words:

"He that is down needs fear no fall; he that is low, no pride; he that is humble ever shall have God to be his guide."

What John Bunyan is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Psalm 77:11-12; Deuteronomy 8:2 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Now it is time for our family discussion. Here are some questions to talk about together. Take your time with each one.

Question 1: Looking back at this month, which week's theme was most meaningful to your family? Why?

Question 2: Can you share one specific thing God taught you through these devotionals?

Question 3: Why does God command us to remember? How does looking back strengthen our faith for the future?

Question 4: What is one practice from this month that you want to continue as a family going forward?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old." -- Psalm 77:11

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a family activity you can do this week to help these truths take root:

Create a 'Pilgrimage Map' of your family's April journey. On a large piece of paper, draw the path from Day 1 to Day 29. Mark key landmarks — the City of Destruction, the Cross, the Hill Difficulty, the Valley of the Shadow. Then write your own family's spiritual landmarks alongside them — moments of growth, answered prayers, lessons learned. Keep this map as a memorial of what God has done.


Let us close in prayer. Heavenly Father, thank You for gathering our family together in Your Word tonight. Thank You for the truth we have heard from Psalm 77:11-12; Deuteronomy 8:2. Help us to live out what we have learned. Guard our hearts, guide our steps, and fill our home with Your love and peace. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

Goodnight, dear ones. Walk in wisdom tomorrow.
