PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 10
"Trusting God in Family Trials"
John Flavel — Providence and Family Trust

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight John Flavel addresses one of the hardest aspects of family life — walking through trials together — and how the doctrine of providence sustains us when the road is rough. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, You have promised never to leave us or forsake us. When trials come to our family, help us to hold fast to that promise. Teach us tonight how to trust You in the hard seasons and to find Your grace sufficient for every need. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from James 1:2-4 and 1 Peter 1:6-7. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing. Wherein ye greatly rejoice, though now for a season, if need be, ye are in heaviness through manifold temptations: That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ."

That is James 1:2-4 and 1 Peter 1:6-7. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Every family will face trials. This is not a possibility but a certainty. Illness will visit your home. Financial pressures will squeeze your budget. Relationships will be strained. Children will face difficulties that break their parents' hearts. Loss, disappointment, and pain are woven into the fabric of life in a fallen world. The question is not whether trials will come, but how your family will respond when they do.

John Flavel knew trials intimately. His first wife died in childbirth. His second wife also died. He was ejected from his pulpit and hunted by authorities for years. He preached in forests and on the seashore to avoid arrest. And yet, through it all, Flavel maintained a faith that was unshakable — not because his circumstances were easy, but because his God was faithful.

Flavel taught that every trial a family faces is designed by God for a specific purpose. Trials are not random blows struck by a capricious fate. They are measured instruments wielded by a loving Father who knows exactly what His children need. James tells us that the testing of our faith produces patience — that steady, enduring quality that enables us to persevere under pressure without losing heart. Peter tells us that our trials are refining fires that purify our faith as gold is purified by flames, burning away the dross of self-reliance, worldliness, and spiritual laziness.

For families, trials have a unique bonding power. When a family walks through difficulty together — holding hands, praying together, encouraging one another — the experience creates a depth of connection that prosperity alone cannot produce. Flavel observed that some of the strongest and most loving families he knew were not those that had been sheltered from difficulty but those that had walked through the fire together and come out on the other side with their faith intact and their love deepened.

Flavel also warned against the spiritual dangers that trials present. When suffering comes, we are tempted to doubt God's love, to blame Him for our pain, or to turn away from Him in anger. Flavel addressed each of these temptations with pastoral tenderness. He reminded suffering families that God's love is not measured by the absence of pain but by the presence of His grace in the midst of pain. He pointed them to Christ, who Himself was "a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief" (Isaiah 53:3). The God we worship is not a distant deity who observes our suffering from afar. He is a God who entered into our suffering, bore it on His own body, and conquered it through His death and resurrection.

Flavel offered three practical counsels for families in seasons of trial. First, pray more, not less. The natural tendency in suffering is to withdraw from God, but this is precisely when we need Him most. Gather your family for prayer. Be honest with God about your pain. Pour out your hearts before Him. He can handle your tears and your questions. Second, remember God's past faithfulness. In the midst of present suffering, it is easy to forget what God has already done. Take time as a family to recount His mercies — the times He provided, the prayers He answered, the ways He sustained you in previous trials. Past faithfulness is a pledge of future faithfulness. Third, keep your eyes on eternity. Our present sufferings are not the end of the story. They are a brief chapter in an eternal narrative that ends in unimaginable glory. Paul wrote, "For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory" (2 Corinthians 4:17). The trials of today are producing a harvest of glory that will last forever.

Tonight, if your family is currently walking through a trial, take heart. You are not alone. God is with you. He is at work in your suffering. And He will bring you through to the other side, refined and strengthened and more deeply dependent on Him than ever before.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Why does God allow families to go through trials? What purposes might He have in our suffering?

Question 2: Flavel said that trials have a unique bonding power for families. Have you seen this in our own family? How has a shared difficulty drawn us closer together?

Question 3: What are Flavel's three practical counsels for families in trials — pray more, remember God's faithfulness, and keep your eyes on eternity? Which one does our family need to practice most right now?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ." -- 1 Peter 1:7

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Father, we do not always understand Your purposes in our trials, but we trust that You are good and that You are working all things for our good. Strengthen our family in whatever difficulties we face. Draw us closer to You and to one another. Let our faith be refined like gold in the fire. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that the refining fire is held in the hands of a loving Father.
