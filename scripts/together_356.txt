PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 25
"Owe Nothing but Love"
Romans for Marriage — Day 25

---

Good evening. What a blessing it is to see a husband and wife seeking the Lord together. The Puritans believed that a praying couple is an unstoppable force for the kingdom of God. Let us pray together tonight.

The Word of God has sustained marriages for thousands of years. Tonight it speaks to yours from Romans 13:8:

"Owe no man any thing, but to love one another: for he that loveth another hath fulfilled the law."

That is Romans 13:8. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher. William Gouge reflected deeply on the truths of Scripture and how they apply to the covenant of marriage.

Paul reduces all human obligation to a single, inexhaustible debt: the debt of love. 'Owe no man any thing, but to love one another.' Every other debt can be paid in full. But the debt of love can never be fully discharged. You can never reach a point where you have loved enough. The obligation to love is permanent, daily, and increasing.

William Gouge, whose domestic theology shaped Puritan family life for generations, applied this verse with particular force to the marriage covenant. In marriage, Gouge taught, the debt of love is the primary obligation that encompasses all others. The husband's duty to provide, protect, and lead — all are expressions of love. The wife's duty to respect, support, and nurture — all are expressions of love. Strip away every other marital obligation, and you are left with this: love one another.

Gouge observed that many couples turn marriage into a ledger of debts and credits. 'I did the dishes, so you owe me.' 'I apologized first last time, so it is your turn.' This transactional approach poisons love because it turns the relationship into a business arrangement. Love keeps no accounts. Love does not calculate returns on investment. Love gives freely and continues giving regardless of what is received in return.

Paul says that 'he that loveth another hath fulfilled the law.' All the commandments — do not commit adultery, do not lie, do not covet, do not steal — are summed up in love. If you truly love your spouse, you will not betray them. If you truly love them, you will not deceive them. If you truly love them, you will not envy what others have. Love is the fulfillment of every obligation.

Gouge challenged couples to ask themselves each evening: 'Did I love well today?' Not 'Did I complete my tasks?' or 'Did my spouse meet my expectations?' but simply 'Did I love?' If the answer is yes, the day was a success — regardless of everything else. If the answer is no, nothing else matters. Love is the measure of all things in marriage.

--- Marriage Application ---

Tonight, simplify your marriage down to its essence: love. You owe each other nothing but love. Not perfect performance. Not flawless housekeeping. Not constant agreement. Just love.

Ask yourselves: Have we been turning our marriage into a ledger? Have we been keeping score of who did what and who owes whom? If so, tear up the ledger tonight. Declare a jubilee of debt forgiveness. You owe nothing but love.

Then ask each other the simplest and most profound question: 'How can I love you better?' Listen carefully to the answer. And then do it — not to earn credit, but because love is the one debt you joyfully owe forever.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Here are some questions to guide your conversation tonight. Take turns sharing, and give each other the gift of your full attention.

Question 1: In what ways have you been keeping a 'ledger' in your marriage — tracking debts and credits?

Question 2: If love is the only debt you owe, how does that simplify the way you approach your marriage?

Question 3: Ask your spouse tonight: 'How can I love you better?' and commit to following through.

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Prayer is the heartbeat of a Christian marriage. Let us pray together now. You do not need eloquent words, just honest ones.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to fill your marriage with love — the kind that keeps no record of wrongs, that gives without expecting return, and that never runs out. Pray that love would be the currency of your home.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May you owe each other nothing but love — lavish, unconditional, inexhaustible love. May it flow between you like a river that never runs dry, fulfilling every law and covering every failure.

May your love for each other grow deeper with every passing day, and may your love for God be the source and the strength of it all. Goodnight, dear ones.
