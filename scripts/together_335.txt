PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 4
"Heart Transformation Over Outward Show"
Romans for Marriage — Day 4

---

Good evening. What a blessing it is to see a husband and wife seeking the Lord together. The Puritans believed that a praying couple is an unstoppable force for the kingdom of God. Let us pray together tonight.

The Word of God has sustained marriages for thousands of years. Tonight it speaks to yours from Romans 2:28-29:

"For he is not a Jew, which is one outwardly; neither is that circumcision, which is outward in the flesh: But he is a Jew, which is one inwardly; and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter; whose praise is not of men, but of God."

That is Romans 2:28-29. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher. John Owen reflected deeply on the truths of Scripture and how they apply to the covenant of marriage.

Paul draws a vital distinction between outward conformity and inward transformation. The Jews of his day prided themselves on external markers of religion — circumcision, dietary laws, Sabbath observance — while their hearts remained unchanged. Paul insists that true identity before God is a matter of the heart, not of outward appearance.

John Owen, the great Puritan theologian, devoted much of his life's work to the theme of inward spiritual reality versus outward religious show. In his treatise on the mortification of sin, Owen argued that the greatest danger facing Christians is not open rebellion against God but the subtle substitution of external religiosity for genuine heart transformation.

This distinction is devastatingly relevant to marriage. It is entirely possible to have a marriage that looks perfect on the outside while the hearts within are cold, bitter, and distant. You attend church together. You say grace before meals. You post smiling family photos. But behind closed doors, there is resentment, contempt, emotional distance, or simply indifference masquerading as peace.

Owen taught that God is not impressed by outward performance. He looks at the heart. 'Man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart' (1 Samuel 16:7). A marriage that is pleasing to God is not one that merely maintains appearances — it is one where both hearts are genuinely turned toward God and toward each other.

True heart transformation in marriage means that your love for your spouse is not an act you perform but a reality that flows from your relationship with Christ. It means that forgiveness is genuine, not merely verbal. It means that kindness comes from the heart, not from a sense of obligation. It means that your marriage is the same behind closed doors as it is in public. Owen would say: let your marriage be 'in the spirit, and not in the letter' — animated by the Holy Spirit, not merely conforming to external expectations.

--- Marriage Application ---

Ask yourselves tonight: Is our marriage more about outward show or inward reality? Are we performing a marriage for others to see, or are we cultivating a marriage that God sees and approves?

This is not about perfection — it is about honesty. An honest marriage that admits its struggles is healthier than a polished marriage that hides them. God is not looking for couples who have it all together. He is looking for couples whose hearts are genuinely turned toward Him and each other.

Identify one area of your marriage where you may be maintaining an outward appearance while the inward reality needs attention. It might be unresolved conflict, emotional distance, spiritual dryness, or unforgiveness. Bring it into the light tonight — with each other and with God.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Here are some questions to guide your conversation tonight. Take turns sharing, and give each other the gift of your full attention.

Question 1: Is there an area where our marriage looks good on the outside but needs work on the inside?

Question 2: How can we cultivate genuine heart transformation rather than just outward religious behavior?

Question 3: What would it look like for our marriage to seek God's praise rather than the praise of people?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Prayer is the heartbeat of a Christian marriage. Let us pray together now. You do not need eloquent words, just honest ones.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray for authentic hearts. Ask God to search your marriage and reveal any areas of pretense or performance. Invite the Holy Spirit to do a deep, inward work that transforms your love from the inside out.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May God give you hearts of flesh where there are hearts of stone. May your marriage be real, honest, and transformed by the Spirit — not for the praise of men, but for the glory of God.

May your love for each other grow deeper with every passing day, and may your love for God be the source and the strength of it all. Goodnight, dear ones.
