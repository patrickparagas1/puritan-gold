PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 1
"The Law of Love in Our Home"
Thomas Watson — The Ten Commandments


Good evening, dear family. Welcome to a new month of Puritan Gold family devotionals. This June, we are exploring what we are calling "Family Virtues," and we begin with one of the greatest Puritan teachers of all, Thomas Watson, and his exposition of the Ten Commandments applied to our life together at home. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Heavenly Father, we thank You for gathering us as a family this evening. We ask that You would open our eyes to behold wondrous things out of Your law. Teach us to love Your commandments and to see them not as burdens but as gifts of love. Shape our home by Your holy Word. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Exodus 20:1-3. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"And God spake all these words, saying, I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage. Thou shalt have no other gods before me."

That is Exodus 20:1-3. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


We begin our month of family virtues with the very first commandment: "Thou shalt have no other gods before me." Thomas Watson, the great Puritan preacher of the seventeenth century, devoted careful attention to each of the Ten Commandments in his masterful exposition of the Westminster Shorter Catechism. Watson understood that the commandments are not cold, legal demands imposed upon unwilling subjects. They are the loving instructions of a Father who has redeemed His children and now teaches them how to live in His household.

Notice how God introduces the commandments. Before He gives a single command, He reminds His people of who He is and what He has done: "I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage." The commandments flow from redemption. God does not say, "Obey Me, and then I will save you." He says, "I have already saved you; now here is how My redeemed children live." This changes everything about how we understand the law. It is not the price of God's love. It is the response to God's love.

Watson taught that the first commandment is the foundation of all the rest. If we get the first commandment right, every other commandment falls into its proper place. And if we get the first commandment wrong, no amount of external obedience to the other nine will matter. The first commandment is about the heart. It is about what we worship, what we treasure, what we love above all else.

Now, what does this mean for your family tonight? A family is a small kingdom, and every kingdom has a throne. The question for every household is simple but searching: Who sits on the throne of our family life? Is it God, or is it something else?

Watson would tell us that idolatry is not limited to bowing before statues. Idolatry is anything that takes the place of God in our affections, our priorities, or our trust. For families today, the idols may be subtle but no less real. We can make an idol of our children's success, pouring all our energy into academic achievement, athletic performance, or social standing, while neglecting the cultivation of their souls. We can make an idol of comfort, arranging our family life around ease and convenience rather than around worship and service. We can make an idol of financial security, living as though our provision comes from our own labor rather than from the hand of God.

Watson wrote that the first commandment requires us to know God, to love God, to trust God, to worship God, and to obey God with our whole hearts. When a family places God first, everything else finds its proper order. The parents lead in worship. The children are taught that God is the center of all things. Family decisions are made in the light of God's Word. Family time is structured around God's priorities. This does not mean the family becomes joyless or severe. Quite the opposite. Watson believed that true joy is found only in the God who made us, and that a family centered on God is a family overflowing with genuine happiness.

Consider tonight what it would look like for your family to renew its commitment to the first commandment. What would change if every member of your household truly said, "The Lord is my God, and I will have no other gods before Him"? Perhaps you would pray more together. Perhaps you would spend less time on screens and more time in the Scriptures. Perhaps you would speak more often of God's goodness at the dinner table. Perhaps you would begin to see every moment of family life — the joyful and the difficult — as an opportunity to glorify the One who sits upon the throne of your home.

Watson reminds us that God is a jealous God — not with the petty jealousy of insecurity, but with the holy jealousy of a husband who loves his bride too much to share her with another. God desires the whole heart of your family, not because He is needy, but because He knows that your family will only flourish when He is first. Every lesser god will eventually disappoint, enslave, and destroy. Only the true God gives life, freedom, and lasting joy.

Let this first evening of our month together be a fresh beginning. Let the first commandment be the foundation upon which we build every family virtue that follows.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: God introduces the Ten Commandments by reminding Israel of their redemption. Why does He begin with grace before giving law? How does remembering God's grace change the way we obey Him?

Question 2: Thomas Watson said that idolatry is not just bowing to statues but giving anything the place that belongs to God alone. What are some subtle idols that families today might struggle with?

Question 3: What would it look like for our family to put God more fully on the throne of our home this month? What is one specific change we could make together?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Thou shalt have no other gods before me." -- Exodus 20:3

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Let us pray together. Lord God, we confess that we have often placed other things before You. Forgive us. Renew our hearts this evening and help us to love You with all our heart, soul, mind, and strength. Make our home a place where You alone are worshipped and honored. Bless our family as we begin this new month together. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that the Lord your God watches over your family with unfailing love.
