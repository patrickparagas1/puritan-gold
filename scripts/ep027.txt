PURITAN GOLD — Episode 27
"The Lord Directs Our Steps"
Week 4, Day 6 (Saturday) — Proverbs 16

---

I want to tell you about a man who had a plan.

He'd mapped out his life with precision. He knew what career he'd pursue, where he'd live, whom he'd marry. He had timelines and milestones. He was disciplined, focused, and determined. And none of it... none of it... went the way he planned.

The career fell through. The relationship ended. The city he'd chosen became a dead end. And for a long time, he was angry. Angry at circumstances. Angry at himself. Angry, if he was honest, at God.

But years later — years and years later — looking back with the clarity that only time and grace can give, he realized something. Every closed door, every broken plan, every detour he never would have chosen... had led him to exactly where God wanted him. Not where he wanted to be — but where God wanted him.

Proverbs 16:9 puts it this way: "The heart of man plans his way, but the Lord establishes his steps."

And that, friends, is the great theme of Proverbs 16. This is the chapter where Solomon pulls back the curtain on the deepest truth of human existence: we plan, but God directs. We propose, but He disposes. And His purposes — always, without exception — are good.

Welcome to Puritan Gold. Let's open this chapter together.

---

Proverbs 16 is unique among the chapters of Proverbs because it contains one of the densest concentrations of verses about God's sovereignty in the entire book. Nearly every other verse points us back to the Lord's absolute control over all things. It's as if Solomon wants to make sure we don't miss it — so he says it again and again, from different angles, in different images.

Let's start at the very beginning. Verse 1: "The plans of the heart belong to man, but the answer of the tongue is from the Lord."

Even your words — the things that come out of your mouth — are ultimately governed by God. You plan what to say, but the Lord directs what actually comes forth. This doesn't mean we're puppets. It means we're creatures — creatures living within the sovereign purposes of a God who is infinitely wiser than we are.

Verse 2: "All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes, but the Lord weighs the spirit."

We justify ourselves constantly, don't we? We rationalize. We explain away. We tell ourselves that our motives are pure. But God weighs the spirit. He looks beneath the surface. He sees what we hide even from ourselves. And His assessment is the one that matters.

And then verse 3 — and this is a verse I wish every anxious, overwhelmed person would memorize: "Commit your work to the Lord, and your plans will be established."

Commit. The Hebrew word here carries the image of rolling something over — like rolling a heavy burden off your back and onto someone stronger. Roll your work onto the Lord. Give it to Him. Stop carrying it alone. And watch what happens: your plans will be established. Not necessarily the plans you had in mind — but the plans He has for you. And His plans are always better.

---

Now we come to verse 4 — a verse we touched on earlier this week, but it deserves a closer look. "The Lord has made everything for its purpose, even the wicked for the day of trouble."

This is one of the most comprehensive statements of divine sovereignty in all of Scripture. Everything has a purpose. Nothing is random. Nothing is meaningless. Nothing falls outside the scope of God's eternal plan.

And yes — even the wicked have a place in that plan. This doesn't mean God is the author of evil. The Westminster Confession is careful to affirm that God ordains whatsoever comes to pass, yet He is not the author of sin, nor is violence done to the will of the creatures. There is a mystery here — a deep, holy mystery — in how a sovereign God governs a world in which evil exists. But the mystery doesn't negate the truth: God is in control. Of everything.

John Flavel, the Puritan pastor who lost two wives and experienced shipwreck and persecution, wrote with remarkable serenity about divine providence. He said: "The providence of God is like Hebrew writing — it can only be read backward." And that's profoundly true. In the moment, God's providences often look confusing, painful, even cruel. But looking backward — with the eyes of faith — you begin to see the pattern. You begin to see the purpose. You begin to see the hand of a wise and loving Father who was working all things together for your good.

---

Let's come now to verse 9 — the verse I opened with. "The heart of man plans his way, but the Lord establishes his steps."

This is the verse for every control freak. And I say that with love, because I'm one of them. We want to plan everything. We want to know the outcome before we take the first step. We want guarantees. We want certainty.

But God says: Plan away. It's good to plan. I've given you a mind for a reason. But know this — your plans are not the final word. My purpose is. I will establish your steps. I will lead you where you need to go. Trust Me.

Spurgeon preached on this verse with characteristic warmth. He said: "We may plan our journey, but God determines our path. And His path is always better than ours, though it rarely seems so at the time." That last phrase is key — "though it rarely seems so at the time." In the moment, God's redirections feel like interruptions. They feel like failures. They feel like the end of the world. But they're not. They're the beginning of something you couldn't see from where you were standing.

---

Now let's look at verse 18 — one of the most quoted verses in all of Proverbs: "Pride goes before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall."

If there is one sin that the book of Proverbs warns against more consistently than any other, it's pride. And Proverbs 16:18 is the sharpest expression of that warning. Pride goes before destruction. Not might go. Goes. It's a law of the spiritual universe, as reliable as gravity.

And why? Because pride is the fundamental posture of the soul that says, "I don't need God. I can direct my own steps. I can make my own way. I am the captain of my fate." And that posture puts you on a collision course with the God who says, "I direct all steps. I govern all things. I alone am sovereign."

Jonathan Edwards, in his great treatise on religious affections, wrote that "nothing is more contrary to the true spirit of Christianity than spiritual pride." And he went on to argue that the truest mark of genuine grace is not boldness or theological precision or even zeal — it's humility. The soul that has truly encountered God is the soul that has been humbled.

And the connection to everything else in Proverbs 16 is clear: if the Lord directs our steps, if He has made everything for its purpose, if our plans only succeed when committed to Him — then what room is there for pride? None. Absolutely none.

---

Before we close, I want to draw your attention to one more verse — verse 31: "Gray hair is a crown of glory; it is gained in a righteous life."

In a culture obsessed with youth, this is a countercultural statement. Solomon says that old age is not a curse — it's a crown. Gray hair is not something to be hidden or erased. It's something to be honored. Because it represents a life lived. Lessons learned. Faithfulness sustained over decades.

The Puritans revered their elders. They sought their counsel. They honored their experience. They understood that wisdom is not something you download from the internet — it's something that grows slowly, over years, in the soil of obedience and suffering.

If you're young, honor the aged. Seek out the gray-haired saints in your church and ask them what they've learned. Their stories are worth more than a thousand books.

And if you're older — if you've walked with God for decades — know this: your life is a crown. Your faithfulness matters. Your testimony is priceless. Don't underestimate the impact of a life well-lived in the fear of the Lord.

---

So as we close this Saturday reflection, let me bring it all together. Proverbs 16 is about surrender. Not the surrender of passivity — but the surrender of trust. It's about releasing your grip on the future and placing it in the hands of a God who has made everything for its purpose.

You can plan. You should plan. But hold those plans loosely. Because the Lord is the one who establishes your steps. And His path — winding, surprising, sometimes painful — is always leading you somewhere good.

Roll your burdens onto Him. Let Him carry what you were never meant to carry alone. And when pride whispers that you've got this, that you don't need help, that you can figure it out on your own — remember verse 18. Remember the fall that follows the haughty spirit. And choose humility instead.

Because the humble life... the surrendered life... the life committed to the Lord — that's the life whose plans are truly established. Not on the shifting sand of human ambition, but on the unshakable rock of God's sovereign purpose.

Let's pray.

Lord God, we come before You with open hands. We release our plans, our timelines, our expectations. We confess that we've held them too tightly, as if our grip on the future could make it secure. But only You can establish our steps. Only You can make our plans succeed. Forgive our pride. Humble us, Father. Teach us to commit our work to You — truly, wholly, without reservation. And when Your path diverges from ours, give us the grace to trust that You see what we cannot. You know what we do not. And You love us more than we will ever fully understand. Lead us, Lord. Every step. All the way home. In the name of Jesus Christ, who walked the hardest road so that we might walk in life. Amen.
