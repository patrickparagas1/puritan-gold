PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 21
"The Legacy of a Faithful Home"
Matthew Henry — Family Worship and Bible Study


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. We close our week with Matthew Henry by considering the legacy that a faithful, Bible-saturated home leaves behind. Henry's own life is the proof: a godly home produced one of the greatest gifts the church has ever received. Let us come before the Lord.

Let us pray. Eternal Father, we thank You that the work we do in our homes is not in vain. The seeds we plant today will bear fruit for generations to come. Give us long-term vision and daily faithfulness. Help us to build a legacy of faith that will outlive us. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Psalm 78:1-7. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Give ear, O my people, to my law: incline your ears to the words of my mouth. I will open my mouth in a parable: I will utter dark sayings of old: Which we have heard and known, and our fathers have told us. We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the Lord, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done. For he established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children: That the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to their children: That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments."

That is Psalm 78:1-7. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Psalm 78 paints one of the most beautiful pictures of generational faithfulness in all of Scripture. The psalmist declares that the truths of God are to be passed from generation to generation — from fathers to children, from children to grandchildren, from grandchildren to those not yet born. Each generation receives the faith from the one before it and is charged with transmitting it faithfully to the one that follows. This is the golden chain of household faith, and Matthew Henry is one of its finest links.

Consider the chain that produced Matthew Henry's commentary. Philip Henry, Matthew's father, was a man of deep personal faith, rigorous theological conviction, and unwavering commitment to family worship. Every day, he gathered his household around the Scriptures and led them in worship and prayer. He catechized his children. He taught them to love God's Word. He modeled for them a life of pastoral faithfulness under severe persecution.

The result? His son Matthew became one of the most influential Bible commentators in the history of the church. Matthew Henry's commentary has been in continuous print for over three hundred years. It has been read by millions. It influenced Charles Spurgeon, George Whitefield, John Wesley, and countless others. Spurgeon called it "the first among the mighty" of Bible commentaries. And it all began — not in a university, not in a publishing house — but at a family altar in a humble Puritan home.

This is the legacy of a faithful home. It reaches further than we can see and lasts longer than we can imagine. The prayers prayed tonight around your table may bear fruit a hundred years from now, in lives you will never meet. The verses memorized by your children this week may sustain them through trials you cannot foresee. The habits of worship and Bible study cultivated in your home may produce pastors, missionaries, teachers, and servants of Christ for generations to come.

Henry himself was acutely aware of this. He wrote that the most lasting contribution any person can make to the world is not a building, not a business, not a fortune — but a family raised in the fear of the Lord. Buildings crumble. Businesses fail. Fortunes are spent. But the truth of God, faithfully transmitted from parents to children, endures forever.

The psalmist gives us the purpose of this generational transmission: "That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments." Three things: hope in God, remembrance of God's works, and obedience to God's commands. This is what a faithful home produces — children who hope, who remember, and who obey. Not perfectly, for no home and no child is perfect. But genuinely, deeply, and enduringly.

Henry also understood that the chain can be broken. If one generation fails to transmit the faith, the next generation may never receive it. The stakes are immeasurably high. We are not merely teaching our children a set of religious practices. We are passing on the very knowledge of God — the most precious treasure in the universe. And if we fail to pass it on, it may be lost to our descendants forever.

But let us not end with a burden. Let us end with a promise. God is the ultimate keeper of the chain. He who "established a testimony in Jacob and appointed a law in Israel" is the same God who sustains His truth across the centuries. Our part is to be faithful — to read, to pray, to teach, to worship, to love. God's part is to bless our faithfulness with fruit that endures. And He is always faithful to His part.

Tonight, as we close this week with Matthew Henry, let us renew our commitment to being a link in the golden chain. Let us not hide the truth from our children. Let us show them the praises of the Lord, His strength, and His wonderful works. And let us trust that the God who sustained the Henry family through persecution and produced a commentary that has blessed millions will sustain our family and use our faithfulness in ways we cannot yet imagine.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Philip Henry's faithful family worship produced a son who blessed millions through his commentary. What legacy do we hope our family's faith will leave behind?

Question 2: Psalm 78 says we must tell our children about God's works so that they will "set their hope in God." What stories of God's faithfulness should our family be passing down?

Question 3: What is one practice of family worship or Bible study that we want to make a permanent part of our family's life?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the Lord, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done." -- Psalm 78:4

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for the legacy of faithful families who have kept the chain of faith unbroken across the centuries. Help us to be faithful links in that chain. Give us the discipline to worship together, the wisdom to teach our children, and the faith to trust You with the results. May the generation to come set their hope in You because of what they learned in this home. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
