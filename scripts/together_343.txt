PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 12
"A Surrendered Marriage"
Romans for Marriage — Day 12

---

Hello and welcome to your couples devotional. Whether you are sitting side by side on the couch or listening together at the end of a long day, God is here with you both. He meets married couples in a special way when they turn their hearts to Him together.

Let me read our passage for this evening. It comes from Romans 6:13:

"Neither yield ye your members as instruments of unrighteousness unto sin: but yield yourselves unto God, as those that are alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness unto God."

That is Romans 6:13. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Richard Baxter was a faithful pastor and theologian whose words still carry weight centuries later. Listen to what the Puritans had to say about the truths in tonight's passage.

Paul calls believers to a decisive act of surrender: yield yourselves unto God. The word 'yield' carries the sense of presenting, offering, placing at someone's disposal. It is the language of total surrender — not holding anything back, not maintaining private reserves, but giving everything over to God's service.

Richard Baxter understood that the greatest obstacle to godliness in marriage is not external pressure but internal resistance — the refusal to fully surrender our lives, our rights, and our desires to God. In his Christian Directory, Baxter taught that a truly surrendered marriage is one where both husband and wife have first surrendered themselves to God. You cannot give yourself fully to your spouse until you have given yourself fully to your Lord.

Paul presents a stark choice: yield your members as instruments of unrighteousness or as instruments of righteousness. Every word you speak to your spouse is yielded to one master or the other. Every action, every response, every decision is an instrument of either sin or righteousness. There is no neutral ground. Your hands either serve God or serve self. Your tongue either builds up or tears down. Your heart either loves sacrificially or loves selfishly.

Baxter counseled couples to begin each day by consciously presenting themselves to God: 'Lord, today my words belong to You. My hands belong to You. My attitudes belong to You. Use me as an instrument of righteousness in my marriage.' This daily surrender transforms the mundane activities of married life — cooking, cleaning, conversation, conflict — into acts of worship.

The phrase 'as those that are alive from the dead' is crucial. You yield yourselves not as slaves resigned to their fate, but as people who have been raised from the dead and are alive with gratitude. Surrender is not drudgery — it is the joyful response of someone who has been given new life. When you remember that you were dead in sin and God made you alive in Christ, every act of surrender becomes an act of thanksgiving.

--- Marriage Application ---

A surrendered marriage is not a marriage where one spouse dominates and the other submits. It is a marriage where both spouses have submitted their entire selves to God and, from that place of surrender, serve each other freely and joyfully.

Tonight, practice a simple act of surrender together. Each of you, in prayer, present your marriage to God: 'Lord, this marriage belongs to You. Our home belongs to You. Our future belongs to You. Use us as instruments of Your righteousness.'

Then ask each other: Is there an area of our marriage where I am holding back — where I am yielding to selfishness rather than to God? Listen without defensiveness. Respond with grace. And together, surrender that area to the Lord.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Let us reflect together. These questions are designed to help you go deeper, both with God and with each other.

Question 1: Are there areas of your marriage where you are yielding to selfishness rather than to God?

Question 2: What does it mean practically to present your words, attitudes, and actions to God each morning?

Question 3: How does remembering you are 'alive from the dead' change the way you approach surrender?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

As we near the end of tonight's devotional, let us bring our hearts before God in prayer. Pray for each other. Pray for your marriage. Pray for your home.

Tonight's prayer focus: Present your marriage to God as a living offering. Surrender your plans, your expectations, your rights, and your future to His lordship. Ask Him to use your marriage as an instrument of righteousness for His kingdom.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May your marriage be a holy offering, yielded completely to God. May every word, every touch, and every decision be an instrument of His righteousness and love.

The Lord bless you and keep you. The Lord make His face shine upon you and be gracious to you. The Lord lift up His countenance upon you both, and give you peace. Amen.
