PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 16
"Satan's Device: Painting Sin with Virtue's Colors"
Pilgrim Families — Week 3, Day 2

---

Hello, dear ones. How wonderful it is to sit together as a family and open the Scriptures. Welcome to Puritan Gold's family devotional. Let us begin with thankful hearts.

Let us pray together. Lord God, we come before You as a family, grateful for Your love and Your provision. We ask that You would quiet our minds and soften our hearts as we hear from You tonight. Help us to understand, to remember, and to obey what You teach us. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from 2 Corinthians 11:14; Isaiah 5:20. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"And no marvel; for Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light. Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness; that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!"

That is 2 Corinthians 11:14; Isaiah 5:20. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Satan's second major device is more subtle than the first. Not only does he make sin look small — he makes sin look good. He paints sin with the colors of virtue so that what is actually rebellion against God appears to be something noble, brave, or even righteous.

Paul warns us in 2 Corinthians 11:14 that "Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light." The devil does not always appear in red with horns and a pitchfork. More often, he disguises himself as something beautiful, something reasonable, something enlightened. His most effective temptations do not look like temptations at all — they look like wisdom, progress, compassion, or freedom.

Isaiah pronounces a devastating woe in Isaiah 5:20: "Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness; that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!" This is one of the most relevant warnings in all of Scripture for our present age. We live in a time when good is increasingly called evil and evil is increasingly called good. Biblical morality is labeled intolerance. Sin is celebrated as authenticity. Rebellion against God is praised as courage.

Brooks observed that Satan takes every sin and gives it a virtuous-sounding name. Covetousness is called "ambition." Pride is called "self-confidence." Gossip is called "sharing prayer requests." Laziness is called "self-care." Anger is called "passion for justice." Disobedience to parents is called "finding your own voice." The sin does not change — but the label makes it seem acceptable, even admirable.

This device is particularly dangerous for children growing up in a culture that has inverted God's moral order. They are told that standing for biblical truth is "hateful" and that affirming sin is "loving." They hear that self-expression is the highest good, regardless of what is being expressed. They learn that feelings are the ultimate authority, not God's Word. In such an environment, the ability to discern between true virtue and painted sin is a survival skill.

How do we combat this device? First, we must know God's Word thoroughly. The only way to recognize counterfeit virtue is to be deeply familiar with the real thing. A bank teller trained to detect counterfeit bills does not study counterfeits — they study genuine currency so carefully that anything false stands out immediately. Likewise, the family that is saturated in Scripture will have a built-in detector for painted sin.

Second, we must test everything by the standard of God's Word, not by the standard of our culture. Isaiah's woe is directed at those who redefine morality according to human opinion rather than divine revelation. The question is never "What does society think about this?" The question is always "What does God's Word say about this?"

Third, we must look at the fruits, not just the labels. Jesus said, "By their fruits ye shall know them" (Matthew 7:20). If something produces the fruit of the Spirit — love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control — it is from God. If it produces the works of the flesh — strife, jealousy, anger, selfishness, division — it is not from God, no matter what it is called.

Brooks encouraged believers to look beneath the surface of every temptation. Ask: "Is this truly virtuous, or has sin been given a new coat of paint?" The Serpent told Eve that disobedience would make her wise. It sounded virtuous — who does not want wisdom? But the "wisdom" was actually rebellion, and the result was death.

For families, practice discernment together. When you encounter ideas in books, movies, social media, or conversations that seem to contradict Scripture, do not simply reject them — discuss them. Help your children learn to think critically and biblically. Teach them to ask, "What does God's Word actually say about this?" and "Is this sin wearing a disguise?"

Satan is a master painter. He can make the darkest sin look like the brightest virtue. But the light of God's Word exposes every disguise. "Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path" (Psalm 119:105). Walk in that light, and no painted sin will deceive you.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

The Puritans were men and women who treasured God's Word above all else. Let us hear from one of them now.

Thomas Brooks, in his work "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices," wrote these words:

"Satan knows that if he should present sin in its own nature and dress, the soul would rather flee from it than yield to it; and therefore he presents it unto us, not in its own proper colours, but painted and gilded over with the name and show of virtue."

What Thomas Brooks is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in 2 Corinthians 11:14; Isaiah 5:20 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Can you think of examples where our culture calls evil good or good evil?

Question 2: Why is Satan described as an 'angel of light'? How does that make his deceptions more dangerous?

Question 3: Brooks says Satan gives sins virtuous-sounding names. Can you think of a sin that has been renamed to sound acceptable?

Question 4: How can studying God's Word help us see through Satan's disguises?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness." -- Isaiah 5:20

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

To make tonight's lesson come alive, try this activity together as a family:

Play a 'True or False Virtue' game. A parent reads a statement that sounds good on the surface but may actually be unbiblical (examples: 'Follow your heart,' 'You deserve to be happy,' 'Be true to yourself'). Family members discuss whether it is truly biblical or a painted sin. Use Scripture to evaluate each one.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for this time as a family. Thank You for speaking to us through Your Word. Plant these truths deep in our hearts, especially in the hearts of our children. Help us to walk in wisdom tomorrow and every day. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
