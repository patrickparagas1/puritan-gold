PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 17
"True Humility in the Family"
Jonathan Edwards — Religious Affections Applied to the Family

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Jonathan Edwards teaches us about one of the surest marks of genuine faith — humility — and how it transforms the way family members relate to one another. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord Jesus, You humbled Yourself and took the form of a servant. Teach our family the grace of humility tonight. Help us to think less of ourselves and more of others, and to find our significance not in our achievements but in Your love. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Philippians 2:3-8. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves. Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others. Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus: Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross."

That is Philippians 2:3-8. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Jonathan Edwards identified humility as one of the clearest marks of genuine spiritual affection. In his careful analysis of true and false signs of grace, Edwards observed that while counterfeit religion tends to puff up the person with pride and self-importance, true religion invariably produces a deep and genuine humility. The person who has truly seen the beauty and holiness of God cannot help but see their own smallness by comparison. And this awareness — not morbid self-hatred, but honest self-assessment in the light of God's glory — is the foundation of Christian humility.

For families, humility is not merely a personal virtue. It is the essential ingredient for healthy relationships. Consider how many family conflicts are rooted in pride. A parent demands respect without earning it through servant-leadership. A child insists on having their own way. Siblings compete for attention, approval, and dominance. Spouses keep score, nursing grievances and demanding recognition. At the root of every one of these conflicts is the stubborn insistence on self — my rights, my needs, my importance, my perspective.

Edwards would say that the gospel provides the cure for family pride. When we truly understand the gospel, we see two things simultaneously: the staggering holiness of God and the staggering depth of our own sin. The gap between these two realities is bridged only by grace — undeserved, unearned, unrepayable grace. And when we understand that everything we have and everything we are is a gift of grace, the basis for pride evaporates. How can I boast when everything good in my life is a gift? How can I look down on others when I myself am a sinner saved by grace?

Paul's exhortation in Philippians 2 captures the pattern of humble living: "Let each esteem other better than themselves." This does not mean pretending that you have no gifts or abilities. It means valuing others above yourself — putting their needs, their interests, and their well-being ahead of your own. In a family, this looks like a parent who gets up in the night to comfort a child without complaint. It looks like a teenager who helps a younger sibling with homework without being asked. It looks like a spouse who says, "What do you need?" before announcing what they want.

And the ultimate model of humility is Christ Himself. He was God — equal with the Father in power, glory, and majesty. Yet He emptied Himself. He took the form of a servant. He became a man. He humbled Himself even to the point of death on a cross. If the Son of God was willing to lay down His rights and His glory for the sake of others, how much more should we be willing to lay down our pride for the sake of our families?

Edwards observed that humble families are happy families. When each member is seeking to serve the others rather than to be served, the atmosphere of the home is transformed. Conflict decreases because no one is fighting for the top position. Communication improves because humble people are better listeners. Forgiveness flows more freely because humble hearts know their own need for grace.

Tonight, Edwards calls your family to the beautiful discipline of humility. It begins with seeing God rightly — in all His holiness and glory. It continues with seeing ourselves rightly — as sinners sustained by grace. And it flowers in seeing others rightly — as people deserving of our love, our service, and our esteem.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Edwards said that seeing God's holiness produces humility. How does a vision of God's greatness change the way we see ourselves?

Question 2: Paul wrote, "Let each esteem other better than themselves." What would this look like in our family this week? Give a specific example for each family member.

Question 3: Christ humbled Himself by becoming a servant. Where is God calling you to serve someone in our family this week?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves." -- Philippians 2:3

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord Jesus, You are the perfect model of humility. Forgive us for the pride that disrupts our family life. Give us humble hearts that esteem others above ourselves. Make our home a place where service is valued above status and love above recognition. In Your humble name we pray, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, loved by the One who humbled Himself for you.
