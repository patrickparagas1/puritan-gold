PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 5
"Both Sinners, Both Saved by Grace"
Romans for Marriage — Day 5


Hello and welcome to your couples devotional. Whether you are sitting side by side on the couch or listening together at the end of a long day, God is here with you both. He meets married couples in a special way when they turn their hearts to Him together.

Let me read our passage for this evening. It comes from Romans 3:23-26:

"For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God; Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus: Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God; To declare, I say, at this time his righteousness: that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus."

That is Romans 3:23-26. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Thomas Watson was a faithful pastor and theologian whose words still carry weight centuries later. Listen to what the Puritans had to say about the truths in tonight's passage.

Romans 3:23 is the great leveler of all human pride: 'For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God.' There are no exceptions. Every husband and every wife stands equally condemned before a holy God. The most patient spouse has sinned. The most devoted partner has fallen short. Neither brings a record of perfection to the marriage — both bring broken hearts in need of grace.

Thomas Watson, in his exposition of the Westminster Shorter Catechism, beautifully unpacked what it means to be 'justified freely by his grace.' Justification is God's declaration that a guilty sinner is righteous — not because of anything they have done, but because of what Christ has done on their behalf. It is entirely free, entirely unearned, entirely by grace.

Watson taught that the doctrine of justification by grace alone should produce deep humility in every believer. If you are justified freely — if your standing before God has nothing to do with your performance — then you have no grounds for pride. And if you have no grounds for pride before God, you certainly have no grounds for pride before your spouse.

This truth demolishes the scorekeeping that plagues so many marriages. 'I do more around the house.' 'I apologize more often.' 'I am more spiritual.' All of this scorekeeping is rendered absurd by Romans 3:23. Both of you have sinned. Both of you fall short. Both of you are justified only by grace. The playing field is perfectly level at the foot of the cross.

Watson also emphasized the wonder of propitiation — Christ satisfying God's righteous wrath against our sin through His blood. If God was willing to pour out His own Son's blood to reconcile you to Himself, how much more should you be willing to extend grace to reconcile with your spouse? The debt your spouse owes you is infinitely smaller than the debt you owed God — and God paid yours in full.


The single most transformative truth for your marriage may be this: you are both sinners saved by grace. Neither of you is the 'good one' and the other the 'problem.' Both of you need grace every single day.

When conflict arises, the natural response is to build a case for why you are right and your spouse is wrong. But Romans 3:23 demolishes that entire framework. You are not the righteous plaintiff and your spouse the guilty defendant. You are both guilty — and both freely justified by grace.

Tonight, practice radical honesty. Each of you name one area where you have recently fallen short as a spouse. Not to pile on guilt, but to stand together at the foot of the cross and receive grace. Then extend to each other the same grace God has extended to you.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Let us reflect together. These questions are designed to help you go deeper, both with God and with each other.

Question 1: Do you tend to keep score in your marriage — tracking who does more, gives more, or sacrifices more? How does Romans 3:23 challenge this?

Question 2: How does knowing that you are both sinners saved by grace change the way you handle conflict?

Question 3: What would it look like to extend 'free justification' to your spouse — forgiving without requiring them to earn it?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


As we near the end of tonight's devotional, let us bring our hearts before God in prayer. Pray for each other. Pray for your marriage. Pray for your home.

Tonight's prayer focus: Thank God together for the gift of justification by grace. Confess any patterns of scorekeeping or self-righteousness in your marriage. Ask God to help you extend the same free grace to each other that He has lavished upon you through Christ.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the grace that justified you before God flow freely between you. May your marriage be a place where no debt is held and no score is kept, because the cross has paid it all.

The Lord bless you and keep you. The Lord make His face shine upon you and be gracious to you. The Lord lift up His countenance upon you both, and give you peace. Amen.
