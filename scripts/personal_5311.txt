PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Reading, Day 11
"Precious Remedies, Part 1"
Thomas Brooks' Remedies Against Satan's Devices

---

Good evening, friend. Tonight we open one of the most practical and penetrating works of Puritan literature: Thomas Brooks' "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices." If you have ever wondered why you keep falling into the same sins, or why temptation seems so perfectly tailored to your weaknesses, Brooks has answers — and more importantly, he has remedies. This is a book that arms the soul for battle.

--- Scripture ---

Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is 2 Corinthians 2:11:

"Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices."

And Ephesians 6:11:

"Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.

--- Teaching ---

Thomas Brooks was a beloved London pastor during some of the most turbulent decades of the seventeenth century. He ministered through civil war, plague, and the Great Fire. His writing bears the marks of a man who knew suffering personally and who shepherded others through suffering with tenderness and wisdom. "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices," published in 1652, remains his most widely read work, and for good reason: it is a field manual for spiritual warfare, written by a pastor who understood that every Christian is on the battlefield whether he knows it or not.

Brooks' method is beautifully simple. He identifies a specific "device" — a tactic the devil uses — and then provides one or more "remedies" drawn from Scripture. The structure is practical and memorable. You can read a single device and its remedy in a few minutes and carry it with you for the rest of the day. The Puritans understood that theology must be portable. It must fit in the pocket of the working man and the heart of the burdened mother.

The first device Brooks examines is this: Satan presents the bait and hides the hook. The devil is a master angler. He knows which bait to dangle before each soul. He does not tempt the covetous man with lust or the lustful man with covetousness. He studies his prey with terrible patience and offers precisely the temptation most likely to succeed. The remedy? Keep your eye upon the hook, not the bait. Train yourself, when temptation comes, to look past the momentary sweetness to the lasting bitterness. Ask yourself: what did this sin cost David? What did it cost Samson? What did it cost Judas? Brooks writes that if Eve had looked past the beauty of the fruit to the curse that followed, she never would have reached for it.

The second device is that Satan paints sin with the colors of virtue. He takes a vice and dresses it in respectable clothing. Covetousness becomes "prudence." Anger becomes "righteous indignation." Compromise with the world becomes "wisdom" and "balance." Gossip becomes "concern for others." Brooks understood that the most dangerous sins are not the ones that look like sins but the ones that disguise themselves as virtues. The remedy is devastatingly simple: call sin what God calls it, not what the devil calls it. Hold every action up to the clear light of Scripture and let the Word of God strip away every disguise.

The third device is that Satan makes sin appear small. He whispers, "It is only a little sin. A small indulgence. Surely God will overlook something so trivial." Brooks answers with unforgettable logic: there is no small sin, because there is no small God to sin against. Every transgression is committed against infinite holiness, infinite goodness, infinite love. The smallest sin, left unrepented of, can grow into the greatest bondage. Richard Sibbes agrees, writing that a small leak can sink a great ship and a small spark can burn down a great city.

--- Application ---

First, identify which of Satan's devices is most effective against you. Is it the baited hook of immediate pleasure? Is it the disguise of sin as virtue? Is it the lie that your sin is too small to matter? Name it honestly before God tonight. You cannot fight an enemy you refuse to identify.

Second, begin the discipline of looking past the bait to the hook. The next time temptation presents itself — and it will — pause and ask: what will this cost me? What will it cost my walk with God, my peace of conscience, my testimony, my family? Brooks teaches that this momentary pause, empowered by the Spirit, is often enough to break the spell of temptation.

Third, refuse to use the devil's vocabulary. Stop calling your sin by its polite name. Call it what Scripture calls it. This is not harshness toward yourself; it is honesty — and honesty is the first step toward freedom.

--- Closing ---

O Lord, open our eyes to the devices of the enemy. We confess that we have too often taken the bait without seeing the hook. We have called our sins by gentle names and treated them as small things. Give us the wisdom of Thy Word and the power of Thy Spirit to resist every wile of the devil. Through Christ, who has conquered every foe, Amen.

Goodnight, friend. Be sober, be vigilant. Your adversary walks about — but your Savior reigns above.
