PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 15
"Satan's Device: Making Sin Look Small"
Pilgrim Families — Week 3, Day 1


Good evening, dear family. Welcome to tonight's family devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for gathering together around God's Word. Let us quiet our hearts and prepare to listen to what the Lord has for us.

Let us begin with prayer. Heavenly Father, we thank You for this family. We thank You for bringing us safely through another day. Now we ask that You would open our eyes to see wonderful things in Your Word. Speak to each heart gathered here, from the oldest to the youngest. In Jesus' name we pray. Amen.

Tonight we open our Bibles to Genesis 3:1-6; James 1:14-15. Listen carefully as I read our passage:

"Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made. And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden? And the woman said unto the serpent, We may eat of the fruit of the trees of the garden: But of the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, God hath said, Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die. And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die: For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil. And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death. But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed."

That is Genesis 3:1-6; James 1:14-15. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


This week we study one of the most important Puritan works ever written for spiritual warfare: Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices by Thomas Brooks. Brooks wrote this book to expose the strategies the devil uses to tempt believers and to provide biblical remedies for each one. For families, this knowledge is essential — because your enemy is real, his tactics are ancient, and only God's Word can defeat him.

Satan's first and most basic device is to make sin look small. He minimizes sin, trivializes it, makes it seem like no big deal. "It is just a little lie." "Everyone does it." "It will not hurt anyone." "Just this once." These are the whispers of the serpent, and he has been using them since the Garden of Eden.

In Genesis 3, Satan approached Eve with a question designed to create doubt: "Yea, hath God said...?" He did not begin with a direct assault. He began by questioning God's Word. Then he directly contradicted God: "Ye shall not surely die." God had said, "In the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die" (Genesis 2:17). Satan said the exact opposite. He made the consequences of sin seem nonexistent. And then he made the sin look attractive: "Your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods." Sin was repackaged as self-improvement, as enlightenment, as freedom.

Eve's response reveals the process of temptation that James describes. She saw that the tree was "good for food" (lust of the flesh), "pleasant to the eyes" (lust of the eyes), and "desired to make one wise" (pride of life). James 1:14-15 maps this process: "Every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death."

Notice the progression: desire, then enticement, then sin, then death. Sin always starts small. It starts with a thought, a glance, a curiosity. Then it grows into desire. Then desire acts. And the action produces death — death of innocence, death of trust, death of relationship, and ultimately, spiritual death. No sin stays small. Every sin, left unchecked, grows.

Brooks warned that Satan's greatest skill is making sin look insignificant. He points to David's sin with Bathsheba. It began with a look from a rooftop — such a small thing. But that look led to lust, lust led to adultery, adultery led to deception, deception led to murder, and murder led to the death of David's child and years of family turmoil. What started as a glance ended in catastrophe.

For families, this device is especially dangerous because children are naturally inclined to minimize their sin. "It was just a little fib." "I only took one cookie." "I was just teasing." As parents, we must teach our children that no sin is small in God's eyes. Sin is cosmic treason against the Creator of the universe. A "small" sin nailed Jesus to the cross just as surely as a "big" one. The size of the sin is not the issue — the holiness of the God sinned against is.

Brooks offers a powerful remedy: look at sin not through the lens of its pleasure but through the lens of its consequences. Satan shows you the bait but hides the hook. He shows you the sweetness of the honey but hides the sting of the bee. He shows you the fun but hides the funeral. When tempted, ask: "Where does this lead? What did this cost Jesus? How does this look in the light of eternity?"

Another remedy Brooks offers is to remember that the smallest sin can be the beginning of the greatest fall. The camel does not crash through the tent all at once — first it pokes its nose under the flap. A crack in a dam seems tiny until the water breaks through. A "small" compromise in integrity, in purity, in honesty can be the first crack that leads to devastating collapse.

Teach your children to take sin seriously — not to live in fear, but to live in wisdom. The Christian who understands Satan's devices is far better prepared to resist them. And the family that talks openly about temptation, sin, and spiritual warfare is a family that stands strong together.


Now let us hear from one of the great Puritan teachers of the faith.

Thomas Brooks, in his work "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices," wrote these words:

"Satan paints sin with virtue's colours. He knows that if he should present sin in its own nature and dress, the soul would rather flee from it than yield to it."

What Thomas Brooks is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Genesis 3:1-6; James 1:14-15 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Now it is time for our family discussion. Here are some questions to talk about together. Take your time with each one.

Question 1: How did Satan make the forbidden fruit look appealing to Eve? What did he hide from her?

Question 2: Can you think of ways our culture makes sin look small or harmless? Give some examples.

Question 3: James describes a progression: desire, enticement, sin, death. Can you trace this pattern in a real-life example?

Question 4: What does it mean to 'look at the hook, not just the bait' when it comes to temptation?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death." -- James 1:15

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a family activity you can do this week to help these truths take root:

Show your family a fishhook with an attractive lure on it. Discuss how the bait looks appealing but hides something dangerous. Compare this to how sin works — it looks attractive on the outside but has a deadly hook inside. Let each person name a 'bait' that tempts them and discuss the hidden 'hook.'


Let us close in prayer. Heavenly Father, thank You for gathering our family together in Your Word tonight. Thank You for the truth we have heard from Genesis 3:1-6; James 1:14-15. Help us to live out what we have learned. Guard our hearts, guide our steps, and fill our home with Your love and peace. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

Goodnight, dear ones. Walk in wisdom tomorrow.
