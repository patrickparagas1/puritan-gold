PURITAN GOLD -- Study Topic: Female Piety, Part 6 of 10
"The Christian Wife — Submission, Love, and Partnership in Marriage"
John Angell James — Female Piety

---

Welcome back, dear listener, to Puritan Gold. We continue our study of Female Piety by John Angell James. Today we turn to a subject that is both tender and controversial in our modern age: the calling of the Christian wife. James handles this theme with the care of a pastor who has counseled countless marriages and who understands that the biblical model of wifely devotion, rightly understood, is not a yoke of oppression but a channel of grace. Let us hear what Scripture and our author have to say.

Let us read from Ephesians 5:22-25 and 33, in the King James Version:

"Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord. For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body. Therefore as the church is subject unto Christ, so let the wives be to their own husbands in every thing. Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it... Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband."

And from Proverbs 31:11-12:

"The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her, so that he shall have no need of spoil. She will do him good and not evil all the days of her life."

--- Teaching ---

John Angell James approaches the subject of the Christian wife with both firmness and tenderness. He is firm because Scripture is clear in its teaching. He is tender because he knows that the realities of marriage are complex, that many women suffer in difficult unions, and that the biblical ideal must be applied with pastoral wisdom and genuine compassion.

James begins by establishing what he means by marriage. For the Christian, marriage is not merely a social contract or a romantic arrangement. It is a divine institution, ordained by God in the Garden of Eden, sanctified by Christ at the wedding in Cana, and expounded by the apostle Paul as a living picture of the relationship between Christ and His church. Marriage, rightly understood, is a covenant — a solemn, lifelong commitment made before God and sustained by His grace.

Within this covenant, James affirms the biblical teaching that the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church. But he is careful to explain what this headship means and what it does not mean. It does not mean tyranny, domination, or the reduction of the wife to a servant. It means that the husband bears the primary responsibility for the spiritual leadership, protection, and provision of the household, and that the wife is called to support, assist, and encourage him in this role. The model is Christ and the church: Christ leads the church not by coercion but by sacrificial love, and the church responds not with slavish fear but with grateful reverence. This is the pattern that James holds before the Christian wife.

The Puritans had a remarkably high and balanced view of marriage. Thomas Gataker, in his sermon A Good Wife God's Gift, preached in 1623, argued that marriage is a partnership of mutual love and service. He spoke of husband and wife as yoke-fellows, bound together by affection, respect, and a shared commitment to the glory of God. William Gouge, in Of Domesticall Duties, described the duties of wives and husbands in parallel, insisting that the obligations were reciprocal and that neither party was excused from the hard work of love. Richard Baxter, in A Christian Directory, wrote one of the most beautiful passages in Puritan literature on the mutual duties of husband and wife, urging each to outdo the other in kindness, patience, and forbearance.

James stands squarely in this tradition. He urges the Christian wife to cultivate several qualities that strengthen the marriage bond and honor the Lord.

First, reverence for her husband. James takes his cue from Ephesians 5:33 — "the wife see that she reverence her husband." This reverence, he explains, is not a cringing servility but a genuine respect for the position God has given her husband and for the man himself as a fellow image-bearer of God. It is shown in the way she speaks to him and about him, in the deference she shows to his judgment (while not surrendering her own conscience), and in the support she gives to his leadership. A wife who constantly undermines, criticizes, or belittles her husband in public or in private is violating this command, regardless of how justified she may feel.

Second, fidelity and trustworthiness. "The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her." James unpacks this magnificent phrase from Proverbs 31 with great care. The trustworthy wife is one whose husband can confide in without fear, rely upon without anxiety, and leave in charge without doubt. She guards his reputation, manages his resources wisely, keeps his counsel, and remains steadfast in her commitment to him through every season of life. James notes that trust, once broken, is extraordinarily difficult to rebuild, and he urges wives to guard it as a precious treasure.

Third, a desire to do her husband good. "She will do him good and not evil all the days of her life." James observes that this is a comprehensive and lifelong commitment. It encompasses every dimension of the wife's relationship with her husband — the physical, the emotional, the spiritual, and the practical. The Christian wife seeks to be a blessing to her husband in all things. She encourages him when he is discouraged, comforts him when he is grieved, rejoices with him when he succeeds, and prays for him continually. She is, in James's phrase, the helpmeet that God designed her to be — not a mere accessory to his life, but a vital partner in his calling.

Fourth, a commitment to the spiritual growth of the marriage. James insists that the Christian wife should be actively concerned for her husband's soul. She should pray for his spiritual progress, gently encourage him toward greater faithfulness, and create in the home an atmosphere conducive to devotion. If her husband is an unbeliever, she should follow the counsel of the apostle Peter, seeking to win him not by nagging or preaching but by the quiet, compelling witness of a life adorned with the beauty of holiness.

James also addresses the painful reality of difficult marriages. He does not pretend that every husband is loving, wise, or godly. He acknowledges that some wives suffer under the weight of indifferent, harsh, or even cruel husbands. To these women, James speaks with great compassion. He does not counsel blind submission to abuse. He points them to the sufficiency of Christ, the comfort of the Scriptures, the support of the church, and the faithfulness of a God who sees their tears and hears their prayers. He urges them to maintain their own piety even when their circumstances are grievous, trusting that God is able to bring good out of the most painful situations.

John Flavel, in his Keeping the Heart, wrote that the Christian must learn to live by faith in every condition — in prosperity and adversity, in comfort and in trial. James applies this truth directly to the Christian wife, urging her to keep her heart with all diligence, to maintain her communion with God, and to trust His providence even when her marriage does not match the ideal she longs for. The grace that sustains the church in its relationship with Christ is the same grace that sustains the wife in her relationship with her husband. And that grace is always sufficient.

--- Application ---

1. If you are married, examine the quality of your reverence for your husband. Do you speak of him with respect? Do you support his leadership? Are there patterns of criticism or contempt that need to be confessed and corrected? Bring these matters before the Lord in prayer.

2. Consider whether your husband can safely trust you. Are you faithful in your speech, your finances, your friendships, and your commitments? Identify any area where trust has been weakened, and take steps to rebuild it with the help of God.

3. Make a deliberate effort this week to do your husband good — not as a strategy or a project, but as an expression of love. A word of encouragement, an act of service, a prayer offered in secret — these things carry weight in the economy of grace.

4. If you are in a difficult marriage, do not lose heart. Seek the counsel of a godly pastor or mature Christian woman. Pour out your heart to God in prayer, and trust that He is working even when you cannot see His hand. You are not alone.
