PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Doctrine Deep-Dive, Day 3
"The Person of Christ"
Christology Explored Through the Puritans


Good evening. The Puritans were theologians of the highest order, but they were also the most practical of people. They knew that theology untethered from life is useless, and that life untethered from theology is dangerous. Tonight, we hold the two together.

Tonight we draw from the work of John Owen, specifically from "The Person of Christ / Christologia." This text has been a source of spiritual nourishment for Christians across the centuries, and it remains profoundly relevant today.


Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Colossians 2:9-10:

"For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily. And ye are complete in him, which is the head of all principality and power."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.


The doctrine of the Person of Christ stands at the center of all Christian theology. Every other doctrine radiates outward from this one, and every heresy ultimately traces back to a distortion of it. Who is Jesus Christ? The answer to this question determines everything: the nature of salvation, the possibility of communion with God, the hope of eternal life, the meaning of the incarnation, the efficacy of the atonement, and the power of the resurrection.

John Owen, in his masterful work 'Christologia, or a Declaration of the Glorious Mystery of the Person of Christ,' sets forth the orthodox doctrine with a depth and precision that has rarely been equaled. Owen begins with the eternal pre-existence of the Son. Before the world was made, before time began, the Second Person of the Trinity existed in the full glory of His divine nature, in eternal communion with the Father and the Spirit. He was not a created being, not a lesser deity, not an emanation from the Father. He was and is God of God, Light of Light, very God of very God, of one substance with the Father, by whom all things were made.

Owen then expounds the great mystery of the incarnation: this eternal Son, without ceasing to be God, assumed a true human nature. Owen is careful to articulate the Chalcedonian definition: the two natures of Christ — divine and human — are united in one Person, 'without confusion, without change, without division, without separation.' The divine nature was not diminished by the union. The human nature was not absorbed into the divine. Each nature retained its own properties and operations, yet they subsist in one Person, the God-man, Jesus Christ.

Owen explores why this union of natures was necessary for our salvation. If Christ were not truly God, His death could not have infinite value sufficient to atone for the sins of the elect. If Christ were not truly man, He could not have stood as our representative, could not have obeyed the law in our place, could not have died in our stead. Owen writes that the hypostatic union — the union of two natures in one Person — is the hinge upon which the entire plan of redemption turns. Remove either nature and the whole structure collapses.

Owen further develops what the Reformed tradition calls the 'threefold office' of Christ: Prophet, Priest, and King. As Prophet, Christ reveals God to man — He is the final and perfect Word of God, the one in whom all divine revelation finds its culmination. As Priest, Christ represents man to God — He offered Himself as the perfect sacrifice for sin and now intercedes for His people at the right hand of the Father. As King, Christ rules over all things for the benefit of His church — He governs history, subdues His enemies, and will one day bring all things under His feet. Owen insists that Christ cannot be received in one office without the others. The man who wants Christ as Savior but not as Lord has not understood who Christ is.

Owen also addresses the glory of Christ's Person as the object of faith and worship. It is not merely Christ's work that we are to admire but Christ Himself. Owen writes that the contemplation of the Person of Christ is the highest exercise of the human mind and the sweetest occupation of the human heart. All the glories of creation, all the wonders of providence, all the truths of Scripture converge in the Person of the God-man. To know Him is to know the fullness of God in accessible, incarnate form.

The Puritan Thomas Goodwin adds a devotional dimension to Owen's theological rigor. Goodwin writes that meditating on the Person of Christ is not merely an academic exercise but a means of grace. The believer who fixes his gaze upon Christ — who contemplates His deity, His humanity, His offices, His work, His beauty — is transformed by what he sees. 'We all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image' (2 Corinthians 3:18). Christology is not merely for the classroom; it is for the closet, the prayer room, the quiet hour of devotion.

The practical application is direct. Patrick, do you know Christ — not merely facts about Him, but Christ Himself? Is your faith anchored in the Person of the God-man, or has it drifted into a vague spirituality that could survive without the incarnation? The doctrine of Christ's Person is not an abstract theological puzzle; it is the foundation of your salvation, the ground of your hope, and the object of your eternal worship. Press into this doctrine. Let it fill your mind and warm your heart.

Consider this: every other religion in the world can operate without a divine-human mediator. But Christianity cannot. Remove the incarnation, and Christianity collapses. There is no salvation without a Savior who is both God and man. There is no mediation without a Mediator who can lay one hand on divinity and the other on humanity. The Person of Christ is not peripheral to the faith; He is the faith.


Here are some questions to carry with you into the coming days.

Question 1: Do I regularly meditate on the Person of Christ — His deity, His humanity, His glory — or has my faith become disconnected from the incarnation?

Question 2: How does the union of two natures in Christ shape my understanding of salvation and my confidence before God?

Question 3: Am I receiving Christ in all three of His offices — Prophet, Priest, and King — or am I selectively embracing only the aspects that comfort me?

Question 4: How can I cultivate a deeper, more personal knowledge of Christ this month?

Do not rush past these questions. Sit with them. Journal about them if you can. The Puritans believed that truth must be digested, not merely tasted.


Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God and Son of Man, I worship You in the mystery of Your Person. You are truly God and truly man, and in You I find all that I need for salvation and life. Deepen my knowledge of who You are. Let the contemplation of Your Person be the sweetest exercise of my heart and mind. I pray in Your glorious name, Amen.

Rest in His grace tonight. Tomorrow is a new day of growth.
