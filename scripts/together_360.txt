PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 29
"Like-Minded and Welcoming"
Romans for Marriage — Day 29

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to tonight's couples devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for setting aside this time to draw near to God and to each other. The moments you invest in your marriage before the Lord are among the most important moments of your day.

Let us open God's Word together. Our passage tonight comes from Romans 15:5-7:

"Now the God of patience and consolation grant you to be likeminded one toward another according to Christ Jesus: That ye may with one mind and one mouth glorify God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. Wherefore receive ye one another, as Christ also received us to the glory of God."

That is Romans 15:5-7. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Now let us hear from Richard Sibbes, one of the great Puritan voices on this subject. The Puritans wrote extensively about marriage because they saw it as one of God's most profound gifts.

Paul prays for the Roman believers to be 'likeminded one toward another according to Christ Jesus.' This is not a call to uniformity of opinion but to unity of heart. Being likeminded means sharing the same fundamental orientation: toward Christ, toward grace, toward the Gospel. It means having the same priorities even when you have different perspectives.

Richard Sibbes taught that the deepest unity between husband and wife is spiritual unity — being 'of one mind and one mouth' in glorifying God. When a couple shares the same Lord, the same faith, the same hope, and the same love, they have a unity that transcends every surface difference. They may disagree about parenting strategies, financial decisions, or daily routines — but at the deepest level, they are one in Christ.

Paul's phrase 'with one mind and one mouth glorify God' paints a picture of united worship. There is something profoundly beautiful about a husband and wife who glorify God together — who pray with one voice, who serve with one purpose, who face life with one faith. This unity is a powerful witness to the world and a deep source of strength for the couple themselves.

The command to 'receive one another as Christ also received us' echoes Romans 14 but adds a stunning qualifier: as Christ received us. How did Christ receive us? Freely. Unconditionally. While we were sinners. Without demanding that we first clean ourselves up. Without requiring a probationary period. Without holding our past against us. This is how you are to receive your spouse — freely, unconditionally, without reservation.

Sibbes reminded believers that Christ received us 'to the glory of God.' Our reception of each other is not merely for our own benefit — it is for God's glory. When a husband welcomes his wife despite her flaws, God is glorified. When a wife receives her husband despite his failures, the Gospel is displayed. Your marriage is a stage on which the drama of divine grace is performed for a watching world.

--- Marriage Application ---

Are you and your spouse 'likeminded according to Christ Jesus'? Do you share the same fundamental orientation toward God, His Word, and His purposes? If so, thank God for that unity and cultivate it. If not, pray for it.

The command to receive one another 'as Christ received us' sets an impossibly high standard — and that is the point. You cannot love your spouse the way Christ loves you without the help of the Holy Spirit. But with His help, you can grow toward it daily.

Tonight, receive each other fresh. As if for the first time. Without the baggage of past failures. Without the weight of accumulated grievances. Receive each other the way Christ received you — freely, joyfully, to the glory of God.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Now let us pause for reflection. Here are some questions for you and your spouse to discuss together. Be honest. Be gentle. Listen more than you speak.

Question 1: What does it mean for you and your spouse to be 'likeminded according to Christ Jesus'? Where are you already unified? Where do you need to grow?

Question 2: How does the way Christ received you set the standard for how you receive your spouse?

Question 3: What would change in your marriage if you received each other fresh every morning — without the weight of yesterday's failures?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Let us move into a time of prayer together. If you are able, hold hands as you pray. There is something powerful about a husband and wife joining hands before the throne of grace.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray Paul's prayer from Romans 15:5-6 over your marriage: that God would grant you to be likeminded, that you would glorify God with one mind and one mouth, and that you would receive each other as Christ has received you.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May you be one in heart, one in purpose, and one in worship. May you receive each other as Christ received you — freely, fully, and for the glory of God.

May the Lord bless your marriage and keep it strong. May He make His face to shine upon your home, and give you peace. Goodnight, dear couple. Walk together in His love.
