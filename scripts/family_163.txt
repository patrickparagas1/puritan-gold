PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 2
"The Husband's Sacred Charge"
William Gouge — Domestical Duties

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we continue our study of William Gouge's teaching on the Christian household, turning our attention to the duties of the husband. Let us come before the Lord together.

Let us pray. Father in heaven, we thank You for another day of life together. As we open Your Word tonight, give us humble hearts. Teach us what it means to lead and to love as You have called us to. Guard us from pride and self-seeking, and fill us with the mind of Christ. Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Ephesians 5:25-30. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it; That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word, That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish. So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself. For no man ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church: For we are members of his body, of his flesh, and of his bones."

That is Ephesians 5:25-30. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

William Gouge devoted extensive attention to the duties of husbands, and his treatment is remarkable for its tenderness and high standard. In an age when many men ruled their households with an iron fist and considered their wives as little more than domestic servants, Gouge held up the example of Christ as the pattern for every husband to follow.

The first duty Gouge identifies is love. Not merely affection or sentiment, but the deep, abiding, self-sacrificial love that Paul describes in Ephesians 5. Gouge writes that a husband's love for his wife must be like Christ's love for the church — a love that gives, that serves, that sacrifices, and that seeks the highest good of the beloved even at great personal cost. Christ did not love the church because she was lovely. He loved her in order to make her lovely. He did not wait until she was worthy of His affection. He gave Himself for her while she was still stained with sin, in order to "sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word."

This is the pattern. A husband is not to love his wife only when she pleases him, only when she meets his expectations, only when she makes his life comfortable. He is to love her as Christ loved — steadfastly, unconditionally, sacrificially. Gouge warns sternly against husbands who are harsh, critical, cold, or neglectful. Such behavior, he argues, is not merely a failure of duty — it is a misrepresentation of Christ. For if the husband represents Christ to his wife, then a harsh husband preaches a false gospel. He tells his wife, by his actions, that Christ is cruel, demanding, and indifferent. But a loving husband preaches the true gospel — that Christ is tender, patient, faithful, and self-giving.

The second duty Gouge identifies is provision. A husband is to nourish and cherish his wife, as Paul says, "even as the Lord the church." To nourish means to provide for her material needs — food, clothing, shelter, security. To cherish means to provide for her emotional and spiritual needs — warmth, companionship, encouragement, protection. Gouge insists that a man who neglects to provide for his family has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever, echoing Paul's words in 1 Timothy 5:8.

But provision is not merely material. Gouge emphasizes the husband's duty to provide spiritual leadership. He is to lead family worship, to teach the Scriptures, to pray with and for his wife and children, and to set the spiritual tone of the household. The husband who provides bread for the table but neglects bread for the soul has failed in his most essential duty. Gouge writes that many husbands are careful about their business but careless about their families' spiritual welfare, and this is a grave sin that will be answered for at the judgment.

The third duty is respect. Gouge surprises many modern readers by insisting that husbands owe their wives honor and respect. Drawing from 1 Peter 3:7, which commands husbands to dwell with their wives "according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life," Gouge argues that the wife is not the husband's inferior but his companion, his partner, his fellow-heir of eternal life. The phrase "weaker vessel" does not imply lesser value but calls for greater care — as one handles a precious vessel with more delicacy than a common one.

Gouge's teaching is a searchlight that exposes selfishness, laziness, and spiritual negligence in husbands. But it is also a lamp that illuminates the path of Christ-like leadership. The husband who loves, provides, and honors his wife builds a household on the strongest possible foundation — the foundation of the gospel itself.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Gouge says a harsh husband "preaches a false gospel" to his wife. What does he mean by this? How does the way a husband treats his wife reflect what he believes about Christ?

Question 2: What is the difference between "nourishing" and "cherishing"? Why does a wife need both?

Question 3: How can a husband provide spiritual leadership in practical, daily ways — not just on Sunday but throughout the week?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it." -- Ephesians 5:25

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord Jesus, You loved the church and gave Yourself for her. Teach the husbands and fathers among us to love as You love — with patience, tenderness, sacrifice, and faithfulness. Where we have failed, forgive us. Where we are weak, strengthen us. Make the husbands in this family men after Your own heart. In Your precious name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
