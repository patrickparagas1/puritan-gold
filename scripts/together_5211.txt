PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 11
"Forgiving as Christ Forgave"
Ephesians 4:32 — Communication and Holy Living


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we come to what may be the most important verse for the daily health of your marriage. If you can learn to practice what this verse teaches, your marriage will not merely survive; it will thrive. The subject is forgiveness.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 4:32 (KJV):

"And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you."


Three words describe the disposition Paul requires of every Christian, and especially of every married couple: kind, tenderhearted, and forgiving. These are not optional extras for the spiritually advanced. They are the basic requirements of the Christian life, and nowhere are they more needed than in the intimacy of marriage.

Kindness is the first quality mentioned. In marriage, kindness is expressed in tone of voice, in thoughtful gestures, in the willingness to put your spouse's comfort before your own. It is the cup of coffee made without being asked. It is the soft answer that turns away wrath. It is the choice to interpret your spouse's actions in the best possible light. Kindness costs nothing, yet its absence can bankrupt a marriage.

Tenderheartedness goes deeper than kindness. It means having a heart that is soft, responsive, and compassionate. It is the opposite of a hard heart. In marriage, hearts harden gradually. Each unresolved conflict, each unforgiven offense, each moment of neglect adds another layer of callousness. The tenderhearted spouse is one who refuses to let that hardening process take hold. When your spouse is hurting, you feel it. When your spouse is struggling, you respond with compassion rather than impatience. Tenderheartedness is the soil in which forgiveness grows.

And then the great command: forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you. Here is the standard by which all marital forgiveness is to be measured. How has God forgiven you? He has forgiven you completely. He does not bring your sins back up after He has pardoned them. He does not hold them over your head. He does not withdraw His love when you fail again. He forgives freely, fully, and finally, for Christ's sake.

William Gouge taught that unforgiveness is the slow poison of marriage. A husband who keeps a mental ledger of his wife's failures is drinking poison and expecting her to die. A wife who revisits forgiven offenses in every argument is reopening wounds that should have been allowed to heal. Gouge was blunt: if God forgave your sins the way you forgive your spouse, you would be in hell. The measure of our forgiveness of others reveals the depth of our understanding of God's forgiveness of us.

Richard Baxter warned that the refusal to forgive gives the devil more ground in a marriage than almost any other sin. Unforgiveness breeds bitterness. Bitterness breeds contempt. Contempt breeds cruelty. And cruelty destroys what God has built. The antidote to this deadly progression is the immediate, deliberate, Christ-patterned forgiveness that Paul commands in this verse.

But what does forgiveness look like practically? It means choosing not to punish your spouse for an offense they have confessed and repented of. It means choosing not to bring up past sins in present arguments. It means choosing not to gossip about your spouse's failures to friends or family. It means choosing to treat your spouse as though the offense never happened, not because you have forgotten it, but because you have chosen to release it.

Dear couple, is there unforgiveness in your marriage tonight? Is there a wound that has not been treated with the medicine of gospel forgiveness? Is there a ledger of wrongs that needs to be torn up and thrown away? Tonight is the night. Forgive as Christ has forgiven you. Not because your spouse deserves it. Not because the offense was small. But because God, for Christ's sake, has forgiven you of far greater debts. Let the forgiveness you have received from God flow through you into the heart of your spouse.


Question 1: Is there anything I have not fully forgiven you for that is still affecting our marriage? Let us bring it into the light tonight.

Question 2: Am I kind and tenderhearted toward you in our daily interactions, or have I allowed my heart to harden?

Question 3: How can the gospel of God's forgiveness in Christ reshape the way we handle offenses in our marriage?


Heavenly Father, we stand before Thee as forgiven sinners who are called to forgive. We confess that we have not always extended to each other the same grace Thou hast extended to us. We have kept records of wrongs. We have harbored bitterness. We have allowed our hearts to harden. Forgive us, Lord, and soften our hearts toward each other. Help us to be kind, tenderhearted, and forgiving, even as Thou, for Christ's sake, hast forgiven us. Let no root of bitterness spring up in this marriage. May the cross of Christ be the measure of our mercy toward each other. In Jesus' name, Amen.
