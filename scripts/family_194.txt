PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 2
"Worshipping God in Spirit and Truth at Home"
Thomas Watson — The Ten Commandments

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we continue with Thomas Watson and the Ten Commandments, turning our attention to the second commandment and what it means for our family's worship. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Father in heaven, we come before You tonight with humble hearts. Teach us how to worship You rightly — not according to our own ideas, but according to Your holy Word. Guard our family from every form of false worship, and draw us near to You in spirit and in truth. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Exodus 20:4-6. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth: Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them: for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me; And shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments."

That is Exodus 20:4-6. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

The second commandment moves from the question of whom we worship to the question of how we worship. The first commandment says, "Worship God alone." The second commandment says, "Worship God in the way He has prescribed." Thomas Watson understood this distinction with great clarity and devoted significant attention to it in his exposition.

Watson taught that the second commandment forbids us from worshipping God according to our own imagination or invention. We are not free to worship God any way we please. He is the One who determines how He is to be approached, and He has revealed His will in His Word. This principle, which the Puritans called the "regulative principle of worship," means that our worship must be governed by Scripture, not by human tradition, personal preference, or cultural fashion.

Now, why does this matter for your family? Because families worship. Every family has rituals, rhythms, and practices that shape its spiritual life. The question is whether those practices are shaped by God's Word or by the spirit of the age. Watson would urge families to examine their worship carefully. Do we pray because Scripture commands it, or do we neglect prayer because we are busy? Do we read the Bible together because God has ordained it as the primary means of grace, or do we substitute entertainment and distraction for the Word of God? Do we gather with God's people on the Lord's Day because He has called us to corporate worship, or do we treat Sunday as merely another day for recreation?

Watson also drew attention to the powerful warning attached to this commandment: God visits the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation. This is not a statement of injustice but a statement of reality. The spiritual choices of parents have consequences that echo through generations. A father who neglects worship teaches his children to neglect worship. A mother who treats God's commands casually raises children who treat them casually. The patterns we establish in our homes today will shape our grandchildren and great-grandchildren.

But notice the glorious counterpart: God shows mercy unto thousands of them that love Him and keep His commandments. The faithfulness of one generation can bless a thousand generations. When parents worship God rightly and teach their children to do the same, they are laying up a treasury of spiritual blessing that will overflow for centuries. What a motivation for family worship! You are not merely going through a routine. You are building a legacy. Every prayer you pray with your children, every chapter you read aloud, every hymn you sing together is a deposit in an account that will pay dividends for generations yet unborn.

Watson was also concerned about what he called "superstition" in worship — the addition of elements that God has not commanded. In our day, this might take the form of reducing worship to mere entertainment, measuring the quality of worship by how it makes us feel rather than by whether it conforms to Scripture, or introducing practices into our devotional life that have no basis in God's Word. Watson counseled simplicity and sincerity. The best family worship is not the most elaborate or the most emotional. It is the most faithful — faithful to Scripture, faithful to Christ, faithful to the gospel.

Consider tonight how your family worships. Is your family devotional time shaped by the Word of God? Do you come before the Lord with reverence and joy? Do you teach your children that worship is not about us and our preferences but about God and His glory? Watson would say that the second commandment is an invitation to a deeper, richer, more authentic worship — a worship that pleases God because it is offered in the way He has commanded.

Let us worship the Lord tonight not with images made by human hands, but with hearts made new by divine grace.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: What is the difference between the first and second commandments? Why does God care not only about whom we worship but how we worship?

Question 2: Watson warned about the consequences of parents' spiritual choices on future generations. What spiritual habits would you like to pass down to your children and grandchildren?

Question 3: How can our family make our worship — both private and corporate — more faithful to what Scripture teaches?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth." -- John 4:24

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord, forgive us for the times we have worshipped You carelessly or according to our own preferences rather than Your Word. Teach our family to worship You in spirit and in truth. May our home be a house of genuine, Scripture-shaped worship. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that the God who commands your worship also delights in it.
