"The Person and Work of Christ"
Westminster Standards — Lesson 6

---

Welcome back to Puritan Gold's homeschool program. Today we come to the heart of the Christian faith — the person and work of Jesus Christ as presented in the Westminster Standards. Let us pray and begin.

Lord Jesus, You are the center of all theology and the hope of all the world. As we study what the Westminster divines confessed about You, open our eyes to see Your glory and our hearts to love You more deeply. Amen.

--- Lesson ---

The Westminster Standards devote careful attention to the person and work of Christ, for He is the center of the entire covenant of grace. Without Christ, there is no salvation. Without understanding who He is and what He has done, the Christian faith collapses into mere moralism or sentimentality.

The Shorter Catechism, Question 21, asks: "Who is the Redeemer of God's elect?" and answers: "The only Redeemer of God's elect is the Lord Jesus Christ, who, being the eternal Son of God, became man, and so was, and continueth to be, God and man in two distinct natures, and one Person, for ever." This single sentence contains some of the most important truths in all of theology.

First, Christ is "the eternal Son of God." He did not begin to exist at Bethlehem. He has existed from all eternity as the second Person of the Trinity — fully God, equal in power and glory with the Father and the Holy Spirit. John's Gospel affirms this: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (John 1:1).

Second, the eternal Son "became man." This is the doctrine of the incarnation. The infinite God took on finite human nature. The Creator entered His own creation. The Word became flesh and dwelt among us (John 1:14). This is the most stupendous event in the history of the universe. The Westminster Confession, Chapter 8, Section 2, describes it with care: Christ, "the Son of God, the second Person of the Trinity, being very and eternal God, of one substance, and equal with the Father, did, when the fullness of time was come, take upon him man's nature, with all the essential properties and common infirmities thereof; yet without sin."

Third, Christ is "God and man in two distinct natures, and one Person, for ever." This is the Chalcedonian definition of the two natures of Christ, and the Westminster divines embrace it fully. Christ is not partly God and partly man. He is fully God and fully man — two complete natures united in one Person, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation. This hypostatic union is permanent — Christ remains the God-man forever.

Having established who Christ is, the Standards turn to what Christ has done. The Shorter Catechism, Question 23, introduces Christ's three offices: "Christ, as our Redeemer, executeth the offices of a prophet, of a priest, and of a king, both in his estate of humiliation and exaltation."

As Prophet, Christ reveals the will of God. He taught during His earthly ministry, and He continues to teach through His Word and Spirit. As Priest, Christ offers Himself as a sacrifice for sin and intercedes for His people. His priestly work is at the heart of the gospel — He bore our sins on the cross and now pleads our cause before the Father. As King, Christ rules over all things. He governs His church, restrains His enemies, and will one day bring all things under His sovereign authority.

The Confession gives particular attention to Christ's work of atonement. Chapter 8, Section 5 declares that Christ, "by his perfect obedience and sacrifice of himself, which he through the eternal Spirit once offered up unto God, hath fully satisfied the justice of his Father; and purchased not only reconciliation, but an everlasting inheritance in the kingdom of heaven, for all those whom the Father hath given unto him."

This is the doctrine of penal substitutionary atonement. Christ died in our place, bearing the penalty that our sins deserved. His sacrifice fully satisfied the justice of God — it did not merely make salvation possible but actually secured salvation for all whom the Father chose. And the result is twofold: reconciliation (the restoration of our relationship with God) and inheritance (the gift of eternal life in His kingdom).

The Standards also teach that Christ's work includes both His humiliation and His exaltation. His humiliation includes His incarnation, His suffering, His death, and His burial. His exaltation includes His resurrection, ascension, session at the right hand of the Father, and future return in glory. Both are essential to our salvation. His death secures our pardon. His resurrection secures our new life. His ascension secures His ongoing intercession. And His return will secure our final glory.

The person and work of Christ are not merely doctrines to be learned. They are realities to be trusted, loved, and lived. The Christ confessed in the Westminster Standards is the same Christ who calls you today: "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28).

--- Review Questions ---

Review Question 1: What does the Shorter Catechism teach about the two natures of Christ? Why is it important that Christ is both fully God and fully man?

Review Question 2: Explain Christ's three offices — Prophet, Priest, and King. How does each office contribute to our salvation?

Review Question 3: What is penal substitutionary atonement, and how does the Westminster Confession describe Christ's sacrifice?

--- Activity ---

Create a study sheet on the three offices of Christ. For each office (Prophet, Priest, King), write a definition, list at least two Scripture passages that support it, and explain how Christ fulfills this office in both His humiliation and His exaltation. Then write a paragraph explaining which office of Christ means the most to you personally and why.

--- Closing ---

Jesus Christ — fully God, fully man, Prophet, Priest, and King — is the center of the Christian faith and the hope of every believer. Know Him. Trust Him. Worship Him. Until next time, students.
