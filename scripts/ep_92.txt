PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"Praise the Lord from the Heavens"
May 31, 2026

---

"Praise ye the Lord. Praise the Lord from the heavens: praise him in the heights. Praise ye him, all his angels: praise ye him, all his hosts. Praise ye him, sun and moon: praise him, all ye stars of light. Praise him, ye heavens of heavens, and ye waters that be above the heavens. Let them praise the name of the Lord: for he commanded, and they were created. He hath also stablished them for ever and ever: he hath made a decree which shall not pass." -- Psalm 148:1-6

As we come to the close of our month in the Psalms, we are met with a vision of praise so vast, so all-encompassing, so staggering in its scope, that it seems as though the entire creation has been gathered into a single great choir and directed to lift its voice in adoration of the God who made it. This is not merely the praise of a single worshipper, or of a congregation of worshippers, or even of a nation of worshippers. This is the praise of the universe. Every created thing, from the highest angel to the lowest worm, from the brightest star to the humblest stone, is summoned to bless the name of the Lord.

"Praise the Lord from the heavens." The psalm begins at the top and works downward, like a waterfall of adoration cascading from the highest heights to the lowest depths. The heavens are called first, as befitting their exalted position. The angels, those mighty spirits who stand perpetually in the presence of God, are commanded to praise Him. The hosts of heaven, the innumerable armies of celestial beings, are called to join the chorus. And then the inanimate creation is summoned: the sun and moon, the stars of light, the heavens of heavens, and the waters above the heavens.

Can the sun praise God? Can the moon worship? Can the stars sing? In a literal sense, perhaps not, for they are not conscious beings with rational minds and moral wills. But in a deeper sense, they praise God simply by being what He made them to be. The sun praises God by shining. The moon praises God by reflecting. The stars praise God by burning. They fulfil the purpose for which they were created, and in that fulfilment, they glorify their Creator. Their very existence is a hymn of praise. Their every function is an act of worship. They are, as it were, the instruments of a vast cosmic orchestra, playing the notes that the Conductor has assigned to them, and producing a symphony of glory that reverberates through the corridors of eternity.

"For he commanded, and they were created." Here is the reason for all praise: the sovereign creative power of God. Everything that exists owes its existence to the command of God. He spoke, and the heavens appeared. He breathed, and the stars blazed forth. He willed, and the angels sprang into being. Nothing made itself. Nothing evolved by its own effort from nothingness into something. Everything was commanded into existence by the word of the Almighty, and everything therefore owes to Him an infinite debt of gratitude and praise.

"He hath also stablished them for ever and ever: he hath made a decree which shall not pass." Not only did God create all things, but He sustains all things. The stability of the universe is not self-generated. It rests upon a divine decree, and that decree is as unchangeable as the God who made it. The laws of nature, the orbits of the planets, the cycles of the seasons, the order of the cosmos, all are maintained by the word of God's power. And this same God who holds the universe in place holds the lives of His children in His hand.

But the psalm does not remain in the heavens. It descends to the earth. "Praise the Lord from the earth, ye dragons, and all deeps: fire, and hail; snow, and vapour; stormy wind fulfilling his word: mountains, and all hills; fruitful trees, and all cedars: beasts, and all cattle; creeping things, and flying fowl." The earthly creation is as diverse and as eloquent in its praise as the heavenly. The great sea creatures of the deep, the dragons of ancient imagination, the elemental forces of fire and hail and snow, the towering mountains and the gentle hills, the fruit-bearing trees and the stately cedars, the wild beasts and the domestic cattle, the creeping insects and the soaring birds, all are called to add their voices to the universal anthem.

What a vision this is! A world in which nothing is profane, nothing is secular, nothing is outside the scope of worship. The Puritans understood this truth profoundly. They did not divide life into sacred and secular compartments. They believed that every lawful occupation, from ploughing the field to governing the nation, from cooking the dinner to preaching the sermon, could be and should be an act of worship, a means of glorifying the God who made us and redeemed us and sustains us by His grace.

"Kings of the earth, and all people; princes, and all judges of the earth: both young men, and maidens; old men, and children: let them praise the name of the Lord: for his name alone is excellent; his glory is above the earth and heaven." At last, the psalm reaches humanity, the crown of God's creation, the beings made in His own image and endowed with rational minds and moral wills and the capacity for conscious, deliberate worship. Kings and commoners, princes and peasants, young and old, male and female, all are summoned to praise. No one is exempt. No one is too high. No one is too low. No one is too young. No one is too old. The praise of God is the great equaliser, the one occupation in which all distinctions of rank and age and sex are swallowed up in a common act of adoration.

"He also exalteth the horn of his people, the praise of all his saints; even of the children of Israel, a people near unto him. Praise ye the Lord." The psalm closes with a special mention of God's covenant people, those who are near to Him, those who have been brought into His family by grace and who know Him not merely as Creator but as Redeemer. And it is fitting that this final note should be struck, for the praise of the redeemed is the sweetest praise of all. The angels praise God for what they see. The creation praises God for what it is. But the redeemed praise God for what He has done, for the blood that was shed, for the price that was paid, for the love that was poured out upon the cross of Calvary. And this praise, the praise of sinners saved by grace, is the song that shall ring through the halls of heaven for all eternity.

And so we end our study of the Psalms as the Psalter itself ends: with praise. Praise ye the Lord. Let everything that has breath, every soul that has been born from above, every heart that has been touched by grace, lift its voice and join the universal chorus. The Lord is worthy. He is worthy of all honour, all glory, all praise, from every creature He has made, now and forevermore. Hallelujah. Amen.
