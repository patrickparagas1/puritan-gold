PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 2
"Training Children in Godliness"
John Angell James — The Family Monitor

---

Hello, dear ones. How wonderful it is to sit together as a family and open the Scriptures. Welcome to Puritan Gold's family devotional. Let us begin with thankful hearts.

Let us pray together. Lord God, we come before You as a family, grateful for Your love and Your provision. We ask that You would quiet our minds and soften our hearts as we hear from You tonight. Help us to understand, to remember, and to obey what You teach us. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Proverbs 22:6. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it."

That is Proverbs 22:6. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Today we turn to one of the most well-known verses in all of Scripture about raising children. Proverbs 22:6 says, 'Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.'

This is a proverb — a wise saying that gives us a general principle of how God designed things to work. Let us look at it carefully, word by word.

The word 'train' in Hebrew is a rich word. It is the word 'chanak,' and it was originally used to describe what a midwife did when a baby was born. The midwife would take a cloth, dip it in crushed dates or olive oil, and rub it on the roof of the newborn's mouth to stimulate the baby's desire to nurse. The idea is to create a taste for something — to awaken an appetite.

So when God says 'train up a child,' He does not merely mean to give information. He means to create in the child a taste and a hunger for godliness. Think of the difference between forcing a child to eat vegetables and cooking vegetables in a way that makes the child actually enjoy them. Training is about shaping desires, not just giving rules.

Now notice the phrase 'in the way he should go.' Some scholars point out that this can also be translated 'according to his way' — meaning according to the child's own unique nature, bent, and stage of development. God knows that each child is different. A wise parent trains each child according to how God has made that particular child, while always pointing them toward righteousness.

The promise that follows is beautiful — 'when he is old, he will not depart from it.' This does not mean that a well-trained child will never struggle or wander. Proverbs are general principles, not ironclad guarantees. But it does tell us that godly training leaves deep roots. Even when seasons of doubt or rebellion come, the seeds planted in childhood have a way of bearing fruit in due time.

The Apostle Paul echoed this same principle when he wrote to the Ephesians, saying in chapter 6, verse 4, 'And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.' The word 'nurture' there means discipline and training, and 'admonition' means instruction and counsel. Together, they paint a picture of parents who lovingly shape their children through both example and teaching.

Now let us hear from John Angell James. In his work The Family Monitor, James wrote powerfully about the sacred duty of parents. He said that the education of children for God is the most important business done on earth — that it is the one business for which the earth exists, and that all politics, war, literature, and money-making should be subordinated to it.

What a bold statement! James believed, based on Scripture, that there is nothing more important happening on the planet than parents raising their children to know and love God. In a world that measures success by careers, income, and status, James reminds us that the eternal souls of our children are worth more than all the wealth and fame on earth.

James also offered practical wisdom. He urged parents to begin early, because impressions made in childhood are the deepest and most lasting. He warned against merely teaching children religious facts without modeling a living faith before them. Children, he said, are far more affected by what they see in their parents' lives than by what they hear from their parents' lips.

He also counseled patience. Training a child in godliness is a long work. A farmer does not plant a seed today and harvest tomorrow. There are seasons of watering, weeding, and waiting. But the harvest will come.

For our youngest listeners, here is the simple truth: God has a special plan for your life, and your parents are helping you learn about it! For those who are a bit older, think about this: training is not just about following rules. It is about learning to love what is good and true. And for parents and older children, consider how the word 'train' means to create an appetite. Are we creating in our home a taste for godliness — through joyful worship, through loving discipline, through honest conversations about faith?

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

The Puritans were men and women who treasured God's Word above all else. Let us hear from one of them now.

John Angell James, in his work "The Family Monitor," wrote these words:

"The education of children for God is the most important business done on earth. It is the one business for which the earth exists. To it all politics, all war, all literature, all money-making ought to be subordinated."

What John Angell James is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Proverbs 22:6 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: What does it mean to 'train' someone? Is it the same as just telling them what to do?

Question 2: Why do you think God says to start training children when they are young?

Question 3: How is training a child like planting a seed and waiting for it to grow?

Question 4: What is the difference between teaching someone rules and creating in them a love for what is right?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it." -- Proverbs 22:6

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

To make tonight's lesson come alive, try this activity together as a family:

Plant a seed together in a small pot (bean seeds or herbs work well). Label it with today's date. As you water it over the coming weeks, talk about how training and growing in faith takes time and patience, just like watching a plant grow.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for this time as a family. Thank You for speaking to us through Your Word. Plant these truths deep in our hearts, especially in the hearts of our children. Help us to walk in wisdom tomorrow and every day. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
