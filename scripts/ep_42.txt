PURITAN GOLD -- Study Devotional, Day 11
Series: Romans
"Dead to Sin, Alive to God"
Union with Christ in His death and resurrection

---

Welcome to our study devotional. The Apostle Paul told Timothy to study to show himself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed. That is our aim tonight: to study with diligence and devotion.

We are studying the book of Romans together. Tonight's lesson is titled "Dead to Sin, Alive to God." Each session builds on what came before, so if you have been following along, you will see how tonight's passage connects to the larger argument of the book.

--- Scripture Reading ---

Let us read our passage for tonight. It comes from Romans 6:1-14:

"What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid. How shall we, that are dead to sin, live any longer therein? Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death? Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life. For if we have been planted together in the likeness of his death, we shall be also in the likeness of his resurrection: Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin. For he that is dead is freed from sin... Likewise reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God through Jesus Christ our Lord. Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body, that ye should obey it in the lusts thereof."

Read that passage again to yourself. Mark any words or phrases that stand out. The Puritans read with a pen in hand, always ready to note what God was showing them.

--- Key Verse ---

Let us zoom in on a verse that is central to everything we will study tonight.

"Likewise reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God through Jesus Christ our Lord." -- Romans 6:11

This verse is the lens through which we will examine the rest of tonight's passage. Keep it in the front of your mind as we proceed.

--- Exposition ---

Now we turn to the substance of tonight's study.

John Owen offers valuable commentary on this passage. The Puritans were expositors of the highest order, and their insights remain remarkably fresh.

John Owen's treatise 'Of the Mortification of Sin in Believers' is one of the most searching works ever written on the Christian's battle against indwelling sin, and Romans 6 is its scriptural foundation. Owen's famous dictum captures the essence of this passage: 'Be killing sin or it will be killing you.' He argued that the imperative to mortify sin (to put it to death) is grounded in the indicative of our union with Christ. Because we have died with Christ (the indicative fact), we must put sin to death (the imperative duty).

Owen stressed that mortification is not merely behavioral modification — cutting off external sins while leaving the root intact. True mortification strikes at the root of indwelling sin, weakening its power, drying up its springs, and progressively destroying its influence in the soul. Owen identified the means of mortification as the application of the death of Christ to the sinful dispositions of the heart through the power of the Holy Spirit. We do not mortify sin in our own strength; we do it by the Spirit (Romans 8:13).

Owen also warned against two dangerous errors. The first is antinomianism — the belief that since we are under grace, sin does not matter. Paul addresses this directly with his 'God forbid!' The second is legalism — the attempt to mortify sin through sheer willpower, self-discipline, and rule-keeping apart from the Spirit. Both errors miss the gospel. The gospel does not say 'sin freely' or 'try harder.' It says 'you have died with Christ — now live accordingly.' Owen taught that the daily practice of mortification involves recognizing the specific sins that beset you, meditating on their sinfulness and the sufferings of Christ, applying the blood of Christ to your conscience through faith, and pleading with the Spirit for power to resist temptation. This is not a one-time event but a lifelong discipline.

Romans 6 opens with an objection that Paul has anticipated since 5:20: 'Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound?' If grace increases where sin increases, why not sin more to get more grace? This is the perennial caricature of the gospel of free grace — that it encourages immorality. Paul's response is as emphatic as language allows: 'God forbid!' (me genoito — let it never be, may it never happen, perish the thought).

Paul's answer to the objection is not a moral lecture but a theological reality: 'How shall we, that are dead to sin, live any longer therein?' The question is not 'should we?' but 'how can we?' It is as absurd as asking whether a dead man can continue committing crimes. The person who is united to Christ has died to sin — not in the sense that sin no longer exists in them, but in the sense that sin's legal dominion has been broken. The master-slave relationship between sin and the believer has been terminated.

Verses 3-5 explain the basis of this death to sin: union with Christ in His death and resurrection. 'Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death?' Baptism here is not merely water baptism but the spiritual reality that water baptism signifies — our incorporation into Christ. When Christ died, we died with Him. When He was buried, we were buried with Him. When He rose, we rose with Him. 'Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.'

This is the doctrine of union with Christ, which John Calvin called 'the central doctrine of the Christian faith.' Everything in the Christian life flows from our union with Christ. Justification is ours because we are in Christ. Sanctification is ours because we are in Christ. Glorification will be ours because we are in Christ. As the branch draws its life from the vine (John 15), so we draw everything from our union with the Savior.

Verse 6 makes the practical implication explicit: 'Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin.' The 'old man' refers to our former identity in Adam — the entire complex of sinful habits, dispositions, and desires that characterized our unregenerate state. This old man has been crucified with Christ. It has been put to death judicially and positionally. The 'body of sin' — the sin-dominated self — has been rendered powerless (the Greek katargeo means to make inoperative, to render ineffective). The purpose: 'that henceforth we should not serve sin.' We are no longer slaves.

Verse 7 states the liberating principle: 'For he that is dead is freed from sin.' The word 'freed' is literally 'justified' (dedikaioutai). Death pays the debt. When a criminal dies, the law no longer has a claim against him. When we died with Christ, sin's legal claim against us was satisfied. We are justified — freed — from sin's dominion.

Verses 8-10 ground our hope in the permanence of Christ's resurrection: 'Now if we be dead with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with him: Knowing that Christ being raised from the dead dieth no more; death hath no more dominion over him.' Christ's death was a once-for-all event. He will never die again. Death has been permanently conquered. 'For in that he died, he died unto sin once: but in that he liveth, he liveth unto God.' Christ's death dealt with sin; His life is lived entirely unto God. Because we are united to Him, we share in both — death to sin and life to God.

Verse 11 is the crucial command: 'Likewise reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God through Jesus Christ our Lord.' The word 'reckon' (logizomai — the same bookkeeping term used for imputation in chapter 4) means to count, to consider, to regard as true. Paul is not telling us to pretend we are dead to sin; he is telling us to recognize the reality that we are dead to sin. Faith is not make-believe; it is taking God at His word about what is actually true. The daily Christian life begins with reckoning — reminding ourselves every morning that we have died with Christ and risen with Christ.

Verses 12-14 draw practical commands from theological reality. 'Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body.' Sin will tempt, but it must not reign. It is a deposed tyrant still trying to exercise authority it no longer possesses. 'Neither yield your members as instruments of unrighteousness unto sin: but yield yourselves unto God, as those that are alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness unto God.' Every part of your body — your eyes, your tongue, your hands, your feet — is an instrument. You choose whose service it will be in. Yield it to God.

The conclusion in verse 14 is both a promise and a command: 'For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace.' This is not permission to sin but power over sin. The law said 'do not sin' but gave no power to obey. Grace says 'you have died to sin' and provides the Holy Spirit as the power for new obedience. The dominion of sin is broken not by trying harder but by being in Christ.

--- Application ---

The Puritans asked three questions of every passage: What does it teach? What does it rebuke? What does it require? Let us ask those questions now.

Romans 6:1-14 provides the foundation for daily victory over sin. Here are specific action steps.

First, begin each day with reckoning. Before you check your phone, before you read the news, before you engage with the demands of the day, take two minutes to reckon: 'I am dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus.' This is not positive thinking; it is theological reality. Make this your first conscious thought every morning. Write Romans 6:11 on a card and place it by your bed.

Second, identify the specific sins that try to reign in your mortal body and bring them under the lordship of Christ. Owen taught that mortification must be specific, not general. Do not merely pray 'help me sin less.' Name the sin: anger, lust, greed, anxiety, gossip, laziness. Then apply the death of Christ to it: 'This sin has no dominion over me because I have died with Christ and am under grace, not law.'

Third, yield your members as instruments of righteousness. Your eyes — what will you look at today? Your tongue — what will you say? Your hands — what will you do? Your feet — where will you go? Consciously dedicate each part of your body to God's service this morning. When temptation comes to use them for sin, remember: these members belong to God. They have been raised from the dead for His purposes.

Fourth, rely on grace, not willpower. Verse 14 says sin shall not have dominion because you are under grace, not law. Legalistic self-effort will always fail against the power of indwelling sin. Only the grace of God, applied through the Holy Spirit, can mortify sin at its root. When you struggle, do not resolve to try harder; instead, cry out for more grace. 'Lord, I cannot conquer this sin in my own strength. Apply the power of the cross to my heart by Your Spirit.'

Write down one concrete action you will take this week in response to tonight's study. Do not let this truth remain theoretical. The Puritans measured the success of a sermon not by how much the hearer learned but by how much the hearer changed.

--- Closing ---

Lord Jesus, we thank You that when You died, we died with You. When You rose, we rose with You. Our old man has been crucified; sin's dominion is broken. Help us to reckon this truth every day. Forgive us for the times we have yielded our members to sin rather than to righteousness. Fill us with Your Spirit and give us power to mortify the deeds of the body. May sin not reign in our mortal bodies but may grace reign in our hearts through righteousness unto eternal life. We are not under law but under grace — and by that grace, make us holy. Amen.

Go in peace, student of the Word. God rewards those who diligently seek Him.
