PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 19
"Raising Children in the Fear of the Lord"
William Gouge — On the Duties of Parents Together

---

Welcome, dear couple. Tonight we turn to a topic that unites husband and wife in one of their most sacred callings: the nurture and education of children.

Our Scripture tonight is Ephesians 6:4:

"And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord."

And Proverbs 22:6:

"Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it."

--- Teaching ---

William Gouge insisted that the raising of children is a joint venture. While he addressed fathers specifically — since Ephesians 6:4 does — he made clear that mothers are equally involved in the spiritual formation of the next generation. In fact, Gouge argued that mothers often have more daily influence on young children than fathers, simply by virtue of the time they spend together.

Gouge's vision of parenting is built on three pillars. The first is instruction — teaching children the truths of Scripture, the doctrines of the faith, and the principles of godly living. The Puritans were passionate about family worship, and Gouge counseled daily times of Bible reading, prayer, and catechesis in the home. These need not be long or elaborate, but they must be consistent. A child who grows up hearing God's Word read daily absorbs it the way roots absorb water — slowly, deeply, and with lasting effect.

The second pillar is example. Children learn far more from what their parents do than from what they say. A husband and wife who love each other visibly — who speak kindly, who forgive freely, who pray together, who serve the church, who show mercy to the poor — are giving their children a living textbook of the Christian faith. Conversely, parents who preach one thing and practice another are training their children in hypocrisy.

The third pillar is discipline — not merely punishment, but the broader work of shaping character. Gouge counseled both correction and encouragement, both firmness and gentleness. He warned fathers against provoking their children to wrath through excessive harshness, arbitrary rules, or inconsistent standards. And he warned mothers against indulgence that fails to prepare children for the realities of adult life.

Richard Baxter added that husband and wife must present a united front in their parenting. Children are remarkably perceptive, and they quickly learn to exploit divisions between their parents. If father says no and mother says yes, the child learns manipulation rather than obedience. Baxter counseled couples to discuss parenting decisions privately and then present a consistent message to their children.

The Puritans also recognized that parenting is spiritually exhausting and that couples must support each other through the long years of child-rearing. A husband who comes home and immediately retreats to his own pursuits, leaving his wife to manage the children alone, is failing in his calling. A wife who undermines her husband's authority in front of the children is weakening the family structure. Together, as a team, you can raise children who know and love the Lord. Alone, the task is overwhelming.

Tonight, whether your children are young or grown, recommit to the sacred work of raising them together — in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Discuss together:

Question 1: How would you evaluate your family's spiritual rhythms — Bible reading, prayer, worship, faith conversations? What would you like to change or strengthen?

Question 2: Are you presenting a united front in your parenting, or are there areas of inconsistency that your children might be exploiting?

Question 3: How can you better support each other in the demanding work of parenting? What does your spouse need from you this week?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Pray together for your children — by name, specifically. Ask God to save them, to sanctify them, to protect them, and to use them for His glory. Pray for wisdom and unity in your parenting, and for the grace to model the faith you want your children to embrace.

--- Closing ---

May the Lord who entrusted you with these precious souls give you the wisdom, patience, and love to raise them well. May your children rise up and call you blessed, and may they carry the faith into the next generation. Goodnight.
