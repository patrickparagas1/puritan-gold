PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 10
"The Daughters of Sarah"
John Angell James — Female Piety


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we continue with John Angell James as he looks at the godly wife — the woman whose faith transforms her marriage into a reflection of the gospel. Let us open our hearts to the Word.

Let us pray. Heavenly Father, we thank You for the covenant of marriage and the grace You pour into it. Speak to us tonight through the example of godly women in Scripture and through the wisdom of Your servant John Angell James. May every marriage in our family be strengthened by what we hear. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from 1 Peter 3:1-6. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Likewise, ye wives, be in subjection to your own husbands; that, if any obey not the word, they also may without the word be won by the conversation of the wives; While they behold your chaste conversation coupled with fear. Whose adorning let it not be that outward adorning of plaiting the hair, and of wearing of gold, or of putting on of apparel; But let it be the hidden man of the heart, in that which is not corruptible, even the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price. For after this manner in the old time the holy women also, who trusted in God, adorned themselves, being in subjection unto their own husbands: Even as Sara obeyed Abraham, calling him lord: whose daughters ye are, as long as ye do well, and are not afraid with any amazement."

That is 1 Peter 3:1-6. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Tonight John Angell James turns his attention to the godly wife and the power of her influence within marriage. His teaching is anchored in one of the most remarkable statements in the New Testament: that an unbelieving husband may be "won without the word" — won not by arguments, not by nagging, not by lectures on theology, but by the quiet, consistent, beautiful witness of a godly wife's life.

James finds this astonishing. Peter is saying that the daily conduct of a faithful wife can accomplish what the most eloquent sermon cannot — the conversion of a soul. Not because the wife replaces the gospel, but because she embodies it. Her life becomes a living illustration of the transforming power of Christ. Her husband sees her faith in action — her patience under trial, her kindness under provocation, her peace in the midst of difficulty, her joy that does not depend on circumstances — and he is drawn to ask: what is the source of this woman's strength?

James identifies several marks of the godly wife that Peter commends. The first is "chaste conversation" — which in the language of the King James Version means a pure, holy manner of life. The godly wife lives with integrity. Her private character matches her public profession. She is the same person behind closed doors as she is before the world. This consistency is one of the most powerful testimonies any human being can bear.

The second mark is the "meek and quiet spirit" we discussed in our previous devotion. James is careful to explain that this does not mean a wife should be passive, voiceless, or without opinions. The Proverbs 31 woman speaks with wisdom and the law of kindness is on her tongue. She is active, engaged, and influential. But her strength is exercised in a spirit of gentleness rather than domination, of peace rather than contention, of trust in God rather than anxious self-assertion. A meek spirit is not a weak spirit — it is a spirit under the control of the Holy Spirit.

The third mark is fearlessness. Peter concludes by saying the daughters of Sarah "do well, and are not afraid with any amazement." This is a remarkable phrase. It means that the godly wife is not paralyzed by fear — not by fear of the future, not by fear of her circumstances, not by fear of man. Her confidence is in God, and because her confidence is in God, she is free from the anxiety that grips those who trust in lesser things. James writes that this fearlessness is one of the most attractive qualities a woman can possess, because it reveals a soul that is anchored in something deeper and more reliable than anything this world can offer.

James speaks with particular tenderness to wives whose husbands are not believers, or whose husbands are cold and indifferent to spiritual things. He counsels them not to despair but to persevere. Continue to live faithfully. Continue to pray fervently. Continue to serve lovingly. Let your life be the sermon your husband hears every day. And trust that the God who works through quiet, faithful, consistent witness is able to reach the hardest heart.

But James also speaks to wives in believing households. Even where both husband and wife know the Lord, the wife's influence is immeasurable. She sets the emotional and spiritual tone of the home. Her attitude toward her husband's leadership either encourages him or undermines him. Her response to difficulty either models faith for her children or models anxiety. Her words either build her husband up or tear him down. James urges believing wives to see their role not as a limitation but as a calling of the highest order — a calling to reflect the beauty of the church's love for Christ in the way they love their husbands.

Let our family tonight honor the wives and mothers among us, and let us pray that God would make every woman in our family a daughter of Sarah — faithful, fearless, and adorned with the imperishable beauty of a godly heart.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Peter says an unbelieving husband can be "won without the word" by his wife's life. How does a godly example speak louder than words?

Question 2: What does it mean to "not be afraid with any amazement"? How does trust in God produce fearlessness?

Question 3: James says the wife sets the emotional and spiritual tone of the home. Do you agree? How does one person's attitude affect the atmosphere of the whole household?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"The ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price." -- 1 Peter 3:4

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for the godly wives and women in our family. Clothe them with strength and honor. Adorn them with the beauty of a meek and quiet spirit. Make them fearless daughters of Sarah who trust in You without wavering. And use their quiet, faithful witness to draw others to Christ. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
