PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 27
"Preparing Children for the World"
Richard Baxter — The Christian Directory

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Baxter speaks about one of the most bittersweet duties of parenting — preparing children to leave the nest and enter the world as faithful, capable, godly adults. Let us draw near to God together.

Let us pray. Lord God, You have entrusted children to our care for a season, but they belong to You. Help us to prepare them well — not just for careers and responsibilities, but for a life of faith, integrity, and service. Give us wisdom for this sacred task. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Proverbs 22:6 and 3 John 1:4. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it. I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth."

That is Proverbs 22:6 and 3 John 1:4. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Richard Baxter was a realist about the world. He knew that the children raised in Christian homes would eventually step beyond those walls into a world that was often hostile to the faith their parents had taught them. He knew that the temptations they would face would be real and powerful. And he knew that the preparation they received at home would, in large measure, determine whether they stood firm or fell away.

In "A Christian Directory," Baxter addresses the duty of parents to prepare their children for independent life with characteristic thoroughness. His counsel covers four dimensions: spiritual preparation, moral preparation, intellectual preparation, and vocational preparation.

Spiritual preparation is the foundation. Baxter insists that the most important thing a parent can give a child is a genuine, personal, experiential knowledge of God. Not merely information about God — the catechism provides that — but a living relationship with God through Jesus Christ. Parents cannot convert their children. That is the work of the Holy Spirit. But parents can create the conditions in which conversion is most likely to occur: a home filled with prayer, saturated with Scripture, marked by authentic faith, and permeated by the love of God. Baxter writes that the parent's greatest prayer should be for the child's salvation, and the parent's greatest labor should be to set before the child a living example of what it looks like to walk with God.

Moral preparation equips the child with the virtues needed to navigate a fallen world. Baxter identifies honesty, courage, self-control, compassion, and diligence as the essential moral qualities every child needs. These are not innate — they must be cultivated through instruction, example, and practice. A child who has learned to tell the truth at home will tell the truth in the marketplace. A child who has practiced self-control at the family table will exercise self-control in the face of temptation. A child who has been taught compassion for the weak will stand up for justice when others look away.

Intellectual preparation gives the child the tools to think clearly, reason soundly, and evaluate the ideas and philosophies he will encounter in the wider world. Baxter was a passionate advocate for education. He believed that ignorance was one of the greatest enemies of the faith, because an ignorant person is easily deceived. A child who has been taught to read carefully, to think critically, to test every claim against the standard of God's Word, and to distinguish truth from error will be far better equipped to resist the false teachings and deceptive philosophies that abound in every age.

Vocational preparation helps the child discover and develop the particular gifts and callings God has given him. Baxter counsels parents to observe their children carefully — to notice their aptitudes, their interests, their strengths, and their temperaments — and to guide them toward callings suited to their God-given design. He warns against two errors: the error of forcing a child into a calling for which he is unsuited (usually to fulfill the parent's ambitions rather than the child's calling) and the error of leaving the matter entirely to chance, without guidance or direction.

Baxter concludes with a word of hope and a word of trust. The hope is Proverbs 22:6: "Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it." This is not a guarantee that every child will turn out exactly as the parents hope — it is a general principle that faithful parenting produces lasting fruit. The training may not bear visible fruit immediately. A child may wander. A young adult may rebel. But the seeds planted in childhood are not easily uprooted, and God is faithful to honor the labors of faithful parents.

The trust is in God Himself. Baxter reminds parents that their children are not ultimately theirs — they are God's. The same God who entrusted these children to your care is the God who watches over them when they leave your home. He will pursue them with His love. He will draw them with His grace. He will guard them with His providence. Your part is to be faithful in the preparation. God's part is to be faithful in the preservation. And He has never failed.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Baxter identifies four dimensions of preparation: spiritual, moral, intellectual, and vocational. Which of these does our family emphasize most? Which needs more attention?

Question 2: Baxter says the most important preparation is a living, personal knowledge of God — not just information about Him. What is the difference? How can parents cultivate this in their children?

Question 3: For older children: what do you feel you need most to prepare you for adult life? For parents: what do you most want your children to carry with them when they leave home?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth." -- 3 John 1:4

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord, we are preparing our children not just for this world but for eternity. Help us to be faithful in every dimension — spiritual, moral, intellectual, and vocational. And when our children leave this home, go with them. Guard them, guide them, and keep them in the truth. Our greatest joy will be to see them walking with You. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
