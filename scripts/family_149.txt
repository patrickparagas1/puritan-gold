PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 18
"Remedy: Keep Eternity in View"
Pilgrim Families — Week 3, Day 4


Good evening, beloved family. The Puritans believed that the family altar was the most important altar in all the land. Tonight we gather at that altar together. Welcome to Puritan Gold.

Let us come before the Lord. Gracious God, thank You for the gift of family. Thank You that we can come to You together. We pray that You would teach us from Your Word this evening. Let every word sink deep into our hearts. We love You, Lord. In Jesus' name, Amen.

The Word of God is a lamp to our feet and a light to our path. Tonight that light shines from 2 Corinthians 4:17-18; Matthew 25:46:

"For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory; While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal. And these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal."

That is 2 Corinthians 4:17-18; Matthew 25:46. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Having examined three of Satan's devices, we now turn to the remedies Brooks prescribes. The first and greatest remedy against all temptation is to keep eternity in view. When we see our choices in the light of forever, the appeal of sin shrinks and the value of holiness magnifies.

Paul makes an astonishing statement in 2 Corinthians 4:17: "Our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory." Consider what Paul had endured — beatings, stonings, shipwrecks, imprisonments, hunger, constant danger. He calls all of this "light affliction" and "but for a moment." How could he say this? Because he was comparing his suffering to eternity. When measured against an infinite weight of glory, even the worst suffering on earth is light and momentary.

Verse 18 gives the principle: "We look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal." Everything we can see with our physical eyes is temporary — our houses, our possessions, our bodies, our pain, our pleasure. All of it will pass away. But the things we cannot see — God, heaven, our souls, the consequences of our choices — these are eternal.

Jesus Himself taught the reality of eternity in Matthew 25:46: "These shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal." Eternity is not a metaphor. It is not a figure of speech. It is the actual, literal, never-ending reality that awaits every human being. Either everlasting punishment or life eternal. There is no middle ground, no temporary option, no "try again later."

Brooks argued that one of the primary reasons people give in to temptation is that they forget eternity. They live as if this life is all there is. But the Christian who keeps eternity in view makes very different choices. Will I choose a moment of sinful pleasure that will bring eternal regret? Or will I choose a moment of self-denial that will bring eternal reward? When eternity is in the picture, the math changes dramatically.

The Puritans were deeply serious about eternity. They thought about it daily. They preached about it regularly. They evaluated every decision by it. This was not morbid or depressing — it was clarifying. Knowing that this life is short and eternity is long gave them freedom from the tyranny of the moment. They could endure suffering because it was temporary. They could resist temptation because its pleasures were fleeting. They could sacrifice generously because they were investing in forever.

For families, keeping eternity in view does not mean being gloomy. It means being wise. It means helping your children understand that every choice they make has consequences that extend beyond this moment, beyond this week, beyond this life. When a child is tempted to cheat on a test, eternity says: "Integrity lasts forever; a grade is forgotten tomorrow." When a teenager is pressured to compromise their faith, eternity says: "What does it profit a man to gain the whole world and lose his own soul?"

Brooks encourages believers to meditate regularly on the brevity of life and the length of eternity. Psalm 90:12 prays, "So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom." When you realize how few days you have on this earth compared to the infinite ages of eternity, you become very careful about how you spend them.

Practically, take a moment tonight to discuss eternity as a family. Not with fear, but with hope. For the Christian, eternity is not a threat but a promise. It is the reason we can face any trial, resist any temptation, and endure any suffering. Because we know that at the end of the narrow way is a weight of glory so great that it will make every hardship seem like nothing. This is the remedy that defeats every device of the enemy: keep your eyes on eternity.


The wisdom of the Puritans remains as fresh today as when it was first written. Listen to this insight.

Thomas Brooks, in his work "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices," wrote these words:

"Eternity! Eternity! It is this that makes hell to be hell, and that will make heaven to be heaven."

What Thomas Brooks is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in 2 Corinthians 4:17-18; Matthew 25:46 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Here are some questions for us to think about and discuss together. There are no wrong answers here, just honest hearts.

Question 1: How does thinking about eternity change the way we look at our problems today?

Question 2: Paul called his severe sufferings 'light' and 'momentary.' What made him able to see them that way?

Question 3: How does keeping eternity in view help us resist temptation? Can you give a specific example?

Question 4: What excites you most about eternity with God?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal." -- 2 Corinthians 4:18

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a hands-on way to remember what we have learned tonight:

Use a very long piece of string or rope. Mark one tiny section (about one inch) with tape to represent your life on earth. The rest of the string represents eternity. Hold it up and look at how small the marked section is compared to the whole. Discuss how this perspective changes the way we think about our choices today.


Father, we have heard Your Word tonight, and we are grateful. We confess that we cannot live it out in our own strength. Fill us with Your Spirit. Give us wisdom for tomorrow, patience with one another, and a deep love for You. Keep our family close to You always. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Rest well tonight. God is faithful, and His wisdom is sure.
