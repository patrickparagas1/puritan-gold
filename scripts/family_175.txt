PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 14
"The Eternal Weight of Glory"
John Angell James — Female Piety

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. We close our week with John Angell James by lifting our eyes from the daily labors of the household to the eternal reward that awaits every faithful servant of Christ. Let us end this week with our hearts set on heaven.

Let us pray. Eternal God, our hearts are restless until they rest in You. Lift our eyes tonight above the cares and labors of this world and fix them on the glory that awaits us in Christ. Give us a heavenly mind and a hopeful heart. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from 2 Corinthians 4:16-18. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"For which cause we faint not; but though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day. For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory; While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal."

That is 2 Corinthians 4:16-18. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

John Angell James closes the great themes of "Female Piety" by directing the reader's gaze heavenward. After speaking of the duties, the dangers, and the daily disciplines of the godly life, he lifts the curtain on eternity and reminds us that everything we do in this life — every prayer, every sacrifice, every act of love, every moment of faithful obedience — is preparation for an inheritance that will never fade.

Paul's words in 2 Corinthians 4 are among the most magnificent in all of Scripture. He calls our present sufferings "light affliction" — and this from a man who was beaten, stoned, shipwrecked, imprisoned, and constantly in danger of death. How can he call such suffering light? Because he compares it to the glory that is coming. The affliction is "but for a moment" — a vapor, a breath, a fleeting shadow. But the glory is "far more exceeding and eternal" — a weight so heavy, so rich, so immeasurable that no human language can adequately describe it.

James applies this truth directly to the experience of the faithful woman — and by extension, to every faithful member of the household. The mother who rises at dawn and works until midnight, whose labors are unseen and unrewarded by the world — she is storing up an eternal weight of glory. The wife who bears patiently with a difficult husband, who prays when she wants to complain, who serves when she wants to withdraw — she is laying up treasures in heaven. The daughter who honors her parents, the sister who cares for her siblings, the grandmother who prays faithfully for her grandchildren — each one is investing in eternity.

James writes that the great problem with our spiritual vision is that we focus on what is seen rather than what is unseen. We are so absorbed in the visible, tangible, immediate realities of daily life that we forget the invisible, eternal realities that lie just beyond the veil. Paul tells us to reverse this perspective: "Look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen." The seen things are temporal — passing, fading, dissolving like morning mist. The unseen things are eternal — permanent, unshakeable, enduring forever.

What are these unseen things? The love of God that never fails. The intercession of Christ that never ceases. The indwelling of the Spirit that never departs. The promises of Scripture that never break. The inheritance of the saints that never perishes. The crown of righteousness that never tarnishes. The joy of the Lord that never ends. These are the realities on which the godly soul must fix her gaze, especially when the seen things become painful, disappointing, or overwhelming.

James tells the story of a godly woman on her deathbed who was asked if she was afraid. She replied, "Why should I be afraid? I have been living for this moment all my life." That is the fruit of a life lived with eternity in view. Death is not a catastrophe but a coronation. It is not the end of the story but the beginning of the chapter that will never end.

For our family tonight, let us lift our eyes. The daily round of household duties, the struggles of raising children, the challenges of marriage, the demands of work and service — all of these are real and significant. But they are not the whole story. They are the preface. The story itself — the eternal weight of glory — is yet to unfold. And when it does, every sacrifice will be vindicated, every tear will be wiped away, every hidden act of faithfulness will be brought into the light and rewarded by the King of kings.

This is the hope that sustained the Puritans through persecution, poverty, and suffering. This is the hope that sustained John Angell James through fifty years of pastoral ministry. And this is the hope that will sustain our family through whatever lies ahead. We are pilgrims bound for glory, and the best is yet to come.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Paul calls our present sufferings "light affliction." How can he say this, given everything he endured? What makes the comparison with eternal glory so overwhelming?

Question 2: James says we tend to focus on the seen rather than the unseen. What are some "seen things" that most distract our family from eternal priorities?

Question 3: The dying woman said, "I have been living for this moment all my life." What would it look like for our family to live with eternity in view every day?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory." -- 2 Corinthians 4:17

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Eternal God, we thank You for the hope of glory. When the duties of this life feel heavy and the road feels long, remind us that our present struggles are preparing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison. Fix our eyes on things unseen and eternal. Carry us home at last to the joy You have prepared for those who love You. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
