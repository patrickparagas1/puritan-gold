PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"Walk Worthy of the Calling"
July 16, 2026

---

"I therefore, the prisoner of the Lord, beseech you that ye walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called, with all lowliness and meekness, with longsuffering, forbearing one another in love; endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace." -- Ephesians 4:1-6

With chapter four, Paul transitions from doctrine to duty, from creed to conduct, from what God has done to how we should live. The word 'therefore' is the hinge upon which the whole epistle turns. Because God has blessed us, chosen us, redeemed us, quickened us, seated us in heavenly places -- therefore, we must walk worthy of so great a salvation. The Puritans were masters at connecting doctrine to practice. They never taught truth in the abstract; they always pressed it home to the conscience and the daily life. As John Owen said, 'A man may have his head full of truth and his heart empty of grace.' The test of true theology is not how much we know but how we live. And the first virtue Paul commends is lowliness -- humility. Not the groveling false humility that says 'I am nothing,' but the settled, sober self-assessment that comes from knowing God's greatness and our dependence upon Him. From humility flows meekness, patience, forbearance, and love -- the virtues that preserve the unity of the Spirit. Unity is not something we create; it is something the Spirit has already created, and our task is to keep it, to guard it, to protect it from the divisive forces of pride, jealousy, and selfish ambition. Amen.
