PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"Far Above All Principality and Power"
July 6, 2026

---

"And what is the exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward who believe, according to the working of his mighty power, which he wrought in Christ, when he raised him from the dead, and set him at his own right hand in the heavenly places, far above all principality, and power, and might, and dominion, and every name that is named, not only in this world, but also in that which is to come: and hath put all things under his feet, and gave him to be the head over all things to the church, which is his body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all." -- Ephesians 1:19-23

The first chapter of Ephesians ends with one of the most exalted descriptions of Christ's glory in all of Scripture. Paul tells us that God raised Christ from the dead and seated Him at His right hand, far above all principality and power and might and dominion. This is not merely a statement about Christ's location; it is a declaration of His absolute and universal sovereignty.

Thomas Goodwin spent years meditating upon this passage, and he concluded that the exaltation of Christ is the pattern and guarantee of the exaltation of His people. Just as Christ was raised from the lowest depths to the highest heights, so His people shall be raised from the dust of death to the glory of heaven. And just as all things were put under Christ's feet, so all our enemies -- sin, death, Satan, the world -- are put under our feet in Him.

But the most astonishing statement in this passage is the last one: the church is Christ's body, 'the fulness of him that filleth all in all.' Think of it! Christ who fills all things is Himself filled by the church. The infinite God finds His completeness, as it were, in the company of redeemed sinners. Samuel Rutherford, that Scottish saint whose letters breathe the very air of heaven, once wrote that Christ considers Himself incomplete without His bride, just as a head is incomplete without a body. Not that Christ lacks anything in Himself, but that in His condescending love He has bound Himself to His people with bonds that cannot be broken.

What comfort this brings to the weakest and most trembling believer! You are a member of Christ's body. You are part of His fullness. He would no sooner cast you away than a man would amputate his own hand. You are joined to Him by the Spirit, united to Him by faith, and destined to share in His glory forever. As William Gurnall wrote in his great work 'The Christian in Complete Armour,' the believer's security rests not in his grip upon Christ, but in Christ's grip upon him. And the hand that holds you is the same hand that governs the universe. Amen.
