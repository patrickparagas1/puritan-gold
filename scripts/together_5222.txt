PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 22
"A Good Wife, God's Gift"
Thomas Gataker — The Virtuous Wife


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to our fourth week in the Together devotional. This week we meditate on the beauty and dignity of the godly wife, drawing from Proverbs 31 and from the writings of the Puritans, especially Thomas Gataker, whose treatise on marriage is one of the treasures of the Reformed tradition.

Let us read our passage. Proverbs 18:22 (KJV):

"Whoso findeth a wife findeth a good thing, and obtaineth favour of the LORD."


Thomas Gataker, a Puritan minister and Westminster Assembly divine, preached a series of marriage sermons that were published in 1623 under the title A Good Wife God's Gift. In this work, Gataker expounded the simple but profound truth of Proverbs 18:22: a good wife is a gift from God, and the man who finds her has received divine favor.

Gataker was writing against two errors of his age. The first was the tendency to exalt celibacy above marriage, as though remaining unmarried were inherently more spiritual than being wed. Gataker, along with the other Reformers, rejected this view. He argued from Scripture that marriage was instituted by God before the Fall, that it was blessed by Christ at Cana, and that it is called "honourable in all" by the writer of Hebrews (13:4). Far from being a concession to human weakness, marriage is one of God's greatest gifts to humanity.

The second error Gataker confronted was the tendency of men to view marriage as a mere social arrangement, a transaction involving dowries, family alliances, and economic advantage. Gataker insisted that a truly good wife is worth infinitely more than any material gain. Her value cannot be measured in financial terms. She is a gift from the Lord Himself, and the man who receives such a gift should overflow with gratitude.

But what makes a wife "good" in the biblical sense? Gataker was clear that the goodness of a wife is not primarily about her beauty, her social standing, her education, or even her domestic skills, though all of these have their place. The goodness of a wife, for Gataker, is rooted in her relationship with God. A wife who fears the Lord, who walks in wisdom, who governs her tongue with grace, who serves her household with diligence, and who loves her husband with a faithful heart, this is the good wife whom Proverbs celebrates.

Gataker also addressed husbands directly. He warned that many men fail to appreciate the gift God has given them. They take their wives for granted. They notice every flaw and overlook every virtue. They compare their wives unfavorably to other women. They forget that the woman sitting across from them at dinner is a gift from the Almighty, bestowed in His favor. Gataker urged husbands to cultivate the habit of gratitude, to look at their wives with fresh eyes, and to thank God daily for the companion He has provided.

For the wife, Gataker's teaching is an encouragement. Your worth is not determined by the world's standards. You need not compete with impossible ideals of beauty, success, or perfection. If you fear the Lord and walk in faithfulness, you are the good wife of Proverbs, and your value is beyond all earthly reckoning. Your husband is a blessed man, whether he always recognizes it or not.

William Gouge added that the relationship between husband and wife is a channel of God's grace. Through the wife, God blesses the husband with companionship, counsel, comfort, and partnership. Through the husband, God blesses the wife with protection, provision, leadership, and love. Each is a gift to the other, and each should be received with the gratitude that a divine gift deserves.

Dear couple, tonight let gratitude fill your hearts. Husband, look at your wife and thank God for her. Tell her what she means to you. Be specific. Name the ways she blesses your life. Wife, receive this gratitude with a glad heart, and know that you are valued not merely by your husband but by the God who gave you to him.


Question 1: Husband, when was the last time you expressed genuine, specific gratitude to your wife for who she is and what she does?

Question 2: Wife, do you feel valued and appreciated in your marriage? What helps you feel most honored?

Question 3: How can we build a daily practice of expressing gratitude to each other and to God for the gift of our marriage?


Almighty God, we thank Thee for the gift of marriage and for the gift of each other. Thou hast bestowed upon us a treasure more precious than gold, and we have too often failed to recognize its worth. Open the husband's eyes to see his wife as Thy good gift. Open the wife's heart to receive the honor that is her due. Fill this marriage with gratitude so deep and so constant that it transforms every interaction. We praise Thee, O Lord, for Thy favor shown to us in joining our lives together. In the name of Jesus Christ, Amen.
