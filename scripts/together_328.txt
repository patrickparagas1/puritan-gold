PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 28
"Confessing and Forsaking Sin Together"
Proverbs 28:13

---

Welcome, husband and wife. There is something powerful about two hearts turning toward God at the same time. When you both face the same direction, toward Christ, everything in your marriage finds its proper alignment. Let us do that now.

Every marriage needs a foundation that does not shift. Let us hear from that foundation now, from Proverbs 28:13:

"He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy."

That is Proverbs 28:13. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

The Puritans treasured marriage as a covenant made before God. Matthew Henry wrote beautifully about how Scripture speaks to the hearts of married couples.

Matthew Henry treats this verse as one of the clearest statements in the Old Testament of the doctrine of repentance. Two responses to sin are contrasted: covering and confessing. The one who covers — who hides, denies, minimizes, or excuses sin — 'shall not prosper.' Henry writes: 'Those that conceal their sins, that cover them from themselves by willful ignorance, or from others by hypocrisy, shall not prosper. Sin, sooner or later, will find them out.'

The one who confesses and forsakes, however, shall have mercy. Henry notes the two-part requirement: confession alone is insufficient if it is not accompanied by forsaking. And forsaking alone is insufficient if it is not preceded by honest confession. 'We must both acknowledge our sin and abandon it,' Henry writes. 'To confess without forsaking is mere sentiment. To forsake without confessing is mere reformation. Only when both are present is there true repentance.'

Charles Bridges adds that the promise of mercy is extravagant: not merely tolerance or grudging acceptance, but mercy — the compassionate, healing, restoring love of God toward the penitent sinner. Bridges writes: 'God does not meet the confessing sinner with a frown but with open arms. The mercy He offers is not partial but complete, covering every sin that is honestly acknowledged and genuinely forsaken.'

For the married couple, Bridges applies this truth searchingly: 'The marriage that refuses to deal honestly with sin will not prosper. The marriage that practices regular confession and genuine repentance will be continually renewed by mercy.'

--- Marriage Application ---

Sin, left unconfessed, is a cancer in marriage. It does not go away on its own; it grows in the dark. Whether it is a harsh word never apologized for, a deception never brought to light, a habit never acknowledged, or a pattern of selfishness never addressed, hidden sin corrodes the trust and intimacy that marriage requires.

Build a culture of confession in your marriage. This does not mean constant, morbid introspection, but a willingness to say, 'I was wrong. I am sorry. I will change.' And it means a corresponding willingness to extend mercy: 'I forgive you. Let us move forward together.'

Both confession and mercy require courage. Confession requires the courage to be vulnerable, to admit failure, to risk rejection. Mercy requires the courage to release the right to punish, to trust that God will deal with what we cannot, and to extend grace even when it is costly. But this is the path to prosperity in marriage: not material prosperity, but the prosperity of a relationship that is continually healed and renewed.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Now turn to each other. Here are tonight's reflection questions. Answer from the heart, not from the head.

Question 1: Is there any sin I have been covering rather than confessing in our marriage?

Question 2: When my spouse confesses, do I respond with mercy or with punishment?

Question 3: How can we create a safer environment for honest confession and genuine repentance in our relationship?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

The Puritans ended every evening with prayer. Let us follow their example and bring our marriage before the Lord.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God for the courage to confess sin honestly and the grace to extend mercy generously. If there is anything hidden between you, bring it into the light before God and before each other, trusting in His promise of mercy.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the God of mercy who forgives the confessing sinner grant your marriage the grace of honest repentance and generous forgiveness, that you may prosper in His love. Amen.

May the words you have heard tonight bear fruit in your marriage for years to come. The Lord be with you both. Until next time.
