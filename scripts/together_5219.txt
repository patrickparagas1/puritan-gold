PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 19
"Hospitality as a Couple"
Domestical Duties — Puritan Household Duties


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we consider a duty that William Gouge regarded as one of the most beautiful expressions of the Christian marriage: hospitality. The home that opens its doors to others is a home that reflects the heart of God, who opens His arms to all who come to Him.

Let us read our passage. Hebrews 13:2 (KJV):

"Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares."


William Gouge taught that the Christian home is not a fortress to be defended but a garden to be shared. The husband and wife who receive guests into their home are participating in one of the most ancient and honored practices of the people of God. Abraham entertained angels at the oaks of Mamre. The Shunammite woman prepared a room for the prophet Elisha. Lydia opened her home to Paul and his companions in Philippi. In each case, hospitality was an expression of faith, a tangible way of saying, "What God has given us, we share with others."

Gouge observed that hospitality is a joint duty. It requires the cooperation and shared commitment of both husband and wife. The husband who desires a hospitable home but leaves all the work to his wife is not practicing hospitality; he is imposing a burden. The wife who wants to open their home but does not consult her husband about the timing, the frequency, or the cost is not honoring the partnership of marriage. True hospitality flows from a united decision, a shared labor, and a common joy in welcoming others.

The Puritans did not view hospitality as entertaining. There is a difference. Entertaining is focused on impressing the guests, on showcasing a beautiful home, a perfect meal, and a flawless evening. It is performance. Hospitality is focused on serving the guests, on making them feel welcome, valued, and loved, regardless of the state of the house or the simplicity of the meal. Entertaining says, "Look at us." Hospitality says, "We see you, and we are glad you are here."

Gouge identified several spiritual benefits of hospitality for the marriage. First, it combats selfishness. A couple that regularly opens its home to others is reminded that their life is not just about them. Their resources, their time, and their space are gifts from God to be shared. Second, hospitality strengthens the marriage bond. Working together to prepare for guests, serving side by side, and reflecting afterward on the evening creates shared experiences that draw a couple closer. Third, hospitality is a witness to the gospel. When an unbelieving neighbor enters a Christian home and experiences genuine warmth, generosity, and love, they encounter something that the world cannot explain. The Christian home becomes a living sermon.

Richard Baxter added that hospitality also benefits the marriage by preventing isolation. Couples who turn entirely inward, who never open their lives to others, can become stagnant and self-absorbed. The presence of guests brings fresh perspectives, new conversations, and opportunities for ministry that enrich the marriage rather than depleting it. Of course, Baxter was quick to add that hospitality must be balanced with the couple's need for privacy and rest. The home that is always full of guests can become as unhealthy as the home that is always empty. Wisdom is needed.

The Puritans also practiced what might be called spiritual hospitality: inviting others into their home for prayer, Bible reading, and Christian fellowship. This was the seedbed of many of the great revivals and reform movements in church history. When a couple opens their home for the study of God's Word and the fellowship of believers, they are participating in something that has eternal significance.

Dear couple, is your home a place of hospitality? Do you welcome others with warmth and generosity? Or have you become so consumed with the busyness of your own lives that you have forgotten this sacred duty? You need not wait for a perfect house or a perfect schedule. Begin where you are, with what you have. Invite someone over this week. Serve a simple meal. Open your door and your hearts. God will bless the offering.


Question 1: How often do we practice hospitality as a couple? Is this an area where we are strong or where we need to grow?

Question 2: What holds us back from opening our home more often? How can we overcome those barriers together?

Question 3: Who might God be calling us to invite into our home this month? Let us make a plan together.


Gracious God, Thou who hast opened Thy heart to us in Christ, teach us to open our home to others. Give us a spirit of generous hospitality that reflects Thy welcoming love. Help us to work together as a couple to serve those Thou sendest to our door. Deliver us from the fear of imperfection and from the idol of comfort. May our home be a place where strangers become friends and where Thy name is glorified in simple acts of kindness and love. Through Christ our Lord, who had no place to lay His head yet fed the multitudes, Amen.
