PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 4
"The Rose of Sharon"
Song of Solomon 2:1-4 — Christ the Beloved and the Banqueting House

---

Welcome, beloved couple. Tonight we step into chapter two of the Song, where some of the most beloved imagery in all of Scripture unfolds. Roses, lilies, apple trees, and a banqueting house where love's banner is unfurled over us. Draw close to one another and listen.

Song of Solomon 2:1-4:

"I am the rose of Sharon, and the lily of the valleys. As the lily among thorns, so is my love among the daughters. As the apple tree among the trees of the wood, so is my beloved among the sons. I sat down under his shadow with great delight, and his fruit was sweet to my taste. He brought me to the banqueting house, and his banner over me was love."

--- Teaching ---

"I am the rose of Sharon, and the lily of the valleys." Throughout Christian history, these words have been applied to Christ — He is the Rose of Sharon, the flower of surpassing beauty and fragrance. But in the immediate context of the Song, it is the bride who speaks. She identifies herself with the most beautiful flowers of the land. And here is something wonderful: the insecure woman of chapter one, who called herself darkened and neglected, now calls herself a rose. What changed? The bridegroom's love changed her. His admiration, his affirmation, his steady delight in her has transformed her self-understanding. She no longer sees herself through the distorted lens of her family's mistreatment. She sees herself through the eyes of the one who loves her.

This is the power of covenant love. Richard Sibbes wrote that a faithful spouse is like a mirror that reflects back to us who we truly are in the sight of God. When your husband tells you that you are beautiful, believe him. When your wife tells you that she admires you, receive it. The world's mirrors are cruel and distorting. But the mirror of covenant love tells the truth — you are precious, you are wanted, you are worthy of delight.

The bridegroom responds: "As the lily among thorns, so is my love among the daughters." He elevates her above all others. She is not one flower among many. She is the only lily, and everything around her is thorns by comparison. The Puritans saw in this an application for every husband: your wife must know, without any shadow of doubt, that she is your singular delight. Thomas Gataker wrote that a husband who allows his wife to wonder whether she holds first place in his heart has already begun to fail in his duty.

Then the bride responds in kind: "As the apple tree among the trees of the wood, so is my beloved among the sons." The apple tree was rare and prized in ancient Israel — a fruit-bearing tree in a forest of barren wood. Her husband stands out among all other men. He is not merely adequate. He is singular. He bears fruit. He provides shade and sustenance. And here is the tender detail: "I sat down under his shadow with great delight, and his fruit was sweet to my taste." She finds rest in his presence. His protection is not oppressive but delightful. His provision — emotional, spiritual, physical — is sweet to her.

Every husband should ask himself: Is my wife resting in my shadow with delight, or hiding from it in fear? Is the fruit of my character sweet to her taste, or bitter? And every wife: Does your husband know that his presence brings you rest? Have you told him lately that you find delight in who he is?

"He brought me to the banqueting house, and his banner over me was love." This is one of the most magnificent images in the entire Song. A banqueting house is a place of abundance, celebration, and feasting. The beloved does not merely feed his bride; he brings her to a feast. And over this feast, he raises a banner. In the ancient world, a banner identified an army, a kingdom, a family. It was a public declaration. The banner over this banqueting house declares to all the world: this marriage operates under the rule of love.

William Gouge applied this image practically. He wrote that every home should be a banqueting house where husband and wife feast on each other's company — where conversation is rich, affection is generous, laughter is frequent, and the presence of God is the unseen Host at every meal. The banner over your home is not perfection. It is not performance. It is love — the self-giving, other-centered, covenant love that mirrors Christ's love for His church.

The Puritans regularly exhorted couples to cultivate what they called "marriage joys" — intentional practices of delight. Shared meals without distractions. Evening walks. Reading together. Praying together. Playing together. These are the furnishings of the banqueting house. Do not let the busyness of life rob you of the feast that God has prepared for you in each other.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Turn to each other and consider these questions.

Question 1: The bride's self-image was transformed by her beloved's love. How has your spouse's love changed the way you see yourself? Have you told them?

Question 2: Is your home more like a banqueting house or a fast-food drive-through? What would it look like to create more space for feasting on each other's company this week?

Question 3: If there were a banner hanging over your marriage, what word would be written on it? Is it "love"? Or has something else — busyness, stress, distance — become the ruling banner? What needs to change?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray that your home would become a true banqueting house — a place of abundance, delight, and celebration. Ask God to raise the banner of His love over your marriage and to make that love visible to everyone who enters your home.

--- Closing ---

His banner over you is love. Not performance. Not perfection. Love. Rest under that banner tonight, beloved couple, and let its shade bring you great delight. Goodnight.
