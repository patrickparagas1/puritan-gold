PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 7
"The Mother's Gentle Influence"
John Angell James — A Help to Domestic Happiness


Welcome, dear family. Another day has passed, and God has been faithful through it all. Now let us close this day together by sitting at the feet of Jesus and hearing His Word.

Let us pray. Almighty God, You are the giver of every good gift, and one of the greatest gifts You have given us is this family. We dedicate this time to You. Help us to listen well and to grow in wisdom together. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

Our passage tonight is Psalm 127:3-5. Listen with expectant hearts:

"Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD: and the fruit of the womb is his reward. As arrows are in the hand of a mighty man; so are children of the youth. Happy is the man that hath his quiver full of them: they shall not be ashamed, but they shall speak with the enemies in the gate."

That is Psalm 127:3-5. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


As we close our first week with John Angell James, we turn to the beautiful and powerful role of mothers. Our Scripture today is Psalm 127:3-5, which begins with this declaration: 'Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD: and the fruit of the womb is his reward.'

The word 'heritage' means an inheritance — something precious given by God. Children are not burdens. They are not accidents. They are not merely responsibilities to be managed. They are a gift, a reward, an inheritance from the Lord Himself. Every child is a treasure entrusted by God to a family.

The psalmist then uses a vivid image: 'As arrows are in the hand of a mighty man; so are children of the youth.' Arrows are tools of purpose. A warrior carefully crafts each arrow, shapes it, sharpens it, and then sends it forth toward a target. In the same way, parents — and here we especially think of mothers — carefully shape, nurture, and prepare their children to be sent forth into the world with purpose and direction.

The mother's role in Scripture is honored and elevated. Proverbs 31:26-28 describes the virtuous woman: 'She openeth her mouth with wisdom; and in her tongue is the law of kindness. She looketh well to the ways of her household, and eateth not the bread of idleness. Her children arise up, and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praiseth her.' Notice that this woman speaks wisdom and kindness. She is attentive to her household. And the result? Her children rise up and call her blessed.

We see this pattern throughout Scripture. Hannah prayed fervently for a son and then dedicated Samuel to the Lord. Jochebed risked her life to save baby Moses and raised him in the fear of God during his earliest years — years that shaped the rest of his life. Eunice and Lois, Timothy's mother and grandmother, taught him the Scriptures from infancy, as Paul notes in 2 Timothy 1:5 and 3:15.

John Angell James wrote tenderly about the mother's influence. He said that the influence of a godly mother is beyond all calculation — that she impresses her own image on the minds of her children, and her words, prayers, and example are remembered long after she has gone to her rest.

James observed that in many families, it is the mother who spends the most time with the children, especially in their earliest and most impressionable years. The songs she sings, the stories she tells, the prayers she prays at bedtime, the way she responds to difficulty — all of these shape a child's understanding of God and the world.

He also wrote about the particular power of a mother's prayers. James told of children who had wandered far from the faith but who were eventually drawn back by the memory of their mother kneeling beside their bed, praying for them with tears. A mother's prayers, he said, follow her children wherever they go.

James was careful to distinguish between the father's and mother's roles without elevating one above the other. He saw them as complementary — the father leading with authority and instruction, the mother nurturing with tenderness and gentle influence. Both are essential. A family needs both strength and tenderness, both instruction and nurture.

He also encouraged mothers not to underestimate the importance of what might seem like small, ordinary moments — reading a Bible story at bedtime, comforting a frightened child with the promises of God, teaching a little one to pray, modeling patience in the midst of chaos. These quiet, daily acts of faithfulness are the building blocks of a godly home.

For our youngest ones: your mother (or mother figure) is one of God's special gifts to you. She helps take care of you and teach you about God's love. For older children: think about how much you have learned from your mother, not just from her words but from watching her life. And for mothers hearing this: take heart. Your work is not small. It is not invisible. God sees every prayer, every act of kindness, every moment of patient instruction. You are shaping eternal souls, and there is no greater calling on earth.


Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher and learn something that will deepen our understanding.

John Angell James, in his work "A Help to Domestic Happiness," wrote these words:

"The influence of a godly mother is beyond all calculation. She impresses her own image on the minds of her children. Her words are remembered, her prayers are felt, and her example is followed long after she has gone to her rest."

What John Angell James is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Psalm 127:3-5 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Let us discuss what we have heard. Parents, encourage even the youngest to share what they are thinking.

Question 1: What does the Bible say children are? (A gift and reward from God!)

Question 2: How are children like arrows? What does that picture teach us?

Question 3: What is one thing your mother (or mother figure) has taught you about God?

Question 4: Why do you think a mother's prayers are so powerful in a child's life?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD: and the fruit of the womb is his reward." -- Psalm 127:3

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Before we close, here is a family challenge for the days ahead:

Make a 'Blessing Bouquet' for your mother or a mother figure. Each family member writes one blessing or thank-you on a paper flower (cut from colored paper). Attach the flowers to sticks or straws and present them as a bouquet with words of gratitude.


Heavenly Father, we end where we began, in gratitude. Thank You for Your Word, for this family, and for the hope we have in Christ. May tonight's lesson from Psalm 127:3-5 shape the way we live, the way we speak, and the way we love one another. Amen and amen.

Until next time, dear family. The Lord goes before you. Walk with Him.
