From "A Body of Divinity" by Thomas Watson:

PURITAN GOLD -- A Body of Divinity: Complete Summary A Comprehensive Overview of Thomas Watson's Classic Exposition of the Shorter Catechism A Body of Divinity: Contained in Sermons upon the Westminster Assembly's Catechism. By Thomas Watson. A Complete Summary. Welcome to this summary of Thomas Watson's A Body of Divinity, one of the most accessible and devotional systematic theologies ever written. Published posthumously in 1692, it is an exposition of the first part of the Westminster Shorter Catechism, covering the great doctrines of the Christian faith from man's chief end to the day of judgment. THE AUTHOR AND HIS PURPOSE. Thomas Watson was a Puritan minister in London, ejected from his pulpit in 1662 for nonconformity. He was renowned for his vivid, memorable, and spiritually searching style of preaching. A Body of Divinity preserves his sermons on the catechism, which were designed to teach ordinary Christians the deep things of God in language they could understand and remember. MAN'S CHIEF END AND THE SCRIPTURES. Watson begins with the foundational question: What is the chief end of man? His answer: to glorify God and enjoy Him forever. He then establishes the authority and sufficiency of Scripture as the only rule of faith and practice. THE BEING AND ATTRIBUTES OF GOD. Watson proceeds through the divine attributes one by one — that God is a Spirit, infinite, eternal, unchangeable, all-knowing, all-powerful, holy, just, merciful, and true. Each attribute is explored with rich illustration and practical application. Watson's treatment of God's attributes is among the most devotionally warm in all of Puritan literature. THE TRINITY, CREATION, AND PROVIDENCE. Watson expounds the doctrine of the Trinity, the creation of the world, and the providence of God by which He governs all things. He shows that the same God who made the world continues to uphold and direct it by His wisdom and power. THE FALL, SIN, AND MISERY. Watson traces the entrance of sin into the world through Adam's fall, the nature of original sin, the covenant of works, and the miserable condition into which all mankind has been plunged by sin. His treatment of the fall is searingly honest about the depth of human corruption. CHRIST THE MEDIATOR. The heart of the book is Watson's exposition of the person and work of Christ — His prophetical, priestly, and kingly offices, His incarnation, suffering, death, resurrection, and ascension. Watson shows that every aspect of Christ's mediatorial work is necessary for the salvation of sinners. THE APPLICATION OF REDEMPTION. Watson then treats effectual calling, justification, adoption, sanctification, and assurance of grace. He shows how the Holy Spirit applies the benefits of Christ's work to the souls of believers, giving them new life, new standing, new relationship, and new holiness. PERSEVERANCE AND GLORY. The book concludes with the perseverance of the saints, the privileges of believers at death, the resurrection of the body, and the final judgment. Watson ends with a vision of the eternal glory that awaits all who belong to Christ. LASTING SIGNIFICANCE. Watson's
