PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 13
"Guarding the Heart"
John Angell James — Female Piety


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight James speaks to us about the dangers that threaten godliness — the subtle enemies that creep into the heart and steal our devotion to Christ. This is a word for every member of the family. Let us listen carefully.

Let us pray. Lord God, You know the weakness of our hearts. You know how easily we are distracted, deceived, and led astray. Guard us tonight with Your truth. Show us where we are vulnerable, and fortify us with Your grace. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Proverbs 4:23 and Matthew 6:19-21. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life. Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal: But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt, and where thieves do not break through nor steal: For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also."

That is Proverbs 4:23 and Matthew 6:19-21. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


John Angell James was a pastor who saw not only the beauty of godliness but also the dangers that threatened it. In "Female Piety," he devotes searching chapters to the enemies of the Christian soul — the subtle forces that erode faith, cool devotion, and gradually draw the heart away from Christ. His warnings are directed to women, but they apply with equal force to every member of the household.

The first danger James identifies is worldliness. He defines worldliness not primarily as the pursuit of obvious sins but as the gradual shifting of the heart's affections from heavenly things to earthly things. Worldliness creeps in when we begin to treasure the approval of society more than the approval of God. It creeps in when comfort, convenience, and material security become our primary concerns. It creeps in when we spend more time thinking about what we will wear, what we will eat, and what we will buy than about what we will offer to God and how we will serve His kingdom.

Jesus cuts to the heart of the matter: "Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also." This is not merely a prediction — it is a diagnosis. If you want to know where your heart is, look at where you spend your time, your money, your energy, and your attention. These are the true indicators of what you treasure. A family that treasures Christ will spend time in His Word, invest in His kingdom, and orient their lives around His purposes. A family that treasures the world will spend their best hours pursuing the things of the world and give God only the leftovers.

The second danger James identifies is spiritual complacency — the settling into a comfortable, routine, lukewarm faith that no longer hungers for God, no longer grieves over sin, and no longer strives after holiness. James warns that complacency is especially dangerous because it is almost invisible. The complacent soul does not reject Christ — she simply stops pursuing Him with passion. She still attends church, still reads the Bible occasionally, still prays when she remembers. But the fire is gone. The urgency is gone. The deep, aching desire for more of God is gone. And in its place is a thin, formal, respectable religion that satisfies neither God nor the soul.

The third danger is harmful companionship. James writes with great pastoral concern about the influence of ungodly friends and associations. "Evil communications corrupt good manners," Paul warns in 1 Corinthians 15:33. A woman or man who surrounds herself with companions who mock faith, who celebrate sin, or who prize worldly values above godly ones will inevitably be influenced by their attitudes and behavior. James counsels Christians to choose their closest friends carefully — to seek out companions who will strengthen their faith, sharpen their minds, and encourage their walk with God.

The fourth danger, and perhaps the most subtle, is neglecting the means of grace. When a Christian stops praying regularly, stops reading Scripture daily, stops attending worship faithfully, and stops seeking fellowship with other believers, the soul begins to wither like a plant deprived of water and sunlight. James writes that the means of grace — prayer, the Word, the sacraments, and Christian fellowship — are not optional accessories to the Christian life. They are the channels through which God supplies the spiritual nourishment that every believer needs to survive and thrive.

Solomon's charge in Proverbs 4:23 rings out over all of these warnings: "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life." Guard your heart. Watch over it. Tend it as carefully as a gardener tends a precious plant. For the heart is the wellspring from which everything else flows — your words, your actions, your relationships, your worship, your entire life. A heart kept diligently in the love of God will produce a life that glorifies Him. A heart left unguarded will be overrun by weeds.

Tonight, let each of us examine our hearts honestly. Have we allowed worldliness to creep in? Have we grown complacent in our faith? Have we been influenced by companions who draw us away from Christ? Have we neglected the means of grace that God has provided? If so, this is not a reason for despair but a call to return. The God who calls us to guard our hearts also gives us the grace to do so.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Jesus says, "Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also." What are some ways a family can tell where their true treasure lies?

Question 2: James warns against spiritual complacency — a faith that goes through the motions without passion. Have you ever experienced this? What helped you regain your spiritual hunger?

Question 3: How does our family guard against the four dangers James identifies: worldliness, complacency, harmful companionship, and neglecting the means of grace?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life." -- Proverbs 4:23

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord, search our hearts tonight. Show us where we have drifted, where we have grown cold, where we have been careless. Restore the joy of our salvation. Renew a right spirit within us. Help us to guard our hearts with all diligence and to keep our treasure in heaven, where moth and rust cannot corrupt. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
