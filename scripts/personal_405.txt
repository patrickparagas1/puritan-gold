PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Pastoral Encouragement, Day 5
"The Weight of Spiritual Leadership"
Thomas Watson — Counsel for the Godly Man

---

Good evening. The Christian life is a marathon, not a sprint. And every marathon runner needs a voice along the way saying: keep going, you are not alone, the finish line is real. That is tonight's word for you.

--- Scripture ---

Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Ephesians 5:25-27:

"Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it; That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word, That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.

--- Pastoral Word ---

The calling to be a spiritual leader in your home is not a privilege to boast about; it is a burden to bear faithfully. Paul's analogy in Ephesians 5 is staggering: love your wife as Christ loved the church. Christ loved the church by dying for it. Christ loved the church by sanctifying it. Christ loved the church by presenting it to Himself without blemish. This is the standard against which your leadership is measured.

Thomas Watson, in 'The Godly Man's Picture,' describes the marks of a truly godly man, and among them is the mark of spiritual leadership in the home. Watson writes that the godly man does not merely provide materially for his family; he provides spiritually. He feeds their souls as well as their bodies. He guards them from spiritual danger as well as physical danger. He leads them to the throne of grace as well as to the dinner table.

What does this leadership look like in practice? Watson identifies several dimensions.

First, the spiritual leader is a man of the Word. You cannot lead your family spiritually if you are not yourself being fed by Scripture. Watson writes that the man who neglects his own soul cannot be expected to nourish the souls of others. Before you can open the Bible for your family, you must have opened it for yourself. Before you can explain the truth to your children, you must have understood it in your own heart.

Second, the spiritual leader is a man of prayer. Watson observes that the godly man prays not only for himself but for his family. He brings their needs, their sins, their struggles, and their joys before the throne of grace. He prays specifically for his wife: for her spiritual growth, her health, her joy, her protection from temptation. He prays specifically for each of his children: for their conversion, their character, their future spouses, their callings.

Third, the spiritual leader sets the moral and spiritual tone of the home. Watson writes that the father is like the thermostat of the household: when he is warm in his devotion, the whole house warms. When he is cold, the whole house grows cold. If you are irritable, impatient, and worldly, your family will feel the chill. If you are gentle, prayerful, and Christ-centered, your family will be drawn toward the warmth.

Fourth, Watson addresses the specific duty of teaching. The spiritual leader teaches his children the doctrines of the faith: who God is, what sin is, what Christ has done, what the Spirit does, what the church is for, what heaven promises. Watson commends the use of catechisms, those structured sets of questions and answers that have been used for centuries to train young minds in the truths of Christianity. He writes that the father who teaches his children the catechism is laying a foundation that will support them through every storm of life.

But Watson is also honest about the difficulties. Spiritual leadership is exhausting. It requires consistency when you are tired, patience when you are frustrated, and perseverance when you see no immediate fruit. Watson counsels the discouraged father: 'Do not measure your faithfulness by your family's response. Measure it by your obedience to God's command. You are called to sow; God will give the increase.'

Richard Baxter adds a sobering word in 'The Reformed Pastor': the man who leads others must first lead himself. Baxter writes that hypocrisy is the great enemy of spiritual authority. If your children see you demanding a standard you do not yourself keep, they will learn to despise religion rather than embrace it. The spiritual leader must be a man of integrity: the same in private as in public, the same behind closed doors as before the congregation.

The challenge today is not to achieve perfection but to accept the calling. If you have been passive in the spiritual leadership of your home, repent and begin again. If you have been faithful but weary, take heart: the Lord sees your labor and will reward it in His time. The spiritual leader is not a man without weakness; he is a man who leads from his weakness toward the strength of Christ.

--- Reflection ---

Let these questions search your heart. Answer honestly before God.

Question 1: Am I actively leading the spiritual formation of my household, or have I delegated that responsibility to my wife, the church, or no one?

Question 2: What is the spiritual temperature of my home right now, and how much of that is a reflection of my own walk with God?

Question 3: What is one concrete step I can take this week to grow as a spiritual leader in my home?

Do not rush past these questions. Sit with them. Journal about them if you can. The Puritans believed that truth must be digested, not merely tasted.

--- Closing Prayer ---

Lord Jesus, You loved the church and gave Yourself for it. Teach me to love my family with that same sacrificial, sanctifying love. Forgive me for passivity and empower me by Your Spirit to lead my home with faithfulness, humility, and strength. Amen.

Goodnight. Remember: the same God who spoke to the Puritans speaks to you.
