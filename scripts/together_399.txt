PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 7
"Love and Reverence"
Ephesians 5:33 — The Summary Command


Welcome, dear couple. We close our first week in Ephesians 5 with the final verse of the passage — Paul's summary statement that distills everything into two simple commands. Listen:

"Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband."

Ephesians 5:33. Love and reverence. These are the two pillars upon which a Christlike marriage stands.


The Puritans recognized that Paul ends where all good theology ends — in practical application. After soaring to the heights of mystery and theology, Paul brings it down to the kitchen table, the bedroom, the daily routine. Every husband: love your wife. Every wife: reverence your husband.

William Gouge spent considerable time unpacking the word "reverence." He noted that the Greek word used here — phobetai — can also be translated as "respect" or even "fear." But this is not the fear of a slave before a tyrant. It is the reverence of a soul before something sacred. A wife who reverences her husband recognizes the weight of his calling and honors him in it. She speaks of him with dignity. She supports his leadership. She trusts his intentions even when she questions his judgment.

Gouge was equally clear that reverence cannot be demanded — it must be cultivated. A husband who demands respect but does not earn it through Christlike love is building on sand. Reverence flourishes in the soil of sacrificial love. When a wife sees her husband consistently laying down his life for her — in large ways and small — respect grows naturally in her heart.

On the other side, the command to husbands is simply "love" — the same love that has been defined throughout the passage as self-giving, sacrificial, purposeful, and tender. Notice that Paul does not say "rule your wife" or "manage your wife" or "correct your wife." He says "love your wife." The entire weight of the husband's calling rests on this single word.

Richard Baxter wrote that most marital conflict could be resolved if both parties would simply attend to their own calling rather than demanding that their spouse fulfill theirs. The husband who focuses on loving his wife rather than monitoring her respect, and the wife who focuses on honoring her husband rather than measuring his love — this is the couple that will find peace and joy in their marriage.

The Puritans also observed that love and reverence are not opposites — they are partners. A wife who reverences her husband also loves him. A husband who loves his wife also respects her. The distinction Paul makes is one of emphasis, not exclusion. Both partners are called to both love and respect; but each is especially called to the virtue that most deeply meets the other's need.

As you close this first week together, receive Paul's summary command as a gift, not a burden. Love. Reverence. These are not heavy chains but golden threads that, woven together, create a marriage of extraordinary beauty.


Discuss together:

Question 1: Husband, how does your wife most clearly experience your love? Wife, how does your husband most clearly experience your respect? Do these match what each of you most needs?

Question 2: Think back over this past week of study. What is the single most important truth God has spoken to your marriage through Ephesians 5:22-33?

Question 3: How would you like to grow in love and reverence this coming week? Name one specific, practical commitment each of you will make.


Pray together that God would write these two words on your hearts: love and reverence. Ask Him to make your husband a man whose love is so Christlike that respect becomes natural, and to make your wife a woman whose reverence is so sincere that love becomes irresistible. Pray for the grace to focus on your own calling rather than your spouse's.


May the Lord who commands love and reverence also supply them in abundance. May your marriage be marked by both, and may the watching world see in your home a reflection of the love between Christ and His Church. The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirits. Goodnight.
