PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"The Puritan Vision of the Christian Life"
July 30, 2026

---

"Trust in the Lord with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths. -- Proverbs 3:5-6" -- Selected Texts

As we near the end of our July journey, let us pause to reflect on what the Puritans have taught us through their writings and their lives. The Puritan vision of the Christian life was intensely practical, deeply personal, and gloriously God-centered. They believed that every moment of every day was to be lived under the eye of God and for the glory of God. Richard Baxter's 'The Reformed Pastor' challenged ministers to examine their own hearts before presuming to examine others. John Owen's works on the mortification of sin taught that holiness is not passive but active -- a daily war against the flesh, fought with the weapons of Scripture and prayer. Jeremiah Burroughs' 'The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment' showed that true happiness is found not in circumstances but in Christ. William Bridge's 'A Lifting Up for the Downcast' ministered hope to the despairing. And Thomas Watson's 'All Things for Good' demonstrated from Scripture that God's providence works every event -- even the painful ones -- for the eternal benefit of His children. These men were not ivory tower theologians; they were pastors, soldiers, exiles, and prisoners who lived what they taught. They lost their positions, their freedom, and sometimes their lives for the sake of conscience. And through it all, they held fast to the sovereignty of God, the sufficiency of Christ, and the power of the Spirit. May their example inspire us to live with the same depth, the same seriousness, and the same joy. Amen.
