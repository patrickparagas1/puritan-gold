PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 9
"Peace with God, Peace with Each Other"
Romans for Marriage — Day 9

---

Hello, beloved couple. It is a beautiful thing when husband and wife sit down together in God's Word. The Puritans called marriage a little church within the church. Tonight, your little church is in session. Welcome.

Tonight we turn to Romans 5:1-5. Listen with your hearts, not just your ears:

"Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ: By whom also we have access by faith into this grace wherein we stand, and rejoice in hope of the glory of God. And not only so, but we glory in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience; And patience, experience; and experience, hope: And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us."

That is Romans 5:1-5. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Richard Sibbes had deep insight into the truths we have just read. The Puritans did not separate theology from daily life, and their reflections on marriage are remarkably practical.

Romans 5:1 announces the most glorious consequence of justification: 'We have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.' The war is over. The rebellion has been pardoned. The enmity has been destroyed. Through Christ, we stand in a state of permanent peace with the Almighty. This is not a temporary ceasefire — it is an eternal treaty signed in the blood of Jesus.

Richard Sibbes, known as the 'heavenly Doctor Sibbes' for his tender pastoral ministry, taught that peace with God is the headwater from which all other peace flows. If you are at peace with God, you have the foundation for peace with everyone else — including your spouse. But if you are not at peace with God, no amount of marriage counseling, communication techniques, or conflict resolution strategies will produce lasting peace in your home.

Sibbes also expounded beautifully on the progression Paul describes: tribulation produces patience, patience produces experience (proven character), experience produces hope, and hope does not disappoint because God's love has been poured into our hearts. This is the refining process that every marriage undergoes. Trials are not interruptions to God's plan — they are instruments of God's plan. They are producing something in you.

The progression is crucial. Tribulation first. Not comfort first, then tribulation. The starting point is hard. But tribulation is not the ending point — it produces patience. And patience does not stand alone — it produces proven character. And character does not stand alone — it produces hope. And hope does not disappoint. The chain is unbreakable because it is forged by God Himself.

Sibbes encouraged believers to see their trials as the loving hand of a Father who is building something beautiful in them. The goldsmith puts gold in the fire not to destroy it but to purify it. The trials of your marriage are not designed to break you — they are designed to refine you into the image of Christ, individually and together.

--- Marriage Application ---

Begin tonight by establishing this truth: our peace with each other flows from our peace with God. If there is unrest between you, trace it back to the source. Are you individually at peace with God? Are you walking closely with Him? The marriage relationship cannot be healthier than the two individual relationships with God that feed it.

Then consider the trials your marriage has faced. Can you trace the progression Paul describes? Has tribulation produced patience in you? Has patience built character? Has character deepened your hope? If you cannot see the progression yet, it may be because you are still in the tribulation stage. Keep going. The chain is unbreakable.

Remember: hope does not disappoint because God's love has been poured into your hearts by the Holy Spirit. You are not running on empty. The love of God is already inside you. Let it flow outward toward your spouse tonight.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

It is time for reflection. Turn to each other and consider these questions together. There is no rush. Let the conversation go wherever the Spirit leads.

Question 1: How does your individual peace with God affect the peace in your marriage?

Question 2: Can you identify the tribulation-patience-character-hope progression in your marriage journey?

Question 3: In what specific way has God's love been 'shed abroad' in your heart recently? How can you pour that love toward your spouse?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Now let us pray together. The Puritans said that a couple who prays together is bound by cords that cannot be broken. Let us strengthen those cords tonight.

Tonight's prayer focus: Thank God for the peace that comes through justification. Ask Him to deepen your individual walks with Him so that peace flows naturally into your marriage. Pray through the progression: embrace the tribulation, ask for patience, trust the process, and hold onto hope.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the peace of God that passes understanding guard your hearts and your marriage. May every trial produce patience, every test build character, and every struggle deepen your hope in the God who never disappoints.

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you both, tonight and always. Goodnight.
