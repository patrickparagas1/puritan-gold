PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 18
"The Gift of a Good Spouse"
Proverbs 18:22


Good evening. What a blessing it is to see a husband and wife seeking the Lord together. The Puritans believed that a praying couple is an unstoppable force for the kingdom of God. Let us pray together tonight.

The Word of God has sustained marriages for thousands of years. Tonight it speaks to yours from Proverbs 18:22:

"Whoso findeth a wife findeth a good thing, and obtaineth favour of the LORD."

That is Proverbs 18:22. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher. Matthew Henry reflected deeply on the truths of Scripture and how they apply to the covenant of marriage.

Matthew Henry takes up this verse with evident delight. 'He that has a good wife has as much reason to be thankful to God as for any blessing he has,' Henry writes. The institution of marriage is itself a kindness of God, established before the fall when all was still 'very good.' But the gift of a particular spouse — a companion suited to one's soul, a helper fit for the journey — this is a special favour of the Lord.

Henry notes that the verse says 'findeth a wife,' suggesting that this is something to be sought with diligence and received with gratitude. It is not an entitlement but a gift. And like all gifts from God, it comes with responsibility. The man who has obtained favour from the Lord in his wife must steward that favour faithfully. He must cherish her, provide for her, lead her spiritually, and love her as Christ loved the church.

Charles Bridges expands the application to both spouses: while Solomon speaks specifically of finding a wife, the principle extends to the entire marriage relationship. The wife who has a godly husband has likewise obtained favour from the Lord. Both partners should look at one another and see not merely a human companion but a divine gift.

Henry adds a searching application: 'If thy wife be a good wife, acknowledge God's goodness in giving her to thee. If she be not what thou wouldest have her to be, consider whether the fault be not in thyself, that thou art not what thou shouldest be.' This is pastoral wisdom of the highest order: before complaining about your spouse, examine yourself.


When was the last time you looked at your spouse and genuinely thanked God for them? Not as a formality, but with deep, felt gratitude?

It is easy in the dailiness of marriage to lose sight of the extraordinary gift you have been given. The spouse who once filled you with wonder becomes familiar, and familiarity, if we are not careful, breeds not contempt but indifference, which may be worse.

Today, restore the sense of gift to your marriage. Look at your spouse and remember: this person is God's favour to you. Not a burden. Not an obstacle. Not a project. A gift. And respond with the gratitude, care, and delight that such a gift deserves. Express your gratitude not only to God but to your spouse. Let them hear from your lips that you consider them a good thing.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Here are some questions to guide your conversation tonight. Take turns sharing, and give each other the gift of your full attention.

Question 1: Do I regularly thank God for my spouse, or have I allowed familiarity to diminish my gratitude?

Question 2: When was the last time I told my spouse that I consider them a gift from God?

Question 3: Reflecting on Henry's challenge: before I focus on how my spouse could improve, are there ways I need to grow?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Prayer is the heartbeat of a Christian marriage. Let us pray together now. You do not need eloquent words, just honest ones.

Tonight's prayer focus: Spend time simply thanking God for each other. Name specific qualities, specific memories, specific ways your spouse has been a channel of God's favour in your life. Ask God to restore and deepen your gratitude for one another.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the Lord who gave you to each other renew your gratitude daily, that you may always see in your spouse the good gift of His generous hand. Amen.

May your love for each other grow deeper with every passing day, and may your love for God be the source and the strength of it all. Goodnight, dear ones.
