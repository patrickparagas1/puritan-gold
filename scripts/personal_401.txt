PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Guided Puritan Reading, Day 1
"The Duty of Mortification"
John Owen — The Mortification of Sin, Chapter 1


Welcome, friend. Tonight we take up a spiritual discipline that was at the very heart of the Puritan life: the slow, careful, prayerful reading of a great Christian text. We are not skimming. We are not rushing. We are walking through these pages the way the Puritans did, with a Bible in one hand and a prayer in our hearts.

Tonight we draw from the work of John Owen, specifically from "The Mortification of Sin." This text has been a source of spiritual nourishment for Christians across the centuries, and it remains profoundly relevant today.


Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Romans 8:13:

"For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.


We begin our journey through one of the most important works of practical theology ever written: John Owen's 'The Mortification of Sin in Believers.' Owen wrote this treatise in 1656, originally as a series of sermons to the students at Oxford University. His burden was pastoral: he saw young men preparing for ministry who did not understand the nature of indwelling sin or the means of fighting it. The result was a work that has guided believers for nearly four centuries in the daily warfare against sin.

Owen's foundation text is Romans 8:13: 'If ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live.' From this single verse, Owen draws out his entire framework. He begins by establishing several foundational principles.

First, the persons spoken to are believers. Owen is emphatic on this point. The duty of mortification is not addressed to unbelievers; it is addressed to those who are already in Christ, who have already been justified by faith, who already possess the Holy Spirit. This is crucial because it means that mortification is not the means of becoming a Christian but the duty of one who already is. Owen writes that the apostle speaks to those who are 'not in the flesh, but in the Spirit' (Romans 8:9). The call to mortify sin is a call to those who have been freed from sin's dominion but not yet freed from sin's presence.

Second, Owen notes the condition: 'if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die.' This is not a threat to the believer's eternal security but a description of the necessary connection between unmortified sin and spiritual death. Owen explains that a true believer who neglects the duty of mortification will experience a kind of living death: spiritual deadness, loss of assurance, fruitlessness, and misery. The life that Christ purchased is not merely future glorification but present vitality, and that vitality depends upon the ongoing mortification of sin.

Third, Owen identifies the agent of mortification: 'through the Spirit.' This is perhaps his most important point in the entire treatise. Mortification is not a work of human willpower, moral effort, or self-discipline alone. It is the work of the Holy Spirit in the believer. Owen will return to this theme again and again: all other methods of fighting sin that do not rely upon the Spirit of God are doomed to fail. He writes that 'mortification from a self-strength, carried on by ways of self-invention, to the end of a self-righteousness, is the soul and substance of all false religion in the world.'

Fourth, Owen defines what 'mortify' means. The word is violent: it means to put to death. Owen does not soften this language. Sin is not to be managed, negotiated with, or accommodated. It is to be killed. But Owen also clarifies what mortification is not. It is not the complete eradication of sin in this life. No believer achieves sinless perfection before glory. Mortification is the progressive weakening of sin's power, the daily resistance of its motions, and the steady growth of holiness in the soul.

Owen also warns against false mortification. Some people suppress one sin only to indulge another. Some abstain from outward sins while feeding inward ones. Some achieve a kind of moral respectability that has nothing to do with the Spirit's work. Owen insists that true mortification touches the root, not merely the fruit. It addresses the desires of the heart, not merely the actions of the body.

Fifth, Owen lays down a principle that governs the entire work: 'the vigour and power and comfort of our spiritual life depends on the mortification of the deeds of the flesh.' A believer who is not actively mortifying sin will not grow. He will not have assurance. He will not know the sweetness of communion with God. He will not be useful in ministry. The duty of mortification is not an optional extra for particularly zealous Christians; it is the constant, lifelong calling of every person who names the name of Christ.

As we begin this journey with Owen, let us take his opening challenge to heart. The duty before us is not easy, but it is necessary. And the promise is glorious: 'ye shall live.' Not merely exist, but truly live: in the power of the Spirit, in communion with the Father, in conformity to the Son. This is the life that mortification secures.


Let us pause and reflect. Consider these questions in the quiet of your heart.

Question 1: Do I understand mortification as a lifelong duty of the believer, or have I assumed that spiritual maturity means the fight gets easier?

Question 2: Am I relying on the Holy Spirit to fight sin, or on my own willpower and self-discipline?

Question 3: Is there an area of 'false mortification' in my life — an outward conformity that masks an unmortified heart?

Do not rush past these questions. Sit with them. Journal about them if you can. The Puritans believed that truth must be digested, not merely tasted.


O Lord, as I begin this study of the mortification of sin, open my eyes to see the seriousness of indwelling sin and the sufficiency of Your Spirit to overcome it. Grant me the courage to engage in this fight with honesty and dependence upon You. Through Christ my Lord, Amen.

Goodnight, friend. Walk with God tomorrow.
