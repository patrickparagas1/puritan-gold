PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 30
"Looking Forward in Faith"
Pilgrim Families — Synthesis, Day 2


Hello, dear ones. How wonderful it is to sit together as a family and open the Scriptures. Welcome to Puritan Gold's family devotional. Let us begin with thankful hearts.

Let us pray together. Lord God, we come before You as a family, grateful for Your love and Your provision. We ask that You would quiet our minds and soften our hearts as we hear from You tonight. Help us to understand, to remember, and to obey what You teach us. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Philippians 1:6; Jude 24-25. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ. Now unto him that is able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy, To the only wise God our Saviour, be glory and majesty, dominion and power, both now and ever. Amen."

That is Philippians 1:6; Jude 24-25. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


We end where every pilgrim's journey ends — looking forward. Not backward in regret, not downward in despair, but forward in faith. And the foundation of our confidence is not our own strength but the promise of God.

Paul declares in Philippians 1:6: "Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ." God started the work. God will finish the work. Salvation is not a project we complete — it is a work God performs. He began it when He called us, He sustains it as He sanctifies us, and He will complete it when He glorifies us. From start to finish, it is God's work. And God does not leave His work unfinished.

This is the ultimate comfort for pilgrim families. You may feel weak. You may feel unqualified. You may look at your family and see more failures than victories. But the question is not whether you are able to finish the journey — the question is whether God is able to finish what He started. And the answer is a resounding yes.

Jude's doxology is one of the most magnificent in all of Scripture: "Now unto him that is able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy." God is able to keep us from falling. Not might keep us. Not hopes to keep us. Is able. He has the power, the wisdom, and the will to keep every one of His children from ultimate spiritual failure.

And the destination is breathtaking: "to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy." Faultless. Without blemish. Without spot or wrinkle. The same family that stumbles daily, that sins daily, that fails daily — presented faultless before the blazing glory of Almighty God. Not because of our faultlessness, but because of Christ's. His righteousness covers us. His blood cleanses us. His perfection is credited to our account. And when we stand before God, we will stand in Christ — faultless, blameless, radiant with His righteousness.

And it will be with exceeding joy. Not with trembling fear. Not with anxious uncertainty. With exceeding joy. The presentation of the redeemed before God will be the most joyful moment in all of history. Every tear wiped away. Every burden lifted. Every battle won. Every promise fulfilled. Exceeding joy.

Bunyan closes The Pilgrim's Progress with one of the most beautiful sentences in English literature. As Christian crosses the final river — death itself — and arrives at the gates of the Celestial City, Bunyan writes: "So he passed over, and all the trumpets sounded for him on the other side." All the trumpets. Not a quiet, unnoticed entrance, but a royal welcome. The angels themselves sound the fanfare for every pilgrim who arrives home.

For your family tonight, look forward. The road ahead may be long. There may be more hills, more valleys, more battles, more tears. But at the end of the road — the Celestial City. At the end of the race — the crown of life. At the end of the river — the trumpets. And the One who began this good work in your family will be faithful to complete it.

You are pilgrim families. You walk a narrow road in a world that walks the broad way. You serve a King the world rejects. You treasure a Book the world ignores. You follow a Savior the world despises. But you are on the right road. And at the end of it, your Savior is waiting with arms wide open, saying, "Well done, good and faithful servants. Enter into the joy of your Lord."

This month has been a gift. Bunyan gave us the map. Burroughs gave us contentment for the journey. Brooks gave us weapons for the battles. Rutherford gave us love for the King. And God has been with you every step. He will be with you tomorrow, next month, next year, and forever.

Walk on, pilgrim family. The trumpets are waiting.


The Puritans were men and women who treasured God's Word above all else. Let us hear from one of them now.

John Bunyan, in his work "The Pilgrim's Progress," wrote these words:

"So he passed over, and all the trumpets sounded for him on the other side."

What John Bunyan is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Philippians 1:6; Jude 24-25 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: What does it mean that God will 'perform' (complete) the good work He began in us? How is this different from us completing it ourselves?

Question 2: Jude says God will present us 'faultless' before His glory. How is this possible for people who still sin every day?

Question 3: Bunyan wrote, 'All the trumpets sounded for him on the other side.' What does this image mean to you?

Question 4: As we look ahead as a family, what is one commitment we want to make for the next month of our pilgrimage together?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ." -- Philippians 1:6

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


To make tonight's lesson come alive, try this activity together as a family:

Hold a 'Family Commissioning' tonight. Have each family member stand, and let the others speak a blessing or encouragement over them for the road ahead. Read Philippians 1:6 over each person and pray for them by name. Commission your family for the next leg of the pilgrimage.


Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for this time as a family. Thank You for speaking to us through Your Word. Plant these truths deep in our hearts, especially in the hearts of our children. Help us to walk in wisdom tomorrow and every day. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
