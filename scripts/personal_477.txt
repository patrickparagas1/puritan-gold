PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 16
"The Religious Affections — Jonathan Edwards"
Reading Day — Edwards on True and False Religion


Welcome. Today is a Reading day. We engage with one of the most penetrating works of Christian psychology ever written: Jonathan Edwards's A Treatise Concerning Religious Affections — a work that asks the searching question: How can you tell if your religion is genuine?

Let us read 1 Peter 1:8 (KJV):

"Whom having not seen, ye love; in whom, though now ye see him not, yet believing, ye rejoice with joy unspeakable and full of glory."


Jonathan Edwards (1703-1758) wrote Religious Affections in the wake of the Great Awakening, a period of intense religious revival in colonial New England. The revival produced remarkable conversions — but it also produced counterfeits. Many people experienced powerful emotions and dramatic experiences but did not produce lasting spiritual fruit. Edwards set out to distinguish true religion from false, genuine affection from mere enthusiasm.

Edwards begins by arguing that true religion, in great part, consists in holy affections. The affections are not merely emotions. They are the vigorous, active inclinations of the soul — the deep movements of the heart that drive behavior. Love, fear, hope, joy, gratitude, sorrow for sin — these are affections. And Edwards argues that without them, religion is dead. A Christianity that engages the intellect but not the heart is a lifeless shell.

But Edwards is equally emphatic that strong affections alone do not prove genuine faith. The devil can produce powerful emotions. Human nature can generate impressive religious experiences. False prophets can move audiences to tears. The question is not whether you feel strongly but whether your feelings are rooted in a genuine encounter with the beauty of God.

Edwards identifies several signs that do not reliably indicate genuine faith. Intense emotional experiences are not a reliable sign — the stony-ground hearers in Jesus's parable received the Word with joy but had no root. Physical manifestations are not reliable — trembling, weeping, fainting may indicate genuine encounters with God or merely emotional instability. Great knowledge of Scripture is not reliable — the Pharisees knew the Bible better than anyone but did not know God. Bold declarations of faith are not reliable — Peter declared his willingness to die for Christ and denied Him three times within hours.

Then Edwards identifies the signs that do reliably indicate genuine faith. The most important is what he calls a new spiritual sense — a perception of the beauty and excellency of divine things that is not natural to the human heart. The unregenerate person may know that God is holy, but only the regenerate person perceives the beauty of God's holiness. The unregenerate may know that the Gospel is true, but only the regenerate taste the sweetness of the Gospel. This new sense is the work of the Holy Spirit and cannot be manufactured by human effort.

Other reliable signs include: a genuine love for God and for other Christians, not based on self-interest but on the intrinsic beauty of God's character; a spirit of humility — the truly converted person is deeply aware of their own sinfulness and depends entirely on grace; a tender conscience that is sensitive to sin and quick to repent; a hunger for holiness — not merely a desire to avoid punishment but a genuine longing to be like Christ; and the production of lasting fruit in character and conduct.

Edwards's most famous sign is what he calls Christian practice — the consistent, long-term pattern of obedience that characterizes a genuine believer. He writes that Christian practice is the chief of all the signs of grace. Not a perfect practice — for no believer is perfect — but a general trajectory of growth, obedience, and perseverance over time. The tree is known by its fruit, and genuine faith produces genuine holiness.

This treatise is a mirror. It asks you to examine your own religion — not to create anxiety but to create honesty. Is your faith merely intellectual, or does it engage your deepest affections? Is your religion merely emotional, or does it produce lasting fruit? Do you perceive the beauty of God, or only the utility of God? Are you growing in humility, tenderness, and obedience, or are you stagnant?


Examine yourself. Not with morbid introspection but with honest, Spirit-led self-assessment. True religion engages the deepest affections of the soul. Lord, search my heart. Show me whether my religion is genuine or merely formal. Give me the new spiritual sense — the perception of Your beauty that transforms everything. Let my faith be marked not by intensity alone but by lasting fruit. Amen.
