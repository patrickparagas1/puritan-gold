PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 28
"The Crown of Her Husband"
Proverbs 12:4

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we step outside of Proverbs 31 to consider another of Solomon's proverbs, one that uses a striking image to describe the impact a wife has upon her husband. A crown is a symbol of honor, dignity, and glory. And Solomon tells us that a virtuous wife is nothing less than a crown upon her husband's head.

Let us read our passage. Proverbs 12:4 (KJV):

"A virtuous woman is a crown to her husband: but she that maketh ashamed is as rottenness in his bones."

--- Teaching ---

The contrast in this proverb is severe and intentional. On one side stands the virtuous wife, who is a crown, a symbol of the highest honor. On the other stands the wife who causes shame, who is like a disease that corrupts from within, weakening the very structure that holds a man together. Solomon wants us to understand that the influence of a wife upon her husband is not neutral. It is profoundly positive or profoundly destructive. There is no middle ground.

Matthew Henry, in his commentary, drew out the significance of the crown metaphor with great care. A crown, Henry noted, is worn on the head. It is visible to all. The virtuous wife adorns her husband's reputation before the world. When others see her wisdom, her integrity, her gracious conduct, they think more highly of her husband. His standing in the community is elevated by her character. She reflects glory upon him, not because she is his possession but because her excellence testifies to his wisdom in choosing her and to the quality of the love they share.

William Gouge developed this idea further by observing that the wife who crowns her husband does so not through public performance but through private faithfulness. Her virtue is not a show put on for the neighbors. It is the genuine expression of a heart that fears God and loves her husband. It is displayed in the quiet moments when no one is watching: in her prayers for him, in her patience with his faults, in her refusal to speak ill of him to others, in her steadfast support during his seasons of failure and discouragement. This private faithfulness, over time, becomes visible to all, and it adorns her husband more beautifully than any jewel-encrusted crown.

Thomas Watson warned husbands that they bear responsibility for the kind of crown they wear. The husband who treats his wife with contempt, who demeans her or neglects her, who fails to provide the love and security she needs, should not be surprised if she becomes a source of shame rather than honor. Many a wife who might have been a crown has been driven to bitterness by a husband's cruelty or indifference. The husband who desires a virtuous wife must himself be a virtuous husband. He must create the conditions in which her best qualities can flourish, just as a gardener must tend the soil in which he hopes to grow flowers.

Richard Baxter added that the crowning relationship is reciprocal. While Solomon speaks here of the wife crowning the husband, the principle works both ways. The godly husband is also a crown to his wife. She is honored by his integrity, strengthened by his faithfulness, and made secure by his love. The healthiest marriages are those in which both husband and wife seek to adorn and honor each other, each finding their greatest joy in the glory of the other rather than in their own.

Dear wife, know that your faithfulness is not invisible. It crowns your husband with an honor that the world can see, even when you feel unseen yourself. Your character speaks loudly on his behalf.

Dear husband, are you creating the kind of marriage in which your wife can flourish? Are you watering the garden of her heart so that her virtue can bloom? The crown you wear is, in large measure, the crown you have helped to fashion.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: Wife, in what ways do you seek to honor your husband, both publicly and privately? Where do you find this easiest, and where do you find it most challenging?

Question 2: Husband, do you recognize the ways in which your wife crowns you? What can you do to create conditions that help her virtue flourish?

Question 3: How can we guard against the opposite danger, of becoming a source of shame or rottenness to each other through neglect, bitterness, or contempt?

--- Prayer ---

Almighty God, Thou hast designed marriage as a relationship of mutual honor and mutual glory. We thank Thee for the beauty of this design. Help the wife in this marriage to be a crown to her husband, adorning him with the fruit of her godly character. Help the husband to cherish and cultivate the virtue of his wife, creating a home in which her best qualities can flourish. Guard us from bitterness, contempt, and neglect, the diseases that rot from within. May our marriage be a crown of glory that reflects the beauty of Christ and His Bride. In His name we pray, Amen.
