PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 28
"The Crown of Her Husband"
Proverbs 12:4 — The Virtuous Wife

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we close our week on the virtuous wife with a proverb that reveals the profound influence a wife has on her husband's life, for good or for ill. The image Solomon uses is both beautiful and sobering.

Let us read our passage. Proverbs 12:4 (KJV):

"A virtuous woman is a crown to her husband: but she that maketh ashamed is as rottenness in his bones."

--- Teaching ---

A crown is the most public symbol of honor and dignity. In the ancient world, crowns were worn by kings and conquerors. They signified authority, achievement, and the high regard of others. Solomon says that a virtuous wife is this to her husband. She is his crown. She is the source of his honor in the community. She is the visible evidence of the blessing of God upon his life. When people see her wisdom, her character, her faithfulness, and her love, they honor the man whom she has chosen to marry. His reputation is elevated by her virtue. His influence is strengthened by her support. His life is adorned by her presence.

This is a staggering privilege and responsibility. The wife who understands that she is a crown to her husband will take that calling seriously. She will conduct herself in a manner that brings honor to his name. She will speak well of him in public. She will support his leadership in the home and in the community. She will use her gifts and her wisdom to complement and strengthen his calling. This is not servility. This is partnership at its highest level. The crown does not diminish the king; it enhances him. And the virtuous wife does not diminish herself by honoring her husband; she reveals the full beauty of her own character.

William Gouge meditated on this image at length. He observed that just as a crown is the first thing people notice about a king, so a wife is often the first thing people notice about a man. The way she carries herself, the way she speaks, the way she treats others, all of this reflects upon her husband for better or for worse. Gouge did not intend this as a burden upon the wife but as a recognition of her enormous influence. The wife's character shapes the perception of the entire household. When she is wise, the household is respected. When she is kind, the household is welcomed. When she is faithful, the household is trusted.

But Solomon also presents the darker side: "she that maketh ashamed is as rottenness in his bones." This is a devastating image. Rottenness in the bones is an internal decay that weakens the entire frame. It is invisible to the outside world, but it saps the strength and vitality of the man from within. A wife who shames her husband, through public criticism, through disrespect, through unfaithfulness, through a contentious spirit, is slowly destroying him from the inside out. He may appear strong on the outside, but inwardly he is crumbling.

Thomas Gataker connected this proverb to the broader Puritan understanding of marriage as a partnership. The husband and wife are bound together so intimately that each has the power to build the other up or tear the other down. The wife can crown her husband or she can corrode him. The husband, too, can honor his wife or he can wound her. The influence flows in both directions, and both must exercise it with care and love.

Richard Baxter urged wives to consider the long-term impact of their daily conduct. A sharp word spoken in the morning can weigh upon a husband's spirit all day. A critical remark made in front of friends can wound him for weeks. Conversely, a word of encouragement spoken at the right moment can strengthen him for months. The wife who understands this power and uses it for good is a crown indeed.

And for the husband, Baxter had an equally pointed word. If your wife is your crown, how are you treating that crown? Are you honoring her? Are you protecting her? Are you providing an environment in which her virtues can flourish? A king who throws his crown in the mud is a fool. A husband who neglects, demeans, or ignores his wife is treating his crown with contempt. Let the husband honor the crown that God has placed upon his life, and let the wife aspire to be the crown that her husband deserves.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: Wife, in what ways do you feel you serve as a crown to your husband? In what areas would you like to grow?

Question 2: Husband, do you honor your wife as the crown she is? How can you better protect and celebrate her influence in your life?

Question 3: Are there any patterns in our marriage where we are tearing each other down rather than building each other up? How can we change them?

--- Prayer ---

Sovereign Lord, we thank Thee for the beautiful design of marriage in which husband and wife crown each other with honor. Help the wife in this marriage to be a crown of virtue, wisdom, and faithfulness to her husband. Help the husband to honor and cherish the crown Thou hast placed upon his life. Deliver us from the patterns of speech and behavior that rot the bones and weaken our union. Build us up in love, that together we might be a testimony to Thy grace and glory. In the name of Jesus Christ our Lord, Amen.
