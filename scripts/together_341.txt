PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 10
"Loving When Your Spouse Falls Short"
Romans for Marriage — Day 10


Welcome, dear ones. Marriage is a gift from God, and so is this time together in His presence. Whether this has been an easy day or a hard one, you are here, together, and that matters more than you know.

Our Scripture reading this evening is from Romans 5:8. Let us receive it together:

"But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us."

That is Romans 5:8. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


The Puritans understood marriage in a way that the modern world has largely forgotten. Let us hear from Thomas Goodwin, who wrote with great wisdom on this subject.

Romans 5:8 stands as perhaps the single greatest revelation of divine love in all of Scripture. God did not wait until we cleaned ourselves up to love us. He did not love us because we were lovable. He loved us while we were sinners — while we were His enemies, while we were in active rebellion against Him. 'While we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.'

Thomas Goodwin, one of the most Christ-centered of all Puritan theologians, meditated deeply on the timing of God's love. Goodwin marveled that God did not merely tolerate sinners from a distance — He demonstrated His love by sending His Son to die for them at their worst. The cross was not God's response to human improvement. It was God's initiative toward human ruin.

For marriage, this verse is the ultimate standard. If God loved you at your worst, you are called to love your spouse at their worst. Not when they are pleasant, cooperative, and meeting your expectations — but when they are irritable, selfish, forgetful, and failing. 'While we were yet sinners' means love is not a reward for good behavior. Love is a gift given in the face of bad behavior.

Goodwin observed that this kind of love is utterly foreign to human nature. Our natural love is conditional: I love you because you make me happy. I love you as long as you meet my needs. I love you when you deserve it. But God's love is radically unconditional: I love you while you are sinning against me. I love you at your lowest point. I love you with no guarantee of return.

This is the love that transforms marriages. When a husband loves his wife despite her failures — not grudgingly, but genuinely — he displays the Gospel. When a wife loves her husband despite his shortcomings — not with resentment, but with grace — she preaches the cross of Christ without saying a word. This is love that the world cannot understand and cannot produce. Only the Holy Spirit, pouring God's love into our hearts, can enable it.


Tonight, let Romans 5:8 redefine your love for each other. Think about the last time your spouse disappointed you. How did you respond? Did you withdraw, criticize, or punish with silence? Now imagine how God responds when you disappoint Him. He commends His love. He draws closer. He gives more.

This is your model. Love your spouse not because they deserve it but because Christ loved you when you did not deserve it. The most powerful thing you can do in your marriage is to love your spouse at their worst moment — because that is exactly what God did for you.

Speak Romans 5:8 over your marriage tonight: 'While we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.' Then ask each other: How can I love you better when you are struggling, not just when you are shining?

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


The Puritans practiced what they called holy conference, speaking honestly about spiritual things with those they loved most. Do that now with these questions.

Question 1: When your spouse falls short, is your first instinct to love or to withdraw? Why?

Question 2: How does remembering that God loved you 'while you were yet a sinner' change the way you love your spouse?

Question 3: What would it look like to demonstrate your love most clearly during your spouse's worst moments rather than their best?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


It is time to bring what we have heard and discussed before the Lord in prayer. Pray out loud if you can. Let your spouse hear your heart.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to pour His unconditional love into your hearts so that you can love each other the way He loves you — not based on performance, but based on commitment. Pray specifically for the grace to love in the moments when love is hardest.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the love that found you at your worst empower you to love your spouse at theirs. May your marriage be a living demonstration of Romans 5:8 — love without condition, love without limit.

May the God who brought you together continue to bind you together in love, in faith, and in purpose. Sleep well tonight. He watches over your marriage.
