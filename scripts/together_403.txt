PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 11
"Be Ye Kind One to Another"
Ephesians 4:32 — Forgiveness as a Way of Life

---

Welcome, dear couple. If there is one verse that could transform every marriage overnight, it may be the one we study tonight.

Our Scripture is Ephesians 4:32:

"And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you."

Kind. Tenderhearted. Forgiving. Three words that, practiced daily, can rebuild any marriage.

--- Teaching ---

The Puritans were realists about marriage. They did not romanticize it. They knew that two sinners living in close proximity would inevitably wound each other — sometimes deeply. And so the Puritans placed forgiveness at the very center of their theology of marriage.

William Gouge wrote that a marriage without forgiveness is a marriage without hope. "For if forgiveness be removed," he said, "then every offense becomes a permanent scar, every disagreement becomes an unhealed wound, and the accumulation of hurts over the years will bury the love that once flourished." Forgiveness is not optional in marriage. It is as necessary as breathing.

But the Puritans were also careful to define what forgiveness is and what it is not. Forgiveness is not pretending that the offense did not happen. It is not minimizing the pain. It is not excusing sinful behavior. Forgiveness is a deliberate choice to release the offender from the debt they owe — to cancel the record of wrongs, to refuse to hold the sin over the other person's head, and to pursue reconciliation rather than revenge.

And the motivation for this forgiveness is staggering: "even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you." The standard is not human forgiveness, which is always partial and conditional. The standard is divine forgiveness — total, free, costly, and permanent. God forgave you at the cost of His Son's blood. Can you forgive your spouse at the cost of your pride?

Richard Baxter observed that unforgiveness in marriage is like a slow poison. It does not kill the marriage immediately, but it contaminates everything. The unforgiven spouse walks on eggshells, never knowing when the old offense will be brought up again. The unforgiving spouse carries a burden of resentment that grows heavier with each passing day. Both are trapped. Only forgiveness can set them free.

The word "tenderhearted" deserves special attention. The Puritans noted that over time, the daily friction of life together can cause hearts to harden — imperceptibly, gradually, but relentlessly. Couples who were once tender with each other become cold, guarded, defensive. The antidote is deliberate tenderheartedness — a daily choice to keep your heart soft toward your spouse, to refuse the temptation to build walls, to remain vulnerable even when vulnerability is risky.

Thomas Gataker counseled married couples to practice what he called "daily forgiveness" — not waiting until a major offense requires a dramatic act of reconciliation, but cultivating a posture of grace that covers the small irritations, the thoughtless words, the minor disappointments that are part of every marriage. "Love covereth a multitude of sins," he quoted from 1 Peter 4:8, and this covering is the daily work of a forgiving heart.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Discuss together:

Question 1: Is there any unforgiveness lingering in your marriage — an old wound that has never fully healed, a resentment that surfaces in moments of conflict?

Question 2: How quickly do you typically forgive each other after a disagreement? Is there a pattern that needs to change?

Question 3: What would it look like to practice "daily forgiveness" — to develop a posture of grace that covers the small offenses of ordinary life?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Pray together for the grace of forgiveness. If there is a specific offense that needs to be released tonight, bring it before the Lord together. Ask God to soften any hardness that has formed in your hearts and to restore the tenderness that characterized the early days of your marriage.

--- Closing ---

May the God who forgave you at infinite cost give you the grace to forgive each other freely. May kindness and tenderness mark every interaction between you. And may the fresh air of forgiveness fill your home tonight and every night. Goodnight.
