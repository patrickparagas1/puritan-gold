PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 6
"Justified by Faith, Not Performance"
Romans for Marriage — Day 6

---

Good evening, dear couple. The Puritans believed that marriage was one of the greatest means of grace God provides. Tonight we enter into that grace together. Let us open our hearts and our ears to what the Lord has for us.

As we settle in together, let us hear from Romans 3:28:

"Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law."

That is Romans 3:28. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

One of the gifts of the Christian tradition is that we do not study alone. We have the wisdom of the saints who came before us. Tonight we hear from John Owen.

Paul's conclusion in Romans 3:28 shattered the religious assumptions of his day and continues to shatter ours: justification is by faith alone, without the deeds of the law. No amount of law-keeping, no accumulation of good works, no record of religious performance can make a person righteous before God. Righteousness comes only through faith in Jesus Christ.

John Owen explored the implications of justification by faith with profound depth. He taught that this doctrine liberates the believer from the crushing burden of performance-based acceptance. Before God, you are not accepted because of what you do but because of what Christ has done. Your standing is secure not because of your performance but because of His.

Owen's insight has revolutionary implications for marriage. Many marriages operate on a performance-based system — acceptance is earned through doing the right things, saying the right words, meeting the right expectations. When performance is high, love flows. When performance drops, love withdraws. This is law, not grace. It is the very system Paul says cannot justify.

A Gospel-shaped marriage operates on a fundamentally different principle. Your acceptance of your spouse is not based on their performance but on the unconditional commitment you made before God. Just as God accepts you by faith apart from your works, you are called to accept your spouse by covenant apart from their performance.

This does not mean performance does not matter — it means performance is not the basis of acceptance. A child who is accepted by their parents regardless of their grades will actually perform better, not worse, because they are free from the paralyzing fear of rejection. Similarly, a spouse who knows they are loved unconditionally is free to grow, change, and risk vulnerability — because their standing is secure.

Owen would remind us that the law brings condemnation, but grace brings transformation. If you want your spouse to change, do not put them under law. Put them under grace. Accept them by faith — faith in God's power to transform them, faith in the covenant you made, faith in the Gospel that changes hearts.

--- Marriage Application ---

Examine your marriage tonight through the lens of Romans 3:28. Is your love for your spouse conditional on their performance? Do you withdraw affection when they disappoint you? Do you increase warmth when they please you? If so, you may be running a performance-based marriage rather than a grace-based one.

A grace-based marriage does not ignore sin or pretend problems do not exist. It simply refuses to make acceptance contingent on performance. Your spouse is your spouse — accepted, loved, committed to — on their best day and their worst day. This is how God loves you. This is how you are called to love each other.

Identify one area where you have been withholding acceptance from your spouse because of their performance. Release it tonight. Choose to love by faith, not by works.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Discussion time. Husband, listen to your wife. Wife, listen to your husband. Let this be a safe and sacred space.

Question 1: Is our marriage more 'law-based' (performance earns acceptance) or 'grace-based' (acceptance is unconditional)? Be honest.

Question 2: In what areas do you feel pressure to perform in order to be loved? How does the Gospel free you from that pressure?

Question 3: How can you show your spouse this week that your love is not contingent on their performance?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Let us close our eyes and open our hearts to the Lord in prayer. Husband, pray for your wife. Wife, pray for your husband. There is no greater gift you can give each other.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray that your marriage would be built on grace, not performance. Ask God to free you both from the burden of earning each other's love and to anchor your acceptance in the Gospel, where love is given freely through faith.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May your marriage rest on the finished work of Christ, not on the unfinished work of your own efforts. May you love each other by faith, freely, and without condition.

Go in peace, husband and wife. The God who ordained your marriage is faithful to sustain it. Trust Him. Love each other. And come back tomorrow for more. Goodnight.
