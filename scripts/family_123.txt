PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 23
"The Godly Person Loves God's Word"
Thomas Watson — The Godly Man's Picture


Hello, dear ones. How wonderful it is to sit together as a family and open the Scriptures. Welcome to Puritan Gold's family devotional. Let us begin with thankful hearts.

Let us pray together. Lord God, we come before You as a family, grateful for Your love and Your provision. We ask that You would quiet our minds and soften our hearts as we hear from You tonight. Help us to understand, to remember, and to obey what You teach us. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Psalm 119:97-105. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"O how love I thy law! it is my meditation all the day. Thou through thy commandments hast made me wiser than mine enemies: for they are ever with me. I have more understanding than all my teachers: for thy testimonies are my meditation. I understand more than the ancients, because I keep thy precepts. I have refrained my feet from every evil way, that I might keep thy word. I have not departed from thy judgments: for thou hast taught me. How sweet are thy words unto my taste! yea, sweeter than honey to my mouth! Through thy precepts I get understanding: therefore I hate every false way. Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path."

That is Psalm 119:97-105. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Yesterday we saw that the godly person prays. Today Watson shows us the second mark: the godly person loves God's Word. And there is no better place to see this love expressed than Psalm 119.

We read a glorious section today — verses 97-105. The Psalmist bursts out, 'O how love I thy law! it is my meditation all the day.' This is not someone who reads the Bible out of obligation. This is someone who is captivated by it. The word 'meditation' means to muse, to ponder, to turn over in the mind — like a cow chewing its cud, extracting every bit of nourishment from its food.

And notice the result of this meditation. 'Thou through thy commandments hast made me wiser than mine enemies.' God's Word gives wisdom that worldly intelligence cannot match. The Psalmist goes further: 'I have more understanding than all my teachers: for thy testimonies are my meditation.' Not because the Psalmist is arrogant, but because the Word of God contains divine wisdom that surpasses all human learning.

Then comes one of the most delightful verses in the Bible: 'How sweet are thy words unto my taste! yea, sweeter than honey to my mouth!' The Psalmist compares God's Word to the sweetest thing he knows — honey. In the ancient world, honey was the ultimate treat. And God's Word, says the Psalmist, is even sweeter.

Jeremiah expressed the same delight: 'Thy words were found, and I did eat them; and thy word was unto me the joy and rejoicing of mine heart' (Jeremiah 15:16). And Job said, 'I have esteemed the words of his mouth more than my necessary food' (Job 23:12) — more important than daily bread.

In the New Testament, Peter writes, 'As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that ye may grow thereby' (1 Peter 2:2). Just as a newborn baby craves milk — urgently, instinctively, constantly — so should Christians crave the nourishment of Scripture.

Jesus Himself quoted Deuteronomy 8:3 when tempted by Satan: 'Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God' (Matthew 4:4). We need God's Word as much as we need food. Our souls depend on it for survival and growth.

Thomas Watson wrote that the Word of God is the jewel that adorns the soul. The godly person hides it in his heart as his choicest treasure. He does not only read it but feeds upon it, digesting it by meditation until it becomes part of him.

Watson distinguished between different ways people relate to the Bible. Some treat it like a newspaper — they glance at it quickly and move on. Some treat it like medicine — they take it reluctantly when they feel spiritually sick. But the godly person treats it like food — he feeds on it daily, eagerly, and finds it nourishing and satisfying.

Watson also stressed the importance of meditating on Scripture, not just reading it. Reading is like tasting food. Meditation is like digesting it. You can read a verse in seconds, but meditating on it — turning it over in your mind, asking what it means, how it applies, what it reveals about God — can take hours. And it is through meditation that God's Word truly transforms us.

Watson gave practical counsel: read the Bible with a plan, not randomly. Read it prayerfully, asking the Holy Spirit to open your understanding. Read it carefully, paying attention to every word. Read it personally, asking how it applies to your life. And read it obediently, ready to do whatever God says.

For our little ones: the Bible is sweeter than candy! Every time we read it, we learn something wonderful about God and His love for us. For older children: do you read the Bible because you want to or because you have to? Watson says the mark of a godly person is that they genuinely love God's Word. Ask God to give you that love. For the whole family: let us move from simply reading the Bible to feasting on it — savoring it, meditating on it, and letting it shape every part of our lives.


The Puritans were men and women who treasured God's Word above all else. Let us hear from one of them now.

Thomas Watson, in his work "The Godly Man's Picture," wrote these words:

"The Word of God is the jewel that adorns the soul. A godly man hides it in his heart as his choicest treasure. He does not only read it but feeds upon it, digesting it by meditation until it becomes part of him."

What Thomas Watson is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Psalm 119:97-105 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: The Psalmist says God's words are sweeter than honey. What is your favorite sweet treat? Can you imagine loving the Bible that much?

Question 2: What is the difference between reading the Bible quickly and meditating on it?

Question 3: Watson says the godly person treats the Bible like food, not medicine. What does that mean?

Question 4: What is one way we could go deeper in our Bible reading as a family — not just reading more, but understanding more?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"O how love I thy law! it is my meditation all the day." -- Psalm 119:97

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


To make tonight's lesson come alive, try this activity together as a family:

Have a 'Bible tasting' — read a short passage (like Psalm 23 or John 3:16) slowly, one phrase at a time. After each phrase, pause and share what you noticed. Then read it again. Notice how you see more the second time. This is what meditation looks like!


Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for this time as a family. Thank You for speaking to us through Your Word. Plant these truths deep in our hearts, especially in the hearts of our children. Help us to walk in wisdom tomorrow and every day. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
