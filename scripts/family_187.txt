PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 26
"The Peacemaker's Home"
Richard Baxter — The Christian Directory

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Baxter speaks to one of the most practical and necessary topics for any household — how to resolve conflict and maintain peace within the family. Let us come humbly before the Lord.

Let us pray. Prince of Peace, bring Your peace into our home tonight. Where there is tension, bring understanding. Where there is hurt, bring healing. Where there is conflict, bring reconciliation. Teach us from Your Word how to be peacemakers in this family. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Matthew 5:9 and Romans 12:18. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God. If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men."

That is Matthew 5:9 and Romans 12:18. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Every family experiences conflict. This is not a sign of failure — it is a sign of humanity. Wherever sinful people live in close proximity, there will be misunderstandings, hurt feelings, clashing wills, and moments of tension. The question is not whether conflict will arise but how it will be handled. Richard Baxter, with the pastoral wisdom born of decades of counseling families, offers guidance that is both biblical and eminently practical.

Baxter begins with a principle that surprises many readers: conflict itself is not always sinful. There are legitimate disagreements that need to be worked through. There are honest differences of opinion that deserve a hearing. There are even necessary confrontations when sin must be addressed and truth must be spoken. The sin is not in the disagreement but in the way we handle it. Do we handle it with love or with malice? With humility or with pride? With a desire for reconciliation or with a desire for victory?

Baxter identifies pride as the root cause of most family conflict. When I insist on being right, on having my way, on winning the argument, I am acting out of pride — and pride is the great destroyer of household peace. The antidote to pride is humility, and humility begins with a simple but revolutionary question: "Could I be wrong?" Baxter counsels every family member, in every disagreement, to begin by asking this question honestly. Often, the answer is yes — or at least partially. And that admission, made sincerely, opens the door to understanding and resolution.

Baxter offers several practical rules for family conflict. The first is to address problems quickly. Do not let offenses fester. Paul's command in Ephesians 4:26, "Let not the sun go down upon your wrath," is a household rule that Baxter takes literally. When a conflict arises, deal with it the same day. Talk it through before bedtime. Do not go to sleep angry. Unresolved anger hardens into bitterness, and bitterness poisons every relationship it touches.

The second rule is to speak the truth in love. When a family member has wronged you, say so — but say it with gentleness, with respect, and with the goal of restoration, not retaliation. Avoid accusatory language. Instead of saying "You always..." or "You never...," describe the specific behavior and how it made you feel. This is not a technique borrowed from modern psychology — it is the ancient practice of speaking truth in love that Paul commends in Ephesians 4:15.

The third rule is to listen before speaking. James says, "Let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath" (James 1:19). Baxter observes that most family arguments escalate because both parties are talking and neither is listening. The discipline of listening — really listening, with full attention and genuine interest — is one of the most powerful peacemaking tools available to any family. When a person feels heard, the intensity of the conflict often diminishes dramatically.

The fourth rule is to forgive freely. Paul says, "Forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you" (Ephesians 4:32). This is the heart of Christian peacemaking. We forgive not because the other person deserves it but because we have been forgiven. We forgive not because the offense was small but because the cross was great. We forgive not once or twice but, as Jesus said, "seventy times seven" — without limit, without condition, without keeping score.

Baxter writes that a household that practices quick reconciliation, honest communication, attentive listening, and free forgiveness will enjoy a peace that the world cannot give and cannot take away. It will not be a perfect peace — perfection awaits heaven. But it will be a real peace, rooted in the gospel and sustained by grace.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Baxter says pride is the root of most family conflict. How does humility — asking "Could I be wrong?" — help resolve disagreements?

Question 2: Paul says, "Let not the sun go down upon your wrath." Why is it important to resolve conflicts quickly rather than letting them linger?

Question 3: Is there any unresolved tension in our family right now? Can we take a few minutes tonight to address it with honesty, love, and forgiveness?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God." -- Matthew 5:9

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord Jesus, You are our peace. You reconciled us to God through Your cross, and You call us to be reconcilers in our homes. Forgive us for the conflicts we have handled poorly. Heal the wounds we have inflicted on one another. Make us peacemakers who reflect Your grace. In Your name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
