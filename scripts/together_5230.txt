PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 30
"Renewing Your Marriage Covenant"
Final June Together Devotional — Renewal and Recommitment

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight is our final evening together for the month of June. We have spent this final week meditating on the portrait of godly womanhood in Proverbs 31, the beauty of marital affection, and the foundations of a Christ-honoring marriage. As we close this month, it is fitting that we end not with a new teaching but with a renewal, a deliberate, prayerful recommitment to the covenant you made before God on the day you wed.

Let us read our passage. Malachi 2:14-16 (KJV):

"Yet ye say, Wherefore? Because the LORD hath been witness between thee and the wife of thy youth, against whom thou hast dealt treacherously: yet is she thy companion, and the wife of thy covenant. And did not he make one? Yet had he the residue of the spirit. And wherefore one? That he might seek a godly seed. Therefore take heed to your spirit, and let none deal treacherously against the wife of his youth. For the LORD, the God of Israel, saith that he hateth putting away."

--- Teaching ---

God calls marriage a covenant. Not a contract, not an arrangement, not a partnership of convenience, but a covenant. A contract is an agreement between two parties that may be dissolved when its terms are no longer met. A covenant is a sacred bond, witnessed by God Himself, that endures regardless of circumstance. The Puritans understood this distinction well, and they regarded the marriage covenant as one of the most solemn and beautiful commitments a human being could make.

William Gouge wrote that the marriage covenant is unique among human covenants because God Himself is not merely a witness but a participant. He is the one who joins husband and wife together. He is the one who ratifies their vows. He is the one who holds them accountable to the promises they have made. To deal treacherously with your spouse, Gouge warned, is to deal treacherously with God, for He has bound Himself to your covenant as surely as you have bound yourselves to each other.

Richard Baxter urged married couples to revisit their covenant vows regularly, not merely on anniversaries but as a spiritual discipline woven into the fabric of their daily lives. He observed that the pressures of marriage can cause husbands and wives to forget what they promised. The wife forgets that she pledged to respect and follow. The husband forgets that he pledged to love and cherish. Both forget that they promised to remain faithful in sickness and in health, in plenty and in want, for better and for worse. Baxter counseled couples to recall these vows deliberately, to examine their hearts against them, and to repent where they have fallen short.

Thomas Watson taught that covenant renewal is an act of grace, not an admission of failure. Every covenant relationship needs periodic renewal because human beings are forgetful, weak, and prone to drift. The people of Israel renewed their covenant with God at significant moments in their history, not because the original covenant was defective but because their hearts needed to be recalled to its terms. In the same way, husband and wife benefit from pausing at regular intervals to look into each other's eyes and say again what they said on the day they married: I am yours, and you are mine, and this bond shall not be broken.

Tonight, as you close this month of devotional study together, I invite you to renew your covenant. This need not be elaborate. It requires no ceremony or witnesses beyond the God who is always present. Simply take each other's hands, look into each other's eyes, and speak from the heart. Recommit to the promises you made. Confess where you have fallen short. Extend forgiveness where it is needed. And ask God to carry you through the months and years ahead with the same grace that has carried you to this moment.

Dear husband and wife, your marriage is not merely a human institution. It is a living picture of the covenant love between Christ and His Church. Every time you renew your commitment to each other, you proclaim to the watching world that covenant love is real, that faithfulness is possible, and that the God who brought you together is faithful to sustain you.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: As you look back over this month of devotional study, what is the most significant truth you have learned or been reminded of about your marriage?

Question 2: In what specific areas have you fallen short of your marriage vows this past season? Take time to confess to each other and to offer forgiveness.

Question 3: What is one commitment you each want to carry into the summer months ahead? Speak it aloud to each other as a renewal of your covenant promise.

--- Prayer ---

Eternal God, Thou who art the author and witness of our marriage covenant, we come before Thee tonight to renew the vows we made in Thy presence. We confess that we have not always kept our promises as faithfully as we ought. We have sometimes been selfish where we promised to be sacrificial. We have sometimes been cold where we promised to be loving. We have sometimes been distant where we promised to be present. Forgive us, O Lord, and renew us by Thy Spirit. We take each other's hands tonight and say again: I am yours, and you are mine, and this bond shall not be broken, for God Himself has joined us together. Carry us through the days ahead with Thy grace, Thy strength, and Thy unfailing love. May our marriage glorify Thee until the day we stand before Thy throne and behold the perfect union of Christ and His Bride. In the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, Amen.
