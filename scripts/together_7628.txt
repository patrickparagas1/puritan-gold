PURITAN GOLD -- Human Nature in Its Fourfold State: Complete Summary A Comprehensive Overview of Thomas Boston's Classic Work on the Human Condition Human Nature in Its Fourfold State. By Thomas Boston. A Complete Summary. Welcome to this summary of Thomas Boston's Human Nature in Its Fourfold State, one of the most beloved and influential works of Scottish Reformed theology. First published in 1720, this book presents the whole sweep of human existence in four stages: innocence, nature, grace, and eternity. THE AUTHOR AND HIS PURPOSE. Thomas Boston was a minister in the Church of Scotland, serving the small rural parish of Ettrick for twenty-five years. Despite his obscure location, his writings reached a vast audience. The Fourfold State was written to give ordinary Christians a complete understanding of where they came from, where they are, where they must go, and where they will end up. STATE ONE: THE STATE OF INNOCENCE. Boston begins with man as God made him — upright, holy, and perfectly happy. Adam was created in the image of God, with a righteous soul, a sound mind, and a will perfectly conformed to God's law. Boston dwells on the beauty and glory of this original state so that the reader might feel the horror of what was lost in the fall. STATE TWO: THE STATE OF NATURE. This is the longest and most searching section of the book. Boston demonstrates that every human being born after Adam enters the world in a state of total corruption. The understanding is darkened, the will is enslaved to sin, the affections are disordered, and the conscience is defiled. Boston systematically examines the corruption of every faculty of the soul. He then unfolds the misery of this condition — the wrath of God, the curse of the law, the dominion of sin, the tyranny of Satan, and the dreadful prospect of eternal punishment. Finally, he proves the utter inability of fallen man to recover himself. No amount of effort, education, or moral improvement can change the fundamental state of the heart. STATE THREE: THE STATE OF GRACE. Boston turns to the remedy: regeneration by the Spirit of God. He shows how the Holy Spirit creates new life in the soul, giving a new nature, new desires, and new capacities. The sinner who was dead is made alive. The rebel who hated God is made to love Him. He also discusses the mystical union between Christ and the believer — how the Christian is united to Christ by faith and receives all the benefits of His death and resurrection. STATE FOUR: THE ETERNAL STATE. Boston concludes with death, resurrection, judgment, heaven, and hell. He treats each of these solemnly and thoroughly. He shows the difference between how the righteous and the wicked die. He describes the bodily resurrection, the last judgment, the glories of the kingdom of heaven, and the horrors of hell.
