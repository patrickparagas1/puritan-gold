PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 14
"Praying Together"
Colossians 4:2 and 1 Peter 3:7 — The Couple That Prays

---

Welcome, dear couple. We close our second week with what the Puritans considered the single most important practice in married life: praying together.

Our Scriptures tonight are Colossians 4:2 and 1 Peter 3:7:

"Continue in prayer, and watch in the same with thanksgiving."

"Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered."

Notice that last phrase: "that your prayers be not hindered." Peter explicitly connects the health of a marriage to the effectiveness of a couple's prayers.

--- Teaching ---

The Puritans were emphatic: a couple that does not pray together is neglecting the most powerful resource available to their marriage. Richard Baxter called joint prayer "the ligament that holds the bones of marriage together." William Gouge said that prayer is "the breath of the marriage body" — without it, the marriage slowly suffocates.

Yet prayer together is one of the most difficult disciplines for many couples. It requires vulnerability — speaking to God in the presence of your spouse, exposing your needs, your fears, your failures. It requires humility — admitting that you cannot manage your marriage in your own strength. It requires consistency — showing up day after day, even when the words feel dry and the practice feels routine.

Peter's instruction to husbands is revealing. He says that a husband must dwell with his wife "according to knowledge" — that is, with understanding, with sensitivity, with attentiveness to who she is and what she needs. He must give her "honour" — treating her not as inferior but as a fellow heir of God's grace. And the reason for all of this is profoundly practical: so that their prayers will not be hindered.

The Puritans drew a startling conclusion from this verse: unresolved conflict in marriage hinders prayer. A husband who treats his wife harshly, who ignores her needs, who refuses to understand her — his prayers are weakened. A wife who harbors contempt for her husband, who withholds respect, who nurses resentment — her prayers are diminished. God takes the health of your marriage so seriously that He allows it to affect your access to His throne.

Thomas Gataker turned this principle into a positive: a couple that treats each other with honor and grace finds their prayers empowered. When you pray together from a place of mutual love and respect, God delights to answer. Your united prayer has a power that individual prayer, however fervent, cannot match. Jesus Himself said, "If two of you shall agree on earth as touching any thing that they shall ask, it shall be done for them of my Father which is in heaven" (Matthew 18:19).

The Puritans offered practical advice for couples beginning to pray together. Start simply. Even two minutes of prayer together before bed is a good beginning. Take turns. Let one spouse pray one night and the other the next. Pray about specific things — your children, your finances, your health, your spiritual growth. Pray for each other — not in a way that corrects or preaches, but in a way that blesses and supports. And do not give up. Like any discipline, prayer together becomes more natural with practice.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Discuss together:

Question 1: How would you describe your prayer life as a couple? Is it consistent, occasional, or nonexistent? What barriers have prevented you from praying together regularly?

Question 2: Based on 1 Peter 3:7, are there any relational issues that might be hindering your prayers? Is there anything that needs to be resolved before you can pray freely together?

Question 3: What would a realistic, sustainable prayer practice look like for your marriage? Can you commit to a specific plan starting tonight?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Tonight, simply pray together. If this is unfamiliar territory, start with this: hold hands, close your eyes, and take turns thanking God for three specific blessings in your marriage. Then take turns asking God for one thing your marriage needs. End with the Lord's Prayer together. It does not need to be eloquent. God is not grading your prayers. He is delighting in your unity.

--- Closing ---

May the Lord who hears the prayers of His children receive your united prayers with joy. May your prayer life together deepen and grow, and may you experience the power that comes when two heirs of grace agree before the throne of God. Goodnight.
