PURITAN GOLD -- Study Devotional, Day 15
Series: Romans
"No Condemnation"
Life in the Spirit, the law of liberty, and the adoption of sons


Good evening, and welcome to our study devotional from Puritan Gold. We are continuing our journey through one of the most important books in all of Scripture. The Puritans treasured careful, verse-by-verse study, and tonight we follow in their footsteps.

We are studying the book of Romans together. Tonight's lesson is titled "No Condemnation." Each session builds on what came before, so if you have been following along, you will see how tonight's passage connects to the larger argument of the book.


Let us read our passage for tonight. It comes from Romans 8:1-17:

"There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit. For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus hath made me free from the law of sin and death. For what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh: That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit... For ye have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear; but ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father. The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God: And if children, then heirs; heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ; if so be that we suffer with him, that we may be also glorified together."

Read that passage again to yourself. Mark any words or phrases that stand out. The Puritans read with a pen in hand, always ready to note what God was showing them.


Let us focus on one verse that captures the heart of tonight's passage.

"There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit." -- Romans 8:1

This verse is the lens through which we will examine the rest of tonight's passage. Keep it in the front of your mind as we proceed.


Now let us move into the exposition of this passage.

Martyn Lloyd-Jones offers valuable commentary on this passage. The Puritans were expositors of the highest order, and their insights remain remarkably fresh.

Martyn Lloyd-Jones considered Romans 8 to be the greatest chapter in the Bible, and he devoted an enormous number of sermons to its exposition. He saw Romans 8:1 as the answer to the cry of Romans 7:24 and the most comforting verse in all of Scripture for the struggling believer. Lloyd-Jones was emphatic that 'no condemnation' means no condemnation whatsoever — not 'less condemnation,' not 'condemnation only for big sins,' but absolutely zero condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.

Lloyd-Jones gave extensive attention to the distinction between the two 'laws' in verse 2: the law of the Spirit of life and the law of sin and death. He argued that a 'law' here means a principle or governing power, not a code of regulations. The law of sin and death is the principle by which sin leads inevitably to death — the dynamic Paul described in chapter 7. The law of the Spirit of life is the counter-principle: the Holy Spirit's liberating power that breaks sin's stranglehold and produces genuine spiritual life. Lloyd-Jones stressed that this is not merely a better set of instructions but an entirely new power at work within the believer.

Lloyd-Jones was deeply moved by the doctrine of adoption in verses 14-17. He argued that adoption goes beyond justification. Justification changes our legal status from condemned to acquitted. Adoption changes our relational status from strangers to children. We are not merely pardoned criminals but beloved sons and daughters. The Spirit of adoption enables us to cry 'Abba, Father' — the intimate Aramaic term that Jesus Himself used in addressing God (Mark 14:36). Lloyd-Jones taught that the witness of the Spirit described in verse 16 is the believer's deepest assurance of salvation. It is not merely logical deduction from Scripture but an immediate, experiential awareness produced by the Holy Spirit that we belong to God. This witness may wax and wane in its experiential intensity, but the reality it attests is permanent and unshakeable.

Romans 8 is the crown jewel of the epistle — indeed, many have called it the greatest chapter in the entire Bible. After the anguished struggle of chapter 7, chapter 8 opens with a trumpet blast of triumph: 'There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus.'

The word 'therefore' connects this declaration to everything that has preceded it. Because of justification by faith (chapters 3-5), because of union with Christ in His death and resurrection (chapter 6), because of deliverance from the law (chapter 7) — therefore, no condemnation. The word 'now' emphasizes the present reality — not 'there will someday be no condemnation' but 'there is right now, at this very moment, no condemnation.' And 'no condemnation' is absolute — the Greek ouden katakrima means nothing of condemnation, not the slightest shred.

Who enjoys this freedom? Those who are 'in Christ Jesus.' This is the phrase that defines the Christian's existence. We are not merely followers of Christ, admirers of Christ, or students of Christ. We are in Christ — vitally, organically, eternally united to Him. What is true of Christ is true of us. He was condemned; therefore we are not condemned. He bore the wrath; therefore no wrath remains for us. He satisfied justice; therefore justice has no further claim against us.

Verse 2 explains the mechanism: 'For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus hath made me free from the law of sin and death.' The law of sin and death is the principle described in chapter 7: sin uses the law to produce death. The law of the Spirit of life is a higher, more powerful principle that overrides and liberates from the lower one. Just as the law of aerodynamics overcomes the law of gravity (the plane still weighs tons, gravity still pulls, but a superior force keeps it aloft), so the Spirit of life overcomes the power of sin and death.

Verses 3-4 describe how God accomplished what the law could not: 'For what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh: That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us.' The law could command righteousness but could not produce it, because the raw material it had to work with — human flesh — was corrupted by sin. So God took another approach. He sent His own Son — not merely a prophet, an angel, or a teacher, but His own Son — in human flesh. Christ took upon Himself the likeness of sinful flesh (not sinful flesh itself, for He was without sin, but the genuine human nature that sin had corrupted in all others). And 'for sin' — as a sin offering — He 'condemned sin in the flesh.' God condemned sin, not by condemning the sinner, but by punishing sin in the flesh of His Son on the cross. The result: 'the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us.' What we could never accomplish by our own law-keeping, God accomplishes in us through the Spirit.

Verses 5-8 contrast two ways of living: after the flesh and after the Spirit. 'For they that are after the flesh do mind the things of the flesh; but they that are after the Spirit the things of the Spirit. For to be carnally minded is death; but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.' The mind set on the flesh — on self, on sin, on worldly values — leads to death. The mind set on the Spirit — on God, on holiness, on eternal values — leads to life and peace. Verse 7 explains why the carnal mind is so deadly: 'Because the carnal mind is enmity against God: for it is not subject to the law of God, neither indeed can be.' The unregenerate mind is not merely indifferent to God but actively hostile — and it cannot change itself. Only the Spirit can transform a carnal mind into a spiritual one.

Verses 9-11 assure believers of the Spirit's indwelling presence: 'But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.' The indwelling Spirit is the distinguishing mark of every true Christian. If you have the Spirit, you belong to Christ. If you do not have the Spirit, you do not belong to Christ — regardless of what church you attend or what creed you confess. And if the Spirit dwells in you, He 'shall also quicken your mortal bodies' — the same Spirit who raised Christ from the dead will raise your body at the last day.

Verses 12-13 draw the practical implication: 'Therefore, brethren, we are debtors, not to the flesh, to live after the flesh. For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live.' We owe the flesh nothing. It has done nothing for us but lead us toward death. Our obligation is to the Spirit, who gives life. And the means of victory is clear: 'through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body.' Not through willpower, not through religion, not through self-discipline alone — but through the Spirit.

Verses 14-17 reach the pinnacle: the doctrine of adoption. 'For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God.' Justification makes us legally righteous; adoption makes us relationally children. 'For ye have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear; but ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father.' The Spirit does not produce fear but familial intimacy. We cry 'Abba' — the word a child uses with a beloved father. This is not presumption; it is the birthright of every child of God.

'The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God' (verse 16). This is the inner testimony of the Holy Spirit — an immediate, experiential assurance that transcends mere logical deduction. The Spirit bears witness to our spirit that we belong to God. This is the deepest comfort available to the human soul.

And verse 17 takes us higher still: 'And if children, then heirs; heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ.' We are not merely children; we are heirs. Not merely heirs of some secondary inheritance; heirs of God Himself. And not merely heirs alongside Christ but joint-heirs with Christ — sharing in everything that belongs to the Son of God. Everything the Father has given to Christ, He shares with His adopted brothers and sisters. 'If so be that we suffer with him, that we may be also glorified together.' The path to glory passes through suffering, but the suffering is temporary while the glory is eternal.


Now let us consider: how does this truth apply to our lives? The Puritans never separated doctrine from practice. Every truth they studied was immediately applied.

Romans 8:1-17 is a passage to return to again and again. Here are specific applications.

First, memorize Romans 8:1 and preach it to yourself daily. When the accuser whispers that you are condemned — that your sin is too great, your failures too many, your record too stained — answer with the voice of God: 'There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus.' This is not your opinion or your hope; it is God's judicial verdict. Believe it. Rest in it. Let it silence every accusation.

Second, set your mind on the Spirit. Verses 5-6 teach that what you set your mind on determines the quality of your life. Carnally minded equals death. Spiritually minded equals life and peace. This is a daily, hourly choice. What are you feeding your mind? What dominates your thoughts? Intentionally fill your mind with Scripture, worship, prayer, and conversations about the things of God. Turn off the noise that feeds the flesh and tune in to the voice of the Spirit.

Third, mortify sin through the Spirit, not through willpower. When you encounter temptation, do not merely grit your teeth and resist. Cry out to the Holy Spirit for power. 'Spirit of God, I cannot overcome this in my own strength. Apply the death of Christ to this sin. Give me power to put it to death.' Dependence on the Spirit is the secret of genuine mortification.

Fourth, live as an adopted child of God. You are not an orphan, a stranger, or a slave. You are a beloved child of your heavenly Father. Let the Spirit of adoption transform how you pray (boldly, intimately), how you face difficulty (as a child confident in the Father's care), and how you view your future (as an heir of God and joint-heir with Christ). This is your identity. Live in it.

Write down one concrete action you will take this week in response to tonight's study. Do not let this truth remain theoretical. The Puritans measured the success of a sermon not by how much the hearer learned but by how much the hearer changed.


Abba, Father, we come to You not as slaves cowering in fear but as beloved children, confident in Your love. Thank You that there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. Thank You for the Spirit of life who sets us free. Thank You for adoption — that You have made us not merely pardoned sinners but dearly loved sons and daughters. Teach us to set our minds on the things of the Spirit. Give us power to mortify the deeds of the body. Assure us by Your Spirit that we are indeed Your children, heirs of God and joint-heirs with Christ. Until the day of glory, sustain us by Your grace. Amen.

Until next time, keep studying. The Word of God will never run dry.
