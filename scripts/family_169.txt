PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 8
"The Power of a Godly Woman"
John Angell James — Female Piety

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to a new week of Puritan Gold family devotions. This week we sit at the feet of John Angell James, the beloved Victorian-era pastor of Carrs Lane Chapel in Birmingham, England. His book "Female Piety" is a tender and powerful tribute to the influence of godly women. Let us begin.

Let us pray. Lord God, we thank You for the women of faith who have shaped our family and our world. Open our hearts tonight to the wisdom of Your servant John Angell James, and through his words, teach us to value and cultivate true godliness. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Proverbs 31:30-31; 1 Peter 3:3-4. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: but a woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised. Give her of the fruit of her hands; and let her own works praise her in the gates. Whose adorning let it not be that outward adorning of plaiting the hair, and of wearing of gold, or of putting on of apparel; But let it be the hidden man of the heart, in that which is not corruptible, even the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price."

That is Proverbs 31:30-31 and 1 Peter 3:3-4. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

John Angell James (1785-1859) served as pastor of Carrs Lane Congregational Chapel in Birmingham for over fifty years. Though he ministered in the Victorian era rather than the Puritan age proper, James stood squarely in the Puritan tradition, preaching the same doctrines of grace, the same commitment to practical godliness, and the same passion for the Christian home that animated men like Gouge, Baxter, and Henry a century before him.

In "Female Piety," James undertakes one of the most comprehensive and appreciative treatments of godly womanhood in the entire evangelical tradition. His opening thesis is arresting: the influence of woman, for good or for ill, is incalculable. She shapes the home, and the home shapes the church, and the church shapes the nation. Therefore, the piety of women is not a secondary concern but a matter of the highest importance for the welfare of civilization itself.

James begins by defining what he means by female piety. It is not mere religiosity — the outward performance of devotional duties. It is not sentimentality — an emotional attachment to spiritual things that lacks depth or conviction. True piety, James insists, is the fear of the Lord rooted in the heart, manifested in the life, and sustained by living faith in Christ. It is that inner orientation of the soul toward God that transforms every relationship, every duty, and every circumstance into an occasion for worship.

Peter's description of "the hidden man of the heart" captures what James is after. The world prizes outward beauty — the arrangement of the hair, the wearing of gold, the elegance of clothing. But God prizes inward beauty — "the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price." James does not despise outward appearance, but he insists that it is the inner life that matters most. A woman whose heart is adorned with faith, humility, gentleness, and love possesses a beauty that time cannot diminish and death cannot destroy.

James argues that women possess a particular aptitude for piety, not because they are morally superior to men but because of the qualities God has woven into their nature. Women tend toward relational depth, emotional sensitivity, and attentiveness to the unseen dimensions of life — all of which are conducive to the life of faith. James observes that throughout the Gospels, women are prominent in their devotion to Christ. It was women who supported His ministry financially. It was women who stood at the foot of the cross when the male disciples had fled. It was women who came first to the tomb on Easter morning. And it was to a woman — Mary Magdalene — that the risen Christ first appeared.

But James is no mere flatterer. He also identifies the dangers that threaten female piety. The first is worldliness — the temptation to pursue fashion, social standing, and material comfort at the expense of the soul. The second is spiritual superficiality — a religion of feeling without substance, of enthusiasm without doctrine, of tears without transformation. The third is neglecting the means of grace — prayer, Scripture reading, public worship, and Christian fellowship — that are essential to sustaining the life of faith.

James's remedy is simple and profound: cultivate the inner life. Spend time alone with God in prayer and meditation. Read the Scriptures not as a duty but as a delight. Seek the company of other godly women who will encourage and challenge you. And above all, look to Christ — the source, the sustainer, and the perfector of all true piety.

For our family tonight, let us give thanks for the godly women in our lives — mothers, grandmothers, sisters, friends — whose quiet faith has shaped us in ways we may only begin to understand. And let us pray that God would raise up a generation of women whose hearts are adorned with the imperishable beauty of a meek and quiet spirit.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Peter says a "meek and quiet spirit" is of great price in God's sight. What does meekness mean in the Bible? How is it different from weakness?

Question 2: James says women played a prominent role in the ministry of Jesus. Can you name some women from the Gospels and describe their faith?

Question 3: What godly women have had the greatest influence on our family? What qualities in them do we most admire?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"A woman that feareth the Lord, she shall be praised." -- Proverbs 31:30

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for the gift of godly women. We praise You for the mothers, grandmothers, and women of faith who have shaped our family and our church. Continue to raise up women whose hearts are devoted to You, whose lives radiate Your grace, and whose influence draws others to Christ. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
