PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 23
"Love Without Hypocrisy"
Romans for Marriage — Day 23

---

Hello, beloved couple. It is a beautiful thing when husband and wife sit down together in God's Word. The Puritans called marriage a little church within the church. Tonight, your little church is in session. Welcome.

Tonight we turn to Romans 12:9-13. Listen with your hearts, not just your ears:

"Let love be without dissimulation. Abhor that which is evil; cleave to that which is good. Be kindly affectioned one to another with brotherly love; in honour preferring one another; Not slothful in business; fervent in spirit; serving the Lord; Rejoicing in hope; patient in tribulation; continuing instant in prayer; Distributing to the necessity of saints; given to hospitality."

That is Romans 12:9-13. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Matthew Henry had deep insight into the truths we have just read. The Puritans did not separate theology from daily life, and their reflections on marriage are remarkably practical.

Paul gives a rapid-fire series of commands that paint a picture of genuine Christian love. The first phrase sets the standard: 'Let love be without dissimulation.' The word 'dissimulation' means hypocrisy or pretense. Paul demands that love be real. Not performed, not faked, not put on for show — but genuine, authentic, and from the heart.

Matthew Henry, whose Commentary on the Whole Bible remains one of the most widely read biblical commentaries in history, applied these verses with particular tenderness to the marriage relationship. Henry observed that the home is where hypocrisy is most easily exposed and where authenticity is most desperately needed. You can perform love in public — smiling for the cameras, holding hands at church, posting happy pictures. But your spouse knows the truth. And God knows the truth.

Henry walked through each command and applied it to marriage. 'Be kindly affectioned one to another' — let genuine tenderness characterize your daily interactions. Not just on anniversaries and date nights, but on Monday mornings and Thursday evenings. 'In honour preferring one another' — put your spouse's needs, desires, and comfort ahead of your own. Not occasionally, but as a way of life.

'Not slothful in business; fervent in spirit; serving the Lord' — be diligent in the work of marriage. Do not coast. Do not become lazy in love. Keep the fire burning. Many marriages die not from dramatic conflict but from gradual neglect — the slow erosion of effort, attention, and intentionality.

'Rejoicing in hope; patient in tribulation; continuing instant in prayer' — this triad covers the full range of married life. Rejoice in the hope of what God is doing and will do. Be patient when trials press in. And pray constantly — for each other, for your children, for your future. A prayerless marriage is a powerless marriage.

Henry summarized: 'True love is the hardest-working, most honest, most generous, most prayerful, and most joyful force in the universe. And it begins at home.'

--- Marriage Application ---

Take each phrase from Romans 12:9-13 and apply it to your marriage tonight:

Love without hypocrisy — Where might we be pretending? Where do we need more authenticity?
Kindly affectioned — Have we been tender with each other lately, or have we grown cold?
In honour preferring one another — Am I putting my spouse first, or am I putting myself first?
Not slothful — Have we been lazy in our marriage? What area needs renewed effort?
Fervent in spirit — Is the fire still burning, or have we let it go out?
Continuing instant in prayer — Are we praying for each other daily?

Be honest. Be gentle. And be committed to growing in every area where you fall short.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

It is time for reflection. Turn to each other and consider these questions together. There is no rush. Let the conversation go wherever the Spirit leads.

Question 1: Where has hypocrisy crept into your marriage — places where you perform love without truly feeling it?

Question 2: Which of Paul's commands in this passage do you most need to grow in as a spouse?

Question 3: What does 'in honour preferring one another' look like in the daily routines of your marriage?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Now let us pray together. The Puritans said that a couple who prays together is bound by cords that cannot be broken. Let us strengthen those cords tonight.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to make your love genuine — without pretense, without performance, without hypocrisy. Pray through each phrase of Romans 12:9-13 together, asking God to make these qualities real in your marriage.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May your love be the real thing — unhypocritical, tenderhearted, fervent, patient, and prayerful. May your home be filled with the genuine article: love without dissimulation.

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you both, tonight and always. Goodnight.
