PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 4
"The Cross and the Burden"
Pilgrim Families — Week 1, Day 4


Good evening, beloved family. The Puritans believed that the family altar was the most important altar in all the land. Tonight we gather at that altar together. Welcome to Puritan Gold.

Let us come before the Lord. Gracious God, thank You for the gift of family. Thank You that we can come to You together. We pray that You would teach us from Your Word this evening. Let every word sink deep into our hearts. We love You, Lord. In Jesus' name, Amen.

The Word of God is a lamp to our feet and a light to our path. Tonight that light shines from Isaiah 53:4-5; 1 Peter 2:24:

"Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted. But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed. Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed."

That is Isaiah 53:4-5; 1 Peter 2:24. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


This may be the most beautiful scene in all of The Pilgrim's Progress. Christian has been carrying a heavy burden on his back since the very beginning of his journey — the burden of his sin. He could not remove it himself. No one else could take it off. He carried it through the Slough of Despond, through the Wicket Gate, up the road. And then he came to a hill, and upon that hill stood a cross.

The moment Christian looked upon the cross, his burden came loose from his shoulders, fell from his back, rolled down the hill, and tumbled into an open grave — never to be seen again. Christian stood there, astonished, with tears streaming down his face. Three Shining Ones appeared and said to him, "Thy sins be forgiven thee."

Our Scripture today explains what happened at that cross. Isaiah 53:4-5, written seven hundred years before Jesus was born, prophesied: "Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows... he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed." Every word matters here. He bore OUR griefs. He was wounded for OUR transgressions. He was bruised for OUR iniquities. The punishment that brought US peace was placed upon HIM.

And Peter confirms it in 1 Peter 2:24: "Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed." Jesus carried our sins on the cross. He took our burden. He bore the weight that was crushing us and let it crush Him instead.

This is the heart of the Christian faith. This is the Gospel. We are sinners carrying an unbearable burden of guilt and shame. We cannot remove it by trying harder, by being better, by doing more religious activities. Only the cross of Christ can take it away. When we look to Jesus and trust in His finished work, our burden rolls away and falls into the grave — His grave — and it is gone forever.

Bunyan chose his imagery carefully. The burden did not simply disappear. It fell into a sepulchre — a tomb. This is the tomb where Jesus was buried after His crucifixion. Our sins were buried with Christ. Romans 6:4 says, "Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life." Our sins are buried. Dead. Gone. They will never rise again to condemn us.

Christian's response is pure worship. He wept with joy. He could not believe the burden was finally gone. He had carried it so long that he had almost forgotten what it felt like to be free. Many of us carry burdens we have grown so accustomed to that we barely notice them anymore — guilt over past failures, shame over secret sins, fear that we are not good enough for God. Tonight, look to the cross. Let the burden roll away.

Isaiah says, "with his stripes we are healed." The stripes are the marks of the whip that tore Jesus' back open before He was crucified. His wounds heal our wounds. His pain removes our pain. His death gives us life. There is no doctrine more glorious, no truth more precious, no comfort more deep than this: Christ bore our sins in His own body on the tree.

For your family, this truth changes everything. When your children sin and feel guilty, point them to the cross. When you as parents fail and feel ashamed, look to the cross. When anyone in your family is burdened by the weight of their mistakes, remind them: Jesus already carried that burden. It is not ours to carry anymore.

Bunyan spent years tormented by guilt before he found peace at the cross. He knew firsthand the weight of conviction and the relief of forgiveness. His portrayal of Christian's burden falling away is drawn from his own experience. And it can be yours tonight. Come to the cross. Look upon the Savior. And let the burden roll away.


The wisdom of the Puritans remains as fresh today as when it was first written. Listen to this insight.

John Bunyan, in his work "The Pilgrim's Progress," wrote these words:

"He ran thus till he came at a place somewhat ascending; and upon that place stood a cross, and a little below, in the bottom, a sepulchre. So I saw in my dream, that just as Christian came up with the cross, his burden loosed from off his shoulders, and fell from off his back, and began to tumble, and so continued to do till it came to the mouth of the sepulchre, where it fell in, and I saw it no more."

What John Bunyan is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Isaiah 53:4-5; 1 Peter 2:24 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Here are some questions for us to think about and discuss together. There are no wrong answers here, just honest hearts.

Question 1: Why could Christian not remove the burden himself? What does this teach us about trying to earn our own salvation?

Question 2: What does it mean that Jesus bore our sins 'in his own body on the tree'?

Question 3: Are you carrying any burdens of guilt or shame right now? How does looking at the cross help?

Question 4: Why did the burden fall into a tomb? What is the connection between our sins and Jesus' burial?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed." -- Isaiah 53:5

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a hands-on way to remember what we have learned tonight:

Fill a backpack with heavy books and let each family member wear it for a few minutes. Talk about how tiring it is to carry a heavy load. Then have them take it off and feel the relief. Explain that this is what Jesus does with our sin — He takes the weight so we can be free.


Father, we have heard Your Word tonight, and we are grateful. We confess that we cannot live it out in our own strength. Fill us with Your Spirit. Give us wisdom for tomorrow, patience with one another, and a deep love for You. Keep our family close to You always. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Rest well tonight. God is faithful, and His wisdom is sure.
