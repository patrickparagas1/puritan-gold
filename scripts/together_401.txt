PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 9
"The Sacrifice of Daily Kindness"
Ephesians 5:2 — Walking in Love Daily


Welcome, dear couple. This week we are exploring the daily applications of mutual submission and sacrificial love. Tonight we anchor ourselves in a verse from earlier in Ephesians 5 that sets the tone for everything that follows.

Our Scripture tonight is Ephesians 5:2:

"And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour."

Walk in love. Not sit in love, not think about love, but walk — one step at a time, day after day.


The Puritans knew that the greatest threat to most marriages is not a dramatic crisis but the slow erosion of daily kindness. It is not the earthquake that destroys most homes but the steady drip of neglect, criticism, and indifference. And so the Puritans were enormously practical in their counsel about the daily rhythms of married life.

William Gouge listed what he called the "common duties" of husband and wife — duties so ordinary that they are easily forgotten, yet so vital that without them a marriage slowly dies. Among these duties he named: speaking kindly to each other first thing in the morning; showing genuine interest in each other's day; eating meals together whenever possible; expressing gratitude for the small, unseen labors that keep a household running; touching each other with affection — a hand on the shoulder, a kiss in passing, an embrace that lingers.

Richard Baxter added his own list: never go to bed angry; always speak well of your spouse in public; never humiliate your spouse in front of others; give your spouse the benefit of the doubt; assume the best about their motives; and when you are wrong, say so quickly and without excuse.

These are not dramatic gestures. They are the daily rhythms of walking in love. And Paul says that this walk is patterned after Christ, who "gave himself for us an offering and a sacrifice." The sacrifice of daily kindness is no less real than the sacrifice of a dramatic rescue. In fact, it may be harder. It is relatively easy to imagine that you would die for your spouse in a moment of crisis. It is much harder to die to yourself every morning — to set aside your preferences, your mood, your agenda, and to greet your spouse with genuine warmth and attentiveness.

Thomas Gataker observed that the phrase "a sweetsmelling savour" suggests that sacrificial love is fragrant — it fills the atmosphere. A home where both spouses walk in daily kindness has a distinct aroma. Visitors sense it. Children grow up breathing it. Neighbors are drawn to it. Conversely, a home where kindness has evaporated has its own atmosphere — tense, cold, guarded. The Puritans believed that the spiritual atmosphere of a home is created primarily by the way husband and wife treat each other.

Tonight, examine the atmosphere of your home. Is it fragrant with kindness? Or has neglect crept in? The good news is that the atmosphere can change — not through one grand gesture, but through a thousand small acts of love, beginning tomorrow morning.


Discuss together:

Question 1: What are the small, daily acts of kindness that mean the most to you? Have you told your spouse about them?

Question 2: Has any pattern of neglect or criticism crept into your daily routine? What would it look like to replace it with intentional kindness?

Question 3: If a visitor spent a week in your home, what would the "atmosphere" feel like? What would you want it to feel like?


Pray together for the grace to walk in daily love. Ask God to help you notice the small opportunities for kindness that you have been overlooking. Pray that your home would be filled with the sweet fragrance of sacrificial love — the kind that makes Christ visible in ordinary life.


May the God who gave Himself as a sweet-smelling sacrifice fill your marriage with the fragrance of daily kindness. May your love for each other be expressed not only in grand moments but in the quiet, faithful rhythms of ordinary days. Grace and peace be with you. Goodnight.
