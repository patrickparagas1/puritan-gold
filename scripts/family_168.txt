PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 7
"The Household at Prayer"
William Gouge — Domestical Duties


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. We close our first week with William Gouge by considering the practice he believed was most essential to the Christian home — family prayer and worship. Let us draw near to God together.

Let us pray. Lord God, we come before You tonight not merely to study prayer but to practice it. Stir in our hearts a fresh desire to seek Your face as a family. Teach us to pray with faith, with fervency, and with unity. Make our home a house of prayer. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Joshua 24:15 and Acts 10:1-2. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"And if it seem evil unto you to serve the Lord, choose you this day whom ye will serve; whether the gods which your fathers served that were on the other side of the flood, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye dwell: but as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord. There was a certain man in Caesarea called Cornelius, a centurion of the band called the Italian band, A devout man, and one that feared God with all his house, which gave much alms to the people, and prayed to God alway."

That is Joshua 24:15 and Acts 10:1-2. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


We come to the crown and summit of William Gouge's teaching on the Christian household: family worship. For Gouge, everything else — the husband's love, the wife's faithfulness, the children's obedience, the gracious speech — finds its source and its sustenance in the practice of worshiping God together as a family. Without family worship, the household may function, but it will not flourish spiritually. With family worship, even the humblest home becomes a temple of the living God.

Gouge found his model in Joshua's ringing declaration: "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." And in Cornelius, the Roman centurion described in Acts 10, who "feared God with all his house" and "prayed to God alway." These were not men who practiced private religion while leaving their households in spiritual neglect. They led their entire families in the worship of God. They made their homes places where God's name was hallowed, His Word was read, and His help was sought in prayer.

Gouge outlines three essential elements of family worship. The first is the reading of Scripture. Every day, the household should gather to hear the Word of God read aloud. Gouge recommends reading through the Bible systematically, so that the family hears the whole counsel of God over time. He suggests that the father, as head of the household, should read the passage and offer a brief explanation suited to the understanding of every member, including the youngest children. Where the father is unable, the mother may lead, or another capable member of the household. The key is that the Bible is read, heard, and discussed together.

The second element is prayer. Gouge urges families to pray together at least twice a day — morning and evening. Morning prayer commits the day to God, asks for His guidance and protection, and sets the spiritual tone for everything that follows. Evening prayer gives thanks for the day's mercies, confesses the day's sins, and entrusts the family to God's care through the night. Gouge counsels that family prayer should include thanksgiving, confession, petition, and intercession — giving thanks for God's blessings, confessing sins and failures, asking for specific needs, and praying for others: for the church, for the nation, for neighbors, and for those in distress.

The third element is the singing of psalms and hymns. Gouge draws from Colossians 3:16: "Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom; teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord." Singing together knits the family's hearts in worship and implants God's truth in the memory. The Puritans were especially devoted to the singing of the Psalms, which they considered the inspired hymnal of the church. Even families who felt their voices were inadequate were encouraged to sing, for it is the heart that God hears, not the pitch.

Gouge addresses the practical obstacles to family worship with characteristic realism. He knows that families are busy, that children are restless, that schedules are unpredictable, and that the flesh resists the discipline of daily devotion. His counsel is straightforward: begin simply, be consistent, and trust God for the fruit. A short time of family worship, faithfully maintained, is far better than an elaborate program that collapses after a week. Start with a few verses of Scripture, a brief prayer, and a simple song. As the habit grows, the practice can deepen and expand.

Gouge also warns against the father who neglects this duty. A man who would never dream of sending his children to bed without supper will often send them to bed without prayer. A man who locks his doors at night against thieves will leave his family's spiritual doors wide open against the enemy of souls. Gouge writes with evident feeling that the neglect of family worship is one of the greatest sins of his generation, and that God will hold household heads accountable for the spiritual welfare of those under their care.

Let this week's study of Gouge end with this call to action: if your family does not yet practice regular worship together, begin tonight. It need not be long. It need not be polished. But let it be faithful. Gather your family, open the Bible, pray together, and if you can, sing together. You will be joining a tradition as old as the church itself and as powerful as the God who inhabits the praises of His people.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Joshua declared, "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." What does it mean to make a family commitment to serve God, not just an individual one?

Question 2: Gouge says the three elements of family worship are Scripture reading, prayer, and singing. Which of these three does our family practice most? Which do we need to strengthen?

Question 3: What are the biggest obstacles to regular family worship in our home? How can we overcome them together?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"But as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." -- Joshua 24:15

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Almighty God, we confess that we have too often neglected the sacred privilege of worshiping You together as a family. Forgive us, and give us fresh resolve. Help us to open Your Word daily, to pray together faithfully, and to lift our voices in praise. Make our home a house of prayer and a dwelling place of Your Spirit. We offer this week's study to You, and we ask You to seal its lessons on our hearts. In the name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
