PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 21
"Remedy: Pray Without Ceasing"
Pilgrim Families — Week 3, Day 7


Welcome, dear family. Another day has passed, and God has been faithful through it all. Now let us close this day together by sitting at the feet of Jesus and hearing His Word.

Let us pray. Almighty God, You are the giver of every good gift, and one of the greatest gifts You have given us is this family. We dedicate this time to You. Help us to listen well and to grow in wisdom together. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

Our passage tonight is 1 Thessalonians 5:17; Matthew 26:41. Listen with expectant hearts:

"Pray without ceasing. Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak."

That is 1 Thessalonians 5:17; Matthew 26:41. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


We close our week with Thomas Brooks by arriving at the ultimate remedy: prayer. If the Word of God is the sword that attacks the enemy, prayer is the hand that wields it. Without prayer, even the sharpest sword lies unused on the ground.

Paul gives us the simplest command in Scripture: "Pray without ceasing" (1 Thessalonians 5:17). Three words that contain an entire lifestyle. To pray without ceasing does not mean kneeling 24 hours a day. It means maintaining an ongoing conversation with God throughout every moment of every day. It means turning your thoughts toward God as naturally as you breathe. It means involving God in every decision, every challenge, every temptation, every joy.

Jesus spoke the words of Matthew 26:41 to His disciples in the Garden of Gethsemane, on the night before His crucifixion: "Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak." He found them sleeping when they should have been praying. And within hours, Peter — the most outspoken of the disciples — denied Jesus three times. The connection is clear: those who do not pray fall to temptation. Those who do pray stand firm.

Jesus acknowledges a tension every Christian feels: "The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak." We want to do right. We intend to resist temptation. Our spirit is willing. But our flesh — our human nature with all its weaknesses — is frail. This is precisely why we need prayer. Prayer is the admission that we are weak and God is strong. Prayer says, "I cannot do this on my own. I need Your help." And God, who loves to help the humble, rushes to our aid.

Brooks taught that prayer should be the Christian's first response to every temptation, not the last resort. Too often we try to fight temptation in our own strength, and only when we are failing do we finally cry out to God. But the wise Christian prays before the temptation comes. The wise Christian prays at the first hint of danger. The wise Christian makes prayer as automatic as breathing.

Remember that in Bunyan's Pilgrim's Progress, when Christian passed through the Valley of the Shadow of Death and his sword was useless in the darkness, he took up the weapon called "All-prayer." When you cannot see the way forward, when the darkness is thick, when the enemy presses in from every side — pray. Just pray. Cry out to God. He hears every word.

The Puritans structured their entire day around prayer. They began the morning with prayer, asking God to guard them through the day ahead. They prayed before meals, acknowledging God as the source of every good gift. They prayed in the evening, reviewing the day and confessing any sins. And they prayed in the moment of temptation, calling out for immediate help.

For families, building a prayer life together is the most powerful thing you can do against the enemy's schemes. When your family prays together, you are building a wall around your home that the devil cannot breach. Brooks compared prayer to armor — the Christian should be clothed with prayer as they are clothed with their garments. You would not leave the house without getting dressed; do not leave without prayer.

There are several practical ways to strengthen your family's prayer life. First, pray together at a set time each day — morning or evening or both. Consistency builds the habit. Second, pray about specific things. Vague prayers produce vague faith. Name the temptations you face. Name the people you are praying for. Be specific, and watch God answer specifically. Third, teach your children to pray conversationally. Prayer does not require fancy language. God wants to hear their real words, their real feelings, their real struggles.

Fourth, pray in the moment of temptation. When you see a family member struggling — a child throwing a tantrum, a teenager sulking, a spouse frustrated — stop and pray right then and there. Do not wait for bedtime. Do not save it for church. Pray in the kitchen, in the car, in the backyard. "Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation." The watching and the praying go together. Be alert, and when you see danger approaching, pray.

Tonight, close this week by praying together as a family. Pray through everything you have learned from Thomas Brooks. Confess the times you have minimized sin, been deceived by painted temptation, hidden secret sin, lost sight of eternity, failed to flee, neglected Scripture, and forgotten to pray. And then thank God that in Christ, there is forgiveness, power, and victory over every device of the enemy.


Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher and learn something that will deepen our understanding.

Thomas Brooks, in his work "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices," wrote these words:

"Prayer is the best armor against all trials and temptations. The Christian must be clothed with prayer as he is with his garments."

What Thomas Brooks is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in 1 Thessalonians 5:17; Matthew 26:41 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Let us discuss what we have heard. Parents, encourage even the youngest to share what they are thinking.

Question 1: What does it mean to 'pray without ceasing'? How can we pray all the time while also doing our daily work?

Question 2: Jesus told the disciples to 'watch and pray.' What happened when they slept instead? What lesson is there for us?

Question 3: Why should prayer be our first response to temptation rather than our last resort?

Question 4: What is one change our family can make this week to strengthen our prayer life together?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak." -- Matthew 26:41

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Before we close, here is a family challenge for the days ahead:

Create a 'family prayer wall' on a poster board or whiteboard. Write down specific prayer requests from each family member — including temptations they face, people they want to pray for, and things they need from God. Commit to praying through the wall together each day this week. When prayers are answered, write the date and the answer next to the request.


Heavenly Father, we end where we began, in gratitude. Thank You for Your Word, for this family, and for the hope we have in Christ. May tonight's lesson from 1 Thessalonians 5:17; Matthew 26:41 shape the way we live, the way we speak, and the way we love one another. Amen and amen.

Until next time, dear family. The Lord goes before you. Walk with Him.
