THE PURPOSE OF GOD Pink demonstrates that God has always had one great purpose running through all ages: the gathering of His elect people into one body under one Head, the Lord Jesus Christ. Dispensationalists err in dividing God's purposes into separate programs for Israel and the Church, as though God has two peoples and two plans. Pink shows from Scripture that there is one olive tree, one flock, one body, one bride, and one new man made from Jew and Gentile alike. THE CHURCH OF GOD The Church did not begin at Pentecost, Pink argues, but has existed in some form since the beginning. Stephen spoke of the church in the wilderness in Acts 7:38. The saints of the Old Testament were saved by the same grace, through the same Mediator, and are part of the same spiritual body as New Testament believers. Dispensationalists create an artificial divide that Scripture does not support. THE ISRAEL OF GOD Pink carefully demonstrates that the New Testament applies the title Israel of God to the Church — all believers, not merely ethnic Jews. Romans 9:6 states plainly that not all who are descended from Israel belong to Israel. The true Israel has always been the faithful remnant, those who share Abraham's faith rather than merely his bloodline. THE ZION OF GOD Similarly, Zion in its highest spiritual meaning refers not to a geographical location but to the heavenly city, the assembly of the firstborn enrolled in heaven. Hebrews 12:22 declares that believers have come to Mount Zion, the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem. THE GRACE OF GOD Pink refutes the dispensationalist claim that grace is unique to the present age. He demonstrates that God has always dealt with His people in grace — from Abel to Abraham to David to the prophets. The suggestion that Old Testament saints lived under a different economy devoid of grace is a grave distortion of Scripture. THE LAW OF GOD In four extensive articles, Pink dismantles the antinomian tendencies of dispensationalism. The moral law of God is eternal and unchanging. Christ did not come to abolish the law but to fulfill it. The law remains the believer's rule of life, written on the heart by the Holy Spirit in the new covenant. Dispensationalists who teach that Christians are not under the law in any sense confuse justification with sanctification. THE WORD OF GOD Pink concludes by defending the unity of Scripture against the dispensationalist tendency to fragment it. The whole Bible is for the whole Church. Pink warns against the practice of dismissing portions of Scripture as not being for this dispensation. Such an approach undermines the authority and sufficiency of God's Word and leaves believers impoverished.
