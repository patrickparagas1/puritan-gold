PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 21
"A Family of Christian Practice"
Jonathan Edwards — Religious Affections Applied to the Family


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we close our week with Jonathan Edwards by considering his final and most important mark of true affections — Christian practice — and what it means for our family to live out our faith in daily action. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, we do not want to be hearers of the Word only, but doers also. This week You have taught us about holy affections — now help us to translate those affections into faithful practice. May our family be known not just for what we believe but for how we live. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from James 1:22-25. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves. For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass: For he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was. But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his doing."

That is James 1:22-25. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Jonathan Edwards concluded his great treatise on Religious Affections with what he considered the most important mark of all: Christian practice. After examining a dozen signs of genuine and counterfeit affections, Edwards declared that the chief evidence of true grace is a life of consistent, daily obedience to God. Not perfect obedience — no one achieves that in this life. But a pattern of obedience, a direction of obedience, a habit of obedience that characterizes the whole course of a person's life.

Edwards put it with striking clarity: the proof of the pudding is in the eating. The proof of genuine faith is in the living. No amount of emotional experience, theological knowledge, or religious activity can substitute for the simple, daily practice of following Christ. A person who claims to love God but does not obey His commands is deceived — not because obedience earns God's love, but because genuine love for God naturally expresses itself in obedience, just as genuine fire naturally produces heat.

For families, this teaching is the capstone of everything we have studied this week. Holy affections are wonderful. The beauty of God is magnificent. Humility is essential. A tender conscience is a gift. Gracious speech is transforming. Persevering love is powerful. But all of these are validated only when they produce a family that actually lives differently — a family that practices the faith it professes.

What does Christian practice look like in a family? It looks like parents who consistently prioritize spiritual nurture over worldly success. It looks like children who obey not just when they feel like it but because they love God and honor their parents. It looks like a family that prays together not just in emergencies but as a daily discipline. It looks like a household where the Bible is not merely displayed on the shelf but opened, read, discussed, and obeyed. It looks like a family that serves its neighbors, gives generously to those in need, forgives quickly when wronged, and speaks the truth in love.

Edwards was careful to emphasize that Christian practice is not legalism. It is not a joyless adherence to rules. It is the natural overflow of a heart that has been captivated by the beauty of God. When you truly love someone, you want to please them. When you truly love God, you want to live in a way that honors Him. Obedience that flows from love is not bondage — it is freedom. It is the freedom to live the way you were designed to live, in harmony with the God who made you.

Edwards also stressed that Christian practice is universal in scope. It does not apply to one area of life while leaving others untouched. It transforms how we spend our money, how we use our time, how we treat strangers, how we handle disappointments, how we respond to injustice, and how we face death. A family whose faith shapes every dimension of life is a family living out genuine religious affections.

As we close this week with Edwards, let us take stock. This week we have learned that true faith involves the whole person — mind, heart, and will. We have seen that genuine affections are produced by spiritual understanding, express themselves in humility and gracious speech, endure through trials, and bear fruit in daily practice. Now the question is: Will we be doers of the Word, or hearers only?

Tonight, choose one specific way your family will practice your faith this week. Perhaps it is an act of service to a neighbor. Perhaps it is a commitment to daily prayer. Perhaps it is a decision to forgive someone who has wronged you. Whatever it is, do it. For Edwards — and for God — the proof of genuine affection is not what we feel but what we do.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Edwards said that Christian practice is the chief evidence of genuine faith. Why is what we do even more important than what we feel?

Question 2: James warns against being "hearers only." In what areas of our faith are we better at hearing than doing? How can we close that gap?

Question 3: What is one specific act of Christian practice our family will commit to this week?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves." -- James 1:22

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord, we want to be a family of practice, not just profession. Help us to translate everything we have learned this week into faithful daily living. May our affections be genuine, our humility sincere, our speech gracious, our love enduring, and our practice consistent. Make us doers of Your Word, for Your glory and our good. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that the God who works in your hearts also works through your hands.
