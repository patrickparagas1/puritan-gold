PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"The King Shall Joy in Thy Strength"
June 9, 2026

---

"The king shall joy in thy strength, O Lord; and in thy salvation how greatly shall he rejoice! Thou hast given him his heart's desire, and hast not withholden the request of his lips. Selah. For thou preventest him with the blessings of goodness: thou settest a crown of pure gold on his head. He asked life of thee, and thou gavest it him, even length of days for ever and ever." -- Psalm 20:1-4 [KJV heading: Psalm 21:1-4]

This royal psalm lifts our eyes from the sorrows and trials that occupy so many of the songs of David and directs our gaze upward to the splendour of divine blessing poured out upon the Lord's anointed king. It is a psalm of triumph, of gratitude, of holy exultation in the goodness of God who gives victory to His chosen one. And while it speaks first of David, the earthly king, it speaks ultimately and most fully of great David's greater Son, the Lord Jesus Christ, in whom all the promises of God find their eternal and perfect fulfilment.

"The king shall joy in thy strength, O Lord." Here is the secret of true royal power, and indeed of all true power whatsoever. The king's joy is not in his own strength but in the strength of God. He does not glory in the size of his army, the thickness of his walls, or the sharpness of his sword. His glory is in the Lord who gave him every advantage he possesses and who alone can turn those advantages into victory. This is a lesson that every ruler needs to learn, and that few rulers have ever fully grasped. The temptation of power is to attribute success to oneself, to imagine that one's achievements are the product of one's own wisdom and ability, and to forget the sovereign hand that raised one up and that can as easily cast one down.

Spurgeon preached with great power upon this truth, reminding his congregation that the Lord Jesus Christ, the King of kings, found all His joy in the strength of His Father. Throughout His earthly ministry, the Son glorified the Father. He did nothing of Himself. He spoke nothing of Himself. Every miracle was wrought by the power of the Father working through Him. Every word was the word that the Father had given Him to speak. And in His great work of redemption, it was the Father's strength that upheld Him in the garden of Gethsemane, sustained Him upon the cross, and raised Him from the dead on the third day. The King joyed in the strength of the Lord, and in that strength He conquered sin, death, and hell.

"Thou hast given him his heart's desire, and hast not withholden the request of his lips." What a marvellous statement of answered prayer. God has granted the king everything his heart desired. This does not mean that God grants every whim and fancy of His people, for our hearts often desire things that would be harmful to us. But when the heart has been renewed by grace, when its desires have been purified and aligned with the will of God, then the desires of the heart and the will of God become one, and God takes pleasure in fulfilling the petitions of His beloved children.

Thomas Goodwin wrote with profound insight upon the prayer life of Christ, showing that the Father never denied the Son any request that was made in accordance with the covenant of redemption. The Son asked for the salvation of His elect, and the Father granted it. The Son asked for the gift of the Holy Spirit to be poured out upon His church, and the Father granted it. The Son asked for authority over all flesh, and the Father granted it. And in all these things, the Father was not reluctantly yielding to pressure but joyfully bestowing upon His beloved Son the rewards of His mediatorial work.

"For thou preventest him with the blessings of goodness." The word "preventest" in the old English of the King James Version means to go before, to anticipate, to precede. Before the king could even ask, God had already prepared the blessing. Before the prayer was formed upon his lips, the answer was already on its way from heaven. This is the nature of God's grace: it is always ahead of us. It does not wait for us to deserve it or to request it. It comes unbidden, unearned, and unlooked-for, surprising us with goodness when we expected nothing but what our own efforts could produce.

John Flavel beautifully captured this truth when he wrote that the mercies of God are like the morning dew -- they fall silently in the night, and when we awake in the morning, we find that the whole landscape of our lives has been quietly and gently refreshed by a hand we never saw and a kindness we never deserved. This is what it means to be prevented with blessings of goodness. God is always working on our behalf, always preparing benefits, always arranging providences for our advantage, long before we are aware of our need.

"He asked life of thee, and thou gavest it him, even length of days for ever and ever." In the fullest and most glorious sense, this promise belongs to the Lord Jesus Christ. He asked life -- not for Himself, for He is the eternal Son of God and has life in Himself -- but He asked life for His people, and the Father gave it to Him. He asked that those whom the Father had given Him should not perish but have everlasting life, and the Father granted this request with an irrevocable decree. And the length of days that belongs to Christ and to His people is not merely a long duration of years upon this earth but an eternity of blessedness in the presence of God, where there is fulness of joy and pleasures forevermore.

Let us therefore join with the psalmist in holy exultation. Let our hearts rejoice in the strength of the Lord. Let us give thanks for the blessings of goodness with which He has prevented us, for the answered prayers, for the desires of our hearts that He has fulfilled, and above all for the everlasting life that He has freely given us in Christ Jesus our Lord. Amen.
