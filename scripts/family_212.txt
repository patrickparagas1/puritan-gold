PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 20
"Persevering Love in the Family"
Jonathan Edwards — Religious Affections Applied to the Family

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Edwards addresses the mark of perseverance — a love that endures through seasons of difficulty — and how lasting affections hold a family together when everything else threatens to pull it apart. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, give our family a love that endures. Not a love that burns hot for a moment and then grows cold, but a steady, persistent, faithful love that reflects Your own love for us. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from 1 Corinthians 13:4-8a. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up, Doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil; Rejoiceth not in iniquity, but rejoiceth in the truth; Beareth all things, believeth all things, hopeth all things, endureth all things. Charity never faileth."

That is 1 Corinthians 13:4-8a. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Jonathan Edwards regarded perseverance as the ultimate test of genuine religious affection. It is relatively easy to feel warmly toward God and others in a moment of spiritual excitement. It is much harder to maintain that warmth through the long, grinding ordinariness of daily life. Edwards observed that many people who experienced dramatic spiritual emotions during the Great Awakening eventually fell away. Their affections were intense but shallow — hot for a season, then cold. The mark of true affections, Edwards argued, is their durability. They last. They endure. They persevere through difficulty, disappointment, and delay.

For families, this teaching could not be more relevant. Family love is tested not primarily in the dramatic moments but in the daily ones. The crisis may draw the family together in a surge of solidarity. But the real test of love is the Tuesday evening after a long day, when everyone is tired and irritable. The real test is the fifteenth time you have had the same argument about the same issue. The real test is the season of financial pressure, or illness, or relational strain that stretches on for months with no resolution in sight. In these moments, a love that is merely emotional will falter and fade. Only a love rooted in genuine spiritual affection — a love that flows from the heart's settled commitment to God and to one another — will endure.

Paul's description of love in 1 Corinthians 13 is essentially a portrait of persevering love. Love "suffereth long" — it is patient, willing to wait, refusing to give up on a person even when progress is slow. Love "is kind" — not just when it is convenient, but consistently, habitually, relentlessly kind. Love "beareth all things" — it carries the weight of the other person's burdens without complaint. Love "endureth all things" — it stands firm when circumstances are crushing. And the conclusion is absolute: "Charity never faileth." True love does not quit.

Edwards connected persevering love to the character of God. God's love for His people is the ultimate model of love that endures. He loved Israel through centuries of rebellion. He loved the disciples through their failures and denials. He loves each of us through every season of spiritual coldness, every act of disobedience, every moment of forgetfulness. His love is not dependent on our performance. It is rooted in His nature. And because He is unchangeable, His love is unchangeable.

When family members are rooted in this kind of divine love, their love for one another takes on the same quality. A parent who loves with persevering love does not give up on a rebellious child. A spouse who loves with persevering love does not abandon the marriage when it becomes difficult. A sibling who loves with persevering love does not write off a brother or sister because of a single offense. Persevering love says, "I will love you through this. I will not quit on you. I will be here tomorrow, and the day after that, and the day after that."

Edwards would hasten to add that persevering love is not produced by human effort alone. It is the fruit of the Spirit — a supernatural love that flows from our union with Christ. When we abide in Him, His love flows through us to those around us, and His love never fails. This is why daily communion with God — through prayer, Scripture, and worship — is not optional for families that want to love well. It is the lifeline that sustains persevering love through every storm.

Tonight, look at the faces of your family members and make a silent commitment: I will love you with a love that endures. Not a love that depends on your behavior, but a love that depends on God's grace flowing through me to you.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Edwards said that perseverance is the ultimate test of genuine affection. Why is it easier to love in the exciting moments than in the ordinary, difficult ones?

Question 2: Which quality of love in 1 Corinthians 13 does our family need most right now? How can we cultivate it together?

Question 3: How does knowing that God's love for us never fails give us strength to love one another with persevering love?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Charity never faileth." -- 1 Corinthians 13:8a

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord, give our family a love that never fails. When we are tired, sustain our love. When we are disappointed, renew our love. When we are provoked, purify our love. Root us so deeply in Your unfailing love that our love for one another becomes a reflection of Yours. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, held by a love that will never let you go.
