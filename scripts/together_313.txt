PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 13
"Walking with the Wise Together"
Proverbs 13:20

---

Good evening, dear couple. The Puritans believed that marriage was one of the greatest means of grace God provides. Tonight we enter into that grace together. Let us open our hearts and our ears to what the Lord has for us.

As we settle in together, let us hear from Proverbs 13:20:

"He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed."

That is Proverbs 13:20. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

One of the gifts of the Christian tradition is that we do not study alone. We have the wisdom of the saints who came before us. Tonight we hear from Matthew Henry.

Matthew Henry observes that this proverb speaks to one of the most powerful forces in human life: the influence of companionship. 'We are apt to think ourselves not concerned in the character of those we associate with,' Henry writes, 'but we are mistaken.' We absorb the values, habits, and attitudes of those we spend the most time with, whether we intend to or not.

Henry notes the deliberateness of the language: 'walketh with.' This is not a passing acquaintance but a chosen companion, one whose company you regularly keep. To walk with the wise is to make wisdom your constant environment. It is to seek out those who fear God, who love His Word, and who order their lives by divine truth, and to make them your closest friends.

The promise is clear: 'shall be wise.' Wisdom is, in a sense, contagious. Henry explains that by observing the wise, by hearing their counsel, by imitating their example, we ourselves grow in wisdom. This is why God has placed us in the church: not merely for instruction from the pulpit but for mutual edification through fellowship.

The warning is equally stark: 'a companion of fools shall be destroyed.' Charles Bridges adds that this destruction often comes gradually. The fool does not announce himself as such; he presents himself as fun, entertaining, and free-spirited. But his influence is corrosive. Bridges writes: 'The companion of fools is not one who occasionally encounters them, but one who chooses them for his intimates. And the end of that choice is destruction.' For a married couple, this means that the friendships you cultivate together will either strengthen or weaken your marriage.

--- Marriage Application ---

Who are you walking with? As a couple, your closest friendships shape your marriage more than you may realize. Couples who spend most of their social time with other couples who complain about their spouses, who prioritize worldly pleasures, or who have low regard for faithfulness will find those attitudes seeping into their own marriage.

Conversely, couples who walk with the wise — who invest in friendships with godly, marriage-honoring people — will find their own marriage strengthened. Seek out couples who love God's Word, who serve the church faithfully, who are honest about their struggles but committed to working through them with grace and truth.

Evaluate your social circle together. Not every friendship needs to be cut off, but you should be intentional about which friendships you invest in most deeply. Walk with the wise, and you shall be wise together.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Discussion time. Husband, listen to your wife. Wife, listen to your husband. Let this be a safe and sacred space.

Question 1: Who are the closest friends in our life right now, and are they drawing us toward wisdom or away from it?

Question 2: Is there a couple we admire for their godliness with whom we could cultivate a deeper friendship?

Question 3: Have we noticed any negative influences from certain friendships on our marriage, and how should we respond?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Let us close our eyes and open our hearts to the Lord in prayer. Husband, pray for your wife. Wife, pray for your husband. There is no greater gift you can give each other.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to bring wise companions into your life as a couple, and to give you discernment about the friendships that may be pulling you away from His purposes. Pray for the courage to invest in relationships that strengthen your faith and your marriage.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord grant you the gift of wise companions, that walking together with those who fear Him, your marriage may grow in wisdom and grace. Amen.

Go in peace, husband and wife. The God who ordained your marriage is faithful to sustain it. Trust Him. Love each other. And come back tomorrow for more. Goodnight.
