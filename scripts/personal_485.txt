PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 24
"The Doctrine of Adoption"
Theological Day — The Privilege of Being God's Children


Welcome. Today is a Theological day. We examine one of the most comforting doctrines in all of Scripture: adoption — the act by which God takes former rebels and makes them His beloved children.

Let us read Romans 8:15-17 (KJV):

"For ye have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear; but ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father. The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God: And if children, then heirs; heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ; if so be that we suffer with him, that we may be also glorified together."


The Puritans considered adoption to be one of the most glorious benefits of redemption — distinct from justification, distinct from sanctification, and carrying privileges all its own. Justification changes your legal standing — from condemned to acquitted. Sanctification changes your character — from sinful to holy. But adoption changes your relationship — from stranger and enemy to beloved child.

The Westminster Confession defines adoption: "All those that are justified, God vouchsafeth, in and for His only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the grace of adoption: by which they are taken into the number, and enjoy the liberties and privileges of the children of God." Notice: adoption is a grace — an undeserved gift. And it brings specific privileges.

John Owen wrote that adoption gives the believer a new name, a new family, a new inheritance, and a new relationship with God that transforms every other relationship in life. Let us consider each.

A new name. You are no longer called sinner, rebel, enemy, or slave. You are called child. Son. Daughter. Beloved. This new name is not based on your performance but on God's decision. He chose to adopt you. He chose to call you His own. And a name given by God cannot be revoked by man.

A new family. Through adoption, you are placed into the family of God — a family that includes every believer from every nation, every century, and every social class. You have brothers and sisters you have never met. You have a heritage that stretches back to Abraham. You are part of something vast, ancient, and eternal. The Puritans prized Christian fellowship because they understood that every fellow believer is a family member — not by blood but by adoption.

A new inheritance. If children, then heirs. The inheritance of the children of God is nothing less than God Himself — the eternal enjoyment of His presence, His love, His glory. Richard Sibbes wrote that the inheritance reserved for the children of God makes every earthly inheritance look like a child's toy. All the wealth, power, and honor in this world will pass away. But the inheritance of the saints is imperishable, undefiled, and unfading.

A new relationship with God. The most transformative aspect of adoption is the change in how you relate to God. Before adoption, God is the righteous Judge whose sentence you fear. After adoption, God is Abba — Father — the tender, personal, intimate name that Jesus Himself used in prayer. The Spirit of adoption enables you to cry "Abba, Father" — to approach God not with slavish fear but with childlike confidence. This does not eliminate reverence. Children can be reverent toward their father while also being comfortable in his presence. Adoption gives you both.

Thomas Watson applied the doctrine of adoption to the daily life of the believer. He wrote that the adopted child of God has the right of access — you can come to God at any time, in any condition, with any need. You have the right of provision — your Father has promised to supply all your needs according to His riches in glory. You have the right of protection — your Father watches over you, and no one can harm you without His permission. You have the right of discipline — your Father corrects you when you stray, not in anger but in love. And you have the right of inheritance — everything your Father has is yours in Christ.

The Puritans used adoption as a weapon against two common spiritual diseases. Against legalism, they said: you are not a servant trying to earn your master's approval. You are a child, already loved, already accepted. Against antinomianism, they said: you are a child of a holy Father, and children should bear the family resemblance. Adoption produces not lawlessness but loving obedience — the obedience of a child who wants to please a Father who has given everything.


You are a child of God. Not a slave. Not a stranger. A child. With all the rights, privileges, and responsibilities that come with that name. Lord, I can hardly believe it — that You would call me Your child. But You have. By grace, through adoption, I am Yours. Let me live today as a child of the King — confident in Your love, dependent on Your provision, and eager to bear the family resemblance. Abba, Father. Amen.
