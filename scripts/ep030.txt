PURITAN GOLD — Episode 30
"The Name of the Lord Is a Strong Tower"
Proverbs 18 | Day 30 of 31

---

[INTRO]

Have you ever been in a storm — a real one — where the wind is shaking the walls and the rain is hammering the roof... and you just pull the blanket a little tighter and think, "I'm safe in here"? There's something primal about that feeling. Shelter. Refuge. A place where the fury outside cannot reach you.

That's where Proverbs 18 takes us tonight. And friends, of all the places we've visited this month... this may be the one your soul needs most.

This is Puritan Gold, day thirty of our journey through the book of Proverbs. Tomorrow is our final day — our month-long review. But tonight, we're in chapter 18. And at the very heart of this chapter stands one of the most beloved verses in all of wisdom literature. A verse that has been whispered by saints in dungeons, on deathbeds, in the watches of the night when fear pressed in from every side.

Let me read it to you.

---

[PART ONE — THE STRONG TOWER]

Proverbs 18:10:

"The name of the LORD is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe."

Now I want you to sit with that image for a moment. Solomon doesn't say that the name of the Lord is a nice idea. He doesn't say it's a theological concept. He says it is a strong tower. A fortress. A citadel. A place of absolute, unassailable security.

And the righteous — the one who has been made right with God, not by their own merit but by grace through faith — the righteous runs into it. Notice the urgency of that word. Runs. This isn't a casual stroll. This is a person who knows they are in danger and knows exactly where to go. They don't hesitate. They don't look for alternatives. They run to the name of the Lord... and they are safe.

Now, what does it mean that the name of the Lord is this tower? In the ancient world — and in biblical theology — a name was not just a label. It was a revelation of character. The name of God is everything God has revealed Himself to be. Every attribute. Every promise. Every act of faithfulness across the whole sweep of redemptive history.

A.W. Pink, in his writings on the attributes of God, made this point with wonderful clarity. He reminded us that when Scripture speaks of the name of the Lord, it gathers up all the divine perfections — His holiness, His justice, His mercy, His omnipotence, His faithfulness, His unchangeableness — and presents them as one glorious, unshakeable refuge. The name is the sum of who God is. And who God is... is your safety.

Think about the names of God scattered throughout the Old Testament. Jehovah-Jireh — the Lord will provide. He spoke that name on a mountain when a father's heart was breaking, and then He provided the ram. Jehovah-Rapha — the Lord who heals. Jehovah-Shalom — the Lord is peace. Jehovah-Rohi — the Lord is my shepherd. El-Shaddai — God Almighty. Every single name is a door into the strong tower. Every single name is a room in the fortress where the righteous find shelter.

And here's what I want you to notice: the text says the righteous "runneth into it." Present tense. Ongoing action. This isn't a one-time event. The Christian life is a life of running again and again to the name of the Lord. Every morning. Every crisis. Every temptation. Every grief. You run to Him. Not because you're strong enough to face the storm yourself... but because you've learned that He is strong enough. And His strength becomes yours when you shelter in His name.

The very next verse, Proverbs 18:11, provides a devastating contrast:

"The rich man's wealth is his strong city, and as an high wall in his own conceit."

Do you see it? The rich man has a tower too — but it's imaginary. His wealth looks like a high wall... but it's only "in his own conceit." In his imagination. It's a mirage. When the real storm comes — when death comes, when judgment comes, when the things of this world are shaken — the rich man's tower crumbles to dust. But the name of the Lord stands forever.

So the question this chapter poses to each of us is simply this: where are you running? What is your strong tower? Is it your bank account? Your reputation? Your health? Your accomplishments? Because all of those towers have cracks in them, friend. They will not hold. But the name of the Lord — that tower has never fallen. Not once. Not in all of history. And it never will.

---

[PART TWO — THE HEART OF THE PRUDENT]

Now, let me take you to another verse in this chapter that I think pairs beautifully with our main text. Proverbs 18:15:

"The heart of the prudent getteth knowledge; and the ear of the wise seeketh knowledge."

This is a verse about posture. About orientation. The prudent person — the wise person — is someone whose heart is always open to learning. Their ear is always inclined toward truth. They are not stagnant. They are not unteachable. They are perpetual students of wisdom.

And I want to connect this to what we just talked about — the name of the Lord as a strong tower. Because here's the thing: you cannot run to a tower you don't know exists. And you cannot trust in a name you've never studied. The reason so many believers live in anxiety and fear is not that God has failed to be a refuge — it's that they've never taken the time to learn His name. They've never sat with the Scriptures long enough to know His character. They've never meditated on His attributes until those truths became the furniture of their minds.

Pink made this point powerfully. He argued that the root of most spiritual weakness is ignorance of God. Not intellectual ignorance — you can pass a theology exam and still not know God. But experiential ignorance. A failure to press into the knowledge of who He is. A failure to get that knowledge into the heart, not just the head.

And that's exactly what Proverbs 18:15 calls us to. Get knowledge. Seek it. Pursue it. Not knowledge for its own sake — not the cold, academic kind that puffs up — but the warm, living knowledge of the God who is your tower. Know His names. Know His promises. Know His character. So that when the storm comes — and it will come — you know exactly where to run.

This is why we read our Bibles, friends. This is why we pray. This is why we sit under faithful preaching and gather with the saints and meditate on the Word in the watches of the night. We are getting knowledge. We are fortifying our souls. We are learning the name of the Lord so that we can run to it and be safe.

---

[PART THREE — A FRIEND CLOSER THAN A BROTHER]

Now, I want to close tonight with the very last verse of Proverbs 18. And if you were with me yesterday when we talked about friendship in Proverbs 17, this is going to feel like the capstone of that whole conversation. Proverbs 18:24:

"A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly: and there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother."

Now, that first clause is practical enough — if you want friends, be friendly. Be warm. Be generous. Be the kind of person who draws others in rather than pushing them away. That's good counsel.

But it's the second clause that stops me in my tracks every single time: "there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother."

Who is this friend? Well, on one level, Solomon may be speaking generally — some friendships are deeper even than blood ties. And that's true. We've all known friendships like that. But the Christian cannot read this verse without seeing a greater fulfillment. Because there is one Friend — one — who sticks closer than any brother, any spouse, any companion this world can offer.

And His name is Jesus.

He is the Friend of sinners. That's what they called Him — His enemies meant it as an insult, and He wore it as a crown. He is the One who, when every other friend forsakes you, remains. When your sin makes you unlovable, He loves. When your failure makes you ashamed to draw near, He draws near to you. He sticks closer than a brother because He has made you His brother, His sister, by His own blood.

"Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends" — that's John 15:13. And Jesus did exactly that. He didn't just befriend you from a distance. He entered your suffering. He bore your sin. He endured your hell. He died your death. And then He rose — He rose — so that the friendship would never end. So that He could say to you, for all eternity, "I am with you. I will never leave you nor forsake you."

This is the ultimate strong tower. Not just the name of the Lord in the abstract — but the Lord Himself, in the person of Christ, who has made Himself your refuge. You run to Him. And He receives you. Every time. Without hesitation. Without conditions. Without limit.

Pink wrote that when we speak of the name of the Lord as a strong tower, we must understand that this tower was purchased for us at infinite cost. It is the cross of Christ that opened the door. It is the blood of Christ that secured our right to enter. And it is the risen, reigning Christ who stands within, welcoming every weary, sin-sick, storm-tossed soul who comes.

---

[CLOSE AND PRAYER]

Friend, I don't know what storm you're walking through tonight. I don't know what fear is pressing in. I don't know what you've been running to for safety — what false towers you've been trusting that are crumbling in your hands. But I know this: the name of the Lord is a strong tower. And the door is open. Run to Him.

He will not turn you away. He never has. He never will.

Let me pray for us.

O Lord our God, our tower, our fortress, our refuge and our strength — we come to You tonight not because we are worthy, but because You are willing. We come because Your name is strong and Your arms are open and Your promises are true. Forgive us for running to lesser towers. Forgive us for trusting in wealth, in reputation, in our own wisdom, in anything other than You. Teach us Your name, Lord. All Your names. Write them on our hearts so deeply that when trouble comes, we run to You by instinct, the way a child runs to a father. And thank You — oh, thank You — for the Friend who sticks closer than a brother. Thank You for Jesus, who laid down His life so that we might find our life in Him. Keep us in Your tower tonight. Keep us safe. Keep us Yours. In the precious and powerful name of Christ we pray. Amen.
