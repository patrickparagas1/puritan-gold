PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"I Will Praise Thy Name, O Lord, for It Is Good"
June 29, 2026

---

"I will praise thee, O Lord, among the heathen: I will sing unto thee among the nations. For thy mercy is great unto the heavens, and thy truth unto the clouds. Be thou exalted, O God, above the heavens: let thy glory be above all the earth. That thy beloved may be delivered; save with thy right hand, and hear me." -- Psalm 57:9-11 [heading: combined from Psalm 36:5-7]

The mercy of God is the great theme of this psalm, and the psalmist measures it by the only standard vast enough to contain it: the heavens themselves. "Thy mercy is great unto the heavens, and thy truth unto the clouds." When David lifts his eyes to the sky and traces the limitless expanse of the firmament stretching out above him in every direction, further than the eye can see, deeper than the mind can fathom, he finds in that immensity a picture of the mercy of God. As the heavens are higher than the earth, so are God's mercies higher than our sins. As the sky stretches from horizon to horizon without boundary or limit, so the mercy of God reaches to every corner of human need and covers every dimension of human guilt.

Spurgeon preached upon the mercy of God with a fullness and a warmth that made his hearers feel as though they were standing beneath a summer sky of infinite blue, with the sunlight of divine grace pouring down upon them from every direction. He taught that the mercy of God is not a narrow stream but a boundless ocean, not a flickering candle but the noonday sun, not a thin veneer applied to the surface of a harsh and unforgiving nature but the very essence of who God is. God is merciful not because He has decided to be merciful but because mercy is inherent in His character, as light is inherent in the sun and as heat is inherent in fire.

"And thy truth unto the clouds." The mercy of God is always accompanied by the truth of God, for these two attributes are not in conflict but in harmony. God's mercy does not compromise His truth, and His truth does not limit His mercy. Rather, they work together in perfect concert, as the two wings of a bird work together in flight. The truth of God means that His promises of mercy are absolutely reliable. When God says, "I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and their sins and their iniquities will I remember no more," that promise is as sure as the throne of heaven, because the God who made it is true.

Stephen Charnock demonstrated with meticulous theological reasoning that the truthfulness of God is the guarantee of all His other attributes. If God were not true, His mercy would be uncertain, His justice would be unreliable, His promises would be empty, and His threatenings would be hollow. But because God is true, utterly and absolutely true, incapable of falsehood and immune to deception, every word that He has spoken stands firm as the everlasting hills, and every promise that He has made will be fulfilled to the last syllable.

"I will praise thee, O Lord, among the heathen: I will sing unto thee among the nations." Here David resolves to carry his praise beyond the borders of Israel into the hearing of the Gentile nations. This is a remarkable declaration for an Old Testament saint, and it anticipates the worldwide mission of the gospel church. The mercy of God is not confined to one nation. The truth of God is not limited to one people. The glory of God is meant to fill the whole earth, and the praises of God are meant to be sung in every language and in every land.

John Flavel urged his congregation to be bold in their witness to the goodness of God, not hiding their faith behind the walls of the church but carrying it into the marketplace, the workshop, the field, and the public square. Every believer is a missionary, he taught. Every Christian is called to praise God among the heathen and to sing His praises among the nations. This does not mean that every Christian must travel to foreign lands, but it does mean that every Christian must live and speak in such a way that the mercy and truth of God are made visible to those who do not yet know Him.

Thomas Watson observed that the highest ambition a human being can have is not to make a name for himself but to exalt the name of God. "Be thou exalted, O God, above the heavens: let thy glory be above all the earth." This is the prayer that should govern every thought, every word, every action of the believer's life. Not my will but Thine. Not my glory but Thine. Not my kingdom but Thine. When this prayer becomes the ruling desire of the heart, every other desire falls into its proper place, and the soul finds a peace and a satisfaction that all the pleasures of the world cannot provide.

Let us then join our voices with David and declare the mercy and truth of God to all who will hear. Let us pray that God would be exalted above the heavens and that His glory would fill all the earth. For His mercy is great, reaching to the heavens, and His truth endures to all generations. Amen.
