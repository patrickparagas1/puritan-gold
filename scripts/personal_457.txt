PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Pastoral Encouragement, Day 26
"Pressing On to the Celestial City"
John Bunyan — Pastoral Counsel on Perseverance


Good evening. The Christian life is a marathon, not a sprint. And every marathon runner needs a voice along the way saying: keep going, you are not alone, the finish line is real. That is tonight's word for you.


Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Philippians 3:13-14:

"Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before, I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.


The Christian life is not a sprint but a marathon. It is not a moment of decision followed by a lifetime of coasting. It is a daily, sustained, lifelong pressing on toward the goal. The apostle Paul, writing from prison near the end of his life, confessed that he had not yet arrived. He was still pressing, still reaching, still running. If Paul needed to persevere, how much more do we?

John Bunyan, the tinker from Bedford who became one of the most influential Christian writers in history, knew more about perseverance than most. He spent twelve years in Bedford jail for the crime of preaching without a license. During those years, he wrote 'The Pilgrim's Progress,' the most widely read allegory in the English language. The entire book is about perseverance: Christian's journey from the City of Destruction to the Celestial City, through every conceivable obstacle, temptation, and danger.

Bunyan's genius is that he portrays the Christian life as it actually is: a journey filled with both glory and grief, both encouragement and exhaustion. Christian does not walk a straight, smooth path to heaven. He falls into the Slough of Despond. He is deceived by Mr. Worldly Wiseman. He is imprisoned in Doubting Castle by Giant Despair. He wanders into Bypath Meadow. He passes through the Valley of the Shadow of Death. At every turn, there are temptations to stop, to turn back, to give up.

And yet Christian presses on. Why? Because his eyes are fixed on the Celestial City. Because he carries the scroll that assures him of his acceptance there. Because at critical moments, companions come alongside him — Faithful, Hopeful, the Shepherds of the Delectable Mountains — to encourage him. And because the King of the Celestial City Himself has promised that all who persevere will be received.

Bunyan's allegory contains several critical lessons for perseverance. First, expect difficulty. Many believers are surprised by suffering, temptation, and spiritual warfare, as though these were abnormal features of the Christian life. But Bunyan portrays them as the normal terrain of the pilgrim's path. The man who expects an easy road will be disillusioned when it turns rough. The man who expects a hard road will be prepared for whatever comes.

Second, beware of shortcuts. Mr. Worldly Wiseman offers Christian a path that avoids the cross. Bypath Meadow looks easier than the rough highway. The City of Vanity Fair offers pleasures that seem harmless. But every shortcut leads away from the Celestial City. Bunyan warns that the most dangerous temptations are not the ones that look obviously sinful but the ones that look merely easier.

Third, the value of companions. Christian rarely travels alone. The Puritan understanding of the Christian life was profoundly communal. You need fellow pilgrims who will encourage you when you are discouraged, rebuke you when you stray, carry you when you are weak, and remind you of the truth when you are confused. The man who tries to make the journey alone is unnecessarily vulnerable to every attack of the enemy.

Fourth, remember the promises. When Christian is imprisoned in Doubting Castle, he discovers that he has had the key to his escape all along: the key called Promise. The promises of God — His promise of presence, provision, protection, and perseverance — are the key that unlocks every prison of doubt and despair. But they must be remembered and applied. The man who knows the promises but forgets to use them is like a man who carries a sword but never draws it.

Patrick, the road to the Celestial City is long, and you are not yet home. There will be valleys and mountains, battles and rest stops, dark nights and bright mornings. But the King has promised that all who endure to the end will be saved. Press on. Forget what is behind. Reach forward to what lies ahead. The prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus is worth every step of the journey.


Let these questions search your heart. Answer honestly before God.

Question 1: Am I in a season where I am tempted to give up, coast, or take a shortcut in my spiritual walk? What is drawing me away from the path?

Question 2: Do I have faithful companions on the pilgrimage — brothers who will encourage, rebuke, and support me on the journey?

Question 3: What specific promises of God do I need to 'draw the key of promise' from my pocket and apply to my current circumstance?

Do not rush past these questions. Sit with them. Journal about them if you can. The Puritans believed that truth must be digested, not merely tasted.


Lord Jesus, Author and Finisher of my faith, I confess that there are days when the road seems long and my strength seems small. But I press on toward the mark for the prize of Your high calling. Give me endurance for the journey, companions for the road, and eyes fixed firmly on the Celestial City. When I am tempted to take shortcuts, redirect my steps. When I am locked in Doubting Castle, remind me of the key of promise. I will not turn back. Bring me safely home. Amen.

Goodnight. Remember: the same God who spoke to the Puritans speaks to you.
