PURITAN GOLD -- Study Devotional, Day 4
Series: Romans
"Circumcision of the Heart"
True religion is inward transformation, not outward ceremony

---

Welcome to our study devotional. The Apostle Paul told Timothy to study to show himself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed. That is our aim tonight: to study with diligence and devotion.

We are studying the book of Romans together. Tonight's lesson is titled "Circumcision of the Heart." Each session builds on what came before, so if you have been following along, you will see how tonight's passage connects to the larger argument of the book.

--- Scripture Reading ---

Let us read our passage for tonight. It comes from Romans 2:17-29:

"Behold, thou art called a Jew, and restest in the law, and makest thy boast of God, And knowest his will, and approvest the things that are more excellent, being instructed out of the law; And art confident that thou thyself art a guide of the blind, a light of them which are in darkness, An instructor of the foolish, a teacher of babes, which hast the form of knowledge and of the truth in the law. Thou therefore which teachest another, teachest thou not thyself?... For circumcision verily profiteth, if thou keep the law: but if thou be a breaker of the law, thy circumcision is made uncircumcision... For he is not a Jew, which is one outwardly; neither is that circumcision, which is outward in the flesh: But he is a Jew, which is one inwardly; and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter; whose praise is not of men, but of God."

Read that passage again to yourself. Mark any words or phrases that stand out. The Puritans read with a pen in hand, always ready to note what God was showing them.

--- Key Verse ---

Let us zoom in on a verse that is central to everything we will study tonight.

"But he is a Jew, which is one inwardly; and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter; whose praise is not of men, but of God." -- Romans 2:29

This verse is the lens through which we will examine the rest of tonight's passage. Keep it in the front of your mind as we proceed.

--- Exposition ---

Now we turn to the substance of tonight's study.

Thomas Goodwin offers valuable commentary on this passage. The Puritans were expositors of the highest order, and their insights remain remarkably fresh.

Thomas Goodwin, one of the greatest of the Puritan theologians and a member of the Westminster Assembly, wrote extensively on the work of the Holy Spirit in the heart. His reflections on the inward nature of true religion illuminate Romans 2:17-29 with particular force. Goodwin insisted that the new birth is not a reformation of manners but a transformation of nature. External religious observance — whether circumcision in Paul's day or baptism, church membership, and communion in ours — cannot substitute for the internal work of the Spirit.

Goodwin was especially penetrating on the danger of what he called 'resting in ordinances.' He observed that many professing Christians treat the means of grace as though they were grace itself. They attend church as though attendance saved them. They take communion as though the bread and wine were magical. They read their Bibles as though the physical act of reading, apart from faith and obedience, earned merit before God. This is precisely the error Paul confronts in the Jew who 'restest in the law' — placing confidence in the possession of truth rather than in the God of truth.

Goodwin gave detailed attention to what Paul means by circumcision 'of the heart, in the spirit.' Drawing from Deuteronomy 30:6, where God promises to 'circumcise thine heart, and the heart of thy seed, to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart,' Goodwin argued that this is nothing less than regeneration — the sovereign act of God whereby He removes the stony heart and gives a heart of flesh (Ezekiel 36:26). This is a work that no human effort can produce. Just as physical circumcision was performed on a passive infant, so spiritual circumcision is performed by God on a spiritually helpless sinner. The result is a heart that loves God, delights in His law, and seeks His glory rather than the praise of men.

In Romans 2:17-29, Paul brings his argument to bear directly and explicitly upon the Jewish people, who are the most religious segment of his audience. Having shown in chapter 1 that the Gentile world stands condemned, and in 2:1-16 that the moralist who judges others condemns himself, Paul now confronts the one who has the greatest religious advantages of all — the Jew who possesses the law, the covenants, and the sign of circumcision.

Verses 17-20 lay out the impressive spiritual resume of the Jewish person. They bear the honored name 'Jew.' They rest in the law — they lean upon it as the foundation of their identity. They boast in God — they claim a special relationship with the Almighty. They know His will. They approve the things that are excellent, having been instructed out of the law. They are confident that they are 'a guide of the blind, a light of them which are in darkness, an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of babes.' They have 'the form of knowledge and of the truth in the law.'

All of these things are true. The Jews did possess genuine advantages. Paul will affirm this in Romans 3:1-2 and again in 9:4-5. God did choose Israel, give them His law, speak through their prophets, and entrust them with His oracles. But possession of truth is not the same as transformation by truth. Having the law is not the same as keeping the law.

In verses 21-24, Paul fires four devastating questions. 'Thou therefore which teachest another, teachest thou not thyself? thou that preachest a man should not steal, dost thou steal? Thou that sayest a man should not commit adultery, dost thou commit adultery? thou that abhorrest idols, dost thou commit sacrilege?' These are not hypothetical charges. The Old Testament record is full of Israel's failures to live up to the law they professed. And the prophets thundered against this hypocrisy. Isaiah declared, 'This people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me' (Isaiah 29:13). Jesus applied these very words to the Pharisees of His day (Matthew 15:8).

The result of this hypocrisy is stated in verse 24, quoting Isaiah 52:5 and Ezekiel 36:22: 'For the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you.' This is one of the most sobering statements in Scripture. When those who profess to know God live in a manner that contradicts their profession, they cause the world to mock God Himself. The unbeliever says, 'If that is what God's people look like, I want nothing to do with their God.' The damage done to the name of Christ by hypocritical Christians is incalculable.

In verses 25-27, Paul addresses the specific issue of circumcision, the sign of God's covenant with Abraham (Genesis 17). Circumcision was the badge of Jewish identity, the physical mark that distinguished Israel from the nations. Many Jews believed that circumcision itself guaranteed a place in the world to come. But Paul demolishes this notion: 'For circumcision verily profiteth, if thou keep the law: but if thou be a breaker of the law, thy circumcision is made uncircumcision.' The external sign is meaningless without the internal reality it was meant to signify.

Conversely, 'if the uncircumcision keep the righteousness of the law, shall not his uncircumcision be counted for circumcision?' An uncircumcised Gentile who obeys God's law stands in a better position than a circumcised Jew who breaks it. The sign without the reality is nothing; the reality without the sign is everything.

This principle applies directly to Christian ordinances. Baptism does not save. Communion does not save. Church membership does not save. These are precious means of grace ordained by Christ, but they are signs and seals, not the thing signified. A baptized person who lives in unrepentant rebellion is no better off than an unbaptized pagan. As Peter said, baptism is 'not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God' (1 Peter 3:21).

Verses 28-29 deliver the climactic conclusion: 'For he is not a Jew, which is one outwardly; neither is that circumcision, which is outward in the flesh: But he is a Jew, which is one inwardly; and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter; whose praise is not of men, but of God.' This is a revolutionary declaration. True membership in God's covenant people is not determined by ethnicity, ceremony, or external religion but by the inward reality of a heart transformed by the Spirit of God.

Moses had called for this very thing: 'Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart, and be no more stiffnecked' (Deuteronomy 10:16). Jeremiah echoed it: 'Circumcise yourselves to the LORD, and take away the foreskins of your heart' (Jeremiah 4:4). And God Himself promised to do it: 'The LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart' (Deuteronomy 30:6). What the law commanded, only grace could accomplish. The new covenant promise of Ezekiel 36:26-27 is the fulfillment: 'A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes.'

Notice the final phrase: 'whose praise is not of men, but of God.' There is a wordplay here. The name 'Judah' (from which 'Jew' derives) means 'praise' (Genesis 29:35). The true Jew — the one whose heart is circumcised — receives praise not from human observers who see the outward religion, but from God who sees the heart. This is the only approval that ultimately matters.

--- Application ---

The Puritans asked three questions of every passage: What does it teach? What does it rebuke? What does it require? Let us ask those questions now.

This passage searches the heart of every religious person. Here are concrete applications.

First, audit your spiritual confidence. On what does your assurance of salvation rest? Is it on church membership, baptism, family heritage, theological knowledge, or regular attendance? Or is it on the inward work of the Holy Spirit who has given you a new heart that loves God, grieves over sin, and hungers for righteousness? External religion without internal transformation is worthless in God's sight. Take time this week to examine the foundations of your confidence before God.

Second, close the gap between your profession and your practice. Where are you teaching truths you are not living? Where are you telling others to do what you are not doing yourself? Hypocrisy is not just a Pharisaic problem; it is a human problem. Choose one area where your walk does not match your talk, and take a concrete step of obedience this week. If you preach generosity, give sacrificially. If you teach forgiveness, forgive that person you have been holding a grudge against.

Third, consider the impact of your life on God's reputation. Verse 24 warns that God's name is blasphemed because of hypocritical believers. Are there people in your life — coworkers, family members, neighbors — who might be turned away from Christ because of the inconsistency they see in you? This is a weighty responsibility. Ask a trusted friend: 'Does my life commend the gospel or contradict it?' Be willing to hear an honest answer.

Fourth, seek heart-circumcision through prayer. Since this is a work of God, not of human effort, plead with Him for it. Pray David's prayer: 'Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me' (Psalm 51:10). Ask the Holy Spirit to do the deep inner surgery that no external religion can accomplish.

Write down one concrete action you will take this week in response to tonight's study. Do not let this truth remain theoretical. The Puritans measured the success of a sermon not by how much the hearer learned but by how much the hearer changed.

--- Closing ---

Father, we confess that we are often more concerned with the outward appearance of religion than with the inward reality. We rest in our rituals, boast in our knowledge, and judge others while neglecting our own hearts. Circumcise our hearts by Your Spirit. Remove the stony hardness and give us hearts of flesh that love You, delight in Your law, and seek Your glory alone. May our praise come not from men who see the outside, but from You who see the heart. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Go in peace, student of the Word. God rewards those who diligently seek Him.
