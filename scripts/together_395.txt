PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 3
"That He Might Sanctify Her"
Ephesians 5:26-27 — Christ's Purifying Love


Welcome, beloved couple. We continue our journey through Ephesians 5, and tonight we encounter one of the most breathtaking descriptions of Christ's purpose in loving the Church — a purpose that has profound implications for your marriage.

Our Scripture tonight is Ephesians 5:26-27:

"That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word, That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish."

What extraordinary words. Christ loved the Church not merely to rescue her, but to beautify her. And this is the pattern for marriage.


The Puritans saw in these verses a vision of love that goes far beyond mere affection or duty. Christ's love is purposeful — it aims at the sanctification and glorification of His Bride. He does not love the Church because she is already beautiful; He loves her in order to make her beautiful. He does not wait until she is perfect to commit Himself to her; He commits Himself to her and then works to perfect her.

William Gouge saw in this a powerful lesson for husbands. A husband's love, like Christ's, should aim at his wife's spiritual good. This does not mean that a husband becomes his wife's pastor or her moral policeman. The Puritans were clear that husbands should never use this text as a weapon — never say, "I am sanctifying you" as an excuse for criticism or control. Rather, a husband sanctifies his wife by creating an environment of grace, safety, and spiritual nourishment in the home.

How does a husband create such an environment? The Puritans offered practical counsel. He leads family worship — not as a performance, but as a genuine sharing of God's Word. He prays for his wife and with his wife — consistently, fervently, specifically. He speaks words of life over her — affirming her gifts, acknowledging her sacrifices, celebrating her growth. He protects her from unnecessary burdens and shields her from spiritual dangers. He models repentance when he sins, showing her that the home is a safe place to be honest and vulnerable.

Thomas Gataker observed that the phrase "washing of water by the word" points to the cleansing, nourishing power of Scripture in the marriage. When a couple reads the Bible together — as you are doing right now — they are participating in the very process Paul describes. The Word of God washes away the grime of the world's false narratives about marriage, about gender, about love. It replaces lies with truth, bitterness with grace, selfishness with service.

But there is something even deeper here. Christ's goal is to present the Church "to himself" — glorious, spotless, radiant. This is not a distant future hope; it is an ongoing process. Every day, Christ is at work making His Bride more beautiful. And in marriage, every day is an opportunity for husband and wife to contribute to each other's spiritual beauty.

Wife, you too play a vital role in your husband's sanctification. The Puritans recognized that a godly wife is one of God's chief instruments for shaping a man's character. Your prayers, your example, your gentle words of truth — these are means of grace in your husband's life. When you call him to his best self, not with nagging but with love, you are participating in God's sanctifying work.

Together, you are called to be agents of each other's holiness. Not critics. Not judges. But partners in the sacred work of becoming more like Christ. This is the highest calling of marriage — not merely to make each other happy, but to make each other holy.


Consider these questions together:

Question 1: In what ways has your marriage made you more like Christ? Can you name specific areas of growth that your spouse has helped bring about?

Question 2: Is there an area of spiritual growth you would like to pursue together this summer? A book to read, a practice to begin, a habit to cultivate?

Question 3: How can you create a home environment that feels spiritually safe — where both of you can be honest about struggles without fear of judgment?


Pray tonight that your marriage would be a sanctifying relationship — one that makes both of you more holy, more loving, more Christlike. Ask God to show you how to speak words of life over each other and to create a home where the Word of God is treasured and lived.


May Christ, who loved the Church and gave Himself for her, pour His sanctifying grace into your marriage. May your home be a place of spiritual growth, mutual encouragement, and increasing beauty. The Lord bless you and keep you both. Goodnight.
