PURITAN GOLD -- Institutes of the Christian Religion, Book I: Complete Summary A Comprehensive Overview of Calvin's Teaching on the Knowledge of God the Creator Institutes of the Christian Religion, Book One: Of the Knowledge of God the Creator. By John Calvin. Translated by John Allen. A Complete Summary. Welcome to this summary of Book One of John Calvin's Institutes of the Christian Religion. First published in 1536 and expanded through five editions until 1559, this is the greatest systematic theology of the Protestant Reformation. Book One contains eighteen chapters treating the knowledge of God as Creator. THE AUTHOR AND HIS PURPOSE. John Calvin was born in France in 1509 and became the leading theologian of the Reformed tradition. He wrote the Institutes as a comprehensive introduction to Christian doctrine. Book One lays the foundation for everything that follows by establishing how we come to know God. CHAPTERS ONE THROUGH FOUR: THE KNOWLEDGE OF GOD AND OURSELVES. Calvin opens with the famous declaration that true wisdom consists of two parts: the knowledge of God and the knowledge of ourselves. These are so intimately connected that it is impossible to have one without the other. He shows that every human mind is naturally endued with a sense of the divine, but that this knowledge is corrupted by ignorance and wickedness. Fallen humanity suppresses the truth about God. CHAPTERS FIVE THROUGH NINE: THE SCRIPTURE AS THE SOURCE OF TRUE KNOWLEDGE. Though God's existence is evident from creation, Calvin argues that fallen human beings need the guidance and teaching of Scripture to arrive at saving knowledge. He establishes the authority of the Bible on the testimony of the Holy Spirit, not merely on the authority of the church. He then provides rational proofs to support the Scripture's divine origin, while warning against fanatical claims to private revelation that bypass the written Word. CHAPTERS TEN THROUGH TWELVE: THE NATURE OF TRUE WORSHIP. Calvin next addresses the proper worship of God. All idolatrous worship — including the use of images in churches — is contrary to Scripture. God alone must be worshiped, and He has strictly forbidden the making of any visible representation of His nature. Calvin traces the history of image worship in the church and shows it to be a departure from apostolic practice. CHAPTER THIRTEEN: THE TRINITY. This is the longest and most theologically dense chapter in Book One. Calvin demonstrates from Scripture that one divine essence contains three distinct Persons — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — and refutes the ancient and modern heresies that deny this doctrine. CHAPTERS FOURTEEN AND FIFTEEN: CREATION AND HUMAN NATURE. Calvin discusses the creation of the world, the nature of angels and demons, and the original state of humanity. He shows that the human soul possesses understanding and will, and that Adam was created in true righteousness and holiness. CHAPTERS SIXTEEN THROUGH EIGHTEEN: DIVINE PROVIDENCE.
