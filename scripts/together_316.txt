PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 16
"Committing Your Plans to the Lord"
Proverbs 16:3


Hello, beloved couple. It is a beautiful thing when husband and wife sit down together in God's Word. The Puritans called marriage a little church within the church. Tonight, your little church is in session. Welcome.

Tonight we turn to Proverbs 16:3. Listen with your hearts, not just your ears:

"Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established."

That is Proverbs 16:3. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Matthew Henry had deep insight into the truths we have just read. The Puritans did not separate theology from daily life, and their reflections on marriage are remarkably practical.

Matthew Henry finds in this verse a precious promise wedded to a simple duty. To 'commit thy works unto the LORD' is, in the Hebrew, to 'roll' them upon Him, as one would roll a burden from one's own shoulders onto those of one who is stronger. Henry writes: 'Cast the burden of thy care upon God, by faith and prayer. Let him manage all thy affairs for thee, and do not go about them in thine own strength.'

The result of this commitment is remarkable: 'thy thoughts shall be established.' Henry explains that when we entrust our plans to God, He brings order to our disordered minds. Anxiety gives way to peace. Confusion gives way to clarity. Indecision gives way to settled purpose. This is not because our plans always turn out as we expected, but because we have placed them in the hands of One whose purposes cannot be frustrated.

Charles Bridges draws out the comprehensive nature of the command: 'Commit thy works — not some, but all; not the great matters only, but the small.' Every plan the couple makes — from career decisions to vacation choices, from financial strategies to daily schedules — is to be rolled onto the Lord. Bridges warns against a selective submission that commits the 'spiritual' matters to God while managing the 'practical' matters independently.

Henry adds that this commitment is not a one-time act but a daily posture. Each morning, as new plans and anxieties arise, they are to be rolled afresh onto the Lord. 'Those that commit their works to God shall have the satisfaction of seeing their thoughts established,' Henry writes, 'even if their plans are changed, for God's plans are better.'


As a couple, you make plans constantly: financial plans, family plans, career plans, ministry plans. The question is whether you make those plans and then ask God to bless them, or whether you commit them to God from the very beginning and allow Him to direct them.

Committing your works to the Lord as a couple means praying together before making significant decisions. It means holding your plans with open hands, willing to let God redirect them. It means trusting that when plans fall apart, God is not absent but active, working all things according to the counsel of His will.

The promise is that your thoughts shall be established. This does not mean every plan will succeed as you envisioned, but that your minds will be settled and secure because your confidence is in God's sovereignty, not in the success of your schemes.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


It is time for reflection. Turn to each other and consider these questions together. There is no rush. Let the conversation go wherever the Spirit leads.

Question 1: Are we in the habit of committing our plans to God together, or do we tend to plan independently and then pray?

Question 2: Is there a specific plan or decision we need to roll onto the Lord right now?

Question 3: How does committing our works to God change the way we respond when our plans don't work out?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Now let us pray together. The Puritans said that a couple who prays together is bound by cords that cannot be broken. Let us strengthen those cords tonight.

Tonight's prayer focus: Take whatever plans, anxieties, and decisions are currently weighing on you and roll them together onto the Lord. Ask Him to establish your thoughts, give you clarity, and direct your steps according to His perfect will.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the Lord upon whom you cast your burdens establish your thoughts and direct your plans, granting you the peace that comes from trusting His sovereign care. Amen.

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you both, tonight and always. Goodnight.
