PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 27
"Heaven Is Our Home"
Pilgrim Families — Week 4, Day 6


Good evening, family. There is no place God loves to meet His people more than when families gather in His name. Welcome to Puritan Gold. Let us open our Bibles and our hearts.

Let us begin in prayer. Lord Jesus, You said that where two or three are gathered in Your name, there You are in the midst of them. We claim that promise tonight as a family. Be here with us. Teach us. Shape us. We pray in Your holy name. Amen.

Every word of Scripture is breathed out by God. Tonight we receive His breath from Philippians 3:20; John 14:1-3:

"For our conversation is in heaven; from whence also we look for the Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ. Let not your heart be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me. In my Father's house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also."

That is Philippians 3:20; John 14:1-3. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Philippians 3:20 declares a truth that should shape every day of our lives: "Our conversation is in heaven." The word "conversation" in the King James Version means citizenship. Our citizenship is in heaven. This earth is not our permanent home. We are foreigners, ambassadors, pilgrims passing through on our way to our true country.

Jesus Himself speaks the most comforting words ever uttered in John 14:1-3: "Let not your heart be troubled." He says this on the night before His crucifixion, when His disciples are about to face the worst night of their lives. And His comfort is not about this world getting better — it is about another world being prepared. "In my Father's house are many mansions... I go to prepare a place for you."

Jesus is in heaven right now preparing a place for His people. Not a generic place. A place for you. A specific, personal dwelling prepared by the Son of God Himself for each believer. And His promise is absolute: "I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also." The goal of heaven is not primarily streets of gold or pearly gates — it is being with Jesus. "Where I am, there ye may be also." That is heaven's greatest treasure: the unhindered, face-to-face, eternal presence of Christ.

Rutherford longed for heaven with an intensity that is almost painful to read. He wrote of wanting to see Christ's face, of yearning to be done with sin and sorrow, of looking forward to the day when every tear would be wiped away. Yet his longing was not escapism — it was hope. He served Christ faithfully in this world precisely because he knew a better world was coming.

The Puritans believed that thinking about heaven made them better Christians on earth, not worse. When you know your citizenship is in heaven, you hold this world's treasures more loosely. When you know you are going home to Jesus, you face death without terror. When you know that your loved ones who have died in Christ are already there, you grieve with hope rather than despair.

For families, talking about heaven is one of the most important things you can do. Children naturally fear death and loss. They need to know that for those who trust in Jesus, death is not the end — it is the beginning. It is not a wall — it is a door. It leads not to darkness but to light, not to separation but to reunion, not to sorrow but to joy.

Heaven is also the answer to every injustice on earth. There are wrongs that will not be made right in this life. There are losses that will not be restored in this world. There are sorrows that will not be comforted on this side of eternity. But heaven will make all things right. Revelation 21:4 promises: "God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away."

Tonight, talk about heaven with your family. Not in abstract theological terms, but in personal, real terms. Who will be there? What will it be like to see Jesus face to face? What are you most looking forward to? These conversations build faith, strengthen hope, and produce the kind of eternal perspective that carries families through the hardest seasons of life.

Rutherford wrote near the end of his life: "Glory, glory dwelleth in Immanuel's land!" He died with these words on his lips, full of hope, full of joy, full of longing for home. And that home is prepared for you and your family too.


One of the great blessings of the Christian tradition is that we stand on the shoulders of faithful saints who came before us.

Samuel Rutherford, in his work "Letters of Samuel Rutherford," wrote these words:

"O, if we could but see the face of our Elder Brother! I know that He shall be my Husband, my Brother, my King, and my all! O sweet, sweet would be the day of my dissolution!"

What Samuel Rutherford is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Philippians 3:20; John 14:1-3 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Now we get to the best part: talking about God's Word together as a family. Here are tonight's questions.

Question 1: What does it mean that our citizenship is in heaven? How should that change the way we live on earth?

Question 2: Jesus said He is preparing a place for us. What do you imagine that place will be like?

Question 3: What is the greatest thing about heaven — the place, the people, or the presence of Jesus?

Question 4: How does thinking about heaven help us face the hard things in this life?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"In my Father's house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you." -- John 14:2

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a practical way to live out tonight's devotional:

Have each family member draw or describe their picture of heaven — not based on imagination alone, but based on what Scripture actually says. Look up Revelation 21-22 together and note every detail. Discuss what excites each person most about our heavenly home.


Gracious God, thank You for meeting with our family tonight. Your Word never returns void, and we trust that what we have heard will bear fruit in each of our lives. Strengthen our family bonds, deepen our faith, and make us a light in this world. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

May the peace of God guard your hearts and minds tonight. Goodnight, family.
