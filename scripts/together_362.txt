PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 1
"The Song of All Songs"
Song of Solomon 1:1-4 — The Beloved's Opening Declaration


Welcome, dear couple. What a privilege it is to begin a new month together in God's Word. This month we enter the most intimate book of the Bible — the Song of Solomon. The Puritans were not shy about this book. They saw in it a celebration of marital love that honored God and glorified the covenant bond between husband and wife. Thomas Gataker called marriage "the sweetest of all earthly relations." Tonight, let us taste that sweetness together.

Let us read our passage. Song of Solomon 1:1-4:

"The song of songs, which is Solomon's. Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth: for thy love is better than wine. Because of the savour of thy good ointments thy name is as ointment poured forth, therefore do the virgins love thee. Draw me, we will run after thee: the king hath brought me into his chambers: we will be glad and rejoice in thee, we will remember thy love more than wine: the upright love thee."


The very first words tell us something remarkable. This is not merely a song. It is the Song of Songs — the superlative, the greatest of all songs. Just as the Holy of Holies is the most sacred place, and the King of Kings is the supreme ruler, so the Song of Songs is the highest, most excellent song. And what is this supreme song about? It is about love — the love between a bridegroom and his bride.

The Puritans understood this on two levels. First, they saw here a portrait of Christ's love for His church — the mystical marriage between the Redeemer and the redeemed. But they also saw, without apology, a celebration of the love between husband and wife in the covenant of marriage. Richard Sibbes wrote that God gave this book so that married couples would know that their love, rightly ordered, is a holy thing — a reflection of heaven's own affections.

Notice how the song begins. The bride speaks first: "Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth." There is no embarrassment here. There is no false modesty. The bride desires her beloved and she says so. The Puritans, contrary to popular caricature, celebrated this aspect of marriage. William Gouge, in his massive treatise on domestic duties, wrote that the mutual delight of husband and wife is not merely permitted but commanded — it is part of the duty and privilege of the marriage covenant.

"For thy love is better than wine." Wine gladdens the heart, but the love of the beloved gladdens it more. This is a powerful statement about the priority of relational intimacy over all other earthly pleasures. Many couples chase after the wine of career success, material comfort, social standing, or entertainment. But the Song tells us that none of these compare to the simple, profound joy of loving and being loved by your spouse.

"Thy name is as ointment poured forth." In the ancient world, a person's name represented their character, their reputation, their very essence. To say that the beloved's name is like poured-out ointment means that everything about him — his character, his presence, his way of being — fills the room with fragrance. What a thing to say about a husband! And what a challenge it is to every husband hearing this: does your character fill your home with fragrance? Does your presence bring gladness or anxiety? Does your name, your reputation in the small kingdom of your household, smell like ointment poured forth?

"Draw me, we will run after thee." Here is the beautiful dynamic of pursuit and response that marks every healthy marriage. The bride asks to be drawn — she wants to be pursued, invited, sought after. And when she is drawn, she does not walk reluctantly. She runs. This is the picture of a marriage alive with mutual desire: one pursues, the other responds with eagerness, and together they run toward deeper intimacy.

"The king hath brought me into his chambers." Intimacy is reached. The chambers represent the private, sacred space of marital love — the place where only the two of them belong. The Puritans fiercely guarded this private dimension of marriage. Thomas Gataker wrote that the marriage bed is a sanctuary, a place where husband and wife minister to one another in ways that the rest of the world never sees and never should. This privacy is not shameful; it is precious. It is the inner sanctum of your covenant.

"We will be glad and rejoice in thee." Notice the plural. The gladness is shared. The rejoicing is mutual. This is not one-sided pleasure but the deep, reciprocal delight that God intends for the marriage relationship.

As you begin this month in the Song of Solomon, let this opening passage remind you of something the world desperately needs to hear: marital love is a gift from God. It is designed to be celebrated, cultivated, and protected. The Puritans knew this. They wrote more about the joys and duties of married love than almost any other group of Christians in history. Let their wisdom guide you this month as you rediscover the song that God has written over your marriage.


It is time for reflection. Turn to each other and consider these questions together. There is no rush. Let the conversation go wherever the Spirit leads.

Question 1: When you think about the bride's declaration that her beloved's love is "better than wine," what does that stir in your own heart? Is there anything in your life that you have allowed to become more satisfying than the love you share with your spouse?

Question 2: The bride says "Draw me, we will run after thee." What does healthy pursuit look like in your marriage right now? Who is pursuing whom, and how can you both become more intentional about drawing each other closer?

Question 3: How would you describe the "fragrance" of your marriage — the atmosphere of your home? If your name is like ointment poured forth, what scent does it leave in the rooms of your household?


Now let us pray together. Tonight's prayer focus: Thank God for the gift of marital love. Ask Him to renew the delight you have in one another — not the superficial delight of romance alone, but the deep, covenant delight that comes from knowing you belong to each other under God. Pray that this month in the Song of Solomon would rekindle whatever has grown cold and strengthen whatever is already burning brightly.


May the Lord who wrote this Song over every covenant marriage fill your hearts tonight with gladness and your home with the fragrance of love. Go in peace, beloved couple. Goodnight.
