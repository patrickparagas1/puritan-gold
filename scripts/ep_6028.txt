PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"The Fool Hath Said in His Heart, There Is No God"
June 28, 2026

---

"The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God. Corrupt are they, and have done abominable iniquity: there is none that doeth good. God looked down from heaven upon the children of men, to see if there were any that did understand, that did seek God. Every one of them is gone back: they are altogether become filthy; there is none that doeth good, no, not one. Have the workers of iniquity no knowledge? who eat up my people as they eat bread: they have not called upon God." -- Psalm 53:1-4

This psalm echoes the fourteenth psalm with deliberate and solemn repetition, as though the Holy Spirit wished to drive home this dreadful truth a second time, lest any man imagine that the first declaration was exaggerated or exceptional. The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God. The repetition is not redundant but emphatic. It is the voice of a physician who repeats his diagnosis because the patient refuses to believe him the first time. It is the voice of a watchman who sounds the alarm again because the city has not awakened to its danger.

Spurgeon remarked that if there is one truth that mankind is most reluctant to receive, it is the truth of total depravity. Men will acknowledge that they are imperfect. They will concede that they occasionally make mistakes. They will even admit, under sufficient pressure, that they sometimes do things they know to be wrong. But tell them that there is none that doeth good, no not one, and they will resist with every fibre of their being. For this truth strikes at the root of human pride, and human pride is the last stronghold that fallen man will surrender.

"God looked down from heaven upon the children of men, to see if there were any that did understand, that did seek God." The picture is of a divine investigation, a search conducted from the vantage point of omniscience. God does not merely glance at the surface of human behaviour. He looks down from heaven -- that is, He examines the whole race from the perspective of infinite knowledge and perfect righteousness. And His verdict is comprehensive and devastating: "Every one of them is gone back." Not most of them. Not ninety-nine percent. Every one. The whole race, from the highest to the lowest, from the most civilised to the most barbarous, from the most moral to the most debased, has turned away from God and has plunged into the darkness of sin.

Thomas Goodwin, in his exposition of the epistle to the Ephesians, used this psalm to illustrate the condition of mankind before the gospel is applied by the Holy Spirit. He described the human race as a vast cemetery, full of dead souls lying in their graves, unable to see, unable to hear, unable to move, unable to respond to any stimulus whatsoever. And into this cemetery comes the voice of God in the gospel, and wherever that voice is accompanied by the power of the Spirit, the dead rise. Eyes that were blind are opened. Ears that were deaf begin to hear. Hearts that were stone become flesh. And men who were dead in trespasses and sins are made alive together with Christ. This is the miracle of regeneration, and apart from it, every son of Adam remains in the grave of his natural corruption, doing no good, seeking no God, understanding nothing of the truth.

"Have the workers of iniquity no knowledge? who eat up my people as they eat bread." Here is a question of astonished indignation. Do the wicked not know what they are doing? Do they not realize that the people they are devouring are the people of God? Do they not understand that every act of cruelty against the saints is an act of aggression against the Almighty Himself? The answer, of course, is that they do not know, because the same corruption that blinds them to the existence of God also blinds them to the dignity of His people. They eat up God's people as they eat bread -- thoughtlessly, casually, without a moment's reflection upon the horror of what they are doing.

Richard Sibbes taught that the persecution of the saints has always been one of the great mysteries of divine providence. Why does God allow His people to be devoured by the wicked? Why does He not intervene immediately to protect those He loves? The answer lies in the larger purposes of God, which are often hidden from our eyes but are always working toward the ultimate good of His church and the ultimate glory of His name. The blood of the martyrs, as Tertullian famously declared, is the seed of the church. And the apparent triumph of the wicked over the righteous is always and everywhere reversed in the final account.

John Owen reminded the church that the psalm ends not with the triumph of the fool but with the salvation of God's people. "Oh that the salvation of Israel were come out of Zion! When God bringeth back the captivity of his people, Jacob shall rejoice, and Israel shall be glad." The last word belongs not to the fool who denies God, nor to the workers of iniquity who devour His people, but to the God who saves, who redeems, who brings back the captivity, and who fills the hearts of His people with rejoicing. Let us hold fast to this hope, for the salvation of Israel has indeed come out of Zion, in the person of Jesus Christ, and all who trust in Him shall rejoice with joy unspeakable and full of glory. Amen.
