PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 10
"Building Each Other Up"
Ephesians 4:29 — Communication and Holy Living

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome back to our time together in the Word. Tonight we come to a verse that should be framed and hung on the wall of every married couple's bedroom. It is a verse about the power of words, and in marriage, words have more power than almost anywhere else.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 4:29 (KJV):

"Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers."

--- Teaching ---

Paul gives us both a prohibition and a prescription. The prohibition: let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth. The word "corrupt" in the original language means rotten, as in rotten fruit. It is speech that tears down, that poisons, that spreads decay. In marriage, corrupt communication includes sarcasm intended to wound, criticism that shames rather than corrects, gossip about your spouse to others, and words spoken in contempt or disgust. It includes the silent treatment used as a weapon, and it includes the offhand remarks that seem small but accumulate like drops of acid on the surface of the soul.

The prescription is breathtaking in its beauty: speak that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers. Your words are meant to build up, not tear down. They are meant to minister grace, to be a channel of God's kindness flowing through your lips into the heart of your spouse. When you speak to your husband or wife, you are either constructing something beautiful or demolishing something precious. There is no neutral ground.

William Gouge noted that the tongue is the thermometer of the soul. What comes out of the mouth reveals what is in the heart. A husband whose speech is consistently harsh and critical has a heart problem, not merely a speech problem. A wife whose words are perpetually sharp and complaining is revealing the condition of her inner life. Gouge counseled couples to examine their words as a physician examines symptoms, looking beneath the surface to find the disease that produces them.

But Gouge also emphasized the constructive power of godly speech. The husband who speaks words of encouragement, affirmation, and gratitude to his wife is building something magnificent. He is constructing within her a sense of security, value, and belonging that nothing else can provide. The wife who speaks words of respect, admiration, and appreciation to her husband is giving him a gift more valuable than any material possession. Words of grace, spoken consistently, create a marriage that can withstand any storm.

Richard Baxter offered a simple test for married couples. Before you speak, ask yourself three questions. First, is it true? Do not exaggerate, distort, or misrepresent. Second, is it necessary? Not everything that is true needs to be said, and certainly not everything needs to be said right now. Third, is it kind? Can I say this in a way that builds up rather than tears down? If your words pass all three tests, speak freely. If they fail any one of them, hold your tongue and seek a better way.

The Puritans understood that words are not merely sounds that vanish into the air. Words are seeds. They take root. They grow. The words you speak to your spouse tonight will bear fruit for years to come. The encouraging word spoken at the right moment will be remembered long after you have forgotten it. The harsh word spoken in anger will leave a scar that takes years to heal. Choose your words with the care of a gardener choosing seeds.

Dear couple, commit tonight to a discipline of edifying speech. Look for reasons to praise each other. Notice the good. Comment on the beautiful. Express gratitude for the small things. And when correction is needed, let it come wrapped in so much love and grace that it feels like a gift rather than an assault.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: What is the most encouraging thing my spouse has said to me recently? Share that with each other now.

Question 2: Are there patterns of corrupt communication in our marriage that we need to repent of and replace?

Question 3: How can we build a daily habit of speaking words that minister grace to each other?

--- Prayer ---

O God, who spoke the world into being with Thy Word, teach us to use our words wisely in this marriage. Cleanse our lips of every corrupt and rotten word. Fill our mouths with speech that edifies, that builds up, that ministers grace. Let our home be a place where kind words are as common as breath and where harsh words are as rare as they are unwelcome. May the words of our mouths and the meditations of our hearts be acceptable in Thy sight, O Lord, our strength and our redeemer. Amen.
