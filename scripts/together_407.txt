PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 15
"The Husband's Duty of Love"
William Gouge — Domestical Duties, Chapter 1


Welcome, dear couple. We begin our third week by turning to one of the greatest Puritan works ever written on marriage: William Gouge's "Of Domestical Duties." This massive volume, first published in 1622, was the fruit of decades of pastoral ministry and is filled with wisdom that speaks directly to our marriages today.

Our Scripture tonight is Colossians 3:19:

"Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter against them."

Simple, direct, and devastating in its implications.


William Gouge opens his treatment of the husband's duties with a striking claim: the husband's primary calling in marriage is not to lead, not to provide, not to protect — though all of these are included. The husband's primary calling is to love. Everything else flows from love. Leadership without love is tyranny. Provision without love is mere duty. Protection without love is control.

Gouge identifies several specific expressions of this love. First, the love of the heart — genuine, inward affection for his wife. A husband must not merely act lovingly; he must feel love. Gouge warns against the husband who performs his duties mechanically while his heart is cold or distant. God sees the heart, and the wife often discerns it.

Second, the love of the tongue — kind, encouraging, affectionate speech. Gouge devotes considerable space to the way husbands speak to their wives, because he observed that many husbands who would never physically harm their wives wound them regularly with harsh, dismissive, or contemptuous words. He counsels husbands to speak to their wives with the same gentleness they would use with a dear friend — and more so, because a wife is closer than any friend.

Third, the love of the hand — tangible acts of service and affection. Gouge recognized that love must be embodied, not merely spoken. A husband who says "I love you" but never lifts a finger to help with the burdens of the household is speaking empty words. Love serves. Love works. Love shows up in practical, visible ways.

Gouge then turns to the negative command in Colossians 3:19: "Be not bitter against them." He observes that bitterness in a husband often manifests as chronic irritability — a short temper, a critical spirit, a tendency to find fault. This bitterness may have nothing to do with the wife herself; it may arise from work stress, personal insecurity, or spiritual dryness. But wherever it comes from, it poisons the marriage.

Gouge's remedy is twofold. First, the husband must examine his own heart before God, identifying the true source of his bitterness and bringing it to the cross. Second, the husband must make a deliberate choice to treat his wife with sweetness rather than sourness — not because he always feels like it, but because he is called to it by Christ.

Richard Baxter added that a husband's love should be constant, not conditional. "Do not love your wife only when she is agreeable," Baxter counseled. "Love her when she is difficult. Love her when she is weary. Love her when she has failed you. For this is how Christ loves you — not because you deserve it, but because He has chosen to."


Discuss together:

Question 1: Husband, which of Gouge's three expressions of love — heart, tongue, or hand — is your strongest? Which needs the most growth?

Question 2: Wife, how do you most clearly experience your husband's love? What makes you feel most cherished? Share this gift of knowledge with him.

Question 3: Is there any bitterness or chronic irritability that has crept into the husband's disposition? Can you identify its source and bring it to God together?


Pray for the husband tonight. Ask God to fill his heart with genuine affection, to season his speech with kindness, and to energize his hands for service. Pray against bitterness in all its forms, and ask the Lord to replace it with the sweetness of Christlike love.


May the Lord who commands husbands to love also supply the love they need. May every husband who hears this be transformed by the grace of Christ into a man of tenderness, constancy, and sacrificial devotion. Peace be with you both. Goodnight.
