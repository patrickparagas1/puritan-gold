PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 27
"The Evil of Murmuring"
Jeremiah Burroughs — The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment for Families


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Burroughs addresses the dark opposite of contentment — murmuring and complaining — and why this common family habit is far more serious than we often realize. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, convict us tonight of the sin of complaining. We confess that our mouths are quick to murmur and slow to give thanks. Change our hearts, and change our speech. Replace our complaints with praise. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Philippians 2:14-15 and Numbers 14:27. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Do all things without murmurings and disputings: That ye may be blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom ye shine as lights in the world. How long shall I bear with this evil congregation, which murmur against me? I have heard the murmurings of the children of Israel, which they murmur against me."

That is Philippians 2:14-15 and Numbers 14:27. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Jeremiah Burroughs devoted the second half of The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment to exposing what he called "the evil of murmuring." This is the opposite of contentment — the chronic, corrosive habit of complaining about our circumstances, questioning God's goodness, and expressing displeasure with the portion He has assigned us. Burroughs treated murmuring with great seriousness, and his reasons were deeply biblical.

The children of Israel were the original murmurers. God delivered them from slavery in Egypt with signs and wonders. He parted the Red Sea. He provided manna from heaven. He led them with a pillar of cloud by day and fire by night. And what was their response? They complained. They complained about the food. They complained about the water. They complained about the leadership. They complained about the journey. They longed for the leeks and garlic of Egypt, forgetting that Egypt was a house of bondage. And God declared their murmuring to be a grave evil.

Burroughs drew several observations from this biblical pattern that are remarkably relevant to family life. First, murmuring is ultimately directed at God, even when it seems to be directed at circumstances or people. When Israel murmured about the manna, they were really murmuring against God's provision. When we complain about our house, our income, our situation, we are implicitly saying, "God, You have not provided well enough." This is a serious charge against the wisdom and goodness of the Almighty.

Second, murmuring is contagious — especially within a family. When one person starts complaining, others quickly join in. A parent who habitually complains about work comes home and infects the entire household with a spirit of discontent. A child who complains about everything — the food, the chores, the rules — creates an atmosphere that drags everyone's spirits down. Burroughs observed that murmuring spreads through a household like a disease, and if it is not checked, it can poison the entire family culture.

Third, murmuring is ungrateful. It focuses exclusively on what is lacking while ignoring what has been given. A murmuring family can sit in a warm house, eating a good meal, surrounded by people who love them, and still find something to complain about. Burroughs would say that such ingratitude is an offense against the generosity of God, who has given us far more than we deserve.

Fourth, murmuring is useless. Burroughs made the practical observation that complaining has never improved anyone's circumstances. It does not put food on the table, pay the bills, or solve the problems. It only makes the burden heavier by adding the weight of bitterness and self-pity to whatever difficulty already exists.

But Burroughs did not merely condemn murmuring. He also provided the cure. The cure for murmuring is thanksgiving. When the urge to complain rises in your heart, deliberately choose to give thanks instead. When the words of complaint form on your lips, replace them with words of praise. This is not suppression or denial. It is a spiritual discipline — the deliberate redirection of the heart from what is lacking to what has been given, from the difficulty of the present to the faithfulness of God.

For families, this discipline can be practiced together. When someone starts to complain, another family member can gently redirect: "That's hard. But what can we thank God for right now?" Over time, this practice transforms the culture of the home. The family that used to bond over shared complaints begins to bond over shared gratitude. The atmosphere shifts from heavy to light, from bitter to sweet, from discontent to joy.

Tonight, let your family make a covenant: we will do all things without murmuring. Not perfectly — we will stumble. But the direction is set. We are moving from complaint to contentment, from murmuring to praise.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Burroughs said that murmuring against our circumstances is really murmuring against God. Why is this such a serious charge?

Question 2: How is murmuring contagious in a family? Can you think of a time when one person's complaining affected the mood of the whole household?

Question 3: What is one area of life where our family tends to complain? How can we practice replacing that complaint with thanksgiving this week?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Do all things without murmurings and disputings." -- Philippians 2:14

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord, forgive us for our murmuring. We confess that we complain too easily and give thanks too rarely. Purify our speech. Transform our hearts. Help our family to do all things without murmuring, that we might shine as lights in a dark world. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, choosing gratitude over complaint as you drift to sleep.
