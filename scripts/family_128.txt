PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 28
"The Godly Person Has Heavenly-Mindedness"
Thomas Watson — The Godly Man's Picture


Welcome, dear family. Another day has passed, and God has been faithful through it all. Now let us close this day together by sitting at the feet of Jesus and hearing His Word.

Let us pray. Almighty God, You are the giver of every good gift, and one of the greatest gifts You have given us is this family. We dedicate this time to You. Help us to listen well and to grow in wisdom together. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

Our passage tonight is Colossians 3:1-3. Listen with expectant hearts:

"If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ sitteth on the right hand of God. Set your affection on things above, not on things on the earth. For ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God."

That is Colossians 3:1-3. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


As we close our week with Thomas Watson, we come to the final mark he describes in his portrait of the godly person: heavenly-mindedness. Paul writes in Colossians 3:1-3 one of the most piercing exhortations in all his letters.

'If ye then be risen with Christ' — Paul begins with the gospel. Christians have been united with Christ in His death and resurrection. What happened to Christ happened to us spiritually. We died with Him to our old life, and we rose with Him to new life. This is not a metaphor. It is a spiritual reality.

Because of this union with Christ, Paul gives two commands. First, 'seek those things which are above, where Christ sitteth on the right hand of God.' The word 'seek' means to pursue, to make it your aim, to devote your energy to. We are to actively pursue heavenly things — things that have eternal value, things that matter in God's kingdom.

Second, 'Set your affection on things above, not on things on the earth.' The word 'affection' means to set your mind, to fix your thinking, to orient your heart. It is not just about what you do but about what you love, what occupies your thoughts, what excites your heart. Where is your mind most naturally drawn? That reveals where your true affections lie.

Then Paul gives the reason: 'For ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God.' Your old identity — the person defined by earthly ambitions, worldly pleasures, and self-centered goals — is dead. Your true life is now hidden with Christ in God. It is secure, protected, eternal. And one day it will be revealed in glory (verse 4).

Jesus taught this principle in the Sermon on the Mount: 'Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt, and where thieves break through and steal: But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt, and where thieves do not break through nor steal: For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also' (Matthew 6:19-21). Whatever you treasure most will capture your heart. If your treasure is earthly, your heart will be bound to earth. If your treasure is heavenly, your heart will soar.

Paul himself demonstrated this heavenly-mindedness. In Philippians 1:21, he writes, 'For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.' For Paul, living meant serving Christ, and dying meant being with Christ — either way, Christ was the center. In Philippians 3:20, he says, 'For our conversation is in heaven' — the word 'conversation' means citizenship. Our true homeland, our permanent address, is heaven.

Thomas Watson wrote that the godly person is heavenly-minded. His heart is where his treasure is. He uses the world as a traveler uses an inn — lodging for a night but never making it his home. His citizenship is in heaven, and his heart is already there.

Watson was careful to clarify that heavenly-mindedness does not mean being so 'spiritual' that you are no earthly good. Some people use otherworldliness as an excuse for laziness, irresponsibility, or disengagement. That is not what Scripture teaches. The most heavenly-minded Christians in history — people like William Wilberforce, George Mueller, and Amy Carmichael — were also among the most practically useful people on earth. Because they valued eternity, they worked hard to make a difference in time.

Watson compared the heavenly-minded Christian to a merchant ship. The ship sails on the sea — it is in the world. But the ship is headed to its home port. It does not drift aimlessly. It uses every wind and current to move toward its destination. Likewise, the Christian lives in the world but is heading home to heaven. Everything he does — his work, his relationships, his use of time — is oriented toward that final destination.

Watson also offered a test of heavenly-mindedness: what do you think about when you have nothing you must think about? In your free moments, where does your mind naturally go? The heavenly-minded person finds himself thinking about God, His promises, His goodness, and the glory that awaits. This does not happen overnight. It is the fruit of years of meditating on Scripture, praying, and cultivating an awareness of God's presence.

For our youngest: this world is like a hotel we are staying in for a little while. Our real home — heaven — is where we will live forever with God! For older children: if you knew you were moving to an amazing new home next year, would you spend all your time and money decorating the temporary house? How does thinking about heaven change how we live today? For the whole family: let us live with one eye on earth and one eye on heaven — fulfilling our responsibilities here while remembering that this world is not our final home.


Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher and learn something that will deepen our understanding.

Thomas Watson, in his work "The Godly Man's Picture," wrote these words:

"The godly man is heavenly-minded. His heart is where his treasure is. He uses the world as a traveler uses an inn — he lodges there for a night but does not make it his home. His citizenship is in heaven, and his heart is already there."

What Thomas Watson is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Colossians 3:1-3 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Let us discuss what we have heard. Parents, encourage even the youngest to share what they are thinking.

Question 1: What does Paul mean when he says 'set your affection on things above'?

Question 2: If heaven is our real home, how should that change the way we live right now?

Question 3: Watson says the Christian uses the world like a traveler uses an inn. What does that picture mean to you?

Question 4: What are some 'things above' that we should think about and value more? What are some 'things on earth' that we might value too much?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Set your affection on things above, not on things on the earth." -- Colossians 3:2

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Before we close, here is a family challenge for the days ahead:

Create a 'Two Homes' drawing. On one side of a paper, draw things that represent earthly treasures (toys, money, fame). On the other side, draw things that represent heavenly treasures (love, kindness, knowing God, helping others). Discuss: which treasures will last forever?


Heavenly Father, we end where we began, in gratitude. Thank You for Your Word, for this family, and for the hope we have in Christ. May tonight's lesson from Colossians 3:1-3 shape the way we live, the way we speak, and the way we love one another. Amen and amen.

Until next time, dear family. The Lord goes before you. Walk with Him.
