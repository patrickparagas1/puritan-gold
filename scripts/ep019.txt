PURITAN GOLD — Episode 19
"Integrity and Uprightness"
Week 3, Day 5 (Friday) — Proverbs 11

---

I want to tell you about a tree.

Not a famous tree. Not a tree from a story. Just an ordinary tree that stood in the yard of a man I once knew — a man of quiet, steady faith. An older gentleman, nothing flashy about him. He never wrote a book. Never spoke at a conference. Never had a platform. But everyone who knew him trusted him. His word was his bond. His handshake was better than a contract. And when he died, the church was overflowing — not with people who admired his accomplishments, but with people whose lives he had quietly shaped by the sheer force of his integrity.

At the funeral, someone pointed to the old oak tree in his yard and said, "That's what he was like. Deep roots. Strong trunk. And always bearing fruit — even in seasons when nobody was watching."

That image has stayed with me for years. And it's what I think of when I read Proverbs chapter 11. Because this chapter is about trees. About roots and fruit. About what happens when a life is built on integrity... and what happens when it isn't.

This is Puritan Gold, and today we open Proverbs 11 — a chapter that speaks with remarkable clarity about the value of integrity, the fruit of righteousness, and the importance of wise counsel.

Let's start with verse 3: "The integrity of the upright shall guide them: but the perverseness of transgressors shall destroy them."

Integrity guides. That's the first thing to notice. Integrity is not just a moral quality — it's a compass. When you are a person of integrity, you don't have to agonize over every decision, wondering what's the clever move, the strategic play, the angle that will benefit you most. You simply do what's right. Integrity simplifies your life.

John Owen wrote about this principle with characteristic depth. He said that the person who walks in integrity walks in liberty — because they are not enslaved to the shifting calculations of self-interest. They don't have to remember which lie they told to which person. They don't have to manage competing narratives. They don't have to live in fear of exposure. Their yes is yes, and their no is no. And that simplicity... is a kind of freedom that the double-minded person never knows.

But notice the contrast — the perverseness of transgressors shall destroy them. The word "perverseness" here means crookedness. Twistedness. It's the opposite of straightness, of uprightness. And what does it produce? Destruction. Not always immediately. Sometimes the crooked path seems to work for a long while. The dishonest man prospers. The deceitful woman advances. But the trajectory is clear. Crookedness leads to collapse. Always. Because you can't build a lasting structure on a twisted foundation.

The Puritans understood that integrity is not primarily about reputation — it's about reality. Richard Baxter put it plainly: "A man's character is not what he is observed to be, but what he truly is." The world judges by appearances. God judges by the heart. And a life of integrity means bringing those two things into alignment — so that what you appear to be is what you actually are.

Now, let's move to one of the richest verses in this chapter. Proverbs 11:30: "The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life; and he that winneth souls is wise."

The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life. Do you see the image? A righteous person doesn't just benefit himself. He becomes a source of life for others. His integrity nourishes. His faithfulness shelters. His wisdom provides shade and fruit for everyone around him.

Think about the people who have most deeply impacted your life. I suspect they weren't the most talented or the most charismatic. They were the most faithful. The most consistent. The most genuinely good. People whose lives bore fruit that you could taste — kindness, patience, honesty, generosity, courage. They were trees of life.

And that's what God is calling you to be. Not a celebrity. Not a success story by the world's metrics. A tree. A tree that is planted by streams of water, as Psalm 1 says, that brings forth its fruit in its season, whose leaf does not wither.

Thomas Watson loved to speak of the Christian life in terms of fruitfulness. He said that "grace is the seed, but fruitfulness is the harvest." It's not enough to have the seed of faith planted in your heart. The seed must grow. It must break through the soil. It must bear fruit that blesses others.

And notice what kind of fruit the verse describes — it's a tree of life. Not a tree of impressiveness. Not a tree of influence. A tree of life. The righteous person gives life to others. Through their words of encouragement. Through their faithful prayers. Through their quiet example. Through their willingness to serve without recognition. Through their steady presence in a world of constant change.

And then that second clause — "he that winneth souls is wise." The Hebrew here is rich and layered. It can mean "he who captures hearts" or "he who gathers souls." It's the image of a person whose integrity and fruitfulness draw others toward wisdom, toward God, toward life.

You don't win souls by arguing people into submission. You don't win souls by impressive performances. You win souls by being a tree of life — by living a life so saturated with the grace of God that people are drawn to the source. They taste your fruit and want to know where you got it. They see your peace and want to know the secret. They observe your integrity and recognize something they're missing.

This is evangelism at its deepest level — not a technique, but a life.

Now, let's look at verse 14: "Where no counsel is, the people fall: but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety."

Here's a truth that our individualistic age desperately needs to hear. You were not designed to navigate life alone. You were not designed to make every decision in isolation, relying solely on your own judgment. You need counsel. You need other voices. You need the wisdom of brothers and sisters who can see what you can't see, who can challenge what you won't challenge, who can encourage what you're tempted to abandon.

The Puritans were deeply communal in their faith. Despite the popular caricature of Puritans as grim, isolated individuals, the reality is that they practiced robust Christian community. They gathered in small groups for prayer and mutual edification. They confessed sins to one another. They held each other accountable. They sought counsel before making significant decisions.

John Bunyan, in The Pilgrim's Progress, gives Christian a companion — Faithful, and later Hopeful — because he understood that the journey of faith is not a solo expedition. We need fellow travelers. We need friends who will speak truth when we're wandering, who will hold us up when we're falling, who will remind us of the promises when we've forgotten them.

And notice — the verse doesn't say "in one counselor there is safety." It says "in a multitude of counselors." Why? Because no single person sees the whole picture. One friend may see your blind spot in this area; another may see it in that area. One counselor may be strong in practical wisdom; another may be strong in theological insight. Together, they provide a more complete picture than any one of them could alone.

Are you living in isolation right now? Are you making important decisions without seeking wise counsel? Are you carrying burdens alone that were meant to be shared?

If so, I want to gently say — that's not wisdom. That's pride. And pride, as Proverbs will tell us again and again, goes before a fall.

Seek counsel. Open your life to trusted brothers and sisters. Be vulnerable enough to say, "I don't know what to do. I need help." That's not weakness. That's wisdom.

Now, let me bring these three threads together — integrity, fruitfulness, and counsel — because they're deeply connected.

Integrity is the root. It's what you are when no one is watching. It's the hidden life of your heart before God.

Fruitfulness is the result. When the root is healthy, the tree bears fruit. When your inner life is marked by honesty, humility, and devotion, your outer life naturally produces blessing for others.

And counsel is the garden. Even the best tree needs care. It needs pruning. It needs other trees around it to break the wind and share the soil. You cannot bear fruit in isolation. You need the body of Christ. You need the means of grace. You need the community of faith.

Richard Baxter wrote that "no Christian is sufficient for himself. God has so ordered His church that every member needs every other member — as the eye cannot say to the hand, I have no need of thee." We are dependent on one another. And that's not a design flaw — it's a design feature.

Let me speak one more word about integrity before we close, because I think it's the most urgent word for our time. We live in an age of curated images. Of personal branding. Of carefully managed online personas that bear little resemblance to the real person behind the screen. We live in an age where it's easier than ever to appear righteous without actually being righteous.

But God is not fooled by our profiles and our posts. He sees the real you. The you that exists at two in the morning when the house is quiet and no one is watching. The you that exists in the privacy of your thoughts, your desires, your ambitions. The you that exists behind closed doors.

And the call of Proverbs 11 is this: let that private person be righteous. Let the root be healthy. Don't settle for a polished exterior over a rotting interior. Don't trade integrity for image.

Because in the end, it's the integrity of the upright that guides them. It's the fruit of the righteous that becomes a tree of life. And it's in the multitude of counselors that there is safety.

Build your life on these things, friend. Slow, deep, steady integrity. The kind that doesn't make headlines but changes lives. The kind that doesn't trend on social media but echoes in eternity.

Be a tree. Put your roots down deep into the soil of God's Word, God's grace, and God's community. And trust that in His time, in His way... you will bear fruit.

Let me pray.

God of truth, make us people of integrity. Not for the sake of our reputations, but for the sake of Your glory. Root us deep in Your Word. Make us trees of life in a world that is dying for want of fruit. Surround us with wise counselors who love us enough to tell us the truth. And keep us honest before You — in our thoughts, our words, and our ways. We ask this in the name of Jesus, the Righteous One, in whom all fullness dwells. Amen.
