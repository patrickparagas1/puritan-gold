PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 7
"Faithfulness, Truth, and Contentment in the Home"
Thomas Watson — The Ten Commandments

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we close our first week with Thomas Watson by looking at the remaining commandments together — the seventh through the tenth — and how they shape our family into a community of faithfulness, honesty, and contentment. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Faithful God, You are true in all Your ways and righteous in all Your works. As we close this week with the final commandments, shape our hearts to reflect Your faithfulness, Your truthfulness, and Your sufficiency. May our family be marked by these virtues. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Exodus 20:14-17. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Thou shalt not commit adultery. Thou shalt not steal. Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour. Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house, thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's wife, nor his manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is thy neighbour's."

That is Exodus 20:14-17. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Tonight we survey the final four commandments, and Thomas Watson shows us that each one speaks powerfully to the life of the family.

The seventh commandment — "Thou shalt not commit adultery" — is first and foremost a commandment about faithfulness. Watson taught that this commandment guards the marriage covenant, which is the foundation of the family. When husband and wife are faithful to one another, the entire family rests on solid ground. Children who grow up in a home where their parents love and honor one another have a security that nothing else can provide. Watson expanded this commandment beyond the marriage bed to encompass the broader principle of purity in all relationships. Families are to be places of emotional fidelity, where commitments are kept, trust is maintained, and no one's loyalty is divided. Parents who are faithful to each other model for their children what covenant love looks like, and children who see that model are better prepared to build faithful relationships of their own.

The eighth commandment — "Thou shalt not steal" — teaches the virtue of honesty in all our dealings. Watson applied this broadly. We steal not only when we take what does not belong to us, but when we fail to give what we owe. Parents owe their children time, attention, and spiritual instruction. When they withhold these things, they are, in a sense, stealing from their children what is rightfully theirs. Children owe their parents obedience and respect. When they withhold these, they take what does not belong to them — their own autonomy before it is properly theirs to exercise. In the family, everyone has something that belongs to others, and the eighth commandment calls each member to give generously what is owed.

Watson also taught that the eighth commandment cultivates a spirit of generosity rather than grasping. A family shaped by the eighth commandment is a family that shares freely, gives to those in need, and holds material possessions with open hands, recognizing that everything we have is a gift from God entrusted to us for a season.

The ninth commandment — "Thou shalt not bear false witness" — brings the virtue of truthfulness into the family. Watson was deeply concerned about the damage that lies do to relationships. In a family, truthfulness is the atmosphere in which trust grows. When family members are honest with one another — even when the truth is difficult — relationships deepen and strengthen. When family members lie, deceive, or mislead, trust erodes, and once trust is broken, it is very difficult to rebuild.

Watson applied this commandment in several directions. Parents must be truthful with their children, keeping their promises and not making commitments they do not intend to honor. Children must be truthful with their parents, even when confession of wrongdoing is painful. And all family members must resist the temptation to speak falsely about others — whether through gossip, exaggeration, or slander. A truthful family is a strong family, because truth is the soil in which love grows best.

The tenth commandment — "Thou shalt not covet" — is perhaps the most searching of all, because it alone deals explicitly with the desires of the heart. Watson taught that covetousness is the root of countless sins. When we desire what others have, we open the door to jealousy, bitterness, dishonesty, and conflict. For families, covetousness is a particular danger in our consumer culture. We see what other families have — their homes, their vacations, their possessions — and we want the same. Children compare their belongings with those of their friends. Parents compare their lifestyles with those of their neighbors. And the result is discontentment, which poisons the joy of family life.

Watson's antidote to covetousness was contentment rooted in the character of God. When we truly believe that God is good, that He provides for our needs, and that His gifts are perfectly suited to our situation, covetousness loses its power. A content family is a joyful family — not because they have everything they want, but because they want everything they have, recognizing it as God's gracious provision.

As we close this week with Watson, let us gather all ten commandments together and see them for what they truly are: a portrait of a beautiful family. A family that worships God alone, worships Him rightly, speaks His name with reverence, rests in His Sabbath, honors authority, protects life, practices faithfulness, gives generously, speaks truthfully, and rests contentedly in God's provision. This is the family God designed. This is the family the commandments create.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Watson said that faithfulness in marriage creates security for the whole family. Why is covenant-keeping between parents so important for children?

Question 2: The ninth commandment calls us to be truthful. What makes honesty difficult in family relationships, and how can we create a home where truth is safe to speak?

Question 3: The tenth commandment addresses covetousness. What do we tend to covet as a family, and how can we cultivate contentment instead?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

Forgive us where we fall short, and strengthen us by Your Spirit to live as a family that honors You in every way. As we enter a new week, go before us and prepare the way. In Jesus' name, Amen.
