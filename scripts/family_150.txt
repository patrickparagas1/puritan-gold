PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 19
"Remedy: Flee Temptation"
Pilgrim Families — Week 3, Day 5


Hello and welcome to our family devotional. Come, young and old alike. God's Word has something for every member of this family tonight. Let us draw near to Him.

Shall we pray? Our Father, we gather tonight hungry for Your truth. We know that man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from Your mouth. Feed us now, Lord. Bless our family time together. In the name of Christ we pray. Amen.

God has preserved His Word for thousands of years so that families like ours could hear it tonight. Let us read from 2 Timothy 2:22; 1 Corinthians 10:13:

"Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart. There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it."

That is 2 Timothy 2:22; 1 Corinthians 10:13. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


The second remedy Brooks prescribes is beautifully simple: flee. Do not negotiate with temptation. Do not reason with it. Do not see how close you can get without falling. Run.

Paul commands young Timothy in 2 Timothy 2:22: "Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart." Notice the double movement: flee from sin AND follow after righteousness. The Christian life is not merely running away from bad things — it is running toward good things. You cannot just empty your life of temptation; you must fill it with godliness. The person who merely avoids sin without pursuing holiness has left a vacuum that temptation will quickly fill again.

The promise of 1 Corinthians 10:13 is one of the most encouraging in Scripture: "There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it." Three truths shine here.

First, your temptation is not unique. Whatever you are struggling with, others have faced the same battle and won. You are not alone, and you are not a hopeless case. Second, God is faithful. He controls the intensity of every temptation. He will never allow you to be tempted beyond what you can bear. If you are facing it, God has already determined that — with His help — you can overcome it. Third, God always provides a way of escape. Always. Every temptation comes with an exit door. The problem is not that there is no escape — the problem is that we often do not want to take it.

Brooks offers this vivid counsel: "He that would not fall into the ditch must not dance upon the brink." How many people fall into sin because they got too close? They did not intend to fall. They just wanted to see how close they could get to the edge. But the edge crumbled, and they tumbled in. The wise Christian stays far from the edge.

Joseph is the Bible's greatest example of fleeing temptation. When Potiphar's wife grabbed his garment and tried to pull him into sin, Joseph did not pause to consider, did not try to reason with her, did not pray about it. He ran. He left his coat in her hand and fled the house (Genesis 39:12). And God honored his obedience by eventually making him ruler of all Egypt.

For families, fleeing temptation means making practical decisions about what you allow into your home, onto your screens, and into your conversations. It means having the courage to turn off a movie that is leading in a wrong direction. It means putting the phone down when social media is stirring up envy or lust. It means leaving a conversation when gossip starts. It means teaching your children that retreat is not weakness — it is wisdom.

Brooks also notes that fleeing is most effective when done immediately. The longer you linger near temptation, the harder it becomes to leave. Sin has a gravitational pull — the closer you get, the stronger the pull becomes. But if you flee at the first sign of danger, you escape with your soul intact.

The "way of escape" God provides is not always dramatic. Sometimes it is as simple as walking away, changing the subject, calling a friend, opening your Bible, or going for a walk. Sometimes it is removing yourself from a situation entirely — changing your route to avoid a certain place, blocking a website, ending a toxic friendship. Whatever the escape route looks like, God promises it is there. Your job is to take it.

Tonight, discuss as a family: What are the temptations we face most often? Where are the "edges" we tend to dance near? And what practical escape routes can we establish? A family that plans for temptation before it arrives is far more likely to overcome it than a family that waits until they are already on the brink.


There is much we can learn from those who walked with God before us. Consider these words.

Thomas Brooks, in his work "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices," wrote these words:

"He that would not fall into the ditch must not dance upon the brink. He that would not be singed must not play with fire."

What Thomas Brooks is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in 2 Timothy 2:22; 1 Corinthians 10:13 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Family, let us talk about what we have learned. Consider these questions together.

Question 1: Why does Paul say 'flee' rather than 'fight' when it comes to certain temptations? When is running the best strategy?

Question 2: God promises a 'way of escape' with every temptation. Can you think of a time when you saw that escape route?

Question 3: What does Brooks mean by 'dancing on the brink'? How do people get too close to temptation in everyday life?

Question 4: What practical 'escape routes' can our family set up for the temptations we face most?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart." -- 2 Timothy 2:22

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Learning sticks better when we do something with it. Try this activity as a family:

Play a game of 'Escape Route.' Set up a simple obstacle course in your home. At various points, place cards with common temptations written on them. At each card, the family member must identify a practical way to 'flee' that temptation before they can continue. Discuss each escape route as a family.


Lord, as we close tonight's devotional, we place our family in Your hands. Watch over us as we sleep. Guard our dreams. Prepare our hearts for tomorrow. May the truths from 2 Timothy 2:22; 1 Corinthians 10:13 follow us into the new day. We love You, Lord. Amen.

Goodnight. Remember your memory verse, and carry it with you tomorrow.
