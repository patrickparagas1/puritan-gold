PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 29
"A Summer of Puritan Wisdom"
Summer Synthesis — Carrying Puritan Wisdom into Summer Family Rhythms


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. As June draws to a close and summer stretches out before us, tonight we pause to gather the treasures we have collected this month and consider how to carry them into the warm, relaxed days ahead. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, thank You for the riches You have poured into our family this month. As we enter the summer season, help us not to leave these truths behind but to weave them into every sunny day, every vacation, every lazy evening, and every family adventure. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Deuteronomy 6:6-9. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up. And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes. And thou shalt write them upon the posts of thy house, and on thy gates."

That is Deuteronomy 6:6-9. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


This month has been a feast. We sat with Thomas Watson and let the Ten Commandments examine every corner of our family life. We walked with John Flavel through the doctrine of providence and learned to trust God in every circumstance. We soared with Jonathan Edwards into the heights of holy affections and discovered how genuine spiritual emotion transforms the family from within. And we lingered with Jeremiah Burroughs beside the rare jewel of Christian contentment, learning to be satisfied with God Himself in every season.

Now summer is upon us. The routines change. School ends. Vacations begin. The pace of life shifts from structured to spontaneous. And there is a real danger that the spiritual momentum built during the regular season dissipates in the warmth of summer's leisure.

Moses anticipated this danger three thousand years ago. His instruction in Deuteronomy 6 is a comprehensive plan for integrating God's truth into every dimension of family life — not just during formal devotional times, but in the flowing, unstructured rhythms of daily life. Talk of these things when you sit at home. Talk of them when you walk along the road. Talk of them at bedtime and at breakfast. In other words, let God's truth permeate every moment.

This is the vision for your family's summer. The truths you have learned this month should not be filed away like school notebooks at the end of a term. They should become the lens through which you see everything.

From Watson, carry the commandments into your summer. When you see the beauty of creation on a hike, remember the first commandment: this God alone deserves our worship. When you enjoy Sabbath rest by the lake or on the porch, remember the fourth commandment: God designed rest as a gift, and it is holy. When tensions arise during a family road trip, remember the sixth commandment: protect one another's hearts with kind words.

From Flavel, carry providence into your summer. When an unexpected rainstorm cancels your beach day, remember: God's providence governs even the weather. When you stumble upon a hidden gem of a restaurant or meet a kind stranger, remember: God arranges the details. Train your children to spot God's providence in the small moments of summer.

From Edwards, carry holy affections into your summer. Let the beauty of a sunset stir your heart to worship. Let the laughter of your children remind you of the joy God takes in His own children. Let the stillness of a summer evening become a moment of communion with God. Do not let the busyness of summer activities crowd out the cultivation of your family's inner life.

From Burroughs, carry contentment into your summer. Whether your family's summer plans are grand or modest, practice contentment. Do not compare your vacation with anyone else's. Do not let the pressure to create "perfect" summer memories steal the joy of the ordinary ones. Be present. Be grateful. Be content.

Moses also instructs families to use physical reminders — writing God's words on the doorposts and gates of the house. In a modern application, consider placing a memory verse on the refrigerator, setting a Bible verse as your phone's lock screen, or starting a summer journal where the family records one way they saw God at work each day.

Summer is not a break from faith. It is an opportunity to practice faith in a different setting — under open skies, beside running water, in the company of the people you love most.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Of the four Puritan teachers we studied this month — Watson, Flavel, Edwards, and Burroughs — which one's teaching resonated most deeply with you, and why?

Question 2: Moses said to talk about God's truth everywhere — at home, on the road, at bedtime, and in the morning. How can our family build spiritual conversations into our summer routines?

Question 3: What is one practical way our family can keep growing spiritually this summer?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way." -- Deuteronomy 6:7

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord, we do not want to forget what You have taught us this month. As summer begins, help us to carry these truths with us — on our walks, at our meals, in our travels, and in our rest. Let every summer day be an opportunity to grow closer to You as a family. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, warmed by the love of God as summer unfolds before you.
