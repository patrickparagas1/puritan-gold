PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 9
"A Life of Prayer"
Matthew Henry — A Method for Prayer

---

Hello, dear ones. How wonderful it is to sit together as a family and open the Scriptures. Welcome to Puritan Gold's family devotional. Let us begin with thankful hearts.

Let us pray together. Lord God, we come before You as a family, grateful for Your love and Your provision. We ask that You would quiet our minds and soften our hearts as we hear from You tonight. Help us to understand, to remember, and to obey what You teach us. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from 1 Thessalonians 5:17-18. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Pray without ceasing. In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you."

That is 1 Thessalonians 5:17-18. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Yesterday we learned about morning prayer. Today we go further — the Bible calls us to pray without ceasing. Paul writes in 1 Thessalonians 5:17-18, 'Pray without ceasing. In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.'

Now, 'pray without ceasing' can sound impossible. Does God really expect us to be on our knees twenty-four hours a day? Of course not. Paul is not talking about the posture of prayer but the spirit of prayer. To pray without ceasing means to live in a constant attitude of dependence on God, awareness of His presence, and readiness to speak to Him at any moment.

Think of it this way. A mother with a newborn baby does not spend every second staring at the baby. She goes about her work — cooking, cleaning, talking with others. But she is always aware of the baby. Her ear is always listening. If the baby cries or needs something, she responds immediately. That is what 'pray without ceasing' looks like. We go about our daily lives, but our hearts stay attuned to God. We talk to Him throughout the day — a quick prayer before a test, a moment of thanks when something good happens, a cry for help when things go wrong, a whisper of praise when we see a beautiful sunset.

Notice that Paul joins prayer with thankfulness: 'In every thing give thanks.' Not for everything — we do not thank God for evil and suffering. But in everything — in every circumstance, we can find something to thank God for. Even in hard times, we can thank God for His presence, His promises, and His faithfulness.

Paul says this is 'the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.' Many Christians wonder, 'What is God's will for my life?' Here is one clear answer — it is God's will that you pray constantly and give thanks in every situation. You do not need a special revelation to know this. God has plainly told you.

Jesus Himself taught us about persistent prayer. In Luke 18:1, 'he spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint.' The parable of the persistent widow shows that God honors those who keep coming to Him, who do not give up. And in Philippians 4:6, Paul writes, 'Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.' The antidote to anxiety is prayer with thanksgiving.

Matthew Henry wrote extensively about prayer. He even wrote a book called A Method for Prayer to help ordinary Christians know how to pray. Henry said that prayer is the key that opens the day and the bolt that shuts in the night — it is the soul breathing itself into the bosom of its heavenly Father.

What a beautiful picture — prayer as breathing. You do not have to think about breathing. You do not schedule it. You just do it, constantly, naturally. That is what prayer should become for us — as natural as breathing.

Henry taught that prayer has several parts. There is adoration — praising God for who He is. Confession — honestly admitting our sins. Thanksgiving — expressing gratitude for His blessings. Supplication — bringing our needs and the needs of others before Him. An easy way to remember these is the acronym ACTS: Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving, Supplication.

Henry also believed that the best prayers are filled with Scripture. When we pray God's own words back to Him, we know we are praying according to His will. For example, instead of just saying, 'God, help me not to worry,' you could pray, 'Lord, You said to be careful for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving to make my requests known to You. I bring my worries to You now and ask for Your peace that passes understanding.'

Henry modeled this in his own life. He was known as a man of constant prayer. Whether studying, preaching, walking, or eating, Henry's heart was turned toward God. And his writings reflect it — they are not cold academic comments but warm, prayerful reflections on God's Word.

For our little ones: you can talk to God anytime — when you are happy, when you are sad, when you are scared, when you are thankful. God always hears you! For older children: try something this week. Every time something happens — good or bad — pause for three seconds and say something to God about it. See how it changes your day. For the whole family: prayer is not complicated. It is simply talking to the God who loves you. The more we practice it, the more natural it becomes.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

The Puritans were men and women who treasured God's Word above all else. Let us hear from one of them now.

Matthew Henry, in his work "A Method for Prayer," wrote these words:

"Prayer is the key that opens the day, and the bolt that shuts in the night. It is the soul's breathing itself into the bosom of its heavenly Father."

What Matthew Henry is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in 1 Thessalonians 5:17-18 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Can you really pray ALL the time? What do you think that means?

Question 2: What are some times during your regular day when you could talk to God?

Question 3: What does it mean to give thanks 'in everything' — even hard things?

Question 4: How can praying Scripture help us know what to say when we do not know how to pray?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Pray without ceasing. In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you." -- 1 Thessalonians 5:17-18

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

To make tonight's lesson come alive, try this activity together as a family:

Make a 'Prayer Breathing' exercise. Practice taking slow, deep breaths. On the inhale, silently say 'Thank You, God,' and on the exhale, silently say 'I trust You.' Do this five times together. Talk about how prayer can be as natural and constant as breathing.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for this time as a family. Thank You for speaking to us through Your Word. Plant these truths deep in our hearts, especially in the hearts of our children. Help us to walk in wisdom tomorrow and every day. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
