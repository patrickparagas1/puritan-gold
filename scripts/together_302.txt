PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 2
"Seeking Wisdom Together"
Proverbs 2:1-6

---

Hello, beloved couple. It is a beautiful thing when husband and wife sit down together in God's Word. The Puritans called marriage a little church within the church. Tonight, your little church is in session. Welcome.

Tonight we turn to Proverbs 2:1-6. Listen with your hearts, not just your ears:

"My son, if thou wilt receive my words, and hide my commandments with thee; So that thou incline thine ear unto wisdom, and apply thine heart to understanding; Yea, if thou criest after knowledge, and liftest up thy voice for understanding; If thou seekest her as silver, and searchest for her as for hid treasures; Then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God. For the LORD giveth wisdom: out of his mouth cometh knowledge and understanding."

That is Proverbs 2:1-6. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Matthew Henry had deep insight into the truths we have just read. The Puritans did not separate theology from daily life, and their reflections on marriage are remarkably practical.

In these opening verses of Proverbs 2, Solomon sets forth the conditions upon which wisdom may be obtained. Matthew Henry carefully notes the progression: receiving, hiding, inclining, applying, crying, lifting up the voice, seeking, and searching. This is no passive endeavor. Wisdom does not fall upon the idle; it is given to those who pursue it with the earnestness of a man seeking buried treasure.

Henry observes that each of these verbs describes an increasing intensity of pursuit. To 'receive' is the first step: a willingness to hear. To 'hide' the commandments is to store them in the heart as one would store a precious jewel in a safe place. To 'incline the ear' is to turn the whole attention toward divine instruction. To 'apply the heart' is to engage the affections, not merely the intellect. And when Solomon speaks of crying after knowledge and lifting up the voice, Henry notes that this is the language of fervent prayer: 'We must be earnest in prayer to God for wisdom. We must cry to heaven for it, as those that prize it and are importunate for it.'

The promise is glorious: 'Then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD.' Henry comments that the result of this diligent search is not merely intellectual knowledge but a transformative understanding that reshapes the entire life. And the source is unmistakable: 'The LORD giveth wisdom.' It is not earned by human effort alone but bestowed by divine grace upon those who seek it earnestly.

For the married couple, this passage issues a profound call. Charles Bridges notes that the husband and wife who seek wisdom together with this kind of earnestness will find their marriage strengthened beyond measure. When both partners are digging for the same treasure, they are drawn closer to one another even as they are drawn closer to God. The shared pursuit of wisdom creates a bond that superficial pleasures can never forge.

--- Marriage Application ---

A marriage that does not grow in wisdom will inevitably stagnate or decline. Solomon's call to seek wisdom 'as silver' and search for it 'as for hid treasures' should describe the posture of every Christian couple toward the Word of God.

What would it look like for you as a couple to seek wisdom with this kind of intensity? Perhaps it means establishing a regular practice of reading Scripture together, not merely as a duty but as a shared delight. Perhaps it means praying together specifically for wisdom in the decisions you face: where to live, how to raise your children, how to steward your resources, how to serve your church.

Notice that the promise is conditional: 'If thou seekest... then shalt thou understand.' God does not withhold wisdom from those who genuinely desire it, but neither does He force it upon the indifferent. The couple who makes the pursuit of divine wisdom the central project of their marriage will find that every other area of their life together is enriched and ordered by it.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

It is time for reflection. Turn to each other and consider these questions together. There is no rush. Let the conversation go wherever the Spirit leads.

Question 1: Are we actively seeking God's wisdom together, or have we settled into spiritual complacency as a couple?

Question 2: What is one specific area of our life where we need divine wisdom right now, and have we earnestly prayed for it?

Question 3: How can we make the study of God's Word a more central and joyful part of our daily life together?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Now let us pray together. The Puritans said that a couple who prays together is bound by cords that cannot be broken. Let us strengthen those cords tonight.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask the Lord together to give you hearts that seek wisdom as for hidden treasure. Pray specifically for wisdom in whatever decisions or challenges you currently face. Thank God that He gives wisdom liberally and without reproach to those who ask.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the God who gives wisdom generously grant you understanding in every season of your marriage, that together you may walk in His light. Amen.

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you both, tonight and always. Goodnight.
