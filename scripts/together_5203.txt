PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 3
"Husbands, Love Your Wives"
Ephesians 5:25-27 — Mutual Submission and Love


Good evening, dear couple. Tonight we turn to one of the most searching and demanding passages addressed to husbands anywhere in Scripture. If last night laid a charge upon the wife, tonight the husband is called to account. And the standard set before him is nothing less than the love of Christ Himself.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 5:25-27 (KJV):

"Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it; That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word, That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish."


Husbands, consider the magnitude of what you have just heard. You are not merely told to love your wives. You are told to love them as Christ loved the church. And how did Christ love the church? He gave Himself for it. He died for it. He laid down His life, His comfort, His glory, His very body, for the sake of His bride. This is the standard of love to which every Christian husband is called.

William Gouge, in his Domestical Duties, wrote at great length on this passage. He observed that Christ's love for the church was not a response to the church's worthiness. Christ did not love the church because she was beautiful and spotless. He loved her while she was stained with sin, while she was rebellious and wandering, while she deserved nothing but judgment. His love was not earned. It was given. And this, Gouge argued, is the pattern for the husband. A husband's love for his wife must not depend on her performance, her mood, her appearance, or her behavior. It must be a love that gives and gives again, regardless of what is received in return.

This is what makes the husband's calling so staggering. The world teaches men that love is a transaction: I will love you if you love me back. I will serve you if you serve me first. I will be gentle if you are respectful. But the love of Christ knows no such conditions. Christ loved the church when she was at her worst. And the husband is called to love his wife with that same unconditional, sacrificial, relentless love.

Notice the purpose of Christ's love: "that he might sanctify and cleanse it." Christ's love is not passive. It is active and purposeful. He loves the church in order to make her holy. He washes her with the water of the Word. He works to present her to Himself glorious, without spot or wrinkle. The husband, therefore, is not merely called to feel affection for his wife. He is called to actively pursue her spiritual good. He is called to lead her in the Word, to pray for her sanctification, to encourage her growth in grace, and to gently help her become all that God intends her to be.

Richard Baxter counseled husbands to examine themselves carefully. Are you leading your wife closer to Christ or further from Him? Is your home a place where holiness is cultivated or where it withers? Do your words build her up or tear her down? Baxter was blunt: a husband who neglects his wife's spiritual welfare while attending to his own is a hypocrite. The husband who prays earnestly at church but speaks harshly at home has not begun to understand the love of Christ.

The Puritans were not naive about the difficulty of this calling. They knew that loving a wife as Christ loves the church requires daily dying to self. It means coming home after a hard day and still choosing to be present, to listen, to serve. It means leading family worship when you are tired. It means apologizing when you are wrong, even when your pride screams against it. It means looking at your wife and seeing not her faults but the woman God has entrusted to your care.

Dear husband, your wife is watching you tonight. She is listening to these words. And she needs to know that you hear this passage and take it to heart. She needs to know that you aspire to love her as Christ loves the church, even though you will fall short. Your aspiration itself is an act of love. Your desire to grow is a gift to her.

And dear wife, pray for your husband in this calling. It is a high and heavy one. He cannot do it in his own strength. He needs the grace of God and the support of the wife God has given him.


Question 1: Husband, in what specific ways can you more intentionally pursue your wife's spiritual growth and well-being?

Question 2: Wife, how can you support and encourage your husband as he seeks to lead and love sacrificially?

Question 3: What is one area of our marriage where we need more of Christ's sanctifying love at work?


O Lord Jesus Christ, Thou who loved the church and gave Thyself for her, teach the husband in this marriage to love his wife with that same sacrificial, sanctifying love. Forgive him for the times he has loved himself more than he has loved her. Give him strength to die daily to his own comfort for the sake of her flourishing. And grant to the wife a heart that receives this love with gratitude and that supports her husband in this high calling. May our marriage be a living portrait of Thy love for Thy church. In Thy precious name we pray, Amen.
