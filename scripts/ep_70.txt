PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"God Is Our Refuge"
May 9, 2026

---

"God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea; Though the waters thereof roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with the swelling thereof. There is a river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacles of the most High. God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved: God shall help her, and that right early." -- Psalm 46:1-5

Martin Luther, when the storms of opposition raged most fiercely against the work of the Reformation, would turn to his beloved friend Philip Melanchthon and say, "Come, Philip, let us sing the forty-sixth psalm." And from this very passage Luther drew the inspiration for that great battle hymn of the Protestant church, known to all the world. But the psalm itself is far older than Luther, far older than the Reformation, and its truths are as enduring as the God it celebrates.

"God is our refuge and strength." These words do not describe what God does for us in some secondary or incidental way. They describe what God is. He is our refuge. He is our strength. The distinction matters profoundly. A man may have access to a refuge and yet fail to reach it in time. A man may possess strength and yet find it insufficient for the crisis. But when God Himself is the refuge, then the refuge is as vast as omnipresence and as strong as omnipotence. When God Himself is the strength, then the strength is as limitless as the power that spoke the universe into being.

"A very present help in trouble." Not a distant help, not a delayed help, not a help that arrives when the battle is already lost, but a very present help, a help that is here and now, as close as the breath in our lungs, as near as the beating of our hearts. The Puritans delighted in this phrase. Thomas Watson remarked that God is not only a help, but a help found, a help discovered by experience, a help that has been tested in a thousand trials and has never once been found wanting. He is a present help, meaning that He is ready to hand in the very moment of need. And He is a very present help, meaning that His readiness exceeds all our expectations and anticipations.

"Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea." Here is the practical consequence of having God as our refuge. Fear is banished. Not because the dangers are imaginary, but because the refuge is real. The psalmist does not deny that the earth may be removed. He does not pretend that the mountains cannot be shaken. He does not minimize the terrors that may assail the children of God. He simply declares that in the face of the worst catastrophes that the physical universe can produce, the believer has no reason to fear. And the reason is not the believer's own courage, but the believer's God.

Consider the images employed. The earth removed. The mountains carried into the sea. The waters roaring and troubled. The mountains shaking with the swelling of the deep. These are pictures of total and absolute upheaval. Everything that men count stable and permanent is being overturned. The very foundations of the world are being torn up. And yet, in the midst of this cosmic cataclysm, there is a scene of perfect peace. "There is a river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God."

What a contrast! Outside, the waters roar and rage in destructive fury. But inside the city of God, there is a river whose gentle streams bring gladness and refreshment. The roaring waters represent the tumults of the world, the upheavals of nations, the convulsions of history. But the river of God represents the quiet, unfailing, never-ceasing supply of grace that flows to the people of God from the throne of the Almighty. This river does not ebb and flow with the tides of circumstance. It does not dry up in seasons of drought. It does not overflow its banks in seasons of plenty. It is a constant, steady, life-giving stream that makes glad the city of God in every age and in every condition.

Ezekiel saw this river in his great vision, flowing from beneath the threshold of the temple, growing deeper and wider as it went, bringing life to everything it touched. The apostle John saw it again in the Revelation, clear as crystal, proceeding from the throne of God and of the Lamb. And in every age, the people of God have experienced this river in their own souls: the river of the Holy Spirit's comforting, strengthening, sanctifying presence, flowing through the Word of God and through the ordinances of grace, making glad the hearts of believers even in the darkest and most turbulent times.

"God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved." This is the ultimate ground of the church's security. Not the strength of her walls, not the skill of her leaders, not the number of her members, not the wisdom of her theologians, but the presence of God in her midst. A church without God's presence is a body without a soul, a shell without substance, a lamp without oil. But a church in which God dwells, however small, however poor, however despised by the world, is an impregnable fortress that all the powers of hell cannot overthrow.

"God shall help her, and that right early." The Hebrew here carries the sense of "at the turning of the morning," that is, at the first light of dawn. The darkest hour, as the proverb says, is just before the dawn. And God's help often arrives at precisely that moment when the night seems longest and the hope seems faintest. He does not always deliver us from the trial, but He always delivers us in the trial, and His timing is perfect. The morning always comes.

"Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth." With these words, the Lord Himself speaks to the tumultuous nations and to the anxious hearts of His own people. Be still. Cease your striving. Lay down your frantic efforts to save yourselves. Let go of your desperate attempts to control what only God can control. And know, with a settled, unshakeable, experiential knowledge, that He is God. He is sovereign over every nation, every ruler, every army, every storm. And He will be exalted, not may be, but will be, in all the earth. Let the nations rage. Let the mountains tremble. Let the waters roar. Our God is our refuge, and He cannot be moved. Amen.
