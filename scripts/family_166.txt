PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 5
"Honor Thy Father and Mother"
William Gouge — Domestical Duties


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight William Gouge speaks to the children — about the sacred duty of honoring father and mother. This is a word for every member of the family. Let us listen together.

Let us pray. Lord God, You are our heavenly Father, and You have placed us in families where we learn to love, to obey, and to trust. Speak to each of us tonight — parents and children alike. Help us to hear Your Word with soft hearts and willing spirits. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Ephesians 6:1-3 and Exodus 20:12. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right. Honour thy father and mother; which is the first commandment with promise; That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth. Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the Lord thy God giveth thee."

That is Ephesians 6:1-3 and Exodus 20:12. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Having addressed the duties of husbands and wives, and then the duties of parents toward their children, William Gouge now turns the glass around and addresses the duties of children toward their parents. And he does so with the full weight of the fifth commandment behind him: "Honour thy father and thy mother."

Gouge notes that the apostle Paul calls this "the first commandment with promise" — the first of the Ten Commandments that carries an explicit blessing attached to it. God promises that those who honor their parents will find that "it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth." This is a remarkable promise. It tells us that the way we treat our parents has consequences not only in eternity but in the here and now. God blesses the child who honors father and mother.

But what does it mean to honor your parents? Gouge identifies three essential dimensions of this duty.

The first is inward reverence. To honor your parents begins in the heart. It means regarding them with respect, esteeming their wisdom, valuing their experience, and recognizing the authority God has given them. A child who outwardly obeys but inwardly despises his parents has not truly honored them. The commandment reaches deeper than behavior — it reaches the heart. Gouge writes that children should think of their parents as God's appointed representatives in the home. When a child respects his father and mother, he is learning to respect all lawful authority — and ultimately, to reverence God Himself.

The second is outward obedience. Paul says simply: "Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right." Obedience means doing what your parents ask you to do, when they ask you to do it, with a willing spirit. It means accepting correction without resentment. It means submitting to household rules even when you do not fully understand them. Gouge acknowledges that obedience is not always easy, especially as children grow older and begin to form their own judgments. But he insists that the habit of obedience is one of the most valuable things a child can learn, because it prepares the soul for the greater obedience that God requires of every believer.

The qualifier "in the Lord" is important. Gouge notes that children are to obey their parents in all lawful things — that is, in everything that does not require them to disobey God. If a parent commands a child to sin, the child must respectfully decline, for obedience to God always takes precedence over obedience to man. But Gouge is quick to add that such cases are rare, and children should not use this exception as an excuse for general disobedience.

The third dimension is grateful care. This duty extends beyond childhood into adulthood. Gouge writes movingly about the obligation of grown children to care for aging parents — to provide for their needs, to visit them, to comfort them in their declining years, and to repay, insofar as possible, the love and sacrifice that their parents poured into their lives. He cites the example of Joseph, who provided for his father Jacob in Egypt, and of Ruth, who clung to her mother-in-law Naomi with the famous words: "Whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God."

This duty of grateful care is especially relevant in our own time, when the elderly are too often neglected, abandoned in institutions, or treated as burdens rather than as treasures. The Puritan vision calls us to a higher standard — one that honors the gray head, cherishes the wisdom of the aged, and repays the sacrifices of parents with loving, faithful care.

Children, hear this word tonight: honor your father and mother. Not because they are perfect, but because God has placed them over you in love. Obey them with a willing heart. Respect them with genuine reverence. And as you grow, care for them with the same tenderness they showed you when you were small and helpless and entirely dependent on their love.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Gouge says honoring parents starts in the heart, not just in outward behavior. What is the difference between obeying with a willing spirit and obeying with a resentful heart?

Question 2: What does "obey your parents in the Lord" mean? Can you think of situations where this distinction matters?

Question 3: As children grow up, the duty to honor parents takes new forms. What does honoring your parents look like as an adult?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right." -- Ephesians 6:1

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Father God, we thank You for the gift of parents and children. Teach us to honor one another as You have commanded. Give our children hearts that are willing to obey. Give our parents hearts that are tender and wise. Bind us together in love that reflects Your own unfailing love for us. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
