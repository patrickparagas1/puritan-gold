PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 5
"One Flesh"
Ephesians 5:30-31 — The Union of Marriage

---

Welcome back, dear couple. Tonight we arrive at one of the most ancient and mysterious declarations in all of Scripture — words that reach all the way back to the Garden of Eden and forward to the consummation of all things.

Our passage tonight is Ephesians 5:30-31:

"For we are members of his body, of his flesh, and of his bones. For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and they two shall be one flesh."

Paul reaches back to Genesis 2:24, the very first words spoken about marriage in all of Scripture, and he places them in the light of the Gospel. This changes everything.

--- Teaching ---

The Puritans held the doctrine of "one flesh" with reverent awe. This was not, for them, merely a reference to physical intimacy — though it certainly includes that. It was a description of the profound, mysterious, total union that marriage creates between two people.

William Gouge taught that when God joins a man and woman in marriage, He creates something new — a union so deep that it can only be compared to the union between Christ and the Church. The two do not merely live together or cooperate together; they become, in God's eyes, one entity. Their lives are intertwined at every level: spiritually, emotionally, physically, economically, socially.

This has profound practical implications. If you are truly one flesh, then your spouse's pain is your pain. Your spouse's joy is your joy. Your spouse's shame is your shame to bear together. Your spouse's triumph is your triumph to celebrate together. There is no such thing as "my problem" and "your problem" in a one-flesh marriage. There are only "our problems" and "our blessings."

Thomas Gataker observed that the command to "leave father and mother" establishes a new priority. Marriage creates a new primary loyalty. This does not mean that you cease to honor your parents — the fifth commandment still stands. But it does mean that your spouse now holds the first place in your earthly affections and commitments. The Puritans counseled newlyweds to establish clear boundaries with extended family, not out of disrespect, but out of reverence for the new union God had created.

The word "joined" in the original Greek carries the sense of being glued together — a permanent, inseparable bond. Richard Baxter wrote that this joining is not merely a legal contract but a covenant before God, sealed by His own hand. What God has joined together, let no man put asunder — and that includes the man and woman themselves. The one-flesh union is to be guarded, nurtured, and treasured as one of God's most sacred gifts.

The Puritans were also remarkably candid about the physical dimension of one-flesh union. They rejected the medieval notion that physical intimacy in marriage was somehow less spiritual than celibacy. Gouge wrote at length about the duty of husband and wife to delight in each other physically, to meet each other's needs with generosity and joy, and to view their intimate life as a gift from God to be celebrated, not merely tolerated.

But the deepest meaning of "one flesh" is theological. Paul is saying that marriage is a mirror of the Gospel. Just as Christ became one with His Church — taking on human flesh, bearing our sin, sharing our suffering, giving us His righteousness — so husband and wife are called to enter into a union of total self-giving. Your marriage is meant to tell the story of Christ and the Church in miniature.

Tonight, marvel at the mystery. You are one flesh. You are bound together by the hand of God. And your union, however imperfect, is a living picture of the greatest love the universe has ever known.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Discuss together:

Question 1: What does "one flesh" mean to you practically? How does this concept change the way you approach conflict, finances, or decision-making?

Question 2: Are there areas where you still operate as two separate individuals rather than as one unit? How can you grow in unity?

Question 3: How well have you established healthy boundaries with extended family while still honoring your parents? Is there an adjustment that needs to be made?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Pray tonight for the deepening of your one-flesh union. Ask God to remove every barrier to intimacy — every unresolved conflict, every competing loyalty, every area of self-protection. Pray that your marriage would reflect the total, self-giving love of Christ for His Church.

--- Closing ---

May the God who created marriage and who calls two to become one flesh strengthen the bond between you. May your union grow deeper, richer, and more beautiful with each passing year. The Lord bless you and keep you. Goodnight.
