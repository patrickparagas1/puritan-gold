PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Doctrine Deep-Dive, Day 4
"The Doctrine of the Trinity"
One God, Three Persons — The Foundation of the Faith

---

Good evening, friend. Tonight we turn our minds to the highest and most glorious doctrine of the Christian faith: the doctrine of the Holy Trinity. The Puritans were not content to merely affirm this doctrine; they sought to understand it, to worship within it, and to live in light of it. Let us follow their example.

--- Scripture ---

Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Matthew 28:19:

"Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.

--- Doctrine Study ---

The doctrine of the Trinity stands at the very center of the Christian faith. It is not a peripheral speculation or an academic curiosity. It is the essential truth about who God is. Without the Trinity, there is no gospel, no salvation, no Christian worship worthy of the name. The Puritans understood this with great clarity, and their writings on this doctrine are among the richest in the history of the church.

The doctrine may be stated simply, though its depths are inexhaustible: there is one God who exists eternally in three persons — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — each fully God, each distinct in person, yet together constituting one undivided divine essence. This is not three gods. It is not one God wearing three masks. It is one God in three persons, a mystery that transcends human reason but does not contradict it.

John Owen, in his magnificent work "Communion with God," argues that the Christian's spiritual life is enriched immeasurably by understanding the distinct roles of each person of the Trinity. Owen teaches that the believer has communion with the Father in love, with the Son in grace, and with the Holy Spirit in comfort and fellowship. This is not mere theological abstraction. Owen insists that the believer who learns to relate to each person of the Godhead distinctly will find his devotional life transformed. When we pray, we are not addressing an undifferentiated divine being. We are speaking to the Father who chose us, through the Son who redeemed us, in the power of the Spirit who indwells us.

The Westminster Confession of Faith, that great Puritan document, articulates the doctrine with careful precision: "In the unity of the Godhead there be three Persons of one substance, power, and eternity: God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Ghost. The Father is of none, neither begotten nor proceeding; the Son is eternally begotten of the Father; the Holy Ghost eternally proceeding from the Father and the Son." Every word of this statement was weighed with the utmost care. The Puritans who drafted it knew that imprecision on the Trinity leads to heresy, and heresy on the Trinity leads to a false god.

Consider first the Father. Scripture reveals Him as the fountain of the Godhead, the one from whom the Son is eternally begotten and from whom the Spirit eternally proceeds. He is the author of the plan of redemption, the one who chose a people for Himself before the foundation of the world (Ephesians 1:4). He sent the Son into the world not reluctantly but lovingly: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son" (John 3:16). The Father is not a distant deity who must be persuaded to be gracious. He is the source of grace, the initiator of salvation, the one whose love set the whole plan of redemption in motion.

Consider next the Son. He is eternally begotten of the Father — not made, not created, but begotten. This means that the Son shares the very nature of the Father. He is, as the Nicene Creed declares, "God of God, Light of Light, very God of very God." In the fullness of time, the Son took upon Himself human nature, uniting deity and humanity in one person forever. He lived the life we should have lived. He died the death we deserved to die. He rose in triumph over sin, death, and hell. And He ascended to the right hand of the Father, where He intercedes for His people and rules over all things for their good.

Consider finally the Holy Spirit. He is the third person of the Trinity, proceeding from the Father and the Son, equal in glory and majesty with both. The Spirit's work is to apply the redemption that the Father planned and the Son accomplished. He regenerates the dead sinner, giving new life and new affections. He illuminates the mind to understand the Scriptures. He sanctifies the believer, producing the fruit of holiness. He seals the believer, guaranteeing the final inheritance. He intercedes for the believer with groanings that cannot be uttered (Romans 8:26). Without the Spirit, the gospel would remain a dead letter, a historical fact without present power.

Thomas Watson, in his exposition of the Shorter Catechism entitled "A Body of Divinity," reflects on the practical implications of the Trinity for the believer. Watson writes that the Trinity assures us that God is eternally relational. Before there was a creation, before there were angels or men, there was love within the Godhead: the Father loving the Son, the Son loving the Father, and the Spirit as the bond of that love. This means that when God loves us, He is not doing something foreign to His nature. He is extending to us the very love that has existed within Himself from all eternity.

The Puritans also insisted that the doctrine of the Trinity is essential to true worship. Stephen Charnock, in his monumental work "The Existence and Attributes of God," argues that all true worship is Trinitarian. We worship the Father as the source of all blessing, through the mediation of the Son, in the power of the Spirit. Any worship that neglects one person of the Trinity is deficient worship. Any theology that diminishes one person of the Trinity is deficient theology.

Let this great doctrine fill your heart with awe and your lips with praise. The God you serve is not a solitary monarch but a Trinity of persons in perfect, eternal, self-giving love. And He has drawn you into that love through the gospel of His Son.

--- Reflection ---

Here are some questions to carry with you into the coming days.

Question 1: Do I relate to each person of the Trinity distinctly in my devotional life, or do I tend to think of God in undifferentiated terms?

Question 2: How does the doctrine of the Trinity assure me that God's love is not arbitrary but essential to His nature?

Question 3: In what ways can I make my prayers more explicitly Trinitarian — addressing the Father, through the Son, in the Spirit?

Do not rush past these questions. Sit with them. Journal about them if you can. The Puritans believed that truth must be digested, not merely tasted.

--- Closing Prayer ---

Almighty God, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, we worship Thee in the mystery of Thy triune being. We praise the Father for choosing us, the Son for redeeming us, and the Spirit for sealing us. Teach us to know Thee more fully and to worship Thee more worthily. In the name of the blessed Trinity, Amen.

Rest in His grace tonight. Tomorrow is a new day of growth.
