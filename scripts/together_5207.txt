PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 7
"Reverence and Love"
Ephesians 5:33 — Mutual Submission and Love

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. We close our first week together with the final verse of Ephesians 5, a verse that distills the entire teaching on marriage into two words: love and reverence. Think of tonight as the capstone of everything we have studied this week.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 5:33 (KJV):

"Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband."

--- Teaching ---

Here Paul brings his grand, sweeping vision of marriage down to the most personal and particular application. "Let every one of you in particular." Not husbands in general, but you, this husband, sitting here tonight. Not wives in general, but you, this wife, listening to these words right now. This is personal. This is for your marriage, your home, your evening together.

The husband's charge is to love his wife as himself. We have seen this week what that love looks like: it is sacrificial, giving itself up as Christ gave Himself for the church. It is sanctifying, pursuing the wife's holiness and growth. It is nourishing and cherishing, tending to her needs with the same instinctive care a man gives his own body. And now, in this summary verse, Paul adds one more dimension: love her as yourself. Treat her interests as your own interests. Feel her burdens as your own burdens. Delight in her joys as your own joys. There is no room in this command for indifference or neglect.

The wife's charge is to reverence her husband. The word translated "reverence" in the King James Version carries the meaning of deep respect, honor, and esteem. It is related to the word for fear, but in the context of marriage, it points not to terror but to a high regard that expresses itself in speech, in attitude, and in action. The wife who reverences her husband speaks of him with honor, both to his face and behind his back. She respects his leadership, even when she sees his weaknesses. She builds him up rather than tearing him down.

William Gouge observed that love and reverence are not alternatives but complements. The husband who truly loves will also reverence his wife, though that is not his primary charge. The wife who truly reverences will also love, though that is not her primary charge. But Paul highlights these particular duties because they address the particular temptations of each. Husbands are tempted to become harsh, distant, or self-absorbed. The command to love confronts that temptation directly. Wives are tempted to become critical, controlling, or contemptuous. The command to reverence confronts that temptation directly.

Baxter wrote that a wife's reverence is one of the most powerful forces in a marriage. A husband who feels respected by his wife is empowered to lead with confidence and gentleness. A husband who feels constantly criticized becomes either tyrannical or withdrawn. The wife has enormous influence over the atmosphere of the home through the way she speaks to and about her husband. Baxter urged wives to use this influence wisely and generously.

Likewise, a husband's love is one of the most transformative forces in his wife's life. A wife who knows she is deeply loved is free to flourish. She is secure enough to take risks, to grow, to serve. A wife who feels unloved becomes anxious, defensive, and withdrawn. The husband has the power to create an environment of safety and abundance through his consistent, sacrificial love.

Dear couple, as we close this first week, take inventory. Husband, has your wife felt loved this week? Not loved in the way you prefer to express it, but loved in the way she needs to receive it. Wife, has your husband felt reverenced this week? Not reverenced in a forced or artificial way, but honored with genuine respect and esteem. If the answer to either question is uncertain, tonight is a beautiful opportunity to begin again.

Love and reverence. These are the twin pillars of the Christian marriage. They are not feelings that come and go. They are commitments that endure. They are disciplines that grow stronger with practice. And they are gifts that, when given faithfully, create a marriage that reflects the glory of Christ and His church.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: Husband, on a scale of one to ten, how loved do you think your wife has felt this week? Ask her to confirm or correct your estimate.

Question 2: Wife, on a scale of one to ten, how reverenced do you think your husband has felt this week? Ask him to confirm or correct your estimate.

Question 3: What is one specific way each of you can better express love or reverence in the coming week?

--- Prayer ---

Father in heaven, we thank Thee for this week of rich instruction from Thy Word. We have seen the beauty of Thy design for marriage: mutual submission, sacrificial love, nourishing tenderness, and reverent honor. We confess that we fall short daily. But we trust in Thy grace to make up what we lack. Help this husband to love his wife as Christ loves the church. Help this wife to reverence her husband with genuine and joyful respect. Bind us together in love and in truth, that our marriage may bring glory to Thy great name. Through Jesus Christ our Lord, Amen.
