PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 19
"Depending on God's Mercy"
Romans for Marriage — Day 19


Hello and welcome to your couples devotional. Whether you are sitting side by side on the couch or listening together at the end of a long day, God is here with you both. He meets married couples in a special way when they turn their hearts to Him together.

Let me read our passage for this evening. It comes from Romans 9:16:

"So then it is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy."

That is Romans 9:16. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


John Flavel was a faithful pastor and theologian whose words still carry weight centuries later. Listen to what the Puritans had to say about the truths in tonight's passage.

Romans 9:16 strips away every illusion of human self-sufficiency: 'It is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy.' Our willing does not save us. Our running does not earn God's favor. Everything depends on the mercy of God. This is humbling — and it is liberating.

John Flavel, the beloved Puritan pastor of Dartmouth, spent his ministry teaching believers to depend entirely on divine mercy. Flavel knew from personal experience the utter bankruptcy of human effort apart from God's grace. He lost two wives and watched his congregation scattered by persecution. Through it all, he learned that human determination, however noble, is insufficient without God's mercy.

For marriage, this verse addresses the deepest frustration couples face: trying hard and still failing. You may will with all your heart to be a good spouse. You may run tirelessly in pursuit of a healthy marriage. You may read every book, attend every seminar, and follow every principle. And still the marriage struggles. Why? Because success in marriage — like success in salvation — ultimately depends not on human effort but on divine mercy.

This does not mean effort is meaningless. Paul uses the language of willing and running — both of which are active and important. But effort alone is not sufficient. Your best efforts need God's mercy to succeed. Your strongest determination needs God's empowerment to bear fruit. The marriage that thrives is not merely the marriage that tries hardest — it is the marriage that depends most fully on God.

Flavel counseled couples to bring their efforts under the umbrella of dependence on God. Work hard at your marriage, yes. Communicate, serve, sacrifice, forgive. But underneath all of that effort, rest in the mercy of God. He is the one who makes marriages flourish. He is the one who softens hard hearts, heals deep wounds, and bridges impossible gaps. Without His mercy, all our willing and running is in vain.


Have you been exhausting yourselves trying to fix your marriage in your own strength? Have you been running hard — reading books, implementing strategies, having difficult conversations — and still feeling like you are not getting anywhere?

Tonight, stop running for a moment and rest in God's mercy. Acknowledge together: our best efforts are not enough. We need God's mercy. We need His intervention. We need Him to do what we cannot do.

This is not an excuse to stop trying. It is an invitation to stop trying alone. Bring your efforts to God and ask Him to bless them with His mercy. He is the one who makes things grow.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Let us reflect together. These questions are designed to help you go deeper, both with God and with each other.

Question 1: Have you been trying to fix your marriage by willpower alone? Where has that approach fallen short?

Question 2: What does it mean practically to depend on God's mercy in your marriage rather than your own effort?

Question 3: How can you continue to work hard at your marriage while simultaneously resting in God's mercy?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


As we near the end of tonight's devotional, let us bring our hearts before God in prayer. Pray for each other. Pray for your marriage. Pray for your home.

Tonight's prayer focus: Confess together your inability to sustain your marriage by your own strength. Ask for God's mercy — His undeserved, supernatural intervention — to do in your marriage what your willing and running cannot accomplish.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the mercy of God rest upon your marriage like morning dew. May your effort be empowered by His grace, your love sustained by His faithfulness, and your future secured by His sovereign goodness.

The Lord bless you and keep you. The Lord make His face shine upon you and be gracious to you. The Lord lift up His countenance upon you both, and give you peace. Amen.
