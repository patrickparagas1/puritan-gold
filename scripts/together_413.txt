PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 21
"When Trials Come to the Marriage"
William Gouge — On Suffering Together

---

Welcome, dear couple. We close our third week by addressing something every marriage will face: suffering. The Puritans did not shy away from this topic, and neither should we.

Our Scripture tonight is 1 Peter 4:12-13:

"Beloved, think it not strange concerning the fiery trial which is to try you, as though some strange thing happened unto you: But rejoice, inasmuch as ye are partakers of Christ's sufferings; that, when his glory shall be revealed, ye may be glad also with exceeding joy."

--- Teaching ---

William Gouge wrote that trials are one of God's most powerful instruments for deepening a marriage. "In fair weather," he observed, "any vessel can sail. It is the storm that reveals the strength of the ship." A marriage that has weathered suffering together — illness, financial hardship, the loss of a child, the betrayal of a friend, the testing of faith — emerges with a bond that comfort alone could never forge.

The Puritans identified several ways that trials can strengthen a marriage. First, trials reveal character. When life is easy, it is possible to hide our weaknesses — from ourselves and from each other. But suffering strips away the veneer and exposes what is underneath. This can be painful, but it is also an opportunity for honesty and growth. A couple that has seen each other at their worst and chosen to love anyway has a foundation that nothing can shake.

Second, trials create dependence on God. When your own resources are exhausted — when you cannot solve the problem, fix the situation, or make the pain go away — you are driven to prayer. And a couple that prays together in desperation discovers a closeness with God and with each other that ordinary times rarely produce.

Third, trials produce compassion. A couple that has suffered is uniquely equipped to comfort others who suffer. Paul wrote in 2 Corinthians 1:4 that God "comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble." Your pain is not wasted. It is preparation for ministry.

Richard Baxter, who suffered greatly throughout his life — including the early death of his beloved wife Margaret — wrote with deep feeling about the temptation to blame each other during times of trial. "When trouble comes," he warned, "the enemy will whisper that your spouse is the cause of your suffering, or that your spouse is failing to support you adequately. These are lies. Your enemy is not your spouse. Your enemy is the devil, the world, and the flesh. Turn toward each other, not against each other."

Baxter counseled couples in suffering to be honest about their pain without being consumed by it, to grieve together without losing hope, and to cling to the promises of God even when their circumstances seem to contradict those promises. "The storm will pass," he wrote. "The marriage that endures it will be stronger than the one that entered it."

The Puritans also acknowledged that some trials threaten to destroy a marriage rather than strengthen it. Prolonged illness, financial ruin, infidelity, the death of a child — these are trials that test the very foundations of a relationship. In such cases, the Puritans counseled seeking the help of a wise pastor, surrounding yourselves with a supportive church community, and refusing to make permanent decisions in the midst of temporary pain.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Discuss together:

Question 1: What is the most difficult trial your marriage has faced? How did it affect your relationship — for better or for worse?

Question 2: Are you currently in a season of trial? How are you handling it? Are you turning toward each other or away from each other?

Question 3: How has suffering deepened your faith or your marriage? Can you see God's hand in the midst of your pain?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Pray together for strength in whatever trial you are currently facing — or for preparation for the trials that will surely come. Ask God to bind you closer together through suffering, to give you hope in the midst of pain, and to use your experience to comfort others.

--- Closing ---

May the God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our tribulation, hold your marriage together through every storm. May you emerge from every trial stronger, closer, and more deeply convinced that nothing can separate you from His love. Goodnight.
