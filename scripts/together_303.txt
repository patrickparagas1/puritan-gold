PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 3
"Trusting God as a Couple"
Proverbs 3:5-6

---

Welcome, dear ones. Marriage is a gift from God, and so is this time together in His presence. Whether this has been an easy day or a hard one, you are here, together, and that matters more than you know.

Our Scripture reading this evening is from Proverbs 3:5-6. Let us receive it together:

"Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths."

That is Proverbs 3:5-6. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

The Puritans understood marriage in a way that the modern world has largely forgotten. Let us hear from Charles Bridges, who wrote with great wisdom on this subject.

These are among the most beloved verses in all of Proverbs, and rightly so. Charles Bridges observes that the command is both simple and profound: trust in the Lord not partially, not occasionally, but with all the heart. Bridges writes that this trust 'is the child-like confidence of one who knows the wisdom, power, and love of his Father.' It is not blind faith; it is informed faith, grounded in the character of God as revealed in His Word.

The negative command is equally important: 'lean not unto thine own understanding.' Bridges notes that this does not forbid the use of reason, but forbids the idolizing of reason. Human understanding is a gift of God, but it is a limited, fallen gift. When we lean upon it as our primary support, we lean upon a broken reed. The husband who trusts his own business acumen above divine guidance, the wife who trusts her own emotional intuition above scriptural counsel, both are leaning on understanding that will eventually fail them.

Matthew Henry adds that to 'acknowledge him in all thy ways' means to recognize God's authority and seek His direction in every department of life, not merely in what we consider 'spiritual' matters. Henry writes: 'In all our ways that are of any importance... we must take God along with us.' This includes the mundane decisions of daily life, the management of the household, the raising of children, the navigation of conflict, and the stewardship of resources.

The promise is magnificent: 'He shall direct thy paths.' Bridges comments that God does not merely point the way; He makes the way. He goes before His people, removing obstacles, opening doors, and leading them in paths of righteousness for His name's sake. The couple who trusts God with all their heart and acknowledges Him in all their ways will find that their paths, though sometimes unexpected, are always good.

--- Marriage Application ---

Trust is the currency of marriage. You must trust one another, but more fundamentally, you must trust God together. Every marriage faces moments of uncertainty: job loss, health crises, relational conflict, parenting failures, financial pressure. In those moments, you have a choice: lean on your own understanding or trust in the Lord with all your heart.

Trusting God as a couple means that when you face a difficult decision, your first instinct is prayer rather than panic. It means that when your plans fall apart, you remind one another that God's purposes cannot be thwarted. It means acknowledging Him not only in your Sunday worship but in your Monday morning schedules, your budget meetings, your bedtime conversations.

When one spouse is struggling to trust, the other has the sacred privilege of pointing them back to the faithfulness of God. This is one of the great gifts of marriage: you are not alone in your faith. You have a partner who can remind you of what is true when your own heart is clouded by doubt or fear.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

The Puritans practiced what they called holy conference, speaking honestly about spiritual things with those they loved most. Do that now with these questions.

Question 1: In what area of our life together are we most tempted to lean on our own understanding rather than trusting God?

Question 2: Can you share a time when God directed our path in a way we did not expect but later recognized as good?

Question 3: How can we practically 'acknowledge Him in all our ways' this week?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

It is time to bring what we have heard and discussed before the Lord in prayer. Pray out loud if you can. Let your spouse hear your heart.

Tonight's prayer focus: Surrender together the areas of your life where you have been leaning on your own understanding. Ask God to increase your trust in Him, and to direct your paths according to His perfect wisdom and unfailing love.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord in whom you trust direct every step of your journey together, making your paths straight and your hearts secure in His faithfulness. Amen.

May the God who brought you together continue to bind you together in love, in faith, and in purpose. Sleep well tonight. He watches over your marriage.
