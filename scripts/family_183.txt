PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 22
"The Pastor's Guide to the Home"
Richard Baxter — The Christian Directory

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to our fourth week of Puritan Gold family devotions for May. This week our guide is Richard Baxter, the great Puritan pastor of Kidderminster, whose massive work "A Christian Directory" is one of the most thorough guides to practical Christian living ever written. Let us draw near to God.

Let us pray. Lord God, we come before You tonight seeking practical wisdom for our home. Not merely knowledge about godliness, but the ability to live it out in the daily details of family life. Speak to us through Your servant Richard Baxter and through Your eternal Word. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Colossians 3:12-17. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Put on therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, longsuffering; Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye. And above all these things put on charity, which is the bond of perfectness. And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to the which also ye are called in one body; and be ye thankful. Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom; teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord. And whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God and the Father by him."

That is Colossians 3:12-17. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Richard Baxter (1615-1691) is one of the towering figures of the Puritan movement. As pastor of Kidderminster in Worcestershire, he transformed a spiritually desolate parish into one of the most vibrant Christian communities in England. When he arrived, the town was marked by ignorance and immorality. When he left, nearly every household practiced family worship, and the town had been transformed from the inside out.

Baxter's secret was not merely his preaching — though his preaching was powerful. It was his commitment to personal pastoral care and to the Christian household as the foundational unit of the church. He visited every family in his parish. He catechized them in their homes. He counseled husbands and wives, parents and children, masters and servants. And he poured his accumulated wisdom into "A Christian Directory," a work of staggering scope that addresses virtually every aspect of the Christian life.

In the sections on family life, Baxter begins with the principle that the household is the nursery of both church and commonwealth. As the household goes, so goes everything else. A nation of godly households will produce a godly church and a just society. A nation of spiritually neglected households will produce churches in decline and a society in moral chaos. Baxter believed this with his whole heart, and it drove his tireless efforts to strengthen family life in Kidderminster.

Baxter's practical counsel for the home begins with the atmosphere of the household. Paul's description in Colossians 3 provides the blueprint: mercy, kindness, humility, meekness, patience, forbearance, forgiveness, and above all, love — "the bond of perfectness." These are the virtues that create the atmosphere in which a family can thrive. Where these virtues are present, there is peace, safety, and growth. Where they are absent, there is strife, fear, and spiritual stagnation.

Baxter is especially insistent on the centrality of love. He writes that love is not merely one virtue among many — it is the environment in which all other virtues grow. A home governed by love will naturally produce kindness, patience, forgiveness, and joy. A home governed by selfishness will produce its opposite, no matter how many religious activities are performed within its walls. Baxter warns that a household can have daily Bible reading, regular prayer, and faithful church attendance and still be spiritually dead if love is not the ruling principle of its life together.

He also emphasizes thankfulness. Paul says, "Be ye thankful." Baxter observes that a grateful household is a happy household. When family members cultivate the habit of gratitude — thanking God for His provisions, thanking one another for acts of service, noticing and acknowledging the good rather than fixating on the imperfect — the whole atmosphere of the home is transformed. Ingratitude, by contrast, is a poison that kills joy and breeds discontent.

Baxter's counsel is intensely practical. He is not interested in lofty ideals that cannot be lived out in the daily grind of household life. He wants to know: How do you handle conflict at the dinner table? How do you respond when a child disobeys? How do you treat your spouse when you are tired and irritable? How do you manage money, time, and work in a way that honors God? These are the questions that "A Christian Directory" addresses, and Baxter's answers are unfailingly biblical, pastoral, and realistic.

Tonight, let us begin with the foundation Baxter lays: the atmosphere of the home. Is our household characterized by mercy, kindness, humility, patience, and love? Is gratitude the default setting of our hearts? If so, we are building on solid ground. If not, let us begin to cultivate these virtues — starting tonight, starting with one another, starting with a simple act of kindness or a word of thanks.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Baxter says love is the "environment" in which all other virtues grow. What does a home governed by love look and feel like? What does a home without love look and feel like?

Question 2: Paul says, "Be ye thankful." What is our family most grateful for right now? How can we cultivate a habit of expressing gratitude to God and to each other?

Question 3: Baxter says the atmosphere of the home matters more than the activities performed in it. Do you agree? Why or why not?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"And above all these things put on charity, which is the bond of perfectness." -- Colossians 3:14

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, fill our home with the virtues Paul describes — mercy, kindness, humility, patience, forgiveness, and above all, love. Let gratitude be the language of this household and peace be its atmosphere. Transform our home from the inside out, beginning with our own hearts. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
