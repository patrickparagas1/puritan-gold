PURITAN GOLD -- Study Topic: The Book of Enoch, Part 6 of 10
"The Son of Man (1 Enoch 45-57)"
The Book of Enoch — Christ-like figure in Jewish apocalyptic

---

Beloved friends, welcome back to our study. In our last episode, we introduced the figure of the Son of Man as he appears in the Parables of Enoch. Today we continue into chapters 45 through 57, where the portrait of this messianic figure reaches its fullest and most glorious expression. Here we find passages that, for any Christian reader, resonate powerfully with the person and work of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Let us read from Daniel 7:13-14:

"I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed."

--- Teaching ---

In the chapters before us today, the Parables of Enoch present the most sustained and detailed portrait of a messianic deliverer found anywhere in pre-Christian Jewish literature outside the canonical Scriptures. The Son of Man — also called the Elect One and the Righteous One — is depicted as a figure of cosmic authority who will overthrow the kingdoms of this world, vindicate the suffering righteous, and establish the reign of God forever.

Chapter 45 opens with a vision of the final judgment. The Elect One is seated upon the "throne of glory" — a phrase that our Lord Himself employs in Matthew 25:31: "When the Son of man shall come in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory." The verbal parallel is remarkable. Whether Jesus was consciously alluding to the Enochic tradition or drawing upon the broader stream of prophetic expectation, the connection is unmistakable.

From this throne, the Elect One executes judgment. The sinners and those who have denied the name of the Lord of Spirits are driven from His presence. The righteous are gathered and given new garments of glory. The earth itself is transformed — a new heaven and a new earth, where righteousness dwells. Again, the parallel with Revelation 21:1 is striking: "And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away."

Chapter 46 provides a physical description of the Son of Man that is deeply evocative. His countenance has "the appearance of a man" and yet is "full of graciousness, like one of the holy angels." He walks beside the Head of Days — the Ancient of Days of Daniel's vision — and Enoch asks the interpreting angel who this glorious figure is. The answer comes: "This is the Son of Man who hath righteousness, with whom dwelleth righteousness, and who revealeth all the treasures of that which is hidden, because the Lord of Spirits hath chosen him."

Notice the cluster of attributes: righteousness, revelation of hidden things, divine election. Each of these finds its canonical counterpart in Christ. He is "THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS" (Jeremiah 23:6). In Him "are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Colossians 2:3). He is the "elect" or "chosen" of God (Isaiah 42:1, 1 Peter 2:4).

Chapters 48 and 49 deepen the portrait further. The Son of Man is described as one whose name was "named" — that is, whose identity and role were determined — before the creation of the sun and stars. He was hidden in the presence of God from before the world began, and in the last days he is revealed. He is described as "the light of the Gentiles" and "the staff of the righteous." He possesses "the spirit of wisdom, the spirit of understanding, the spirit of knowledge, and the spirit of might."

Compare this with Isaiah 11:2, the great messianic prophecy: "And the spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD." The author of the Parables was clearly drawing upon Isaiah's prophecy and applying it to the Son of Man figure. For the Christian reader, the connection is obvious and wonderful — Isaiah's Messiah and Enoch's Son of Man find their common fulfillment in Jesus of Nazareth.

Chapter 51 presents the resurrection of the dead. Sheol gives up its dead, and the Elect One sits in judgment, separating the righteous from the wicked. The righteous are clothed in garments of glory and life, while the wicked are delivered to the angels for punishment. This is strikingly similar to our Lord's parable of the sheep and the goats in Matthew 25:31-46, where the Son of Man separates the nations as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats.

Chapters 52 through 57 describe the overthrow of earthly kingdoms and the final establishment of God's rule. The mountains of iron, copper, silver, gold, tin, and lead — representing the power and wealth of worldly empires — are melted before the presence of the Elect One "as wax before the fire." Here we hear the echo of Psalm 97:5: "The hills melted like wax at the presence of the LORD, at the presence of the Lord of the whole earth." The message is clear: no earthly power can stand before the Son of Man when he comes in judgment.

Now, the great question that these chapters pose for us is this: How did the Jewish world, before the coming of Christ, come to conceive of such a figure? The answer lies in the progressive unfolding of messianic revelation throughout the Old Testament. The seed of the woman who would crush the serpent's head (Genesis 3:15). The royal son of David who would reign forever (2 Samuel 7:12-16). The Suffering Servant of Isaiah who would bear the sins of many (Isaiah 53). The Son of Man of Daniel who would receive everlasting dominion. The Parables of Enoch represent a synthesis of these strands — an attempt by a devout Jewish writer to bring together the various messianic prophecies into a unified portrait.

John Owen, reflecting on the progressive nature of divine revelation, wrote that God revealed His purposes "by degrees, as the church was able to bear it." Each generation received more light, building upon what had gone before. The Enochic tradition represents one such moment of deepening understanding — imperfect, incomplete, and extra-canonical, but nonetheless a witness to the longing of Israel for a divine deliverer.

And that deliverer came. He came in a way that exceeded all expectation — not merely as a heavenly judge but as a suffering servant, not only as a glorious king but as a crucified savior. The Son of Man of whom Enoch dreamed was more wonderful and more merciful than even the most exalted apocalyptic vision could have imagined.

--- Application ---

1. See in the Son of Man tradition the breadth of God's preparation for Christ. The Father was preparing hearts and minds for centuries before the incarnation — through prophets, through visions, through the longings of the faithful.

2. Remember that Christ's authority is not merely spiritual but cosmic. He does not merely save souls; He overthrows kingdoms. Every mountain of human pride and power will melt before Him like wax.

3. Take comfort in the resurrection hope. Sheol shall give up its dead. The graves shall open. Those who sleep in Christ shall be raised in glory. This is not merely an Enochic dream but the sure promise of the gospel.

4. Rest in the preexistence and eternal purpose of Christ. He was chosen before the stars were made. Your salvation was planned before the foundation of the world. Nothing in all creation can separate you from the love of God which is in Christ Jesus.

--- Closing ---

Eternal Father, we bless Thee for the glorious revelation of Thy Son — the Son of Man, the Elect One, the Righteous One, our Lord Jesus Christ. From before the foundation of the world, Thou didst purpose our redemption in Him. We worship Him who sits upon the throne of glory and who shall judge the living and the dead. Come quickly, Lord Jesus. Amen.

Grace be with you, dear friends. May the vision of Christ enthroned sustain you in every trial until we meet again.
