PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 25
"The Mystery of Providence — John Flavel"
Reading Day — Flavel on Tracing God's Hand


Welcome. Today is a Reading day. We return to John Flavel for his remarkable treatise The Mystery of Providence — a work that trains the believer to recognize and trace the hand of God in the events of daily life.

Let us read Psalm 77:19 (KJV):

"Thy way is in the sea, and thy path in the great waters, and thy footsteps are not known."


John Flavel wrote The Mystery of Providence to help Christians develop what he called spiritual sight — the ability to see God's hand in the events of life that seem random, confusing, or even cruel. His thesis is simple: God is always working, even when His footsteps are not known.

Flavel begins with a foundational principle: nothing happens by chance. Every event in your life — every meeting, every delay, every loss, every gain, every opened door and every closed one — is ordered by the sovereign providence of God. This does not mean that every event is pleasant or immediately comprehensible. It means that every event is purposeful. God is writing a story with your life, and He does not waste a single page.

Flavel then offers practical counsel for tracing providence — learning to recognize God's hand in retrospect, even when it was invisible in the moment.

First, keep a record. Flavel strongly recommended what he called a providence journal — a written record of God's dealings with you over time. When you receive an unexpected blessing, write it down. When you are delivered from danger, write it down. When prayer is answered, write it down. When circumstances align in ways that cannot be explained by coincidence, write it down. Over months and years, patterns will emerge. You will see connections between events that seemed unrelated at the time. You will trace threads of purpose running through the fabric of your life.

Second, look for the timing of God. Flavel noted that God's timing is often the most remarkable evidence of His providence. He arrives not a moment too early and not a moment too late. The help comes at the precise moment when human resources are exhausted. The door opens at the exact time when every other door has closed. The word of comfort reaches you on the very day when despair threatens to overwhelm. These timings are not coincidences. They are signatures — the handwriting of God on the pages of your life.

Third, look for the means God uses. God often accomplishes His purposes through unexpected channels. The Puritans loved to trace how God used ordinary, even unlikely instruments to achieve His ends. A chance conversation leads to a life-changing decision. An illness forces a change of plans that turns out to be a blessing. An enemy's opposition drives you toward an opportunity you would never have pursued on your own. Flavel writes that God delights in using the weak, the unlikely, and the foolish to confound the strong and the wise.

Fourth, compare God's dealings with you to His dealings in Scripture. The Bible is full of providence stories — Joseph, Ruth, Esther, David, Daniel — and the patterns of God's work are remarkably consistent. He brings good out of evil. He exalts the humble and humbles the proud. He rescues at the last moment. He turns mourning into dancing. When you see these same patterns in your own life, you are seeing providence at work.

Fifth, meditate on the providence you have already experienced. Flavel warns against the sin of forgetting — the tendency to enjoy God's blessings in the moment and then forget them entirely. Israel's constant forgetfulness in the wilderness is a warning. They saw the Red Sea parted, ate manna from heaven, drank water from the rock — and then complained at the next difficulty as if God had never done anything for them. The remedy is regular, deliberate meditation on past mercies.

Flavel closes with a beautiful image. He compares the believer's life to a tapestry. On the underside, you see only tangled threads, knots, and loose ends. The pattern is invisible. But on the upper side — which only God can see — a beautiful design is emerging. In eternity, you will see the upper side. You will see how every thread — every joy, every sorrow, every confusion, every delay — contributed to a design of breathtaking beauty.

Until that day, walk by faith. Trust the weaver. And keep your eyes open for His footsteps, even in the deep waters.


God is writing your story. Every page matters. Every event counts. Trust the Author, even when the plot confuses you. Lord, open my eyes to see Your providence. Help me to trace Your hand in the ordinary events of my life. And when Your footsteps are hidden, give me the faith to trust the weaver who sees the tapestry from above. Amen.
