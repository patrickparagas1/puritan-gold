PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 12
"The Woman of Influence"
John Angell James — Female Piety

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight James broadens our view to consider the godly woman's influence beyond the walls of the home — in the church, in the community, and in the world. Let us come before the Lord.

Let us pray. Gracious Father, You have given every member of this family gifts and callings that extend beyond our own household. Show us tonight how faith at home becomes influence in the world. Help us to be salt and light wherever You place us. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Acts 9:36-39. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Now there was at Joppa a certain disciple named Tabitha, which by interpretation is called Dorcas: this woman was full of good works and almsdeeds which she did. And it came to pass in those days, that she was sick, and died: whom when they had washed, they laid her in an upper chamber. And forasmuch as Lydda was nigh to Joppa, and the disciples had heard that Peter was there, they sent unto him two men, desiring him that he would not delay to come to them. Then Peter arose and went with them. When he was come, they brought him into the upper chamber: and all the widows stood by him weeping, and shewing the coats and garments which Dorcas made, while she was with them."

That is Acts 9:36-39. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

John Angell James understood that the godly woman's influence does not stop at the threshold of her home. While the home is her primary sphere and her most important field of labor, her faith overflows into the church, the community, and the wider world. James devotes an entire section of "Female Piety" to what he calls the "philanthropy" of the Christian woman — her active benevolence toward those in need.

The story of Dorcas, or Tabitha, provides a vivid biblical example. Here was a woman whose life was summed up in one phrase: "full of good works and almsdeeds which she did." When she died, the widows of Joppa gathered around her body and wept, holding up the coats and garments she had made for them with her own hands. Her legacy was not wealth, status, or fame — it was the tangible evidence of love expressed in practical service. Every garment was a sermon. Every coat was a testimony. The work of her hands proclaimed the compassion of her heart, and the compassion of her heart reflected the compassion of her Savior.

James writes that the godly woman possesses a sensitivity to suffering and a readiness to help that can reach people who might be overlooked by others. She notices the neighbor who is grieving. She sees the child who is hungry. She hears the cry of the widow. And she responds — not with grand gestures that attract attention, but with quiet, practical, personal acts of mercy. A meal delivered. A letter written. A visit paid. A prayer offered. These small acts of love, repeated over a lifetime, create a legacy of influence that radiates far beyond what the eye can see.

James also addresses the woman's role in the church. While the Puritans and their heirs generally reserved the office of preaching and formal teaching to men, they recognized that women played an indispensable role in the life of the congregation. Women taught other women and children. They organized charitable efforts. They visited the sick and the poor. They offered hospitality to strangers and to visiting ministers. They prayed — and their prayers were often the hidden engine that powered the visible work of the church. James observes that in almost every congregation he had served, the most faithful, most devoted, most prayerful members were women.

James extends the woman's influence to the realm of social reform. He wrote during an era of great moral and social upheaval, and he saw godly women at the forefront of efforts to combat slavery, to rescue children from factory exploitation, to establish schools for the poor, and to care for the sick and the imprisoned. He argues that female piety, far from confining women to a narrow sphere, actually liberates them to serve in the widest possible arena — wherever there is suffering, wherever there is injustice, wherever there is a need that compassion and faith can address.

But James is always careful to insist that outward activism without inward piety is empty. Charitable work that does not flow from a heart devoted to Christ becomes mere philanthropy — commendable, perhaps, but powerless to address the deepest needs of the human soul. The godly woman serves not to earn God's favor but because she has received it. She gives not out of obligation but out of overflow. Her love for others is the natural fruit of God's love for her.

Tonight, let us consider: how is our family's faith flowing outward? Are we serving our church, our neighbors, our community? Are we noticing the needs around us and responding with practical love? The influence of a single family, living faithfully for Christ, can transform a neighborhood. The influence of a single woman, full of good works like Dorcas, can transform a city.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: The widows wept over Dorcas and showed the garments she had made. What does this tell us about the kind of legacy that matters most?

Question 2: James says that outward service without inward piety is empty. How do we keep our acts of kindness rooted in genuine love for Christ?

Question 3: Is there a need in our church or community that our family could respond to together? What practical step could we take this week?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"This woman was full of good works and almsdeeds which she did." -- Acts 9:36

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, make our family a family of good works. Give us eyes to see the needs around us and hearts willing to meet them. Let our faith overflow the walls of our home and bring blessing to our church, our neighborhood, and our world. Use us as instruments of Your love. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
