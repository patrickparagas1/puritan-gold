PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 4
"Training Up a Child"
William Gouge — Domestical Duties

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight we turn to one of the most urgent topics in Gouge's great work — the duty of parents toward their children. Let us come humbly before the Lord.

Let us pray. Father of all mercies, we thank You for the children in our family. They are a heritage from You, and we steward them with trembling joy. Give us wisdom tonight to hear what You would say to us about the sacred task of raising children for Your glory. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Deuteronomy 6:4-9. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God is one Lord: And thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up. And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes. And thou shalt write them upon the posts of thy house, and on thy gates."

That is Deuteronomy 6:4-9. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Of all the duties William Gouge addresses in "Of Domesticall Duties," none receives more passionate attention than the duty of parents toward their children. Gouge believed — as did all the Puritans — that the single most important work a parent can do is to bring a child to the knowledge of God. Every other parental duty, however important, is subordinate to this one great calling.

Gouge grounds his teaching in Deuteronomy 6, which he calls the great charter of parental instruction. Moses commands the people of Israel to teach God's Word to their children diligently — not occasionally, not casually, but diligently. And this instruction is to be woven into the very fabric of daily life: when you sit in your house, when you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up. In other words, there is no moment of the day when a parent is not teaching, either by word or by example. The home is a school, and the parents are the teachers, and the curriculum is the Word of God.

Gouge identifies two great branches of parental duty: instruction and discipline. Under instruction, he includes teaching children the Scriptures, the catechism, the doctrines of the faith, and the duties of the Christian life. The Puritans were famous for their catechetical practice — systematically teaching children the great truths of the faith through questions and answers. Gouge commends this practice warmly, noting that truths learned in childhood are truths remembered for a lifetime. Many a Puritan who endured persecution and trial in later life traced his strength back to the catechetical instruction he received at his mother's knee.

But instruction is not enough. Gouge also emphasizes the necessity of discipline. Children are born with sinful natures, and without correction, their natural inclinations will lead them into folly and destruction. Proverbs 22:6 says, "Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it." And Proverbs 22:15: "Foolishness is bound in the heart of a child; but the rod of correction shall drive it far from him." Gouge insists that discipline must be administered in love, not in anger. It must be proportionate, consistent, and always accompanied by explanation. A child who is punished but never told why is a child who learns to fear but not to understand. The goal of discipline is not merely outward compliance but inward transformation — a heart that loves righteousness and hates sin.

Gouge also addresses the duty of parents to provide for their children's material and vocational needs. Parents are to feed, clothe, shelter, and educate their children. They are to help them find callings suited to their abilities and temperaments. They are to prepare them for the responsibilities of adult life — marriage, work, civic duty, and church membership. A parent who neglects these duties, Gouge writes, is like a farmer who plants a field but never tends it.

But above all, Gouge returns again and again to the parent's duty to pray for their children. All the instruction and discipline in the world cannot change a child's heart. Only God can do that. And so the faithful parent is, above all, a praying parent — one who brings each child before the throne of grace daily, pleading for their salvation, their sanctification, and their perseverance in faith. Gouge writes that many parents are more diligent about their children's schooling than about their children's souls, and this is a tragedy that will bear bitter fruit in eternity.

Let this word come home to us tonight. Are we teaching our children the Word of God — not just on Sunday but throughout the week? Are we correcting them in love? Are we praying for them with fervent, daily, believing prayer? These are the duties that matter most, and they are the duties that will bear fruit long after we are gone.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Deuteronomy 6 says to teach God's Word "when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way." What are some natural moments in our daily routine where we can talk about God together?

Question 2: Gouge says discipline must be given in love, not in anger, and always with explanation. Why is it important for children to understand the reason behind correction?

Question 3: Gouge says prayer is the most important parental duty of all. Why is prayer even more important than instruction or discipline?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it." -- Proverbs 22:6

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, we confess that parenting is beyond our wisdom and beyond our strength. We need You every hour. Give us grace to teach, to correct, to encourage, and above all to pray for the children You have entrusted to our care. Save our children, Lord. Draw them to Yourself and keep them in the faith all the days of their lives. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
