PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 25
"She Opens Her Mouth with Wisdom"
Proverbs 31:26

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we continue our journey through the Proverbs 31 portrait, and we come to a verse that speaks to one of the most powerful forces in any marriage: the tongue. The words we speak to one another can build up or tear down, can heal or wound, can draw our spouse closer to God or push them further away. Let us listen carefully to what Scripture teaches us about the speech of the virtuous wife.

Let us read our passage. Proverbs 31:26 (KJV):

"She openeth her mouth with wisdom; and in her tongue is the law of kindness."

--- Teaching ---

Solomon gives us two qualities of the virtuous woman's speech: wisdom and kindness. These are not two separate things but two dimensions of the same reality. Her words are wise, meaning they are grounded in truth, shaped by discernment, and aimed at building up rather than tearing down. And her words are kind, meaning they are delivered with gentleness, with compassion, and with a genuine concern for the well-being of the hearer. Wisdom without kindness becomes harsh. Kindness without wisdom becomes flattery. But wisdom and kindness together produce speech that is both truthful and tender.

Matthew Henry, in his commentary on this verse, observed that the law of kindness governs her tongue the way a law governs a nation. It is not merely a suggestion or an occasional practice. It is a settled rule, a binding principle that shapes every word she speaks. She does not speak kindly only when she feels like it or only when her husband deserves it. She speaks with kindness as a matter of principle, because she has determined that her tongue will be an instrument of grace rather than a weapon of destruction.

William Gouge, in his treatment of the duties common to husband and wife, emphasized the critical importance of speech in marriage. He argued that more marriages are damaged by careless, harsh, or bitter words than by almost any other cause. The tongue, Gouge warned, is a small member but capable of setting the whole household on fire. A single cutting remark spoken in anger can undo months of patient love. A pattern of critical, nagging, or contemptuous speech can erode the very foundation of marital trust.

But Gouge also recognized the extraordinary power of gracious speech. The wife who speaks with wisdom and kindness becomes a source of counsel, comfort, and strength to her husband. He comes to her not with dread but with anticipation, knowing that her words will bring light to his confusion, peace to his anxiety, and encouragement to his weariness. She becomes, as Gouge put it, not merely a companion but a counselor, a trusted voice whose wisdom he seeks because he knows it flows from a heart that fears the Lord.

Thomas Watson extended this principle to both husband and wife. He taught that godly speech in marriage requires deliberate cultivation. It does not come naturally to fallen human beings. Our natural inclination is to speak rashly, to respond in anger, to use our knowledge of our spouse's vulnerabilities as ammunition in arguments. The law of kindness must be written on our hearts by the Holy Spirit before it can govern our tongues. This means that gracious speech begins with prayer, with the daily surrender of our tongues to God and the daily request for His grace to season our words with salt and sweetness.

Dear wife, your words carry more weight with your husband than perhaps you know. When you speak wisdom, he is strengthened. When you speak kindness, he is healed. Guard this gift carefully, and use it generously.

Dear husband, do you create a home in which your wife feels safe to speak? Do you receive her wisdom with humility, or do you dismiss it with pride? The man who listens to his wife's wise counsel is not weak. He is, as Proverbs teaches, truly wise.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: How would each of you describe the general tone of speech in your marriage? Is it characterized more by wisdom and kindness, or by criticism and carelessness?

Question 2: Can you recall a specific time when your spouse's words brought you genuine comfort, wisdom, or encouragement? Share that memory tonight.

Question 3: What is one practical step each of you can take this week to bring your speech more fully under the law of kindness?

--- Prayer ---

O Lord, who hast made us in Thine image and given us the gift of speech, we confess that we have too often used our tongues carelessly and even cruelly. Forgive us for the words that have wounded rather than healed, that have torn down rather than built up. Write the law of kindness upon our hearts, that it may govern every word that proceeds from our mouths. Give the wife wisdom and gentleness in her speech, and give the husband ears to hear and a heart to receive. May our words to one another be seasoned with grace, that they may minister life and not death. Through Jesus Christ, our living Word, Amen.
