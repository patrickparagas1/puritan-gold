PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 13
"Providence and the Protection of Our Children"
John Flavel — Providence and Family Trust


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Flavel speaks to one of the deepest concerns of every parent's heart — the safety and protection of our children — and how God's providence covers them even when we cannot. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Heavenly Father, our children belong to You. We entrust them to Your care tonight, asking that Your providence would surround them like a shield. Where we cannot be, You are. Where we cannot protect, You can. Help us to trust You with our most precious treasures. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Psalm 91:1-2, 9-11. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty. I will say of the LORD, He is my refuge and my fortress: my God; in him will I trust. Because thou hast made the LORD, which is my refuge, even the most High, thy habitation; There shall no evil befall thee, neither shall any plague come nigh thy dwelling. For he shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways."

That is Psalm 91:1-2, 9-11. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


There is no anxiety quite like the anxiety of a parent for a child. From the moment a child is born, parents carry a weight of concern that never fully lifts. Is the baby breathing? Is the toddler safe? Is the schoolchild making friends? Is the teenager making wise choices? Every stage of a child's life brings new worries, and the parent's instinct to protect is relentless and exhausting.

John Flavel understood this parental anxiety, and he addressed it with the doctrine of providence. He taught that while parents are the instruments of God's care for their children, they are not the ultimate source of that care. God Himself is the protector of children. His angels are dispatched to guard them. His providence surrounds them like a fortress. And His purposes for them cannot be thwarted by any enemy, any danger, or any circumstance.

This does not mean that children are immune from harm. Flavel was too honest and too experienced to make such a claim. Children do get sick. Accidents do happen. The world is a dangerous place. But Flavel taught that even when harm comes, it comes through the filter of God's sovereign permission, and God never permits anything that does not serve His good and wise purposes. The illness that strikes a child may be the means by which the entire family draws closer to God. The difficulty a child faces at school may be the crucible in which their character is forged. The danger that threatens a child may be the occasion for a display of God's power and faithfulness that the family will remember for generations.

Flavel also taught that one of the most important things parents can do for their children is pray. Prayer is not a substitute for practical care — parents must still lock the doors, teach their children wisdom, and provide a safe home. But prayer enlists the aid of the Almighty in the protection of the family. Flavel believed that the prayers of parents create an invisible shield around their children, invoking the protection of a God who is infinitely more powerful than any threat they face.

Flavel told stories from his own pastoral experience of children who were preserved from danger in ways that could only be explained by divine providence. A child who wandered into traffic but was pulled back at the last moment by a passing stranger. A family that narrowly escaped a disaster because of a seemingly random change of plans. These were not coincidences, Flavel said. They were the fingerprints of a providential God who watches over children with a special tenderness.

But what about the times when the worst does happen? What about the families who lose children, or whose children suffer in ways that no parent can fix? Flavel did not shy away from this agonizing question. He acknowledged that some of God's providences are deeply painful and that the death or suffering of a child is one of the heaviest burdens a family can bear. In such moments, Flavel counseled families to cling to two unshakable truths: first, that God is still good, even when the evidence seems to scream otherwise; and second, that this life is not the end of the story. For the children of believers, death is not a final separation but a temporary parting. They are in the arms of Christ, and one day the entire family will be reunited in a place where suffering and death are no more.

Tonight, let parents and children alike find comfort in the providence of God. Parents, release the weight of anxiety and entrust your children to the One who loves them even more than you do. Children, know that God's eye is upon you, His angels surround you, and His purposes for your life are good.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Why is it sometimes hard for parents to trust God with their children's safety? What does the doctrine of providence teach us about God's care for our children?

Question 2: Can you think of a time when God protected someone in our family in a way that seemed providential?

Question 3: How can prayer help parents with the anxiety they feel for their children? What is one thing we can pray for each family member tonight?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"For he shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways." -- Psalm 91:11

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Father, we place our children into Your hands tonight. You formed them in the womb. You called them by name. You know every hair on their heads. Protect them by Your providence, surround them with Your angels, and fulfill Your purposes in their lives. We trust You with these precious lives. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, children, guarded by the God who never slumbers nor sleeps.
