PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 4
"Guarding Your Hearts Together"
Proverbs 4:23


Good evening. What a blessing it is to see a husband and wife seeking the Lord together. The Puritans believed that a praying couple is an unstoppable force for the kingdom of God. Let us pray together tonight.

The Word of God has sustained marriages for thousands of years. Tonight it speaks to yours from Proverbs 4:23:

"Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."

That is Proverbs 4:23. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher. Matthew Henry reflected deeply on the truths of Scripture and how they apply to the covenant of marriage.

Solomon here issues one of the most searching commands in all of Scripture. The heart, in the biblical sense, is not merely the seat of emotions; it is the center of the whole person, encompassing the mind, will, affections, and conscience. Matthew Henry writes: 'The heart is the main fortress; whatever we have in the world, our greatest care must be to keep our heart.' If the heart is not kept, no outward conformity to religion can avail.

Henry emphasizes the word 'diligence.' The Hebrew carries the sense of a military guard keeping watch over a besieged city. The heart must be guarded with that kind of vigilance because it is under constant assault. Temptations approach from every side: the world offers its allurements, the flesh stirs its desires, and the devil whispers his lies. Without diligent keeping, the heart will be taken captive.

The reason for this diligence is profound: 'out of it are the issues of life.' Henry comments that as a spring is the source of all the streams that flow from it, so the heart is the source of all that a person does. If the spring is poisoned, every stream is poisoned. If the heart is corrupt, every action, word, and thought will bear the marks of that corruption. Conversely, if the heart is kept pure and tender before God, the whole life will reflect that purity.

Charles Bridges applies this to the realm of relationships: 'How much of the happiness or misery of life depends upon the state of the heart!' In marriage, this is especially true. A heart harboring bitterness will produce bitter words. A heart indulging in pride will resist humility and repentance. A heart drawn away by worldly desires will neglect spiritual duties. The couple who keeps their hearts with all diligence keeps their marriage with all diligence.


Your marriage will never be healthier than your hearts. Every conflict, every season of distance, every moment of tenderness or coldness flows from the condition of your hearts before God.

Guarding your hearts together means several things. It means being vigilant about what you allow into your minds: the media you consume, the relationships you maintain, the thoughts you entertain. It means being honest with each other about the state of your hearts. When bitterness begins to take root, confess it. When temptation presses in, bring it into the light. When spiritual apathy creeps in, sound the alarm.

You are called to be watchmen for one another. The husband should guard his wife's heart by creating an atmosphere of safety, affection, and spiritual nourishment. The wife should guard her husband's heart by offering respect, encouragement, and honest accountability. Together, you guard the heart of your home.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Here are some questions to guide your conversation tonight. Take turns sharing, and give each other the gift of your full attention.

Question 1: What influences in our life right now may be threatening the health of our hearts?

Question 2: Is there anything in your heart — bitterness, fear, lust, discouragement — that you need to bring into the open today?

Question 3: How can we better serve as watchmen for each other's hearts?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Prayer is the heartbeat of a Christian marriage. Let us pray together now. You do not need eloquent words, just honest ones.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray that God would search your hearts and reveal anything that is not pleasing to Him. Ask for the grace of diligence in guarding your hearts, and pray that the issues flowing from your hearts would bring life and blessing to your marriage and home.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the Lord who searches all hearts keep yours with His own hand, that from the wellspring of your life together may flow rivers of righteousness and peace. Amen.

May your love for each other grow deeper with every passing day, and may your love for God be the source and the strength of it all. Goodnight, dear ones.
