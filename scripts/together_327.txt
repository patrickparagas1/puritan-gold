PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 27
"Iron Sharpens Iron"
Proverbs 27:17

---

Good evening, dear couple. The Puritans believed that marriage was one of the greatest means of grace God provides. Tonight we enter into that grace together. Let us open our hearts and our ears to what the Lord has for us.

As we settle in together, let us hear from Proverbs 27:17:

"Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend."

That is Proverbs 27:17. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

One of the gifts of the Christian tradition is that we do not study alone. We have the wisdom of the saints who came before us. Tonight we hear from Charles Bridges.

Charles Bridges finds in this familiar proverb a profound truth about the nature of godly relationships. Iron sharpens iron through friction: the two pieces must press against one another with force. This is not a comfortable process, but it is a necessary one. Without friction, both pieces remain dull.

Bridges writes: 'The sharpening of iron by iron illustrates the mutual benefit of intimate friendship. It is not a passive process. It requires contact, pressure, and even sparks. But the result is that both pieces are made sharper, more useful, more fit for their purpose.'

Bridges is careful to distinguish sharpening from merely wounding. Sharpening has a constructive purpose: to make the other more effective, more holy, more like Christ. Wounding serves only to harm. The difference lies in motive and method. Sharpening is done in love, with truth, at the right time, and with sensitivity. Wounding is done in anger, with cruelty, without regard for the other's wellbeing.

Matthew Henry adds that the 'countenance' that is sharpened refers not merely to outward appearance but to the whole demeanor of a person: their character, their resolve, their clarity of purpose. Henry writes: 'Good men are made better by conversing with those that are good.' When two faithful friends engage in honest, challenging, encouraging conversation, both emerge sharper. For the married couple, this means that your most intimate relationship is also your greatest opportunity for spiritual growth.

--- Marriage Application ---

Your spouse is God's primary instrument for sharpening your character. This is one of the great purposes of marriage: to sanctify you. But sharpening involves friction, and friction is uncomfortable.

When your spouse challenges you, when they point out a blind spot, when they hold you accountable, do you receive it as sharpening or resent it as wounding? And when you need to sharpen your spouse, do you do it with love, humility, and patience, or with harshness and superiority?

Healthy marriages have a culture of mutual sharpening: both partners give and receive constructive challenge. This requires trust (knowing the other has your good at heart), humility (being willing to hear hard things), and courage (being willing to speak hard things). Build that culture in your marriage, and watch both of you grow sharper together.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Discussion time. Husband, listen to your wife. Wife, listen to your husband. Let this be a safe and sacred space.

Question 1: Do I welcome or resist my spouse's attempts to sharpen my character?

Question 2: When I try to sharpen my spouse, is my motive and method loving and constructive, or harsh and critical?

Question 3: In what area of character do I most need to be sharpened right now, and am I open to my spouse's help?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Let us close our eyes and open our hearts to the Lord in prayer. Husband, pray for your wife. Wife, pray for your husband. There is no greater gift you can give each other.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God for the humility to be sharpened by your spouse and the love to sharpen them well. Pray that the friction in your marriage would be constructive, not destructive, and that both of you would emerge sharper, holier, and more fit for God's purposes.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord who refines as fire and sharpens as iron use your marriage to make you both more effective instruments in His hand. Amen.

Go in peace, husband and wife. The God who ordained your marriage is faithful to sustain it. Trust Him. Love each other. And come back tomorrow for more. Goodnight.
