PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 20
"Walking in Integrity Together"
Proverbs 20:7


Good evening, dear couple. The Puritans believed that marriage was one of the greatest means of grace God provides. Tonight we enter into that grace together. Let us open our hearts and our ears to what the Lord has for us.

As we settle in together, let us hear from Proverbs 20:7:

"The just man walketh in his integrity: his children are blessed after him."

That is Proverbs 20:7. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


One of the gifts of the Christian tradition is that we do not study alone. We have the wisdom of the saints who came before us. Tonight we hear from Matthew Henry.

Matthew Henry treats this verse as a comprehensive portrait of the faithful family man. 'The just man walketh in his integrity,' Henry writes, meaning that he does not merely perform isolated acts of righteousness but walks a consistent path. His integrity is not a mask worn in public; it is the settled character of his heart. He is the same man in his home as he is in his church, the same in private as in public.

Henry notes that 'walketh' implies a manner of life, not a single step. Integrity is not achieved in a moment but maintained over a lifetime. The just man walks through temptation without yielding, through adversity without compromising, through prosperity without becoming proud. His steps are ordered by the Lord because his heart is submitted to the Lord.

The promise extends beyond the individual: 'his children are blessed after him.' Henry explains: 'The posterity of good people may reckon upon blessings, not by virtue of their parents' merit, but by virtue of God's covenant.' The godly parent who walks in integrity leaves an inheritance far more valuable than houses and riches: a legacy of faith, a pattern of righteousness, a testimony that the ways of God are true.

Charles Bridges adds that the reverse is also tragically true: the parent who walks in hypocrisy or compromise leaves a legacy of confusion and spiritual harm to the next generation. Bridges writes: 'What our children inherit most is not our wealth but our character. They observe us more closely than they listen to us, and they will follow our walk more readily than our words.'


Integrity in marriage means that your private life matches your public profession. It means that the way you treat your spouse behind closed doors reflects the faith you profess before the world. It means honesty in finances, faithfulness in sexuality, consistency in discipline, and transparency in communication.

But notice the generational dimension: your children are watching. They are learning from you not merely what you teach but how you live. A father who preaches kindness but speaks harshly to his wife is teaching his children that words and actions need not align. A mother who teaches faithfulness but secretly harbors bitterness is teaching her children that appearances matter more than reality.

Walk in integrity together. Let your children see a marriage that is genuinely striving after godliness: imperfect, yes, but honest, repentant, and growing. That is the greatest inheritance you can leave them.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Discussion time. Husband, listen to your wife. Wife, listen to your husband. Let this be a safe and sacred space.

Question 1: Is our marriage marked by integrity — consistency between what we profess and how we live at home?

Question 2: What are our children learning from watching our marriage, and is that what we want them to learn?

Question 3: Is there an area of our life where we have been living with a gap between our public profession and our private reality?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Let us close our eyes and open our hearts to the Lord in prayer. Husband, pray for your wife. Wife, pray for your husband. There is no greater gift you can give each other.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God for the grace of integrity: that your walk would match your talk, your home life would reflect your faith, and your children would be blessed by the consistency of your godly example. Confess any areas of hypocrisy and ask for renewal.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the God of truth make you a couple of integrity, that your children and your children's children may be blessed by the faithful path you walk together. Amen.

Go in peace, husband and wife. The God who ordained your marriage is faithful to sustain it. Trust Him. Love each other. And come back tomorrow for more. Goodnight.
