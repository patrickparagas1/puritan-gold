PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 10
"Love That Covers"
Proverbs 10:12

---

Welcome, dear ones. Marriage is a gift from God, and so is this time together in His presence. Whether this has been an easy day or a hard one, you are here, together, and that matters more than you know.

Our Scripture reading this evening is from Proverbs 10:12. Let us receive it together:

"Hatred stirreth up strifes: but love covereth all sins."

That is Proverbs 10:12. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

The Puritans understood marriage in a way that the modern world has largely forgotten. Let us hear from Matthew Henry, who wrote with great wisdom on this subject.

Here Solomon presents a stark contrast that speaks directly to the health of every relationship, and especially to marriage. Matthew Henry observes that hatred is a stirrer: it 'stirs up strifes,' raking through old grievances, magnifying small offenses, imputing the worst motives, and refusing to let any fault go unmentioned. Hatred is, as Henry puts it, 'an incendiary that blows the coals of contention.'

In contrast, love covers. Henry explains: 'Love covers all sins; not by conniving at them or approving of them, but by forgiving them and not exposing them.' This is not a love that ignores sin but one that deals with it graciously. Love does not keep a ledger of wrongs. Love does not bring up past failures in the heat of present arguments. Love does not broadcast a spouse's weaknesses to friends and family.

The apostle Peter quotes this proverb in his first epistle (1 Peter 4:8), adding the urgency: 'Above all things have fervent charity among yourselves: for charity shall cover the multitude of sins.' Henry notes that Peter's 'above all things' indicates that this covering love is not optional but is the highest expression of Christian community.

Charles Bridges adds a sobering observation: in marriage, we see one another's sins more clearly than anyone else does. We know each other's weaknesses, failures, and secret struggles. This intimate knowledge gives us a choice: we can use it as ammunition in conflict (hatred stirring up strife), or we can cover it with patient, forgiving, restoring love. Bridges writes that 'the love which covers is not blind love; it is love that sees clearly and chooses mercy.'

--- Marriage Application ---

In the close quarters of marriage, you will sin against each other. This is not a possibility but a certainty. The question is not whether offenses will come, but how you will respond when they do.

Hatred stirs up strife. In a marriage context, this looks like keeping score, bringing up old arguments, refusing to forgive, punishing with silence, and interpreting your spouse's actions in the worst possible light. It is the slow poison that kills marriages from the inside.

Love covers. This looks like choosing to forgive before an apology is offered. It means refusing to bring up forgiven offenses. It means speaking well of your spouse to others, even when you are frustrated. It means dealing with sin directly but gently, seeking restoration rather than retribution. The covering love of marriage is a reflection of the covering love of Christ, who bore our sins upon the cross that we might be forgiven.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

The Puritans practiced what they called holy conference, speaking honestly about spiritual things with those they loved most. Do that now with these questions.

Question 1: Am I more inclined to stir up strife or to cover offenses with love in our marriage?

Question 2: Is there a past hurt that I have claimed to forgive but continue to bring up or hold against my spouse?

Question 3: How can we cultivate a culture of covering love in our home, where grace is the default response to failure?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

It is time to bring what we have heard and discussed before the Lord in prayer. Pray out loud if you can. Let your spouse hear your heart.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask the Lord to give you the kind of love that covers rather than exposes. Confess any areas where you have stirred up strife through unforgiveness or harsh words. Pray for the grace to reflect Christ's covering love in your marriage.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the love of Christ, which covers a multitude of sins, so fill your hearts that your marriage becomes a sanctuary of grace, forgiveness, and peace. Amen.

May the God who brought you together continue to bind you together in love, in faith, and in purpose. Sleep well tonight. He watches over your marriage.
