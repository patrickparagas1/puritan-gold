PURITAN GOLD -- Study Devotional, Day 21
Series: Romans
"The Remnant and the Olive Tree"
God's faithfulness to His remnant and the grafting in of the Gentiles

---

Good evening. Whether this is your first time joining our study series or you have been with us from the beginning, you are welcome. God's Word is inexhaustible, and there is always more to discover.

We are studying the book of Romans together. Tonight's lesson is titled "The Remnant and the Olive Tree." Each session builds on what came before, so if you have been following along, you will see how tonight's passage connects to the larger argument of the book.

--- Scripture Reading ---

Let us read our passage for tonight. It comes from Romans 11:1-24:

"I say then, Hath God cast away his people? God forbid. For I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin... Even so then at this present time also there is a remnant according to the election of grace. And if by grace, then is it no more of works: otherwise grace is no more grace... For if the firstfruit be holy, the lump is also holy: and if the root be holy, so are the branches. And if some of the branches be broken off, and thou, being a wild olive tree, wert graffed in among them, and with them partakest of the root and fatness of the olive tree; Boast not against the branches. But if thou boast, thou bearest not the root, but the root thee... For if God spared not the natural branches, take heed lest he also spare not thee. Behold therefore the goodness and severity of God: on them which fell, severity; but toward thee, goodness, if thou continue in his goodness: otherwise thou also shalt be cut off."

Read that passage again to yourself. Mark any words or phrases that stand out. The Puritans read with a pen in hand, always ready to note what God was showing them.

--- Key Verse ---

The Puritans called the key verse of a passage its 'doctrine text.' Tonight, ours is this:

"Even so then at this present time also there is a remnant according to the election of grace." -- Romans 11:5

This verse is the lens through which we will examine the rest of tonight's passage. Keep it in the front of your mind as we proceed.

--- Exposition ---

The text is before us. Let us dig into its riches.

Charles Hodge offers valuable commentary on this passage. The Puritans were expositors of the highest order, and their insights remain remarkably fresh.

Charles Hodge brought careful exegetical precision to Romans 11:1-24, arguing that this passage demonstrates God's continued faithfulness to Israel while establishing the proper relationship between Jewish and Gentile believers. Hodge stressed that Paul's first proof that God has not rejected Israel is Paul himself: 'For I also am an Israelite.' If God had rejected every Israelite, Paul would not be an apostle. The existence of even one believing Jew disproves total rejection.

Hodge gave extended treatment to the concept of the remnant in verses 2-6. He drew the parallel with Elijah's day, when the discouraged prophet believed he was the only faithful Israelite left. God corrected him: 'I have reserved to myself seven thousand men, who have not bowed the knee to the image of Baal.' Hodge argued that God always preserves a remnant within Israel 'according to the election of grace.' This remnant is not self-selected but God-selected, not earned but given. Verse 6 makes this emphatic: 'And if by grace, then is it no more of works: otherwise grace is no more grace.' Grace and works are mutually exclusive principles of selection.

Hodge was particularly careful with the olive tree analogy in verses 16-24. He argued that the olive tree represents the covenant people of God across all ages — the one people of God stretching from Abraham to the present. The natural branches (Jews) were broken off through unbelief, and wild branches (Gentiles) were grafted in through faith. Hodge warned Gentile Christians against arrogance in three ways. First, the root supports you, not the reverse — Christianity is built on the foundation of the patriarchs and prophets. Second, the natural branches were broken off through unbelief, and you stand only by faith — presume on your position and you too may be removed. Third, God is able to graft the natural branches back in, and He will — as Paul will argue in the following passage.

Romans 11:1-24 addresses the question that has been lurking since chapter 9: has God abandoned Israel? Paul's answer is emphatic and multifaceted: God forbid. God has not cast away His people, and Paul proves it in three ways.

First, Paul himself is living proof (verse 1): 'For I also am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.' If God had rejected all Israel, Paul would not be a believer. His very existence as a Jewish Christian refutes the claim of total rejection.

Second, God has always preserved a remnant (verses 2-6). Paul draws on the story of Elijah from 1 Kings 19. Elijah, fleeing from Jezebel, despaired that he was the last faithful Israelite: 'Lord, they have killed thy prophets, and digged down thine altars; and I am left alone.' But God responded: 'I have reserved to myself seven thousand men, who have not bowed the knee to the image of Baal.' Note the crucial phrase: 'I have reserved to myself.' The remnant was not self-preserved but God-preserved. They did not keep themselves faithful; God kept them faithful.

Paul applies this to his own day: 'Even so then at this present time also there is a remnant according to the election of grace' (verse 5). The Jewish Christians of Paul's day — Peter, James, John, the thousands who believed at Pentecost — were the remnant, chosen by grace. Verse 6 nails down the principle: 'And if by grace, then is it no more of works: otherwise grace is no more grace.' Grace and works cannot be mixed as grounds of election. Either God's choice is free and unmerited, or it is conditioned on human performance. It cannot be both.

Verses 7-10 describe the condition of the rest of Israel: 'Israel hath not obtained that which he seeketh for; but the election hath obtained it, and the rest were blinded.' Paul quotes Isaiah 29:10 and Psalm 69:22-23 to describe a judicial hardening — God gave them 'the spirit of slumber, eyes that they should not see, and ears that they should not hear.' This is the same pattern as Pharaoh: those who persistently reject the light are given over to darkness.

But this hardening is not final or purposeless. Verses 11-12 reveal God's redemptive strategy: 'Have they stumbled that they should fall? God forbid: but rather through their fall salvation is come unto the Gentiles, for to provoke them to jealousy.' Israel's rejection of the gospel opened the door for Gentile salvation. But even this was part of God's plan to provoke Israel to jealousy — to make them desire the blessings they see the Gentiles receiving. 'Now if the fall of them be the riches of the world, and the diminishing of them the riches of the Gentiles; how much more their fulness?' If Israel's failure brought such blessing, imagine what Israel's restoration will bring.

Verses 13-15 explain Paul's strategy: he magnifies his ministry to the Gentiles precisely in order to provoke his own people to jealousy and save some of them. 'For if the casting away of them be the reconciling of the world, what shall the receiving of them be, but life from the dead?' Israel's future restoration will be like resurrection — an explosion of life and spiritual vitality.

Verses 16-24 present the olive tree analogy — one of the most vivid metaphors in the New Testament. The olive tree represents the covenant people of God. The root is the patriarchs — Abraham, Isaac, Jacob — and the covenant promises God made to them. The natural branches are the Jewish people. Some of these branches have been broken off through unbelief. Wild olive branches — Gentile believers — have been grafted in, sharing in the rich sap of the covenant promises.

Paul warns the Gentile believers against three errors. First, 'boast not against the branches' (verse 18). Gentile Christians must not look down on the Jewish people with contempt. Remember: 'thou bearest not the root, but the root thee.' Christianity did not replace Judaism; it grew from the same root. The patriarchs, the prophets, the Scriptures, the covenants — these are the foundation on which the church is built.

Second, do not presume on your position (verses 19-22). The natural branches were broken off through unbelief, and you stand by faith. 'Be not highminded, but fear.' If God did not spare the natural branches, He will not spare grafted-in branches that become proud and unbelieving. 'Behold therefore the goodness and severity of God: on them which fell, severity; but toward thee, goodness, if thou continue in his goodness.' This is not teaching that individual believers can lose their salvation but that professing Christian communities that abandon faith will be cut off just as Israel was.

Third, do not underestimate God's power to restore Israel (verses 23-24). 'For if thou wert cut out of the olive tree which is wild by nature, and wert graffed contrary to nature into a good olive tree: how much more shall these, which be the natural branches, be graffed into their own olive tree?' If God could do the harder thing — grafting wild Gentile branches into a cultivated Jewish tree — He can certainly do the easier thing: regrafting the natural branches back in.

--- Application ---

The true test of understanding is application. If we have truly grasped tonight's passage, it will change the way we live tomorrow.

Romans 11:1-24 shapes how we relate to Israel, to grace, and to our own security in Christ.

First, cultivate gratitude for your spiritual heritage. As a Gentile believer, you have been grafted into a tree whose roots go back to Abraham. You are a beneficiary of promises made to the patriarchs, truths revealed through the prophets, and a covenant sealed in the blood of a Jewish Messiah. Thank God for the Jewish roots of your faith. Read the Old Testament with appreciation, not merely as background material but as the foundation of everything you believe.

Second, guard against spiritual arrogance. 'Be not highminded, but fear.' It is possible for churches, denominations, and entire Christian cultures to become so proud of their traditions, their theology, or their spiritual achievements that they forget they stand by grace alone. When you are tempted to look down on other believers, other traditions, or other peoples, remember: the root supports you. You did not arrive at faith by your own cleverness. You were grafted in by grace.

Third, pray for the Jewish people. If Israel's rejection brought blessing to the world, their acceptance will bring 'life from the dead.' Pray for Jewish people to recognize Yeshua (Jesus) as their Messiah. Support ministries that share the gospel with the Jewish community. And do so with the humility and love that Paul models throughout this chapter.

Fourth, behold both the goodness and severity of God. We tend to emphasize one at the expense of the other. Those who focus only on goodness become presumptuous. Those who focus only on severity become fearful. Hold both truths together: God is infinitely good to those who trust Him and infinitely serious about unbelief. Let this produce a healthy, reverent, joyful faith.

Write down one concrete action you will take this week in response to tonight's study. Do not let this truth remain theoretical. The Puritans measured the success of a sermon not by how much the hearer learned but by how much the hearer changed.

--- Closing ---

Faithful God, You have not cast away Your people. You preserve a remnant by grace in every generation. We thank You for grafting us, wild branches, into Your holy olive tree. Humble us. Remove every trace of arrogance toward Israel or toward any of our brothers and sisters. We behold Your goodness and Your severity, and we worship. Restore Israel according to Your promises. Provoke them to jealousy by the genuineness of our faith and the beauty of our lives. And keep us standing by faith, never presuming on grace. Through Jesus Christ, the Root and Offspring of David. Amen.

May the Lord bless your continued study of His Word. Until next time.
