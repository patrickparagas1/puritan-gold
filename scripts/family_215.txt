PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 23
"The Mystery of Contentment"
Jeremiah Burroughs — The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment for Families

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Burroughs reveals what he called "the mystery of contentment" — the surprising, counterintuitive secret that enables Christians to be content in any circumstance. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, the world says contentment comes from getting more. Your Word says it comes from a different source entirely. Reveal that mystery to our family tonight, and teach us the secret that Paul learned. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from 2 Corinthians 12:9-10. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me. Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong."

That is 2 Corinthians 12:9-10. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Jeremiah Burroughs called contentment a "mystery" — not because it is unknowable, but because it operates according to a logic that is entirely different from the logic of the world. The world says: Get more, and you will be content. Burroughs says: Learn to need less, and you will be content. The world says: Change your circumstances, and you will be happy. Burroughs says: Change your heart, and you will be happy in any circumstance. This is the great mystery of Christian contentment — it is an inside job.

Burroughs identified several elements of this mystery. The first is what he called "the art of drawing from Christ." The content Christian does not find sufficiency in himself or in his circumstances but in Christ. When the apostle Paul said, "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me," he was not making a claim about human potential. He was confessing his total dependence on Christ. Paul could endure hunger because Christ strengthened him. He could endure plenty because Christ kept him humble. He could endure chains because Christ was his freedom. The secret of contentment is not the absence of need but the presence of Christ in the midst of need.

For families, this means that contentment is not about having the perfect house, the perfect income, or the perfect circumstances. It is about having Christ at the center of your family's life. When Christ is your family's treasure, everything else becomes secondary. You can lose your job and still be rich, because Christ is your portion. You can face illness and still be whole, because Christ is your healer. You can downsize your home and still have more than enough, because Christ is your inheritance.

The second element of the mystery is what Burroughs called "bringing the heart down to the condition" rather than lifting the condition up to the heart. Most people respond to unpleasant circumstances by trying to change them. Burroughs said the content Christian takes a different approach: he brings his heart down to the level of his circumstances, accepting them as God's wise appointment and finding grace within them. This does not mean passivity or resignation. It means a peaceful acceptance that says, "God has placed me here, and there is grace to be found in this place."

The third element is the ability to find sweetness in every condition. Burroughs taught that the content Christian has a kind of spiritual alchemy — the ability to find good in even the most difficult circumstances. In poverty, he discovers the sufficiency of God's provision. In sickness, he discovers the tenderness of God's care. In loneliness, he discovers the intimacy of communion with Christ. This is not naivety or denial. It is the spiritual perception that sees past the surface of circumstances to the deeper reality of God's goodness.

For families, this mystery of contentment is profoundly practical. Consider the family dinner table. A discontent family sits down and notices what is missing — the meal they would rather have, the money they wish they had to eat out, the comparison with what other families enjoy. A content family sits down and notices what is present — the food God provided, the faces of loved ones around the table, the grace that sustains them for another day. Same table. Same food. Entirely different experience.

Tonight, let Burroughs show you the mystery: contentment is not about changing what is around you. It is about changing what is within you — and that change comes from drawing ever closer to Christ.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Burroughs said contentment is about "bringing the heart down to the condition" rather than changing the condition. What does this mean practically for our family?

Question 2: Paul found strength in weakness. Can you think of a time when our family's weakness or lack became an opportunity to experience God's strength?

Question 3: Burroughs said content Christians find sweetness in every condition. What is one difficult circumstance in our family right now where we might look for hidden grace?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness." -- 2 Corinthians 12:9

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord, teach us the mystery of contentment. Help us to draw our strength from Christ, to bring our hearts to our circumstances, and to find sweetness in every condition You appoint for us. Your grace is sufficient. We rest in that tonight. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, strengthened by a grace that is more than enough.
