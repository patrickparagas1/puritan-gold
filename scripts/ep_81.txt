PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"The Law of the Lord Is Perfect"
May 20, 2026

---

"The law of the Lord is perfect, converting the soul: the testimony of the Lord is sure, making wise the simple. The statutes of the Lord are right, rejoicing the heart: the commandment of the Lord is pure, enlightening the eyes. The fear of the Lord is clean, enduring for ever: the judgments of the Lord are true and righteous altogether. More to be desired are they than gold, yea, than much fine gold: sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb." -- Psalm 19:7-11

If the first half of the nineteenth psalm is a hymn to the glory of God in creation, then the second half is a hymn to the glory of God in revelation. And as the sun in the heavens exceeds the stars in brightness, so the Word of God exceeds the works of God in the fullness and clarity of its testimony. For while creation tells us that God is, the Word tells us what God is, what He requires, what He promises, and what He has done for the salvation of sinners. And it is this Word, the written revelation of the mind and will of God, that David now celebrates in some of the most magnificent language ever devoted to the praise of Holy Scripture.

Observe the sixfold description that David gives of the Word of God. He calls it the law of the Lord, the testimony of the Lord, the statutes of the Lord, the commandment of the Lord, the fear of the Lord, and the judgments of the Lord. Each of these terms describes the same Scripture from a different angle, as a diamond reveals different facets when turned in the light. The law speaks of God's Word as instruction, as the teaching of a wise Father. The testimony speaks of it as witness, as the declaration of what God has seen and done. The statutes speak of it as authoritative decrees, as the settled, unalterable appointments of the Most High. The commandments speak of it as directive will, as the marching orders of the King of kings. The fear of the Lord speaks of the reverent response that the Word produces in the soul. And the judgments speak of it as the verdicts of the Supreme Judge, the final and infallible pronouncements of right and wrong.

And to each of these descriptions, David attaches a quality and an effect. The law is perfect, and it converts the soul. The testimony is sure, and it makes wise the simple. The statutes are right, and they rejoice the heart. The commandments are pure, and they enlighten the eyes. The fear of the Lord is clean, and it endures forever. The judgments are true and righteous altogether.

Let us consider first the perfection of God's law. "The law of the Lord is perfect." Perfect means complete, whole, lacking nothing. The Word of God does not need to be supplemented by human tradition, by philosophical speculation, or by mystical experience. It is sufficient in itself for all that it was designed to accomplish. It contains everything that the soul needs for salvation, for sanctification, for guidance, and for comfort. The man who has the Bible has a perfect treasure, and he needs no other book to teach him the way of life.

And the effect of this perfect law is the conversion of the soul. The Hebrew word here carries the sense of restoring, turning back, reviving. The soul that has wandered from God is turned back by the law of the Lord. The soul that has grown cold and lifeless is revived by its power. The law of God, applied by the Holy Spirit, is the instrument by which dead sinners are brought to life and wandering saints are brought home.

"The testimony of the Lord is sure, making wise the simple." In an age of uncertainty, where every truth is questioned and every authority is challenged, how precious is a sure testimony! The Word of God does not waver. It does not shift with the winds of cultural fashion. It does not bow to the pressure of popular opinion. What it declared three thousand years ago, it declares today. What it will declare a thousand years hence, it declares now. It is sure, firm, established, immovable. And the effect of this sureness is that it makes wise the simple. The simple man, the man who knows his own ignorance and is willing to be taught, finds in the Scriptures a wisdom that the universities of the world cannot impart.

"The statutes of the Lord are right, rejoicing the heart." Here is a benefit of Scripture that the legalistic mind cannot comprehend. The commandments of God, rightly understood, are not a burden but a joy. They do not weigh the heart down but lift it up. The man who obeys God's statutes out of love, not merely out of duty, discovers that they are the path to the deepest and most lasting happiness. For the statutes of the Lord are right; they align with the true nature of reality, with the way things actually are, with the grain of the universe. And when a man lives in alignment with reality, he experiences a rightness, a fitness, a harmony that produces genuine and unshakeable joy.

William Ames, that influential Puritan theologian, wrote that the Word of God is not merely a book of rules but a revelation of the character of God Himself. To obey the commandments is to align one's life with the nature of the One who gave them. And since God is the source of all joy, to live in conformity with His nature is to drink from the fountain of joy itself.

"More to be desired are they than gold, yea, than much fine gold: sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb." David compares the Word of God to the two things that men in his day valued most: gold for its purchasing power and honey for its sweetness. And he declares that the Word exceeds both. Gold can buy bread, but it cannot buy peace of conscience. Gold can purchase a house, but it cannot purchase a home in heaven. Honey can delight the palate, but it cannot satisfy the soul. But the Word of God can do what neither gold nor honey can do: it can convert the soul, enlighten the mind, rejoice the heart, and guide the feet along the path of eternal life.

"Moreover by them is thy servant warned: and in keeping of them there is great reward." Let us therefore hold fast to this incomparable treasure. Let us read it with reverence. Let us study it with diligence. Let us obey it with gladness. And let us commend it to a world that is perishing for lack of the wisdom that only the perfect law of the Lord can give. Amen.
