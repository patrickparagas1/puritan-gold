PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 6
"The Voice of My Beloved"
Song of Solomon 2:8-13 — Springtime and the Beloved's Call

---

Welcome, beloved couple. Tonight we encounter one of the most lyrical and joyful passages in the entire Song — the arrival of spring and the beloved's invitation to come away. This passage pulses with energy, hope, and the promise of renewal. Let it wash over both of you tonight.

Song of Solomon 2:8-13:

"The voice of my beloved! behold, he cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping upon the hills. My beloved is like a roe or a young hart: behold, he standeth behind our wall, he looketh forth at the windows, shewing himself through the lattice. My beloved spake, and said unto me, Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away. For, lo, the winter is past, the rain is over and gone; The flowers appear on the earth; the time of the singing of birds is come, and the voice of the turtle is heard in our land; The fig tree putteth forth her green figs, and the vines with the tender grape give a good smell. Arise, my love, my fair one, and come away."

--- Teaching ---

"The voice of my beloved!" The bride hears him before she sees him. In marriage, the voice matters more than we realize. The tone in which you speak to each other — across the breakfast table, on the phone during a busy day, in the quiet of evening — shapes the atmosphere of your relationship more than almost anything else. Richard Baxter wrote that a couple's words to each other are either "drops of honey or drops of gall." What does your voice sound like to your spouse? When they hear you coming, do they light up or brace themselves?

"Behold, he cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping upon the hills." The bridegroom is not walking. He is not strolling. He is leaping and skipping. He cannot wait to reach his beloved. There is an eagerness here, an impatience of love, that every married couple should recognize and cultivate. Do you still rush toward each other? When you have been apart, does your heart quicken at the thought of reunion? The Puritans believed that married love should retain this quality of eagerness throughout life. Thomas Gataker wrote that a husband who returns home with dragging feet has forgotten the treasure that awaits him.

"He standeth behind our wall, he looketh forth at the windows, shewing himself through the lattice." Before he enters, he peers in. He catches glimpses of her through the lattice. There is a playfulness here — a kind of holy flirtation — that is delightful. The Puritans, for all their sobriety, understood the place of playfulness in marriage. Samuel Rutherford, known for his deeply spiritual letters, wrote of the "play of love" between Christ and the soul. If the relationship between Christ and His church has a playful dimension, how much more the relationship between husband and wife?

Then comes the invitation: "Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away." This is one of the great calls of Scripture. It is not a command but an invitation — tender, urgent, irresistible. "Come away." Leave behind the ordinary. Step out of the routine. Come with me into something fresh, something alive, something beautiful.

Every marriage needs this invitation. The daily grind of work, bills, children, responsibilities, and routines can slowly drain the life out of a relationship. The beloved's call is a reminder that love requires intentional breaks from the ordinary. William Gouge wrote that husbands and wives should "recreate together" — should deliberately seek out shared experiences of refreshment and delight. When was the last time you said to your spouse, "Come away with me"?

The reason for the invitation is glorious: "The winter is past, the rain is over and gone. The flowers appear on the earth; the time of the singing of birds is come." After every winter in marriage — every season of coldness, distance, difficulty, or grief — spring comes. God is in the business of renewal. The same God who turns winter into spring can turn the coldest season of your marriage into the most fruitful.

The Puritans knew that marriage goes through seasons. Jeremiah Burroughs, in his Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment, taught that contentment in marriage means trusting God through the winters, knowing that He has not abandoned you, and that spring is coming. If you are in a winter season right now — a season of distance, misunderstanding, or weariness — hear the beloved's voice tonight: "Rise up. The winter is past. Come away."

"The fig tree putteth forth her green figs, and the vines with the tender grape give a good smell." Spring is not just about the end of winter. It is about new fruitfulness. Green figs and tender grapes are the first signs of a coming harvest. In your marriage, new seasons of closeness and intimacy often begin with small, tender signs — a kind word after a long silence, a gentle touch after a season of distance, a shared laugh that breaks through the ice. Watch for these tender grapes. Nurture them. Do not trample them with impatience or cynicism.

"Arise, my love, my fair one, and come away." The invitation is repeated. It is not withdrawn when we hesitate. The beloved calls again. Grace upon grace. In your marriage, keep calling each other out of the ordinary and into something beautiful. Never stop inviting. Never stop pursuing. Never stop leaping over mountains to reach the one you love.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Turn to each other and consider these questions.

Question 1: What "season" is your marriage in right now? If it is winter, what signs of spring might be emerging? If it is spring, how can you nurture the new growth?

Question 2: When was the last time you intentionally stepped away from routine together? Plan something this week — even if it is just an evening walk or a shared cup of coffee without screens.

Question 3: The beloved leaps and skips to reach his bride. Does your spouse know that you are eager for them? How can you show that eagerness this week?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray for renewal in your marriage. Ask God to end whatever winter may be lingering and to bring the flowers, the singing, and the tender grapes of a new season. Pray for the courage to answer the beloved's call: "Arise, and come away."

--- Closing ---

The winter is past. The rain is over and gone. Whatever cold season you have endured, the God of springtime is calling you into something new. Arise, beloved couple. Come away together. Goodnight.
