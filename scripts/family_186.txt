PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 25
"Redeeming the Time Together"
Richard Baxter — The Christian Directory


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Baxter addresses a subject that touches every family — the stewardship of time. How do we use our hours, our days, and our years in a way that honors God and blesses our household? Let us listen together.

Let us pray. Lord, our times are in Your hands. Every hour of this day was a gift from You, and we are accountable for how we spent it. Teach us tonight to redeem the time — to use our days wisely, intentionally, and for Your glory. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Ephesians 5:15-17 and Psalm 90:12. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, Redeeming the time, because the days are evil. Wherefore be ye not unwise, but understanding what the will of the Lord is. So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom."

That is Ephesians 5:15-17 and Psalm 90:12. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Richard Baxter was one of the most productive men in the history of the church. He wrote over 140 books. He pastored a large congregation. He counseled hundreds of families. He endured years of persecution and imprisonment. And he did all of this while battling chronic illness that left him weak and in constant pain. How did he accomplish so much? The answer lies in his extraordinary attention to the stewardship of time.

In "A Christian Directory," Baxter addresses the use of time at length, and his counsel for families is as practical as it is searching. He begins with Paul's command to "redeem the time" — literally, to buy up every opportunity, to make the most of every moment, to refuse to squander the irreplaceable resource that God has given us. The days are evil, Paul says, meaning that the pressures of a fallen world constantly conspire to waste our time, to distract us from what matters most, and to fill our hours with trivialities while eternal realities go unattended.

Baxter identifies several ways families waste time. The first is idle talk — conversation that goes nowhere, that edifies no one, that fills the air with words but not with meaning. Baxter does not forbid recreation or lighthearted conversation — he values both. But he warns against the habit of spending hours in empty chatter while neglecting the conversations that really matter: conversations about God, about Scripture, about the state of our souls, about the needs of others, and about the great questions of life and death.

The second time-waster is excessive entertainment. Baxter wrote in an age before screens, but his principle is timeless. He warns against any form of recreation that consumes more time than it refreshes. Recreation is a gift from God — a way of restoring the body and mind for further service. But when recreation becomes an end in itself, when it devours hours that should be given to family, worship, study, and service, it has become an idol. Baxter counsels moderation: enjoy recreation, but set limits, and always ask whether this particular use of time is making you more fit for the duties God has given you or less.

The third time-waster is procrastination — putting off until tomorrow what should be done today. Baxter writes with evident feeling about the spiritual danger of delay. How many parents intend to begin family worship "next week" and never do? How many believers plan to read through the Bible "this year" and never start? How many families resolve to pray together "soon" and find that soon never comes? Baxter urges immediate action. If something is worth doing, do it now. If a duty is clear, obey at once. The habit of delay is the enemy of faithfulness.

Against these time-wasters, Baxter sets the practice of intentional living. He recommends that every household establish a daily order — a rhythm of worship, work, rest, and recreation that reflects the family's values and priorities. Morning worship opens the day. Work fills the productive hours. Meals are shared together with gratitude and conversation. Evening worship closes the day. And rest prepares the family for the day to come. This is not a rigid schedule that allows no flexibility, but a general pattern that ensures the most important things receive the most prominent place.

Baxter also speaks about the stewardship of seasons — the recognition that different stages of family life call for different priorities. The years when children are young are years for intensive instruction and formation. The years when children are growing are years for deepening engagement and expanding responsibility. The years when children leave home are years for continued prayer, counsel, and support. Each season is a gift with its own opportunities, and each season passes quickly. Moses prays, "So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom." Baxter takes this prayer as the motto for all of life: know that your days are numbered, and use them wisely.

Tonight, let us examine how we spend our time as a family. Are we redeeming it or wasting it? Are we giving our best hours to what matters most, or are we letting the trivial crowd out the essential? The time we have together as a family is a treasure that cannot be replaced once it is gone. Let us spend it well.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Baxter identifies idle talk, excessive entertainment, and procrastination as the three great time-wasters. Which of these is the biggest challenge for our family?

Question 2: Moses prays, "Teach us to number our days." What would change in our family if we truly believed that our time together is limited and precious?

Question 3: What is one practical change we could make to our daily routine to better redeem the time God has given us?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, Redeeming the time, because the days are evil." -- Ephesians 5:15-16

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord, teach us to number our days. Help us to use the time You give us wisely — not wasting it on trivialities but investing it in things that matter for eternity. Guard our family from the time-wasters that rob us of the life You intend for us. Let every hour of this household be redeemed for Your glory. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
