PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 12
"Walking in Love Daily"
Ephesians 5:1-2 — Communication and Holy Living

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we step into the opening verses of Ephesians 5 and discover a command that transforms marriage from a duty into a delight. We are called not merely to love in moments of crisis or decision, but to walk in love daily, steadily, as a way of life.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 5:1-2 (KJV):

"Be ye therefore followers of God, as dear children; And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour."

--- Teaching ---

Paul begins with a breathtaking identity statement: you are dear children of God. Before he calls you to any duty, he reminds you of your dignity. You belong to the Father. You are beloved. Everything that follows flows from this identity. You walk in love not in order to earn God's favor but because you already possess it. You imitate God not to become His children but because you already are His children.

The command is to walk in love. The word "walk" is significant. Walking is not a dramatic, once-in-a-lifetime event. It is the steady, step-by-step movement of daily life. Walking in love means loving your spouse in the morning when you are groggy and short-tempered. It means loving in the afternoon when work has drained you. It means loving in the evening when the children have exhausted your patience. It means loving on the ordinary days when there is no romance, no excitement, no special occasion, just the faithful routine of two lives lived together before God.

William Gouge frequently reminded his congregation that the health of a marriage is not measured by its peaks but by its valleys. Any couple can love on a vacation. Any couple can be kind on an anniversary. But the marriage that honors God is the one where love is expressed in the unremarkable moments: in the way you pass the salt, in the tone you use when discussing the budget, in the patience you show when your spouse tells the same story for the third time. These small, daily expressions of love are the bricks from which a lasting marriage is built.

The model for this love is Christ Himself: "as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us." Christ's love was not sentimental. It was sacrificial. He gave Himself. He did not give merely His time, His attention, or His resources, though He gave all of those. He gave Himself, His very life, as an offering and a sacrifice. And Paul says this sacrifice was "a sweetsmelling savour" to God. The love of Christ was pleasing to the Father because it was costly, selfless, and complete.

In marriage, the daily sacrifice of walking in love is pleasing to God. Every act of patience is an offering. Every choice to serve rather than be served is a sacrifice. Every moment of listening when you would rather speak, of yielding when you would rather insist, of giving when you would rather receive, is a sweet aroma rising to heaven. Your marriage, when lived in this spirit, is an act of worship.

Richard Baxter counseled couples to begin each day with a simple prayer: "Lord, help me to walk in love toward my spouse today." He believed that this daily intention, renewed every morning, would transform even the most ordinary marriage into something extraordinary. The Puritans were not romantics in the modern sense. They did not expect marriage to be a perpetual honeymoon. But they believed that marriage, when governed by the love of Christ, could be the deepest and most satisfying of all human relationships.

Dear couple, consider your walk together. Not the grand gestures, but the daily steps. Are you walking in love? Is your default setting toward kindness, patience, and generosity? Or have you slipped into a pattern of irritability, indifference, or self-centeredness? The beautiful thing about walking is that you can always take the next step in the right direction. You do not have to wait for a new year, a new season, or a fresh start. You can begin walking in love right now, tonight, with the very next word you speak and the very next choice you make.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: What does walking in love look like in the ordinary routines of our daily life together?

Question 2: In what small, daily ways can I better imitate Christ's sacrificial love toward you this week?

Question 3: Is there an area of our daily life where we have stopped walking in love and need to begin again?

--- Prayer ---

Loving Father, we are Thy dear children, and we desire to walk worthy of Thy love. Teach us to walk in love daily, not merely in the grand moments but in the small and ordinary ones. Help us to give ourselves to each other as Christ gave Himself for us. May our daily sacrifices of patience, kindness, and service rise before Thee as a sweet-smelling offering. Forgive us for the days we have walked in selfishness rather than love. Set our feet on the right path and help us to take the next step together. In the name of Jesus Christ, Amen.
