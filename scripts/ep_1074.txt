Mortification of Sin in Believers. By John Owen. A Complete Summary.

Welcome to this summary of John Owen's Mortification of Sin in Believers. Owen wrote this work in 1656, and it remains one of the most penetrating and practical treatments of the Christian's ongoing war against indwelling sin ever written. This summary covers all fourteen chapters.

THE AUTHOR AND HIS PURPOSE.

John Owen served as Vice-Chancellor of Oxford University and as chaplain to Oliver Cromwell. He was the greatest theologian of the English Puritan movement. The Mortification of Sin was written to help believers understand their duty and the means of putting sin to death in their lives, based on the text of Romans chapter eight, verse thirteen: "If ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live."

CHAPTER ONE: AN EXPOSITION OF ROMANS 8:13.
Owen establishes the foundation of the whole work from this single verse. The duty is mortification — the killing of sin. The persons addressed are believers only. The means is the Holy Spirit. The promise is life — comfort, vigor, and growth in grace.

CHAPTER TWO: THE CONTINUOUS ACTIVITY OF INDWELLING SIN.
Owen shows that sin remaining in believers is always active, always working, always pressing toward evil. It is never idle, never content, never satisfied. The Christian who ceases to fight is already losing.

CHAPTER THREE: THE HOLY SPIRIT'S WORK IN MORTIFICATION.
Only the Spirit of God can truly mortify sin. All other methods — resolutions, vows, penances — are powerless. The Spirit works through the means of grace, but the power belongs to Him alone.

CHAPTER FOUR: LIFE AND COMFORT DEPEND ON MORTIFICATION.
Owen argues that vigor, comfort, and power in the Christian life are directly proportional to the degree of mortification practiced. Neglect of this duty leads to spiritual weakness.

CHAPTER FIVE: THE NECESSITY OF MORTIFICATION.
Every believer must be always engaged in this work. It is a lifelong duty from which no one is exempt. Owen answers those who think they have progressed beyond the need for vigilance.

CHAPTER SIX: WHAT MORTIFICATION IS AND IS NOT.
Owen carefully defines his terms. Mortification is not the total destruction of sin in this life. It is not merely covering or concealing sin. It is not producing a quiet, composed temperament. True mortification involves the habitual weakening of sin and a constant fighting against it.

CHAPTER SEVEN: THE MEANS OF MORTIFICATION — FAITH.
Faith is the first and chief instrument. Faith fills the heart with a sense of Christ's love, which is the most powerful sin-killing force in the universe. Faith applies the blood and cross of Christ to the soul.

CHAPTER EIGHT: SINCERITY AND DILIGENCE IN MORTIFICATION.
Owen warns against half-hearted efforts. The one who would truly mortify sin must be thorough, honest, and unrelenting. No sin must be spared, and no excuse accepted.

CHAPTERS NINE THROUGH THIRTEEN: PREPARATORY DIRECTIONS.
Owen provides five preparatory directions that must be followed before the actual work of mortification can succeed. These include: considering the dangerous symptoms of sin, maintaining a clear sense of guilt, loading the conscience with the weight of sin's evil, longing earnestly for deliverance, and carefully considering the occasions and provocations of sin.

CHAPTER FOURTEEN: DIRECTIONS FOR THE ACTUAL MORTIFICATION OF SIN.
Owen concludes with specific directions for killing sin. These include: looking to Christ by faith, filling the soul with thoughts of God's majesty, acting immediately against sin's first motions, meditating on God's excellencies, and maintaining communion with God through prayer and the Word.

LASTING SIGNIFICANCE.

The Mortification of Sin remains essential reading because Owen refuses to offer shortcuts. He shows that fighting sin is hard, lifelong, painful work — but that the Holy Spirit provides all the power needed. The book is a masterwork of pastoral theology that has helped Christians for nearly four centuries.
