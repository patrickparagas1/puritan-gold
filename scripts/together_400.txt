PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 8
"Submitting One to Another"
Ephesians 5:21 — The Foundation of Mutual Love

---

Welcome, dear couple. We begin our second week by stepping back to the verse that governs everything we studied last week — a verse that is too often overlooked.

Our Scripture tonight is Ephesians 5:21:

"Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God."

This single verse is the gateway to the entire marriage passage. Without it, everything that follows can be distorted. With it, everything comes alive.

--- Teaching ---

The Puritans understood that Paul's instructions about marriage in Ephesians 5:22-33 do not arise in a vacuum. They flow directly from a description of what it means to be filled with the Holy Spirit. In verses 18-21, Paul describes Spirit-filled believers as people who sing together, give thanks together, and submit to one another. The marriage instructions are a specific application of this larger principle of mutual submission.

William Gouge was careful to emphasize this foundation. He wrote that while husband and wife have distinct callings within the marriage, both are fundamentally called to submit — that is, to put the other person first, to consider the other's needs above their own, to lay down their rights for the sake of love. This is not weakness; it is Christlikeness. For Christ Himself, though He was Lord of all, took the form of a servant and submitted Himself even to the point of death on a cross.

Mutual submission does not erase the unique callings Paul outlines. The husband is still called to lead through sacrificial love. The wife is still called to honor and support her husband's leadership. But both of these callings are expressions of a deeper reality: both husband and wife are servants of Christ, and therefore servants of each other.

Richard Baxter wrote that the happiest marriages he observed in his pastoral ministry were those where both husband and wife competed — not for authority or control, but for the privilege of serving each other. He described couples who raced to be the first to apologize after a disagreement, who looked for ways to lighten each other's burdens, who took genuine delight in the other's happiness. This is mutual submission in action.

The phrase "in the fear of God" anchors the whole passage. Your submission to each other is not ultimately about each other — it is about God. You serve your spouse because you serve Christ. You honor your spouse because you honor Christ. This means that even when your spouse is difficult, even when they fail to fulfill their calling, your submission is not contingent on their performance. It is an offering to the Lord.

The Puritans warned against two opposite errors. The first is the error of domination — where one spouse (typically the husband) uses these texts to justify control, manipulation, or abuse. The Puritans roundly condemned this. Gouge wrote that a husband who dominates his wife has failed to understand the most basic lesson of the passage: Christ leads by dying, not by demanding. The second error is the erasure of all distinction — where the unique callings of husband and wife are dissolved into a vague egalitarianism that ignores Paul's specific instructions. The Puritans held both truths together: genuine equality in dignity and worth, combined with complementary roles in the marriage.

Tonight, let mutual submission be not a grudging duty but a joyful practice. Look for ways to serve each other. Compete in kindness. Race to be the first to forgive.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Discuss together:

Question 1: What does "mutual submission" look like in your marriage? Can you name a recent example where one of you put the other first?

Question 2: Are there areas where one of you tends to dominate the relationship? Are there areas where both of you avoid responsibility? How can you find a healthier balance?

Question 3: How does the phrase "in the fear of God" change the way you think about serving each other? Does it free you from keeping score?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Pray together for the grace of mutual submission. Ask the Holy Spirit to fill your marriage with the same servant-hearted love that characterizes Christ. Pray that your home would be marked not by power struggles but by a holy competition to outserve one another.

--- Closing ---

May the Spirit who fills believers with songs and thanksgiving also fill your marriage with mutual submission and selfless love. May you find in serving each other the deepest joy marriage can offer. Peace be with you both. Goodnight.
