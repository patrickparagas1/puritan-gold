PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"I Will Praise Thee"
May 24, 2026

---

"I will praise thee with my whole heart: before the gods will I sing praise unto thee. I will worship toward thy holy temple, and praise thy name for thy lovingkindness and for thy truth: for thou hast magnified thy word above all thy name. In the day when I cried thou answeredst me, and strengthenedst me with strength in my soul. All the kings of the earth shall praise thee, O Lord, when they hear the words of thy mouth." -- Psalm 138:1-4

There is a resolution here that rings with the clarity of a trumpet. "I will praise thee with my whole heart." Not with half a heart. Not with a divided heart. Not with the reluctant, distracted, half-awake worship that too often passes for praise in the assemblies of God's people. But with the whole heart, every chamber occupied, every affection engaged, every faculty alert and active in the service of adoration.

David had learned by long experience that the worship of God demands everything a man has. You cannot worship God with your lips while your mind is elsewhere. You cannot sing His praises with your voice while your heart is set upon the world. You cannot offer Him the fruit of your lips if the root of your life is planted in the soil of selfishness. True worship is the engagement of the whole man with the whole God, and anything less than this is an insult to the majesty of the One we profess to adore.

"Before the gods will I sing praise unto thee." Some interpreters understand "the gods" here to refer to the false deities of the surrounding nations. Others take it as a reference to earthly rulers and judges, who are sometimes called gods in Scripture because they exercise authority that properly belongs to God alone. In either case, the meaning is clear: David's praise of the true God will not be silenced or diminished by the presence of any rival claimant to divine honours. He will worship Jehovah openly, boldly, without apology, regardless of the audience. He will not trim his praise to suit the sensibilities of the ungodly. He will not moderate his devotion to avoid causing offense. Before the gods, in their very presence, he will sing.

This is the kind of courage that our age desperately needs. We live in a time when the praise of the true God is considered intolerant, when the exclusive claims of Christ are regarded as offensive, when the faithful believer is pressured on every side to soften his worship, to broaden his theology, to accommodate his faith to the reigning idols of the age. But David teaches us that the praise of God is not to be regulated by the approval of the world. It is to be offered with the whole heart, before the gods, without compromise and without shame.

"For thou hast magnified thy word above all thy name." This is one of the most remarkable statements in the entire Psalter. God has magnified His Word above all His name. The name of God represents the sum total of His revealed character, and it is the most glorious thing in the universe. And yet God has exalted His Word even above that glorious name. Why? Because it is through His Word that His name is made known. It is through His Word that His character is revealed. It is through His Word that His promises are communicated, and it is upon His Word that the faith of His people rests. To magnify the Word is to magnify the reliability, the trustworthiness, the absolute faithfulness of everything God has ever said.

The Reformers built their entire theology upon this principle. Sola Scriptura was not merely a slogan but a confession of faith in the supreme authority of God's written Word. They believed, and rightly so, that when God speaks, His Word carries the full weight of His character behind it. To doubt the Word is to doubt the God who spoke it. To believe the Word is to honour the God who gave it. And to obey the Word is to worship the God who commands it.

"In the day when I cried thou answeredst me, and strengthenedst me with strength in my soul." Here David moves from theology to testimony. He has experienced the faithfulness of God's Word in the crucible of personal crisis. He cried, and God answered. Not necessarily with the immediate removal of the trouble, but with something far more valuable: strength in the soul. The outward circumstances may not have changed. The enemies may still have been at the gate. The affliction may still have pressed upon him. But in the midst of it all, God gave him an inward strength, a spiritual fortitude, a supernatural courage that enabled him to stand firm when everything around him was collapsing.

This is one of the most characteristic works of God in the lives of His people. He does not always remove the trial, but He always gives the grace to endure it. He does not always still the storm, but He always sustains the sailor. He does not always deliver from the furnace, but He always walks with His children through the fire. And the strength He gives is not borrowed from human resources but flows directly from the infinite reservoirs of His own omnipotence. It is strength in the soul, a strength that no outward force can touch and no earthly weapon can destroy.

"Though the Lord be high, yet hath he respect unto the lowly: but the proud he knoweth afar off." Here is one of the great paradoxes of divine grace. The God who is high above all, whose throne is in the heavens, whose glory fills the universe, this God has respect unto the lowly. He bends down from His infinite height to attend to the prayers of the humble, to hear the cries of the broken, to lift up those who have been cast down. But the proud, those who exalt themselves and rely upon their own strength, He knows afar off. He keeps them at a distance. He withholds from them the intimate communion that is the privilege of the humble.

Let us therefore approach our God with whole hearts and humble spirits. Let us praise Him without reserve and worship Him without compromise. And let us rest upon His Word, which He has magnified above all His name, knowing that the God who answers prayer and gives strength to the soul will never fail those who trust in Him. Amen.
