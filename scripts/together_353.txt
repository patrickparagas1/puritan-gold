PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 22
"Your Marriage as a Living Sacrifice"
Romans for Marriage — Day 22


Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to tonight's couples devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for setting aside this time to draw near to God and to each other. The moments you invest in your marriage before the Lord are among the most important moments of your day.

Let us open God's Word together. Our passage tonight comes from Romans 12:1-2:

"I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service. And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God."

That is Romans 12:1-2. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Now let us hear from Richard Baxter, one of the great Puritan voices on this subject. The Puritans wrote extensively about marriage because they saw it as one of God's most profound gifts.

Romans 12:1-2 marks the great pivot of the letter. After eleven chapters of doctrine — what God has done — Paul now turns to duty — what we must do in response. And the first command is sweeping: present your bodies as a living sacrifice to God. Not a dead sacrifice, like the animals on the Old Testament altar, but a living sacrifice — a life poured out daily in devotion and service.

Richard Baxter taught that the Christian home is the primary altar on which this living sacrifice is offered. The sacrifice is not made only in the church building but in the kitchen, the bedroom, the living room, the car, and every other space where married life unfolds. Every act of selfless love toward your spouse is a sacrifice on the altar. Every moment of patience when you want to snap. Every choice to serve when you would rather be served. Every decision to forgive when you would rather hold a grudge.

Baxter emphasized the phrase 'by the mercies of God.' Our sacrifice is motivated by mercy, not by obligation. We do not present ourselves because we have to but because we are overwhelmed by what God has done for us. The eleven chapters of mercy — justification, no condemnation, inseparable love, God fighting for us — these mercies compel us to offer everything back to Him. A couple that truly understands the mercies of God will gladly pour out their lives for each other.

'Be not conformed to this world' is a direct warning against allowing worldly patterns of marriage to shape your home. The world's marriage model is built on self-fulfillment: what can this relationship do for me? The Gospel's marriage model is built on self-sacrifice: how can I lay down my life for my spouse? These two models are incompatible. One produces consumers; the other produces servants.

'Be transformed by the renewing of your mind.' Transformation comes not through trying harder but through thinking differently. When your mind is renewed by Scripture — when you see your spouse, your marriage, and your purpose through God's eyes rather than the world's — your behavior naturally changes. A renewed mind produces a transformed marriage.


Present your marriage to God tonight as a living sacrifice. Not your individual lives only, but your marriage itself — your shared life, your shared home, your shared future. Say together: 'Lord, our marriage is Yours. Use it for Your glory. We offer it as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable.'

Then identify one area where the world's pattern has been shaping your marriage rather than God's Word. Perhaps it is the way you handle money (consumer culture vs. stewardship). Perhaps it is the way you approach conflict (winning vs. reconciling). Perhaps it is your expectations of each other (performance-based vs. grace-based). Name it, renounce it, and ask God to transform it by renewing your mind.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Now let us pause for reflection. Here are some questions for you and your spouse to discuss together. Be honest. Be gentle. Listen more than you speak.

Question 1: What does it look like practically for your marriage to be a 'living sacrifice' to God?

Question 2: In what areas has your marriage been 'conformed to this world' rather than transformed by Scripture?

Question 3: How does meditating on God's mercies (Romans 1-11) motivate you to offer your marriage as a sacrifice?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Let us move into a time of prayer together. If you are able, hold hands as you pray. There is something powerful about a husband and wife joining hands before the throne of grace.

Tonight's prayer focus: Offer your marriage to God as a living sacrifice. Ask Him to protect you from worldly patterns and to transform your thinking, your priorities, and your love through the renewing of your minds by His Word.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May your marriage be a holy altar where love is daily sacrificed and daily renewed. May you be transformed together — not conformed to this world but shaped by the mercies of God.

May the Lord bless your marriage and keep it strong. May He make His face to shine upon your home, and give you peace. Goodnight, dear couple. Walk together in His love.
