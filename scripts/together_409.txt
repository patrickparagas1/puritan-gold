PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 17
"Dwelling Together in Understanding"
William Gouge — On Companionship and Intimacy

---

Welcome, dear couple. Tonight we explore what Gouge considered the sweetest dimension of married life: the companionship and intimate knowledge that husband and wife are called to cultivate.

Our Scripture tonight is 1 Peter 3:7a:

"Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge."

To dwell with your spouse "according to knowledge" — this is one of the most practical and most neglected commands in all of Scripture.

--- Teaching ---

William Gouge devoted an entire section of "Domestical Duties" to what he called "the mutual duties of husband and wife" — the obligations that belong equally to both partners. Chief among these is the duty of companionship.

Gouge wrote that God's original reason for creating marriage was companionship: "It is not good that the man should be alone" (Genesis 2:18). Before children, before household management, before any of the practical functions of marriage, there was the simple, profound gift of not being alone. A husband and wife are meant to be each other's closest companions — the person you most want to be with at the end of the day, the one you trust with your deepest joys and darkest fears.

But companionship does not happen automatically. It must be cultivated. And the key to cultivating it, according to Peter, is knowledge. "Dwell with them according to knowledge" means: study your spouse. Learn what brings them joy and what causes them pain. Understand their temperament — are they introverted or extroverted? Do they process emotions verbally or silently? Do they feel loved through words, touch, acts of service, gifts, or quality time?

Gouge observed that many marriages stagnate because one or both spouses stop learning about the other. They assume they know everything there is to know. But a human being is endlessly complex, and the person you married is constantly growing and changing. The husband who studied his wife during courtship must continue studying her throughout the marriage. The wife who once hung on every word her husband spoke must continue listening with that same attentiveness.

The Puritans also spoke frankly about physical intimacy as an essential component of married companionship. Gouge wrote that the marriage bed is "a fountain of delight" that God has given to married couples for their mutual joy. He counseled both husband and wife to approach their intimate life with generosity, attentiveness, and mutual concern for each other's pleasure. He warned against the husband who treats intimacy as his right rather than a shared gift, and against the wife who uses intimacy as a bargaining chip rather than an expression of love.

Richard Baxter added that companionship requires protected time. "You cannot know a person you never see," he wrote. The demands of work, children, and ministry can gradually push a couple apart until they are living parallel lives under the same roof. Baxter counseled couples to guard their time together as fiercely as they guard any other sacred commitment.

Tonight, recommit to knowing each other — truly, deeply, continuously. You are not the same people you were on your wedding day. You have both grown, changed, been wounded, been healed. Take time tonight to learn something new about the person sitting beside you.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Discuss together:

Question 1: What is something your spouse loves that you have not paid enough attention to lately? What is something that causes them stress that you could help alleviate?

Question 2: Do you feel truly known by your spouse — not just the surface facts, but the deeper longings, fears, and dreams? What would help you feel more known?

Question 3: How is your intimate life together? Is it a source of joy and connection, or has it become routine or neglected? What would renew it?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Pray tonight for deeper knowledge of each other. Ask God to give you curiosity about your spouse — a genuine desire to learn, to understand, to discover. Pray for the courage to be known and the grace to know well.

--- Closing ---

May the God who knows you fully and loves you completely help you to know and love each other with the same depth. May your companionship deepen with every passing year. Goodnight.
