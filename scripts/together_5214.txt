PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 14
"Being Filled with the Spirit Together"
Ephesians 5:18-20 — Communication and Holy Living

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. We come to the close of our second week, and tonight's passage brings us to the fountainhead from which all godly marriage flows. Before Paul ever speaks of wives and husbands, before he gives the household instructions we studied in week one, he gives this command: be filled with the Spirit. Everything depends on this.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 5:18-20 (KJV):

"And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit; Speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord; Giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ."

--- Teaching ---

Paul draws a vivid contrast. Do not be drunk with wine, but be filled with the Spirit. As wine controls the person who drinks it in excess, so the Spirit of God is to control the believer who is filled with Him. A drunk person speaks differently, walks differently, and acts differently because they are under the influence of alcohol. In the same way, a Spirit-filled person speaks, walks, and acts under the influence of the Holy Spirit. And this influence is what transforms a marriage from a mere human arrangement into a divine institution.

The command to be filled with the Spirit is in the present tense, which means it is ongoing. It is not a one-time experience but a daily reality. Every morning, the Christian couple needs a fresh filling of the Spirit. Every evening, as they sit together, they need the Spirit's presence to govern their words, their attitudes, and their affections. Without the Spirit, the best marriage advice in the world is powerless. With the Spirit, even imperfect couples can build marriages that honor God.

William Gouge was convinced that the greatest need of every Christian household was the presence and power of the Holy Spirit. He observed that couples who tried to obey the marriage commands of Ephesians 5 in their own strength inevitably failed. The husband could not love his wife as Christ loves the church by sheer willpower. The wife could not reverence her husband through gritted teeth. These duties require a supernatural power that only the Spirit can supply. Gouge counseled couples to pray daily for the filling of the Spirit, both individually and together.

Notice what the Spirit-filled life produces. First, it produces joyful worship: speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord. A Spirit-filled marriage is a singing marriage. Not necessarily in a musical sense, though there is something beautiful about a husband and wife who worship together in song. But in the deeper sense of a marriage characterized by joy, by celebration, by gratitude, by a spirit of worship that pervades everything.

Second, the Spirit-filled life produces thankfulness: giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father. The couple that is filled with the Spirit gives thanks. They give thanks for each other. They give thanks for the good days and the hard days. They give thanks for the blessings they can see and the blessings they cannot yet see. Gratitude is the atmosphere of the Spirit-filled home.

Richard Baxter observed that an ungrateful spouse is one of the heaviest burdens in marriage, and a grateful spouse is one of the greatest joys. When your husband or wife expresses genuine gratitude for who you are and what you do, it fills the well of your soul. When gratitude is absent, the well runs dry. The Spirit produces in us a spirit of thankfulness that we could never manufacture on our own.

Dear couple, as you close this week on communication and holy living, let the command be clear: be filled with the Spirit. Do not try to build your marriage in your own strength. Invite the Holy Spirit to govern your home, your words, your attitudes, and your love. Pray together tonight for a fresh filling. Sing together, even if your voices are imperfect. Give thanks together, naming the specific blessings God has given you. These are the practices that create a marriage overflowing with the life of God.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: Do we regularly pray together for the filling of the Holy Spirit in our marriage? If not, can we start tonight?

Question 2: Is gratitude a consistent feature of our life together, or have we fallen into a pattern of complaint and dissatisfaction?

Question 3: What psalms, hymns, or spiritual songs might we incorporate into our life together as a couple?

--- Prayer ---

Come, Holy Spirit, and fill this marriage with Thy presence and power. We confess that we have tried to love, to serve, and to honor each other in our own strength, and we have failed. We need Thee. Fill us afresh tonight. Govern our tongues with grace. Fill our hearts with gratitude. Let psalms and hymns and spiritual songs rise from our home as an offering of worship to Thee. We thank Thee, Father, for the gift of this marriage. We thank Thee for every blessing, seen and unseen. And we ask that the joy of the Spirit would be the defining characteristic of our life together. In the name of Jesus Christ our Lord, Amen.
