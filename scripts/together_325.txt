PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 25
"Words Fitly Spoken"
Proverbs 25:11


Good evening. What a blessing it is to see a husband and wife seeking the Lord together. The Puritans believed that a praying couple is an unstoppable force for the kingdom of God. Let us pray together tonight.

The Word of God has sustained marriages for thousands of years. Tonight it speaks to yours from Proverbs 25:11:

"A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver."

That is Proverbs 25:11. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher. Charles Bridges reflected deeply on the truths of Scripture and how they apply to the covenant of marriage.

Charles Bridges delights in this exquisite metaphor. The image is of golden fruit displayed in a silver setting: beauty enhanced by beauty, value set off by value. A word 'fitly spoken' — that is, a word spoken at the right time, in the right way, with the right spirit — is a work of art, as lovely and precious as the finest craftsmanship.

Bridges notes that fitness involves multiple dimensions: the content must be true, the timing must be appropriate, the tone must be gracious, and the intent must be loving. A true word spoken at the wrong time is not fitly spoken. A kind word spoken with insincere motives is not fitly spoken. Only when truth, timing, tone, and intent align does the word achieve its golden beauty.

Matthew Henry adds that such words are rare and therefore precious: 'How seldom do we meet with words so well chosen, so seasonably delivered, and so graciously received!' Most speech is careless, hasty, or self-serving. The word fitly spoken stands out against this backdrop like gold against silver.

Bridges applies this to the closest of human relationships: 'Nowhere is the fitly spoken word more needed, or more rare, than in marriage. The couple who masters the art of speaking the right word at the right time has discovered one of the deepest secrets of a happy home.' He adds that this art requires patience (waiting for the right moment), wisdom (choosing the right words), and love (desiring the other's good above the desire to be heard).


Your words have enormous power in your marriage. A single sentence spoken at the right moment can heal years of hurt. A single sentence spoken carelessly can wound more deeply than a sword.

The art of the fitly spoken word requires learning your spouse. When are they most receptive to encouragement? When are they most vulnerable to criticism? What words resonate most deeply with their heart? What phrases shut them down?

Practice speaking apples of gold today. Offer a specific word of affirmation that your spouse needs to hear. Time it well. Say it sincerely. Let it be a gift, not a transaction. And when you need to speak a hard truth, give equal attention to the setting: choose a time of calm, a tone of gentleness, and words that honor the dignity of the one you love.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Here are some questions to guide your conversation tonight. Take turns sharing, and give each other the gift of your full attention.

Question 1: When was the last time my spouse spoke a word to me that felt like 'apples of gold'? When did I last speak such a word to them?

Question 2: Am I more often careless or careful with my words in our marriage?

Question 3: What specific word of encouragement does my spouse need to hear from me today?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Prayer is the heartbeat of a Christian marriage. Let us pray together now. You do not need eloquent words, just honest ones.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to make your words to each other like apples of gold in settings of silver. Pray for wisdom in choosing what to say, patience in waiting for the right moment, and grace in delivering truth with love.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the Lord who spoke the world into being fill your mouths with words of beauty, truth, and love, fitly spoken to build up and adorn your life together. Amen.

May your love for each other grow deeper with every passing day, and may your love for God be the source and the strength of it all. Goodnight, dear ones.
