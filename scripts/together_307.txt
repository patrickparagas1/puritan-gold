PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 7
"Treasuring Wisdom in Your Marriage"
Proverbs 7:1-4

---

Welcome, husband and wife. There is something powerful about two hearts turning toward God at the same time. When you both face the same direction, toward Christ, everything in your marriage finds its proper alignment. Let us do that now.

Every marriage needs a foundation that does not shift. Let us hear from that foundation now, from Proverbs 7:1-4:

"My son, keep my words, and lay up my commandments with thee. Keep my commandments, and live; and my law as the apple of thine eye. Bind them upon thy fingers, write them upon the table of thine heart. Say unto wisdom, Thou art my sister; and call understanding thy kinswoman."

That is Proverbs 7:1-4. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

The Puritans treasured marriage as a covenant made before God. Charles Bridges wrote beautifully about how Scripture speaks to the hearts of married couples.

Charles Bridges sees in these verses a progression from mere hearing to intimate relationship with wisdom. To 'keep' and 'lay up' is to store as a precious treasure. To guard as 'the apple of thine eye' is to protect as one would protect the most sensitive and vital part of the body. To 'bind upon thy fingers' is to make wisdom inseparable from every action. To 'write upon the table of thine heart' is to internalize it so deeply that it becomes part of one's very nature.

Bridges notes that the final command is the most striking: 'Say unto wisdom, Thou art my sister.' In the ancient Near East, 'sister' was a term of intimate endearment, suggesting the closest possible friendship. Wisdom is not to be treated as a distant acquaintance, consulted only in emergencies, but as a beloved family member whose presence is constantly desired.

Matthew Henry adds that this language of intimacy with wisdom stands in deliberate contrast to the seductive woman described later in the chapter. Solomon is saying: if you make wisdom your intimate companion, you will be protected from the flattery and allurements of folly. Henry writes: 'Those that are wise and good should be our chosen companions.' When a man treasures wisdom, he will not be drawn away by the false promises of sin.

For the married couple, Bridges applies this beautifully: the husband and wife who treasure wisdom together create a fortress around their marriage. When both partners are intimate with wisdom, when God's commandments are written on both their hearts, temptation finds no foothold. The marriage that is saturated with biblical truth is a marriage armored against the schemes of the enemy.

--- Marriage Application ---

How deeply has God's wisdom penetrated your marriage? Is it merely a surface coating, applied on Sundays and forgotten by Monday, or is it written on the table of your hearts?

To treasure wisdom in your marriage means making it part of the very fabric of your daily life. It means that Scripture shapes your conversations, your decisions, your conflicts, and your celebrations. It means choosing friends and influences that reinforce godly wisdom rather than undermining it.

Consider the metaphor of binding wisdom on your fingers. Your fingers are instruments of action: everything you do is touched by what is bound upon them. When wisdom is bound to your actions as a couple, every task you undertake together, whether mundane or significant, is guided by divine truth. Let wisdom be not a stranger in your home, but a cherished member of the family.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Now turn to each other. Here are tonight's reflection questions. Answer from the heart, not from the head.

Question 1: Is God's Word truly the 'apple of our eye' as a couple, or have other things taken that place of supreme value?

Question 2: What would it look like to make wisdom a more intimate companion in our daily married life?

Question 3: How does treasuring wisdom together protect our marriage from the temptations described later in Proverbs 7?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

The Puritans ended every evening with prayer. Let us follow their example and bring our marriage before the Lord.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to write His truth upon the tables of your hearts so deeply that it shapes every thought, word, and action. Pray that wisdom would be your most intimate companion as a couple, guarding you from every form of folly.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord engrave His wisdom upon your hearts so that it becomes inseparable from who you are together, a lamp in the darkness and a shield against all that would harm your union. Amen.

May the words you have heard tonight bear fruit in your marriage for years to come. The Lord be with you both. Until next time.
