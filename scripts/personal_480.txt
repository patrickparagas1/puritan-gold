PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 19
"The Valley of Vision — Puritan Prayers"
Reading Day — The Rich Treasury of Puritan Prayer

---

Welcome. Today is a Reading day. We turn to the rich tradition of Puritan prayers — those remarkable compositions that combine theological depth with heartfelt devotion, and that have been collected in anthologies such as The Valley of Vision.

Let us read Psalm 42:1-2 (KJV):

"As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O God. My soul thirsteth for God, for the living God: when shall I come and appear before God?"

--- Teaching ---

The Puritans prayed differently from most modern Christians. Their prayers were long, detailed, theologically rich, emotionally deep, and intensely personal. They poured out their hearts to God with a vocabulary that was shaped by years of Bible reading and doctrinal study. Their prayers are a window into their souls — and a model for our own.

One of the most striking features of Puritan prayer is the combination of deep humility and bold confidence. They approached God as sinners — broken, unworthy, dependent on mercy. But they also approached Him as beloved children — welcomed, accepted, emboldened by the blood of Christ. This combination is captured beautifully in the prayer tradition.

Consider the kind of language the Puritans used in confession. They did not offer vague, general confessions. They named their sins with painful specificity. They confessed not only outward sins but inward ones — pride, unbelief, ingratitude, coldness of heart, wandering thoughts in prayer, self-reliance, love of the world. They understood that confession must be specific to be healing. A general confession — "Lord, forgive me for my sins" — costs nothing and changes nothing. A specific confession — "Lord, forgive me for the envy I felt toward my brother this morning, for the impatience I showed my child this evening, for the pride that swelled in my heart during worship" — cuts deep and opens the wound to the healing power of grace.

The Puritans also excelled in the prayer of adoration. They praised God not with generic superlatives but with careful, specific language drawn from His attributes and His works. They praised His sovereignty — His absolute rule over all things. They praised His holiness — His perfect separation from all evil. They praised His wisdom — His ability to direct all things toward His good purposes. They praised His love — demonstrated supremely at the cross. They praised His faithfulness — His unwavering commitment to His covenant promises.

Their prayers of petition were equally rich. The Puritans asked God for specific things — not because they thought prayer was a way to manipulate God into giving them what they wanted, but because they believed that God invites His children to bring their needs, desires, and longings before Him. They prayed for spiritual blessings more than material ones: for growth in holiness, for deeper knowledge of Christ, for greater love for God's Word, for power to resist temptation, for sensitivity to the Holy Spirit's leading.

The Puritans also practiced intercessory prayer with great earnestness. They prayed for their families, their churches, their pastors, their communities, their nation, and the worldwide advance of the Gospel. Cotton Mather was known to spend entire days in fasting and prayer for revival. David Brainerd — the missionary whose diary deeply influenced Jonathan Edwards — spent hours in the wilderness interceding for the conversion of the Native Americans among whom he served.

Perhaps the most distinctive feature of Puritan prayer is its theological precision. The Puritans prayed their theology. Their prayers were saturated with Scripture, shaped by doctrine, and grounded in the great truths of the faith. When they prayed for forgiveness, they grounded their petition in the atonement. When they prayed for strength, they appealed to the Holy Spirit. When they prayed for guidance, they trusted the providence of God. Their prayers were not emotional outbursts disconnected from truth; they were informed, deliberate engagements with the God whom they knew through His Word.

Let me offer a sample of the kind of prayer the Puritans composed, in the spirit of their tradition: "O Lord, I am a sinful creature in a sinful world, but I come to Thee through the blood of Christ. I am weak, but Thou art strong. I am foolish, but Thou art wise. I am empty, but Thou art full. Fill me with Thy Spirit. Wash me with Thy blood. Guide me with Thy Word. And keep me by Thy power until the day of Christ Jesus."

--- Closing ---

Let the Puritans teach you to pray. Not their exact words, but their depth, their honesty, their theological richness, their heartfelt devotion. Lord, teach me to pray as the Puritans prayed — with deep humility, bold confidence, specific confession, rich adoration, and Spirit-filled petition. May my prayer life honor You as their prayers honored You. Amen.
