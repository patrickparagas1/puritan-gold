PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 5
"Reading Scripture Together"
John Angell James — A Help to Domestic Happiness


Hello and welcome to our family devotional. Come, young and old alike. God's Word has something for every member of this family tonight. Let us draw near to Him.

Shall we pray? Our Father, we gather tonight hungry for Your truth. We know that man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from Your mouth. Feed us now, Lord. Bless our family time together. In the name of Christ we pray. Amen.

God has preserved His Word for thousands of years so that families like ours could hear it tonight. Let us read from Psalm 119:105:

"Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path."

That is Psalm 119:105. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Today we celebrate one of the greatest gifts God has ever given to families — His written Word. The Psalmist declares in Psalm 119:105, 'Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.'

Imagine walking through a dark forest at night with no flashlight. Every step is uncertain. You might trip over a root, walk off a cliff, or wander in circles. Now imagine someone hands you a bright lamp. Suddenly, you can see the path. You can avoid dangers. You know which way to go.

That is exactly what God's Word does for our lives. The world we live in is full of confusing messages. People tell us different things about what is right and wrong, what matters and what does not. Without a reliable guide, we would be lost. But God has not left us in the dark. He has given us the Bible — a lamp for our feet and a light for our path.

Psalm 119 is the longest chapter in the entire Bible — 176 verses — and every single verse is about the Word of God. The Psalmist uses many different words for Scripture: law, testimonies, precepts, statutes, commandments, judgments, word, and ordinances. And throughout this magnificent psalm, the writer expresses deep love and delight in God's Word.

In verse 97, he says, 'O how love I thy law! it is my meditation all the day.' In verse 103, 'How sweet are thy words unto my taste! yea, sweeter than honey to my mouth!' In verse 162, 'I rejoice at thy word, as one that findeth great spoil.' This is not someone who reads the Bible out of mere duty. This is someone who treasures it like a discovered treasure chest.

The New Testament reinforces this. Paul writes to Timothy in 2 Timothy 3:16-17, 'All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: That the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works.' Scripture is God-breathed. It teaches us what is true (doctrine), shows us where we are wrong (reproof), helps us get back on track (correction), and trains us in right living (instruction in righteousness).

John Angell James was passionate about family Bible reading. He wrote that the Bible should be the first book in every family, read with reverence, regularity, and relish. Notice those three words. Reverence — because this is God's Word, not just any book. Regularity — because we need its light every day, not just occasionally. And relish — because reading the Bible should be a delight, not a drudgery.

James gave practical counsel for family Bible reading. He suggested reading a passage aloud together, then having the father or mother explain it in simple language that even the youngest child could understand. He encouraged parents to ask questions — 'What did we learn about God in this passage? What does this teach us to do? Is there a promise here we can claim? Is there a warning we should heed?'

He also urged families to read through whole books of the Bible together rather than jumping around randomly. This helps everyone see the big story of Scripture — how God creates, how humanity falls, how God promises a Redeemer, and how that Redeemer comes in Jesus Christ.

James warned against two extremes. One is reading too much at once, which overwhelms younger children and turns Bible time into an endurance test. The other is reading too little, which robs the family of the depth and richness of God's Word. A good chapter or a meaningful passage, read carefully and discussed warmly, is better than rushing through five chapters with no understanding.

For our youngest family members: the Bible is like a special lamp that shows us the way to go. God gave it to us because He loves us and does not want us to be lost in the dark. For older children: have you ever noticed that the more you read the Bible, the more you understand it? It is like a light that gets brighter the more you use it. And for the whole family: let us make Bible reading together one of the best parts of our day — not a chore, but a treasure hunt where we discover more of God's goodness every time we open His Book.


There is much we can learn from those who walked with God before us. Consider these words.

John Angell James, in his work "A Help to Domestic Happiness," wrote these words:

"The Bible should be the first book in every family. It should be read with reverence, with regularity, and with relish. Let the Word of God dwell richly in your household."

What John Angell James is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Psalm 119:105 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Family, let us talk about what we have learned. Consider these questions together.

Question 1: What does a lamp do? How is the Bible like a lamp for our lives?

Question 2: What is your favorite story or verse in the Bible so far?

Question 3: Why do you think the Psalmist said God's Word is sweeter than honey? Have you ever felt that way about Scripture?

Question 4: How can we make our family Bible reading time more meaningful and enjoyable for everyone?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path." -- Psalm 119:105

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Learning sticks better when we do something with it. Try this activity as a family:

Have a 'Bible Treasure Hunt.' Choose a short passage and hide key words from the passage around the house on slips of paper. Family members search for the words and then put them in order to form the verse. The first person or team to assemble the verse correctly wins.


Lord, as we close tonight's devotional, we place our family in Your hands. Watch over us as we sleep. Guard our dreams. Prepare our hearts for tomorrow. May the truths from Psalm 119:105 follow us into the new day. We love You, Lord. Amen.

Goodnight. Remember your memory verse, and carry it with you tomorrow.
