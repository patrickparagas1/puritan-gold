PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 20
"Hospitality and the Open Home"
William Gouge — On Welcoming Others Together

---

Welcome, dear couple. The Puritans did not view marriage as a private retreat from the world. They saw the married home as a base of operations for ministry, hospitality, and Gospel witness. Tonight we explore this outward-facing dimension of married life.

Our Scripture tonight is Romans 12:13:

"Distributing to the necessity of saints; given to hospitality."

And Hebrews 13:2:

"Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares."

--- Teaching ---

William Gouge wrote that a Christian household should be an open door — a place where the weary find rest, the hungry find food, the lonely find companionship, and the spiritually struggling find encouragement. He saw hospitality not as an optional add-on to family life but as one of its essential purposes.

The Puritans distinguished between entertainment and hospitality. Entertainment impresses. Hospitality blesses. Entertainment focuses on the host. Hospitality focuses on the guest. Entertainment says, "Look at my beautiful home." Hospitality says, "You are welcome in my home, however it looks." The Puritans would have been puzzled by the modern tendency to postpone hospitality until the house is perfectly clean and the menu is perfectly planned. They practiced what we might call "come as we are" hospitality — sharing whatever they had, however simple, with genuine warmth.

Gouge noted that hospitality is a joint ministry of husband and wife. The husband who invites guests but leaves all the preparation and serving to his wife is not practicing hospitality — he is burdening his wife. Similarly, the wife who takes on the entire labor of hospitality without her husband's partnership will quickly burn out. The Puritans counseled couples to discuss their hospitality together — whom to invite, how often, what to serve, how to share the work — so that both partners feel ownership and joy in the ministry.

Richard Baxter observed that some of the most powerful evangelistic opportunities come not from pulpits but from dinner tables. When a married couple opens their home and shares a meal with neighbors, coworkers, or strangers, they create a space where the Gospel becomes tangible. People experience the love of Christ not through a sermon but through the warmth of a home where Christ is Lord.

Thomas Gataker added that hospitality strengthens a marriage by giving the couple a shared mission. Some of the happiest marriages he observed were those where husband and wife were united in serving others. Having a purpose beyond themselves — a reason to cook together, clean together, plan together, pray together — breathed fresh energy into their relationship.

The Puritans also counseled discernment in hospitality. Open doors do not mean no boundaries. A couple must protect their family's health, their children's safety, and their own marriage from the draining effects of over-commitment. Hospitality should be life-giving, not life-depleting. If your hospitality is making you resentful or exhausted, something needs to be adjusted — not the calling, but the practice.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Discuss together:

Question 1: How would you describe your home's hospitality? Is your door open to others, or has your home become a private fortress? What barriers exist?

Question 2: Is there a family, a couple, or an individual whom God might be calling you to invite into your home this month? Who comes to mind?

Question 3: How can you share the work of hospitality more equitably so that both of you find joy in it rather than burden?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Pray together for a spirit of hospitality. Ask God to open your eyes to the people around you who need the warmth of a Christian home. Pray for the courage to open your door, the generosity to share what you have, and the faith to trust that God will multiply it.

--- Closing ---

May the God who welcomed you into His family through the blood of Christ give you the grace to welcome others into your home. May your table be a place of Gospel witness, and may your marriage be strengthened by the joy of shared ministry. Goodnight.
