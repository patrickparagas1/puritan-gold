PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 20
"Remedy: Stay in God's Word"
Pilgrim Families — Week 3, Day 6

---

Good evening, family. There is no place God loves to meet His people more than when families gather in His name. Welcome to Puritan Gold. Let us open our Bibles and our hearts.

Let us begin in prayer. Lord Jesus, You said that where two or three are gathered in Your name, there You are in the midst of them. We claim that promise tonight as a family. Be here with us. Teach us. Shape us. We pray in Your holy name. Amen.

Every word of Scripture is breathed out by God. Tonight we receive His breath from Psalm 119:9-11; Ephesians 6:17:

"Wherewithal shall a young man cleanse his way? by taking heed thereto according to thy word. With my whole heart have I sought thee: O let me not wander from thy commandments. Thy word have I hid in my heart, that I might not sin against thee. And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God."

That is Psalm 119:9-11; Ephesians 6:17. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

The third remedy Brooks prescribes is the most powerful weapon in the Christian's arsenal: the Word of God. If Satan's primary strategy is deception, then truth is our primary defense. And the supreme source of truth is Holy Scripture.

The Psalmist asks a practical question in Psalm 119:9: "Wherewithal shall a young man cleanse his way?" How does a person — especially a young person surrounded by temptation — stay pure? The answer is straightforward: "By taking heed thereto according to thy word." By paying careful attention to God's Word and ordering your life by it.

Then comes one of the most important verses for spiritual warfare: "Thy word have I hid in my heart, that I might not sin against thee" (Psalm 119:11). Hiding God's Word in your heart means more than reading it casually or hearing it on Sunday. It means memorizing it, meditating on it, internalizing it until it becomes part of who you are. It means having Scripture so deeply embedded in your mind that when temptation comes, the right verse rises to the surface like a reflex.

Paul calls the Word of God "the sword of the Spirit" in Ephesians 6:17. In the armor of God, every other piece is defensive — the shield, the breastplate, the helmet. But the sword is offensive. It is the weapon with which we attack. And it is the only offensive weapon listed. The Christian's attack strategy against Satan is not cleverness, not willpower, not good intentions — it is the Word of God.

Jesus Himself demonstrated this in the wilderness temptation (Matthew 4:1-11). When Satan tempted Him to turn stones into bread, Jesus responded, "It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone." When Satan tempted Him to throw Himself from the temple, Jesus responded, "It is written again, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God." When Satan offered Him all the kingdoms of the world, Jesus responded, "It is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve." Three temptations. Three responses. All three from Scripture. Jesus — the Son of God, who had infinite wisdom and power — chose to fight Satan with the Word.

If the Son of God used Scripture to defeat temptation, how much more do we need it? Brooks observed that the Christian who neglects Scripture is like a soldier who leaves his sword at home before going into battle. You may have the best armor in the world, but without a sword, you cannot fight. You can only defend, and eventually your defenses will be overwhelmed.

For families, this means Scripture must be central to your daily life — not an afterthought, not a Sunday-only activity, but the bread your family eats every day. Read it together at meals. Discuss it at bedtime. Memorize it during car rides. Teach your children not just to know about the Bible but to know the Bible itself — its words, its stories, its promises, its commands.

Brooks particularly encouraged the memorization of Scripture. A verse memorized is a verse available at the moment of temptation. You may not have your Bible with you when the temptation strikes, but if the Word is hidden in your heart, it is always there. When anger rises, "A soft answer turneth away wrath" rises to meet it. When fear grips, "Fear thou not; for I am with thee" pushes back the darkness. When lust tempts, "Flee also youthful lusts" sounds the alarm.

The Puritans were people of one Book. They read Scripture morning and evening. They discussed it at meals. They memorized large portions of it. They sang it in worship. They applied it to every area of life. And the result was a generation of Christians who were remarkably strong in the face of persecution, temptation, and trial.

Start tonight. Pick one verse from this week's study and memorize it as a family. Say it before bed. Say it in the morning. Say it at dinner. Within a week, it will be hidden in your hearts. And when the enemy comes — and he will come — you will have a sword ready.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

One of the great blessings of the Christian tradition is that we stand on the shoulders of faithful saints who came before us.

Thomas Brooks, in his work "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices," wrote these words:

"The Scripture is the sun; the church is the clock, which is to be set by the sun. The Scripture is the touchstone; all doctrines, opinions, and practices must be brought to the Scripture to be tried."

What Thomas Brooks is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Psalm 119:9-11; Ephesians 6:17 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Now we get to the best part: talking about God's Word together as a family. Here are tonight's questions.

Question 1: How did Jesus use Scripture to defeat Satan in the wilderness? What does this teach us about the power of God's Word?

Question 2: What does it mean to 'hide' God's Word in your heart? How is this different from just reading the Bible?

Question 3: Why is Scripture called 'the sword of the Spirit'? What makes it an offensive weapon?

Question 4: What verse would you most want to have ready when temptation comes? Why?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Thy word have I hid in my heart, that I might not sin against thee." -- Psalm 119:11

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Here is a practical way to live out tonight's devotional:

Begin a family Scripture memory challenge. Choose one verse to memorize together this week. Write it on index cards and place them around the house — on the bathroom mirror, the refrigerator, the dinner table. Quiz each other throughout the week and celebrate when everyone can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Gracious God, thank You for meeting with our family tonight. Your Word never returns void, and we trust that what we have heard will bear fruit in each of our lives. Strengthen our family bonds, deepen our faith, and make us a light in this world. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

May the peace of God guard your hearts and minds tonight. Goodnight, family.
