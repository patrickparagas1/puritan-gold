PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 14
"The Secret of Seeking God First"
Pilgrim Families — Week 2, Day 7


Welcome, dear family. Another day has passed, and God has been faithful through it all. Now let us close this day together by sitting at the feet of Jesus and hearing His Word.

Let us pray. Almighty God, You are the giver of every good gift, and one of the greatest gifts You have given us is this family. We dedicate this time to You. Help us to listen well and to grow in wisdom together. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

Our passage tonight is Matthew 6:33; Psalm 37:4. Listen with expectant hearts:

"But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you. Delight thyself also in the LORD; and he shall give thee the desires of thine heart."

That is Matthew 6:33; Psalm 37:4. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


We close our week with Jeremiah Burroughs by arriving at the wellspring of all contentment: seeking God first. Everything Burroughs taught about contentment flows from this single fountain. If God is your greatest treasure, you will be content in any circumstance, because the one thing you value most can never be taken from you.

Jesus gives us the key in Matthew 6:33: "But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you." The context is crucial. In the preceding verses, Jesus has been addressing worry about food, clothing, and the necessities of life. The pagans chase after these things (Matthew 6:32). But the children of God are called to a different priority: seek the kingdom first, and everything else will follow.

"First" does not mean "before you do other things." It means "above all other things." It means "as the supreme priority of your life." When God's kingdom and God's righteousness are the consuming passion of your heart, you discover that the things you were so worried about — food, clothing, shelter — are taken care of by your Father who knows what you need before you even ask.

The Psalmist adds a stunning promise in Psalm 37:4: "Delight thyself also in the LORD; and he shall give thee the desires of thine heart." At first glance, this might sound like a blank check — delight in God and He will give you whatever you want. But look deeper. When you truly delight in the Lord, your desires change. You begin to want what God wants. Your heart's desires align with His purposes. And then He gladly grants them, because they are His desires too.

This is the ultimate secret of contentment that Burroughs reveals: the contented Christian has God as their portion. Not God plus a nice house. Not God plus good health. Not God plus financial security. Just God. And God is enough because God is infinite. An infinite God can fill an infinite void. Every other treasure is finite — it can only satisfy partially and temporarily. But God satisfies completely and eternally.

Burroughs taught that the reason most Christians are discontent is that they have too many portions. They want God AND comfort. God AND approval. God AND success. But when any of these secondary things are threatened, their contentment crumbles — proving that God alone was never truly their portion. The person who has God alone as their portion is untouchable. You can take away their health, their wealth, their reputation, even their freedom — and they still have everything, because they still have God.

This is what the Puritans meant by "enjoying God." They did not merely believe in God or obey God — they enjoyed God. They found their greatest pleasure, their deepest satisfaction, their highest delight in knowing and communing with the living God. This is why they could endure persecution, imprisonment, and poverty with songs on their lips. Their treasure was in heaven, where no moth could eat it, no rust could corrode it, and no thief could steal it.

For families, seeking God first is the foundation of everything else. When God is first in your family, everything else falls into its proper place. Your marriage is stronger because it is built on the Rock. Your parenting is wiser because it is guided by the Word. Your finances are healthier because you are free from greed. Your relationships are deeper because you are free from selfish ambition. Contentment flows naturally from a family that has its priorities right.

Tonight, evaluate your family's priorities. Not to feel condemned, but to recalibrate. Are there areas where something other than God has taken first place? A sport? A career? A screen? An ambition? These things are not necessarily bad, but when they are first, they rob us of the contentment that comes from seeking God above all.

Burroughs' great book can be summarized in a single sentence: Find your all in God, and you will never be discontent again. This is the rare jewel. It is rare because few discover it. It is a jewel because its value surpasses everything the world can offer. And it is available tonight to every family that will seek the kingdom of God first.


Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher and learn something that will deepen our understanding.

Jeremiah Burroughs, in his work "The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment," wrote these words:

"The soul that is truly contented has God as its portion, and God is an infinite, all-sufficient good, in whom alone there is enough to fill and satisfy the heart."

What Jeremiah Burroughs is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Matthew 6:33; Psalm 37:4 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Let us discuss what we have heard. Parents, encourage even the youngest to share what they are thinking.

Question 1: What does it mean practically for our family to 'seek first the kingdom of God'? What would change in our daily routine?

Question 2: Psalm 37:4 says God gives us the desires of our heart when we delight in Him. How does delighting in God change what we desire?

Question 3: Burroughs says God is an 'all-sufficient good.' What does it mean for God to be enough?

Question 4: Looking back at this whole week on contentment, what is one thing our family wants to change or practice going forward?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you." -- Matthew 6:33

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Before we close, here is a family challenge for the days ahead:

Have each family member write a 'First Things' list — ranking the top five things that get their time and attention each day. Then rewrite the list with God at the top and discuss what practical changes would help make that a reality.


Heavenly Father, we end where we began, in gratitude. Thank You for Your Word, for this family, and for the hope we have in Christ. May tonight's lesson from Matthew 6:33; Psalm 37:4 shape the way we live, the way we speak, and the way we love one another. Amen and amen.

Until next time, dear family. The Lord goes before you. Walk with Him.
