PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 6
"Leaving and Cleaving"
Genesis 2:24 + Ephesians 5 — Mutual Submission and Love

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Tonight we step back to the very beginning, to the garden where God first joined man and woman together. The passage we studied last evening quoted Genesis 2:24, and tonight we linger there, for this verse contains truths that every married couple must understand deeply.

Let us read our passage. Genesis 2:24 (KJV):

"Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh."

--- Teaching ---

Two commands are given in this verse, and together they form the foundation of every marriage that has ever existed or ever will exist. The first command is to leave. The second is to cleave. Both are essential. Neither can be neglected without harm to the marriage.

To leave father and mother does not mean to dishonor them or to abandon them. The fifth commandment still stands. But it does mean that the marriage bond takes priority over the parental bond. When a man marries, his primary loyalty shifts. His wife becomes his nearest relation, his closest companion, his first earthly responsibility. The same is true for the wife. While she continues to honor her parents, her husband now holds the place of primacy in her affections and her duties.

William Gouge addressed this matter at length, for it was a common problem in Puritan households. Parents who refused to release their married children created untold grief. Gouge counseled newlywed couples to establish clear boundaries with their families of origin, not out of disrespect, but out of obedience to God's design. He noted that a husband who allows his mother to govern his wife, or a wife who runs to her father whenever she disagrees with her husband, has not truly left. The leaving must be emotional and decisional, not merely physical.

The second command is to cleave. The word in Hebrew means to cling, to hold fast, to adhere with loyalty and devotion. It is the same word used in Deuteronomy 10:20 to describe our relationship with God: "Thou shalt fear the LORD thy God; him shalt thou serve, and to him shalt thou cleave." To cleave to your spouse is to bind yourself to them with the same tenacity and devotion with which you bind yourself to God. It is a commitment that does not waver with changing feelings, changing circumstances, or changing seasons.

Richard Baxter warned that one of the greatest threats to cleaving is the slow drift of neglect. Couples do not often break apart suddenly. They drift apart gradually. They stop talking about things that matter. They stop praying together. They stop investing in the relationship. Each day a small distance grows, until one morning they wake up and realize they are strangers sharing a house. Baxter counseled couples to guard their unity with daily intentionality. Speak to each other every day about the things of God. Pray together every night. Let no day pass without some expression of affection and devotion.

The result of leaving and cleaving is becoming one flesh. This is more than a physical union, though it includes that sacred dimension. It is a union of hearts, minds, purposes, and destinies. In marriage, two separate lives are woven together into one new life. Your joys become shared joys. Your sorrows become shared sorrows. Your calling becomes a shared calling. The Puritans spoke of this unity as a reflection of the unity within the Godhead: distinct persons bound together in perfect love and harmony.

Dear couple, examine your marriage tonight in the light of this verse. Have you truly left? Are there loyalties, attachments, or influences from outside your marriage that are competing with the bond between you? Have you truly cleaved? Are you holding fast to each other with daily, intentional devotion? Or have you begun to drift?

The beauty of marriage is that it is never too late to recommit. You can leave and cleave afresh tonight. You can turn toward each other, take each other's hands, and say: "I choose you. I cling to you. Before God, you are my first earthly love." This is the renewal that every marriage needs, not once, but daily.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: Are there any relationships or influences from outside our marriage that are competing with our bond? How can we address them?

Question 2: In what ways have we been faithful in cleaving to each other? In what ways have we begun to drift?

Question 3: What is one practical step we can take this week to strengthen our unity as one flesh?

--- Prayer ---

O Lord God, who in the beginning didst join man and woman together and declare them one flesh, we ask Thee to strengthen the bond of this marriage. Help us to leave all competing loyalties and to cleave to one another with the devotion Thou dost require. Guard us from the slow drift of neglect. Rekindle in us a daily intentionality to love, to serve, and to hold fast. May our unity reflect the perfect love of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. We pray in Jesus' name, Amen.
