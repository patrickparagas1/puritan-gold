PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 22
"The Rare Jewel of a Content Family"
Jeremiah Burroughs — The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment for Families

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to our final full week of Puritan Gold for June. This week we sit with Jeremiah Burroughs, one of the gentlest and most persuasive of all the Puritans, as he opens the treasure chest of Christian contentment and shows us how this rare jewel transforms family life. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, we live in a world that is never satisfied. Teach us this week, through the wisdom of Jeremiah Burroughs, what it means to be content — truly content — in every circumstance, because You are our portion. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Philippians 4:11-13. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Not that I speak in respect of want: for I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content. I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound: every where and in all things I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me."

That is Philippians 4:11-13. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Jeremiah Burroughs was a Puritan minister who lived during the English Civil War, a time of enormous upheaval and uncertainty. Like many of his fellow Puritans, he was ejected from his pulpit and forced into a precarious existence. And yet, during this tumultuous period, he wrote one of the most beautiful and practical books in the entire Puritan library: The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment. The title itself tells us something important: Burroughs regarded contentment not as a common virtue easily attained but as a rare and precious jewel that must be sought, treasured, and guarded.

Burroughs defined Christian contentment as "that sweet, inward, quiet, gracious frame of spirit, which freely submits to and delights in God's wise and fatherly disposal in every condition." Notice the richness of this definition. Contentment is sweet — not bitter or grudging. It is inward — rooted in the heart, not dependent on outward circumstances. It is quiet — peaceful, not anxious or agitated. It is gracious — a work of God's grace, not a product of human willpower. And it freely submits to and delights in God's wise and fatherly disposal — it does not merely tolerate God's will but actually rejoices in it, trusting that the Father's choices are always good.

For families, contentment is one of the most transformative virtues imaginable. Consider the opposite of contentment — discontentment, which is a chronic dissatisfaction with what God has given. A discontent family is a miserable family, regardless of how much it possesses. The house is never nice enough. The income is never large enough. The children are never well-behaved enough. The vacations are never exciting enough. Discontentment poisons gratitude, breeds comparison, and turns every blessing into a disappointment.

By contrast, a content family is a joyful family, regardless of its circumstances. They may live in a small house, but they are grateful for shelter. They may have a modest income, but they are thankful for provision. They may face difficulties, but they trust that God's disposal is wise and fatherly. Contentment does not mean that the family never aspires to improvement or never works toward goals. It means that the family's happiness is not dependent on reaching those goals. Their joy has a deeper source — the unchanging goodness of God.

Burroughs was especially insightful about the roots of discontentment. He taught that discontentment is, at its core, a heart problem. It is not caused by our circumstances but by our interpretation of our circumstances. Two families can face the exact same situation — say, a job loss — and respond in completely different ways. One family panics, blames God, and spirals into anxiety. The other family grieves honestly but then trusts God's providence and looks for His purpose in the trial. The difference is not in the circumstances but in the heart.

Burroughs also observed that contentment is learned, not automatic. Paul himself said, "I have learned to be content." This implies that there was a time when Paul was not content, and that he had to go through a process of learning — through experience, reflection, prayer, and the work of the Spirit — to reach the point where contentment was a settled disposition of his soul. Families should not be discouraged if contentment does not come easily. It is a skill to be developed, a muscle to be strengthened, a grace to be cultivated over time.

Tonight, begin the journey toward contentment as a family. Look around your home and give thanks for what you have rather than lamenting what you lack. Name the blessings. Count them. And let gratitude begin to crowd out discontentment.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Burroughs defined contentment as "a sweet, inward, quiet, gracious frame of spirit." Which word in that definition stands out to you most, and why?

Question 2: Paul said he "learned" to be content. What might the learning process look like for our family?

Question 3: What are five blessings we can name right now that we sometimes take for granted?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content." -- Philippians 4:11b

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord, we confess that we are often discontent. We look at what we lack instead of what You have given. Teach us the rare jewel of contentment. Fill our family with a quiet, settled joy that rests in Your goodness regardless of our circumstances. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, content in the arms of a Father who gives good gifts to His children.
