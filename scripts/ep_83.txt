PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"Thy Word Is a Lamp"
May 22, 2026

---

"Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path. I have sworn, and I will perform it, that I will keep thy righteous judgments. I am afflicted very much: quicken me, O Lord, according unto thy word. Accept, I beseech thee, the freewill offerings of my mouth, O Lord, and teach me thy judgments. My soul is continually in my hand: yet do I not forget thy law. The wicked have laid a snare for me: yet I erred not from thy precepts. Thy testimonies have I taken as an heritage for ever: for they are the rejoicing of my heart." -- Psalm 119:105-112

The hundred and nineteenth psalm is the longest chapter in the entire Bible, and its singular theme from first verse to last is the glory, the sufficiency, and the preciousness of the Word of God. Every one of its one hundred and seventy-six verses makes some reference to the Scriptures, under one title or another: the law, the testimonies, the statutes, the commandments, the judgments, the precepts, the word, the way. And in the section before us today, we find one of the best-loved verses in all of Holy Scripture: "Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path."

A lamp unto my feet. Not a searchlight that illuminates the landscape for miles in every direction, but a lamp, a small, portable light that casts its glow just far enough ahead for the next step. This is a vitally important distinction. Many believers are troubled because they cannot see the whole of their future laid out before them in clear and comprehensible detail. They want to know where they will be in five years, in ten years, in twenty years. They want a road map of their entire journey. But God, in His wisdom, gives us a lamp, not a searchlight. He shows us the next step, not the next mile. And He does so because He knows that if we could see the whole road ahead, we would be overwhelmed by its length and its difficulties, and we would lose heart before we had taken a single step.

The lamp of God's Word shows us enough light for the present moment. It illuminates the duty that lies immediately before us. It reveals the temptation that lurks at our feet. It discloses the path of obedience that God would have us walk today, this hour, this very moment. And when we have taken that step, the lamp moves forward, and the next step is illuminated, and then the next, and then the next. And so, one step at a time, the believer makes his way through the darkness of this world, guided by a light that never fails because it comes from a God who never changes.

"I have sworn, and I will perform it, that I will keep thy righteous judgments." Here is the resolution of a man who has counted the cost and made his choice. He has bound himself by a solemn oath to obey the Word of God. This is not rashness or presumption. It is the deliberate commitment of a heart that has been persuaded of the rightness and the goodness of God's commandments. The Puritans placed great emphasis upon the importance of holy resolutions, not because they trusted in the strength of human willpower, but because they understood that the act of resolving, when done in dependence upon the grace of God, is one of the means by which God strengthens the soul for obedience.

"I am afflicted very much: quicken me, O Lord, according unto thy word." The psalmist does not pretend that the path of obedience is easy. He is afflicted. He is under pressure. The commitment to keep God's Word has brought him into conflict with the world, and the world is making him pay for his faithfulness. But his response to affliction is not to abandon his resolution but to cry out for fresh supplies of spiritual vitality. Quicken me. Give me life. Revive my drooping spirits. Renew my flagging strength. And the ground of his plea is not his own deservingness but the promise of God's Word. "According unto thy word." The Word that guides him is the same Word that sustains him. The lamp that shows the path also supplies the strength to walk it.

"My soul is continually in my hand: yet do I not forget thy law." This is a Hebrew expression meaning that the psalmist's life is constantly at risk. He walks each day in the shadow of danger, with death as a perpetual companion. And yet, in the midst of this mortal peril, he does not forget the law of God. Lesser concerns may be forgiven in times of extreme danger. When a man is fighting for his life, he may be excused for forgetting his manners. But the psalmist, even with his soul in his hand, does not forget the Word. It has become so deeply woven into the fabric of his being that no external pressure can tear it loose.

"The wicked have laid a snare for me: yet I erred not from thy precepts." The snares of the wicked are many and varied. They may take the form of persecution, designed to frighten the believer into abandoning his faith. They may take the form of temptation, designed to lure the believer into abandoning his holiness. They may take the form of deception, designed to lead the believer astray from the truth. But the man whose feet are guided by the lamp of God's Word walks safely through them all. He sees the snare before he steps into it. He recognises the temptation before it overwhelms him. He detects the lie before it deceives him. For the Word of God is not only a lamp for his feet but a weapon in his hand, sharper than any two-edged sword.

"Thy testimonies have I taken as an heritage for ever: for they are the rejoicing of my heart." An heritage is an inheritance, a permanent possession that is passed down from generation to generation. The psalmist has claimed the Word of God as his inheritance, his personal and perpetual possession, his treasure and his legacy. And this inheritance is the rejoicing of his heart. Not a duty grudgingly performed. Not an obligation reluctantly accepted. But a joy. A delight. A source of gladness that no earthly inheritance can rival.

Samuel Rutherford, writing from his prison in Aberdeen, declared that one line of God's Word was worth more to him than all the kingdoms of the earth. And he spoke from experience, for he had been stripped of everything that the world counts valuable, his church, his ministry, his freedom, his comfort, and he had found the testimonies of God more than sufficient to fill the void. The man who has the Word of God has an heritage that moth and rust cannot corrupt, that thieves cannot break through and steal, and that death itself cannot destroy. Amen.
