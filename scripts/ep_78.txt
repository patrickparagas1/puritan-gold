PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"The Lord Reigneth"
May 17, 2026

---

"The Lord reigneth; let the earth rejoice; let the multitude of isles be glad thereof. Clouds and darkness are round about him: righteousness and judgment are the habitation of his throne. A fire goeth before him, and burneth up his enemies round about. His lightnings enlightened the world: the earth saw, and trembled. The hills melted like wax at the presence of the Lord, at the presence of the Lord of the whole earth." -- Psalm 97:1-5

"The Lord reigneth." Three words, and yet they contain more comfort, more courage, and more cause for rejoicing than all the libraries of human philosophy combined. The Lord reigneth. Not fate. Not chance. Not the blind forces of nature. Not the wicked councils of evil men. The Lord. The covenant God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. The God who numbers the hairs of our heads and counts the stars by name. He reigneth. He sits upon the throne of the universe with absolute authority, with unlimited power, and with unfailing wisdom. And because He reigneth, the earth is to rejoice.

Now, this is a bold command. The earth is full of trouble. Everywhere we look, we see suffering, injustice, corruption, and death. How can the earth rejoice? It can rejoice because the troubles of the earth do not contradict the reign of God. They are comprehended within it. The Lord does not reign in spite of the world's troubles but through them and over them. He works all things according to the counsel of His own will, and that will is infinitely wise, infinitely good, and infinitely powerful. Not a sparrow falls to the ground without His knowledge. Not a hair of our heads perishes without His permission. And every sorrow, every trial, every affliction that touches the lives of His people is woven by His sovereign hand into a tapestry of grace that, when finally completed, shall display the glory of God in ways that unfallen angels could never have imagined.

"Clouds and darkness are round about him." This is a profoundly honest statement. The ways of God are not always clear to us. His purposes are often wrapped in mystery. We see the clouds, and we feel the darkness, and we cry out, "Why, Lord? What art thou doing? Where art thou leading?" But the psalmist does not stop with the clouds. He presses through them to the throne that they surround, and there he finds something solid, something certain, something that will never give way: "Righteousness and judgment are the habitation of his throne." The clouds may hide His purposes, but they cannot change His character. Behind every mysterious providence, behind every baffling circumstance, behind every dark and perplexing dispensation of His will, the throne of God remains what it has always been: a throne of perfect righteousness and perfect justice.

This is the great lesson that every believer must learn if he is to walk by faith and not by sight. We cannot always understand what God is doing. But we can always trust what God is. And what He is, is righteous. What He is, is just. What He is, is good. And therefore, whatever He does must partake of these same qualities, even when the doing of it is hidden from our eyes behind clouds and darkness.

"A fire goeth before him, and burneth up his enemies round about." The God who reigns is not a passive monarch who sits upon His throne while His enemies run rampant. He is a consuming fire, and His judgments go forth with irresistible power. The enemies of God may flourish for a season. They may seem to prevail. They may lift up their heads and boast of their strength. But the fire of God goes before Him, and in the day of His appearing, every enemy shall be consumed like stubble in the flame.

"His lightnings enlightened the world: the earth saw, and trembled." The interventions of God in human history are like flashes of lightning that suddenly illuminate a dark landscape. In a single moment, the whole terrain is visible, the mountains and the valleys, the rivers and the roads, the cities and the wildernesses. And men who had been walking in darkness are suddenly confronted with the reality of God's power and God's presence. The earth sees, and it trembles. For the sight of God in His majesty is a fearful thing to those who are not at peace with Him.

"The hills melted like wax at the presence of the Lord." What are the greatest powers of this world in the presence of the Lord of the whole earth? They are wax before a furnace. They are chaff before the wind. They are shadows before the noonday sun. The empires that seemed so permanent, the institutions that seemed so enduring, the movements that seemed so irresistible, all melt away at the presence of the Lord. History is the graveyard of human pretensions, and the God who reigns has outlasted them all.

"Zion heard, and was glad; and the daughters of Judah rejoiced because of thy judgments, O Lord." While the wicked tremble, the righteous rejoice. The judgments of God, which are a terror to His enemies, are a comfort to His friends. For every judgment that falls upon evil is a vindication of good. Every stroke that overthrows the oppressor is a liberation for the oppressed. And every demonstration of God's sovereign power is a confirmation of the faith that His people have placed in Him.

The practical application of this psalm for every believer is simply this: do not be dismayed by what you see in the world around you. Do not be shaken by the apparent triumph of wickedness or the apparent failure of righteousness. The Lord reigneth. He has always reigned. He will always reign. And in His own time, which is the right time, He will make all things plain. The clouds will part. The darkness will lift. The fire of His justice will consume every trace of evil. And the earth shall be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord, as the waters cover the sea.

Until that day, beloved, let us live as citizens of a kingdom that cannot be shaken. Let us worship a King whose throne stands upon righteousness and justice. And let us rejoice, not because our circumstances are pleasant, but because our God is on His throne. The Lord reigneth. Amen.
