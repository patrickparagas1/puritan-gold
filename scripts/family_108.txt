PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 8
"Morning Devotion — Meeting God at Daybreak"
Matthew Henry — Commentary on the Psalms

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome to tonight's family devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for gathering together around God's Word. Let us quiet our hearts and prepare to listen to what the Lord has for us.

Let us begin with prayer. Heavenly Father, we thank You for this family. We thank You for bringing us safely through another day. Now we ask that You would open our eyes to see wonderful things in Your Word. Speak to each heart gathered here, from the oldest to the youngest. In Jesus' name we pray. Amen.

Tonight we open our Bibles to Psalm 5:3. Listen carefully as I read our passage:

"My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up."

That is Psalm 5:3. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Welcome to our second week, where we will learn from the beloved Bible commentator Matthew Henry. Henry is perhaps the most widely read Bible commentator in the history of the English-speaking church. His Commentary on the Whole Bible, written in the early 1700s, is still treasured by Christians today. But Henry was not merely a scholar — he was a devoted pastor and a man of deep personal piety, and his insights glow with practical wisdom for families.

Our Scripture today is Psalm 5:3, where David says, 'My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up.'

Notice the repetition: 'in the morning... in the morning.' David is emphatic. He does not meet God at the end of the day when everything else is done. He meets God first. The morning is the starting line of his day, and he begins it with prayer.

The word 'direct' in Hebrew means to arrange, to set in order — like a priest carefully arranging the wood and the sacrifice on the altar. David is not throwing up a hasty, sleepy prayer. He is deliberately, carefully setting his heart before God as his first act of the day. And then he says, 'and will look up' — he prays with expectation, watching for God's answer like a watchman on the city wall scanning the horizon.

This pattern of morning devotion is woven throughout Scripture. In Psalm 143:8, David prays, 'Cause me to hear thy lovingkindness in the morning; for in thee do I trust.' In Psalm 88:13, 'But unto thee have I cried, O LORD; and in the morning shall my prayer prevent thee' — meaning his prayer comes before everything else. In Mark 1:35, we read of Jesus Himself: 'And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed.' If the Son of God needed morning prayer, how much more do we?

The prophet Isaiah speaks of this same discipline in Isaiah 50:4, 'The Lord GOD hath given me the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary: he wakeneth morning by morning, he wakeneth mine ear to hear as the learned.' God wakens our ear morning by morning. He wants to speak to us at the start of each day.

Matthew Henry commented beautifully on Psalm 5:3. He wrote that it is the best beginning of the day to begin it with God, and that the morning is the gate of the day which should be set open to God. Henry believed that a day begun with prayer is a day fortified against temptation. Just as a soldier puts on armor before going into battle, a Christian should put on the armor of prayer before stepping into the challenges of the day.

Henry also observed that morning prayer has a sweetness to it that other times of prayer sometimes lack. In the morning, the mind is fresh, the heart is quiet, and the distractions of the day have not yet begun their clamor. It is like the stillness of dawn before the noise of traffic fills the streets.

Henry gave practical counsel for morning devotion. He suggested beginning with thanksgiving — thanking God for the gift of a new day, for the rest of the night, for the mercies that are new every morning (as Lamentations 3:23 tells us). Then move to confession — acknowledging any sin or waywardness of heart. Then bring your requests — the needs of the day, the challenges ahead, the people who need your prayers. And finally, commit the day to God — place it in His hands, trusting His wisdom and providence.

Henry also believed that families should pray together in the morning, not just individuals. He practiced this in his own home. The Henry household gathered each morning for Scripture reading and prayer before the day's work began. This was not a lengthy, burdensome ritual. It was a brief, warm, purposeful time of meeting with God together.

For our youngest children: just as we eat breakfast to give our bodies energy for the day, prayer gives our hearts and spirits energy. Talking to God in the morning helps us be strong and kind all day long. For older children: think about how your mornings usually go. Is there room for even five minutes of quiet prayer before the rush begins? And for the whole family: what if we tried starting each morning this month with a brief prayer together? Even one minute of family prayer at the breakfast table can change the whole tone of the day.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

Now let us hear from one of the great Puritan teachers of the faith.

Matthew Henry, in his work "Commentary on the Whole Bible," wrote these words:

"It is the best beginning of the day to begin it with God. The morning is, as it were, the gate of the day, and it should be set open to God."

What Matthew Henry is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Psalm 5:3 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Now it is time for our family discussion. Here are some questions to talk about together. Take your time with each one.

Question 1: When does David talk to God — at night, in the afternoon, or in the morning?

Question 2: Why do you think starting the day with prayer is better than waiting until bedtime?

Question 3: What did Matthew Henry mean by calling the morning 'the gate of the day'?

Question 4: What would our family mornings look like if we added a brief time of prayer before we start our day?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up." -- Psalm 5:3

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

Here is a family activity you can do this week to help these truths take root:

Set a 'Morning Prayer Challenge' for this week. Put a reminder note by the breakfast table. Each morning, before eating, one family member leads a short prayer (even one sentence counts!). At the end of the week, talk about how it felt to start each day with God.

--- Closing ---

Let us close in prayer. Heavenly Father, thank You for gathering our family together in Your Word tonight. Thank You for the truth we have heard from Psalm 5:3. Help us to live out what we have learned. Guard our hearts, guide our steps, and fill our home with Your love and peace. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

Goodnight, dear ones. Walk in wisdom tomorrow.
