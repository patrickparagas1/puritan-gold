PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 11
"Walking in Newness of Life"
Romans for Marriage — Day 11

---

Good evening. What a blessing it is to see a husband and wife seeking the Lord together. The Puritans believed that a praying couple is an unstoppable force for the kingdom of God. Let us pray together tonight.

The Word of God has sustained marriages for thousands of years. Tonight it speaks to yours from Romans 6:4-6:

"Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life. For if we have been planted together in the likeness of his death, we shall be also in the likeness of his resurrection: Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin."

That is Romans 6:4-6. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher. John Owen reflected deeply on the truths of Scripture and how they apply to the covenant of marriage.

Paul proclaims a staggering reality: the believer has been buried with Christ and raised to walk in newness of life. The old self — with its patterns of selfishness, pride, and sin — has been crucified. A new creation has emerged. This is not aspirational language; it is a declaration of accomplished fact. In Christ, the old has died and the new has come.

John Owen spent his career exploring the practical implications of this union with Christ. In his work on the mortification of sin, Owen taught that the power of sin has been decisively broken for every believer. We are no longer slaves to sin. The old master has been deposed. This does not mean sin is absent from the believer's life — it means sin no longer reigns in the believer's life. The dominion is broken, even if the presence remains.

For marriage, this doctrine of newness is profoundly liberating. Every couple carries old patterns into their relationship — patterns of communication learned from dysfunctional families, patterns of conflict inherited from past experiences, patterns of selfishness cultivated over years of singleness. These are the 'old man' that Paul says has been crucified with Christ.

Owen counseled believers to live according to their new identity rather than their old habits. You are not who you used to be. The husband who grew up with an angry father does not have to repeat that pattern — his old man was crucified with Christ. The wife who learned manipulation as a survival mechanism does not have to continue in it — she has been raised to walk in newness of life.

This transformation is not accomplished by willpower but by the resurrection power of God. 'Like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.' The same power that raised Jesus from the dead is available to raise your marriage from whatever dead patterns have been holding it down. Old habits can die. New patterns can emerge. Your marriage can be resurrected.

--- Marriage Application ---

Identify the 'old patterns' in your marriage — the habits, reactions, and behaviors that belong to your old self rather than your new self in Christ. These might include defensive responses, critical speech, emotional withdrawal, controlling behavior, or passive aggression.

Then declare together: these patterns were crucified with Christ. They do not define us. We are new creations. We walk in newness of life.

Choose one specific old pattern to put to death this week. Replace it with a new pattern that reflects your identity in Christ. For example: replace critical speech with encouraging words, replace withdrawal with vulnerability, replace control with trust.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Here are some questions to guide your conversation tonight. Take turns sharing, and give each other the gift of your full attention.

Question 1: What old patterns from your past do you still carry into your marriage? Where did they come from?

Question 2: How does knowing that your 'old man is crucified with Christ' give you hope for change?

Question 3: What 'newness of life' do you want to see in your marriage? What specific new pattern do you want to build?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Prayer is the heartbeat of a Christian marriage. Let us pray together now. You do not need eloquent words, just honest ones.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God for resurrection power in your marriage. Pray that old, destructive patterns would truly die and that new, Christ-honoring patterns would take their place. Thank God that in Christ, you are new creations.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the same power that raised Christ from the dead raise your marriage to newness of life. May old patterns die and new love flourish, for the glory of the Father.

May your love for each other grow deeper with every passing day, and may your love for God be the source and the strength of it all. Goodnight, dear ones.
