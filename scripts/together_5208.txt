PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 8
"Speaking the Truth in Love"
Ephesians 4:15 — Communication and Holy Living

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to a new week in our Together devotional. This week we turn to the vital subject of communication in marriage, drawing from the rich well of Ephesians 4 and 5. How we speak to each other shapes the very soul of our union. Let us listen carefully to what God has to say.

Let us read our passage. Ephesians 4:15 (KJV):

"But speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ."

--- Teaching ---

Two errors destroy communication in marriage. The first is speaking the truth without love. The second is speaking love without truth. The apostle Paul calls us to hold both together, for it is in the marriage of truth and love that real growth happens.

Some husbands and wives pride themselves on being honest. They say what they think, when they think it, however they think it. They call it truth-telling. But truth without love is a weapon. It wounds, it shames, it destroys. The husband who points out his wife's every fault with cold precision is not being godly; he is being cruel. The wife who corrects her husband with sharp and cutting words may be speaking accurately, but she is not speaking as Christ would have her speak.

On the other hand, some couples avoid all difficult conversations in the name of keeping the peace. They never address real problems. They sweep disagreements under the rug. They smile on the surface while resentment builds beneath. This is not love. This is cowardice dressed in the clothing of kindness. Real love speaks the truth, even when it is hard, even when it risks discomfort.

William Gouge taught that the godly couple must learn what he called "holy plainness" in their communication. This means saying what needs to be said with clarity and directness, but always wrapping that truth in genuine love and concern for the other person's well-being. The motive must never be to win an argument or to prove a point. The motive must always be the spiritual growth of the other and the health of the relationship.

Notice the purpose Paul identifies: "may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ." Truth spoken in love produces growth. When a husband gently tells his wife that he has noticed she has been anxious and invites her to pray about it together, that is speaking truth in love. When a wife lovingly tells her husband that his words at dinner were harsh and asks him to consider their effect, that is speaking truth in love. Both are growing toward Christ through honest, tender communication.

Richard Baxter offered practical counsel for married couples. He said: choose the right time. Do not raise difficult subjects when you are tired, hungry, or in the presence of others. Speak privately, calmly, and with a genuine desire for resolution. Begin with what is good before addressing what needs to change. And always end with affirmation of your love and commitment.

The Puritans also warned against a subtle enemy of truthful communication: assumption. Many conflicts in marriage arise not from real offenses but from imagined ones. We assume we know what our spouse meant. We assume we know why they did what they did. We interpret their actions through the lens of our own insecurities and past wounds. Gouge counseled couples to ask before they accuse, to inquire before they judge, to give the benefit of the doubt as often as humanly possible.

Dear couple, how is the communication in your marriage? Are there truths that need to be spoken that you have been avoiding? Are there truths that you have been speaking without wrapping them in love? Tonight is an opportunity to commit to a new pattern: speaking the truth in love, that together you might grow up into Christ in all things.

The marriage that communicates well is the marriage that grows well. And the marriage that grows well is the marriage that endures. Let your words tonight be seasoned with both honesty and grace.

--- For Discussion ---

Question 1: Is there a truth I have been avoiding speaking to you because I fear your reaction? Can I share it now in a spirit of love?

Question 2: Have I spoken truth to you recently in a way that lacked love? How did that affect you?

Question 3: What practical changes can we make to create a safer environment for honest communication in our marriage?

--- Prayer ---

Lord Jesus, Thou art the way, the truth, and the life. Teach us to speak truth to one another in love. Deliver us from the cowardice of silence and from the cruelty of careless words. Give us ears that truly listen and hearts that genuinely care. May our communication draw us closer to each other and closer to Thee. Heal the wounds that unloving words have caused. Build up what careless speech has torn down. Let our tongues be instruments of grace in this marriage. We pray in Thy name, Amen.
