PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 7
"Against Hope, Believed in Hope"
Romans for Marriage — Day 7


Welcome, husband and wife. There is something powerful about two hearts turning toward God at the same time. When you both face the same direction, toward Christ, everything in your marriage finds its proper alignment. Let us do that now.

Every marriage needs a foundation that does not shift. Let us hear from that foundation now, from Romans 4:18-21:

"Who against hope believed in hope, that he might become the father of many nations, according to that which was spoken, So shall thy seed be. And being not weak in faith, he considered not his own body now dead, when he was about an hundred years old, neither yet the deadness of Sara's womb: He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief; but was strong in faith, giving glory to God; And being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform."

That is Romans 4:18-21. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


The Puritans treasured marriage as a covenant made before God. Thomas Manton wrote beautifully about how Scripture speaks to the hearts of married couples.

Paul holds up Abraham as the supreme example of faith — a man who believed God's promise when every human calculation said it was impossible. Abraham was nearly a hundred years old. Sarah's womb was dead. By every natural measurement, the promise of a son was absurd. Yet Abraham 'against hope believed in hope.' He believed not because the circumstances warranted it, but because God had spoken.

Thomas Manton, the great Puritan preacher, expounded on this passage with particular attention to the phrase 'he staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief.' Manton observed that Abraham's faith was not the absence of difficulty but the refusal to let difficulty overthrow God's Word. Abraham saw the obstacles — his aged body, Sarah's barrenness — but he did not let them define reality. God's promise defined reality.

Manton taught that this kind of faith — faith that perseveres despite impossible odds — is precisely what Christian marriages require. Every marriage will face seasons where hope seems unreasonable. Seasons of prolonged conflict, spiritual dryness, financial ruin, health crises, or emotional distance can make reconciliation and restoration seem as impossible as a hundred-year-old man fathering a child. Yet God specializes in the impossible.

The text says Abraham 'considered not his own body now dead.' He did not deny the reality of his limitations — he simply refused to give them more weight than God's promise. In marriage, this means acknowledging the real challenges you face while refusing to let them have the final word. Yes, the conflict is real. Yes, the distance is real. Yes, the pain is real. But God's promise is more real.

Abraham was 'fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform.' This is the essence of faith for marriage: full persuasion that God is able. Able to heal what is broken. Able to restore what has been lost. Able to do what no amount of human effort can accomplish. When you are fully persuaded of God's ability, you can face any crisis in your marriage with steady confidence.


Is there an area of your marriage where you have lost hope? A pattern you have tried to break but cannot? A wound that refuses to heal? A dream that seems dead?

Abraham's story is your story. God called him to believe in the impossible — and God delivered. The same God who opened a dead womb can open a hardened heart. The same God who gave life where there was no life can bring new life to a struggling marriage.

Tonight, name the area where you need 'against hope' faith. Speak it aloud to each other. Then choose together to believe God's promise over your circumstances. You may not see the answer tonight, but Abraham waited twenty-five years — and God was faithful.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Now turn to each other. Here are tonight's reflection questions. Answer from the heart, not from the head.

Question 1: Where in your marriage do you need to believe 'against hope'? What seems impossible right now?

Question 2: Abraham refused to let his limitations define reality. What limitations are you allowing to define your marriage?

Question 3: What does it mean to be 'fully persuaded' that God is able to transform your marriage?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


The Puritans ended every evening with prayer. Let us follow their example and bring our marriage before the Lord.

Tonight's prayer focus: Bring the impossible areas of your marriage before God tonight. Like Abraham, refuse to stagger at God's promises through unbelief. Ask God to strengthen your faith and to do what only He can do in your relationship.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the God of Abraham, who gives life to the dead and calls things that are not as though they were, breathe new hope into your marriage. Believe, and watch Him work.

May the words you have heard tonight bear fruit in your marriage for years to come. The Lord be with you both. Until next time.
