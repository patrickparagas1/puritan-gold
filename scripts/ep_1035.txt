PURITAN GOLD -- Study Topic: The Book of Melchizedek, Part 5 of 8
"A Better Covenant"
The Book of Melchizedek — Hebrews 7:11-22 — The need for a new priesthood

---

Dear friends in Christ, we continue our walk through Hebrews chapter 7, and today we arrive at the heart of the writer's argument. Having established that Melchizedek was greater than Abraham and therefore greater than the Levitical priesthood, the writer now draws a momentous conclusion: the Levitical priesthood was never intended to be permanent, and its impermanence implies the impermanence of the entire Mosaic law. A new priesthood has come — and with it, a new and better covenant.

Let us read Hebrews 7:11-22:

"If therefore perfection were by the Levitical priesthood, (for under it the people received the law,) what further need was there that another priest should rise after the order of Melchisedec, and not be called after the order of Aaron? For the priesthood being changed, there is made of necessity a change also of the law. For he of whom these things are spoken pertaineth to another tribe, of which no man gave attendance at the altar. For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood. And it is yet far more evident: for that after the similitude of Melchisedec there ariseth another priest, Who is made, not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life. For he testifieth, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec. For there is verily a disannulling of the commandment going before for the weakness and unprofitableness thereof. For the law made nothing perfect, but the bringing in of a better hope did; by the which we draw nigh unto God. And inasmuch as not without an oath he was made priest: (For those priests were made without an oath; but this with an oath by him that said unto him, The Lord sware and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec:) By so much was Jesus made a surety of a better testament."

--- Teaching ---

The writer of Hebrews opens this section with a devastating question: "If therefore perfection were by the Levitical priesthood, what further need was there that another priest should rise after the order of Melchizedek?" The logic is irresistible. If the Levitical priesthood could have accomplished the full and final reconciliation of God and man, there would have been no need for Psalm 110:4. God would not have announced a coming priest of a different order if the existing order were sufficient. The very existence of the prophecy about a Melchizedekian priesthood is proof that the Levitical priesthood was inadequate.

This is not a criticism born of disrespect. The Levitical priesthood was divinely instituted. It was glorious. It was given by God Himself at Sinai, attended by thunder and lightning and the voice of the Almighty. But for all its glory, it was provisional. It was a shadow, not the substance. It was a map pointing to a destination, not the destination itself. The blood of bulls and goats could never truly take away sin (Hebrews 10:4). The sacrifices offered year after year could never perfect the conscience of the worshipper (Hebrews 9:9). Something more was needed — someone more was needed.

"For the priesthood being changed, there is made of necessity a change also of the law." This is one of the most consequential statements in the entire epistle. The priesthood and the law were bound together. The law was given "under" the priesthood — the entire sacrificial system, the dietary regulations, the ceremonial requirements — all of these were inseparable from the Levitical order. If the priesthood changes, the law necessarily changes with it. The coming of a new priesthood after the order of Melchizedek means the end of the Mosaic ceremonial law as a binding system.

This was precisely the point that the original recipients of this epistle needed to hear. They were Jewish Christians who were being tempted to return to the familiar practices of the Levitical system — the temple, the sacrifices, the priesthood. The writer of Hebrews is showing them that to go back to the Levitical system would be to go back to what God Himself had superseded. The old covenant was not defective in its design — it accomplished exactly what God intended it to accomplish — but its purpose was to prepare the way for something better. And that something better has now arrived.

"For he of whom these things are spoken pertaineth to another tribe." Here the writer makes the obvious but critical observation that Jesus did not come from the tribe of Levi but from the tribe of Judah. Under the Mosaic law, only Levites could serve as priests. The very fact that the prophesied Messiah came from Judah demonstrates that His priesthood operates on different principles than the Levitical system. He is not a priest by genealogical qualification but by divine appointment.

"Who is made, not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life." The contrast is between the temporal and the eternal. The Levitical priests were appointed "after the law of a carnal commandment" — that is, on the basis of physical descent and fleshly qualification. If your father was a Levite, you could be a priest; if he was not, you could not. It was a system based on flesh — on biology, on genealogy, on the perishable body. But Christ's priesthood is based on "the power of an endless life." He is a priest because He possesses an indestructible, immortal, eternal life. Death cannot end His ministry. He rose from the grave and He lives forevermore, and His priesthood is as permanent as His life.

John Owen meditated extensively on this contrast between the "carnal commandment" and the "endless life." He argued that the phrase "carnal commandment" did not mean the Mosaic law was sinful or ungodly — the word "carnal" here means "pertaining to the flesh," not "pertaining to sin." The Levitical law was a system built upon physical and temporal realities: physical descent, physical sacrifices, physical garments, a physical building. All of these were good in themselves, but they were temporary by nature. They belonged to the old creation, not to the new. Christ's priesthood, by contrast, belongs to the new creation — to the order of resurrection and eternal life.

"For there is verily a disannulling of the commandment going before for the weakness and unprofitableness thereof. For the law made nothing perfect." Again, this is not a dismissal of the Mosaic law but an honest assessment of its limitations. The law was "weak" — not because it was poorly designed, but because it was never intended to do what only Christ could do. A map is not "weak" because it cannot transport you to your destination; it serves a different purpose. The law was a tutor to bring us unto Christ (Galatians 3:24). It revealed the disease of sin but could not provide the cure. It diagnosed the problem but could not effect the remedy.

"But the bringing in of a better hope did; by the which we draw nigh unto God." Here is the positive counterpart to the law's negation. What the law could not do, Christ has done. He has opened the way into the very presence of God. The veil is torn. The barrier is removed. We who were far off have been brought nigh by the blood of Christ (Ephesians 2:13). The "better hope" is not an abstract concept but a living person — Jesus Christ, our Melchizedekian High Priest, through whom we have access to the Father.

The passage concludes with the argument from the divine oath: Christ was made priest with an oath; the Levitical priests were not. This makes Christ "a surety of a better testament" — a guarantor of a better covenant. The word "surety" (Greek: enguos) means a guarantor or pledge. Christ does not merely participate in the new covenant; He guarantees it. His person, His work, His endless life — these are the pledge that the new covenant will never fail.

--- Application ---

1. Do not seek perfection in anything other than Christ. Neither religious rituals, nor moral effort, nor spiritual experiences can perfect your conscience. Only the blood of Christ, applied by the ministry of our Melchizedekian High Priest, can make you truly and permanently clean before God.

2. Embrace the new covenant with full confidence. The old was glorious, but the new is more glorious still. You are not under a system of shadows and types but under the reality to which they pointed. Draw near to God through Christ with boldness and assurance.

3. Rest in the surety of Christ. He is the guarantor of every promise God has made to you. If Christ cannot fail — and He cannot — then neither can the covenant He guarantees.

4. Recognize that the change from law to grace is not loss but gain. The Levitical system served its purpose beautifully, but it was always pointing forward. To cling to the shadow when the substance has come is not loyalty but confusion.

--- Closing ---

Gracious Father, we thank Thee for the better hope that we have in Christ — a hope that is not built upon the shifting foundation of human performance but upon the unshakeable rock of Thy Son's eternal priesthood. We draw near to Thee through Him, confident that the new covenant He guarantees will never be revoked, never be diminished, and never fail. Glory be to Thee, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, now and forever. Amen.

May the better hope of the gospel sustain you in every circumstance, beloved. Grace and peace until we gather again.
