PURITAN GOLD -- Together Devotional, Day 23
"Set Me as a Seal upon Thine Heart"
Song of Solomon 8:5-7 — The Seal of Covenant Love

---

Welcome, beloved couple. Tonight we arrive at the summit of the Song of Solomon — the passage that many consider the theological heart of the entire book. These verses speak of love that is as strong as death, as fierce as the grave, and as unquenchable as the flame of God Himself. Listen with your whole hearts.

Song of Solomon 8:5-7:

"Who is this that cometh up from the wilderness, leaning upon her beloved? I raised thee up under the apple tree: there thy mother brought thee forth: there she brought thee forth that bare thee. Set me as a seal upon thine heart, as a seal upon thine arm: for love is strong as death; jealousy is cruel as the grave: the coals thereof are coals of fire, which hath a most vehement flame. Many waters cannot quench love, neither can the floods drown it: if a man would give all the substance of his house for love, it would utterly be contemned."

--- Teaching ---

"Who is this that cometh up from the wilderness, leaning upon her beloved?" The bride emerges from the wilderness — the place of testing, wandering, and trial — leaning on her beloved. She is not walking independently. She is not striding ahead in self-sufficiency. She is leaning. This is the picture of mature love: two people who have traversed the wilderness of life together and who lean on each other for support.

The Puritans treasured the image of mutual leaning. Richard Baxter wrote that marriage is designed by God as a relationship of mutual dependence — each spouse leaning on the other's strengths to compensate for their own weaknesses. The husband who refuses to lean on his wife denies her the dignity of being his helper. The wife who refuses to lean on her husband denies him the honor of being her support. Leaning is not weakness; it is the wisdom of partnership.

"Set me as a seal upon thine heart, as a seal upon thine arm." This is the bride's climactic request. A seal in the ancient world was a mark of ownership, identity, and authority. Kings sealed documents with their signet rings. Property was sealed to demonstrate possession. To be set as a seal upon the heart means: engrave me on the deepest part of you — the hidden, internal, emotional center of your being. To be set as a seal upon the arm means: let my name be visible in your actions, your work, your public life. Heart and arm — inward and outward — private and public. The bride wants to be inscribed on every dimension of her beloved's life.

Thomas Gataker wrote that the marriage vow is precisely this — a seal upon the heart and the arm. When you said "I do," you engraved your spouse's name on your innermost affections and your outermost actions. Everything you feel and everything you do should bear the seal of your covenant. This is total, comprehensive, all-encompassing commitment.

"For love is strong as death." This is one of the most powerful statements in all of Scripture. Death is the one force that no human power can resist. It claims every person. It breaks every bond. It ends every story. And the bride says: love is as strong as death. Love matches death in power. Love is an unstoppable force that cannot be defeated.

"Jealousy is cruel as the grave." The word translated "jealousy" here is better understood as "zeal" or "passionate devotion." The grave — Sheol — is relentless. It never says "enough." It never releases its hold. And the bride says: the passionate devotion of covenant love is just as relentless. It never lets go. It never gives up. It never says "enough."

"The coals thereof are coals of fire, which hath a most vehement flame." The Hebrew phrase here is extraordinary. Literally, it reads "the flame of Yah" — the flame of the Lord. This is the only place in the Song of Solomon where God is directly named. And where does His name appear? In the description of love's fire. The Puritans were captivated by this. Richard Sibbes wrote that marital love, at its deepest, is a participation in the very fire of God. It is not merely human passion; it is a flame that has its origin in the heart of the Almighty. When you love your spouse with covenant faithfulness, you are burning with a fire that God Himself lit.

"Many waters cannot quench love, neither can the floods drown it." Not many waters. Not floods. Nothing can extinguish this flame. William Gouge applied this to the trials of marriage: financial hardship, illness, betrayal, loss, misunderstanding — all of these are waters that threaten to drown love. But covenant love, fueled by the flame of the Lord, cannot be quenched. It survives what would destroy lesser fires.

"If a man would give all the substance of his house for love, it would utterly be contemned." Love cannot be bought. All the wealth in the world is contemptible — utterly worthless — compared to genuine love. The Puritans warned against the transactional view of marriage that reduces love to an exchange of services or benefits. Love is not a commodity. It is a flame. It is a seal. It is a force as strong as death itself. And it comes not from human effort but from the God whose name is inscribed in its fire.

Tonight, let these words settle deep into your marriage. Your love is not fragile. It is not weak. It is not dependent on feelings or circumstances. It is as strong as death, as relentless as the grave, and as unquenchable as the flame of the Lord.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Turn to each other and consider these questions.

Question 1: "Set me as a seal upon thine heart." Is your spouse engraved on the deepest part of you — your innermost affections and priorities? Or have other things displaced them?

Question 2: What "many waters" have tried to quench the flame of your love — hardship, loss, conflict, weariness? How has your love survived? Give thanks for that survival tonight.

Question 3: The flame of love is called "the flame of the Lord." How does knowing that your love originates in God Himself change the way you view your marriage?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray over Song of Solomon 8:6-7 together. Ask God to set each of you as a seal upon the other's heart and arm. Pray that the flame of the Lord — the unquenchable fire of covenant love — would burn brightly in your marriage, surviving every flood and every trial.

--- Closing ---

Love is strong as death. Your love is the flame of the Lord. Many waters cannot quench it. No flood can drown it. Rest tonight in the power of a love that will never, never be extinguished. Goodnight, beloved couple.
