PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 26
"Putting Out the Fire of Gossip"
Proverbs 26:20

---

Hello and welcome to your couples devotional. Whether you are sitting side by side on the couch or listening together at the end of a long day, God is here with you both. He meets married couples in a special way when they turn their hearts to Him together.

Let me read our passage for this evening. It comes from Proverbs 26:20:

"Where no wood is, there the fire goeth out: so where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth."

That is Proverbs 26:20. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Matthew Henry was a faithful pastor and theologian whose words still carry weight centuries later. Listen to what the Puritans had to say about the truths in tonight's passage.

Matthew Henry takes up this vivid image with characteristic directness. 'As wood is fuel to fire, so a talebearer is fuel to strife,' Henry writes. The principle is devastatingly simple: if you remove the fuel, the fire dies. Strife in relationships is sustained not by the original offense alone but by the constant retelling of it: to friends, to family, to anyone who will listen.

Henry identifies the talebearer as one of the most destructive figures in any community, including the family. The talebearer carries private information from one person to another, often adding interpretation and embellishment along the way. 'Many a friendship has been broken,' Henry writes, 'and many a family divided, by the busy tongue of a talebearer.'

In the context of marriage, Henry applies this with precision. When a husband or wife shares the private matters of their marriage with outsiders — complaining about their spouse to friends, airing grievances to family members, posting veiled criticisms on social media — they are throwing wood on the fire. Every retelling deepens the wound, hardens the heart, and makes reconciliation more difficult.

Charles Bridges adds that the solution is twofold: stop speaking and stop listening. 'The talebearer can only function where there are willing ears,' Bridges writes. If you refuse to entertain gossip about your spouse, if you refuse to gossip about your spouse, the fire of strife will have no fuel. Bridges counsels: 'When strife arises in a marriage, the first question should be: have I added fuel to this fire by my words?'

--- Marriage Application ---

One of the most harmful things you can do to your marriage is talk about your spouse's failings to people who have no business knowing about them. When you complain about your husband to your mother, you are adding wood to the fire. When you mock your wife's habits to your friends at work, you are adding wood to the fire. Even when the complaints are valid, the audience is wrong.

Agree together on a principle: what happens in our marriage stays in our marriage, with limited exceptions for pastoral counsel or trusted mentors. Do not process your marital frustrations through social media, casual friendships, or family members who will take sides.

When you are hurt or frustrated, bring it to God first, then to your spouse. If you need outside help, seek it from a pastor or counselor who will point you back to one another and to Christ, not from someone who will simply validate your complaints and add fuel to the fire.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Let us reflect together. These questions are designed to help you go deeper, both with God and with each other.

Question 1: Have I been adding wood to any fires in our marriage by talking about our problems to the wrong people?

Question 2: Is there someone in our lives who acts as a talebearer, fueling strife rather than peace?

Question 3: Can we agree together on appropriate boundaries for how and with whom we discuss our marriage struggles?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

As we near the end of tonight's devotional, let us bring our hearts before God in prayer. Pray for each other. Pray for your marriage. Pray for your home.

Tonight's prayer focus: Ask God to guard your tongues from gossip and talebearing. Pray for the discipline to bring your frustrations to Him and to each other rather than to outsiders who will add fuel to the fire. Pray that where strife has been kindled, it may be extinguished by grace.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord who is the Prince of Peace extinguish every fire of strife in your marriage and guard your lips from every word that would feed the flames. Amen.

The Lord bless you and keep you. The Lord make His face shine upon you and be gracious to you. The Lord lift up His countenance upon you both, and give you peace. Amen.
