The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment. By Jeremiah Burroughs. A Complete Summary.

Welcome to this summary of Jeremiah Burroughs's The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment, one of the most beloved and practical works of Puritan devotion. Based on Philippians 4:11, where Paul declares that he has learned in whatsoever state he is therewith to be content, Burroughs unfolds the mystery of Christian contentment with remarkable depth and tenderness.

Burroughs defines contentment as that sweet, inward, quiet, gracious frame of spirit which freely submits to and delights in God's wise and fatherly disposal in every condition. It is not a natural temperament but a spiritual grace — a mystery to be learned.

The mystery of contentment is explored in rich detail. Burroughs shows that the contented Christian draws from inward resources that the world knows nothing of. He has learned to make up the deficiency of his condition from what he finds in God and in his own soul. He can bring his heart down to his condition when he cannot bring his condition up to his heart.

Christ teaches contentment through many lessons: by changing the heart rather than the circumstance, by revealing the vanity of the creature and the fullness of God, by teaching submission to the Father's will, and by showing the believer the eternal weight of glory that far outweighs all present affliction.

The excellence of contentment is set forth in glowing terms. It is the honor of a Christian, the beauty of grace, the secret of true happiness, and the evidence of genuine faith.

Burroughs then turns to the evils of a murmuring and discontented spirit, showing that discontent dishonors God, disturbs the soul, unfits us for duty, and provokes the Lord to further chastisement. He explores the aggravations of this sin, answers the common excuses of the discontented heart, and provides practical directions for attaining true contentment.

This book remains a treasure because it addresses a universal human struggle with uncommon spiritual wisdom. May this summary lead you to the full work and to the contentment that can only be found in Christ.
