PURITAN GOLD -- Study Devotional, Day 30
Series: Romans
"The Mystery Revealed"
Paul's final doxology — to the only wise God be glory forever


Welcome, friend. Tonight we open our Bibles again for another session of deep, prayerful study. The Puritans believed that the richest treasures of Scripture are found not on the surface but beneath it, by those willing to dig.

We are studying the book of Romans together. Tonight's lesson is titled "The Mystery Revealed." Each session builds on what came before, so if you have been following along, you will see how tonight's passage connects to the larger argument of the book.


Let us read our passage for tonight. It comes from Romans 16:25-27:

"Now to him that is of power to stablish you according to my gospel, and the preaching of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of the mystery, which was kept secret since the world began, But now is made manifest, and by the scriptures of the prophets, according to the commandment of the everlasting God, made known to all nations for the obedience of faith: To God only wise, be glory through Jesus Christ for ever. Amen."

Read that passage again to yourself. Mark any words or phrases that stand out. The Puritans read with a pen in hand, always ready to note what God was showing them.


Within tonight's passage, one verse stands out as a key that unlocks the rest.

"Now to him that is of power to stablish you according to my gospel, and the preaching of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of the mystery, which was kept secret since the world began." -- Romans 16:25

This verse is the lens through which we will examine the rest of tonight's passage. Keep it in the front of your mind as we proceed.


With our key verse before us, let us examine the broader teaching.

Thomas Goodwin offers valuable commentary on this passage. The Puritans were expositors of the highest order, and their insights remain remarkably fresh.

Thomas Goodwin regarded the closing doxology of Romans as the fitting capstone to the most glorious epistle ever written. He saw in these three verses a compressed summary of the entire letter, a theological telescope through which the whole panorama of Romans can be viewed.

Goodwin focused on the word 'mystery' — the musterion that was 'kept secret since the world began, but now is made manifest.' He argued that this mystery is not one truth but a complex of truths that Paul has been unfolding throughout the entire letter: the righteousness of God revealed in the gospel, justification by faith alone, the inclusion of the Gentiles in the people of God, the death and resurrection of Christ as the ground of salvation, the work of the Spirit in applying redemption, and the sovereign purposes of God in election. All of these were foreshadowed in the Old Testament but fully revealed only in the gospel age.

Goodwin stressed that this mystery was revealed 'by the scriptures of the prophets' — the Old Testament Scriptures. This confirms what Paul has argued throughout Romans: the gospel is not a novelty but the fulfillment of what the prophets foretold. The Old and New Testaments form one unified revelation of one unified plan of one sovereign God.

Goodwin was particularly moved by the purpose clause: 'made known to all nations for the obedience of faith.' The mystery is not revealed for mere intellectual satisfaction but for a practical response — the obedience of faith. This phrase, which brackets the entire epistle (appearing also in 1:5), defines the proper response to the gospel: faith that obeys, trust that transforms, belief that produces behavior.

Finally, Goodwin meditated on the ascription: 'To God only wise, be glory through Jesus Christ for ever.' He argued that the word 'only' (mono) does not merely distinguish the true God from false gods but ascribes to Him a wisdom that is utterly unique, infinitely superior, and absolutely sufficient. No created mind could have devised the plan of redemption. Only the omniscient God could have conceived a way to be both just and the justifier of the ungodly. All glory belongs to Him — through Jesus Christ, the mediator through whom all blessings flow and all praise ascends — forever. Amen.

Romans 16:25-27 is Paul's final word in the greatest theological letter ever written. It is a doxology — an eruption of praise — that gathers up every theme of the epistle and offers it all to the glory of God. Just as Romans 11:33-36 concluded the doctrinal section with worship, so Romans 16:25-27 concludes the entire letter with worship. Paul began with the gospel (1:1-17) and he ends with the gospel. He began with God's power (1:16) and he ends with God's power. He began with faith (1:17) and he ends with the obedience of faith. The epistle is a perfect circle, beginning and ending in God.

Verse 25 opens with the ground of praise: 'Now to him that is of power to stablish you.' The God Paul worships is not a philosophical abstraction but a God of power — specifically, the power to establish believers, to make them firm, stable, unshakeable. After sixteen chapters of theology, Paul's final concern is pastoral: that his readers would be established — rooted and grounded in the truth so that no wind of doctrine or wave of persecution could move them.

This establishing is 'according to my gospel.' Paul calls it 'my gospel' not because he invented it but because it was entrusted to him by revelation (Galatians 1:12). It is the same gospel he has been expounding throughout the entire letter — justification by faith, union with Christ, life in the Spirit, the sovereignty of God, the inclusion of the Gentiles, and the transformation of daily life. This gospel has the power to establish believers because it is not human wisdom but divine revelation.

It is also 'the preaching of Jesus Christ' — the proclamation of Christ crucified, risen, ascended, and returning. Christ is the content of the gospel. Everything Paul has written circles back to the Person and work of Jesus Christ. He is the center of Romans, the center of the Scriptures, and the center of all reality.

This gospel is further described as 'the revelation of the mystery, which was kept secret since the world began.' The word 'mystery' (musterion) in the New Testament does not mean a puzzle or a riddle but a truth that was hidden in God's counsel and has now been revealed. This mystery includes the entire plan of redemption that Paul has been unfolding: how God can justify the ungodly, how Jews and Gentiles are united in one body, how the old covenant points forward to Christ, how the Spirit applies what Christ accomplished, and how all of history moves toward God's glory.

Verse 26 describes the unveiling: 'But now is made manifest, and by the scriptures of the prophets, according to the commandment of the everlasting God, made known to all nations for the obedience of faith.' Several crucial truths are packed into this verse.

First, the mystery is 'now made manifest' — revealed, displayed, brought into the light. The gospel is not esoteric knowledge for a spiritual elite; it is public truth proclaimed to all.

Second, it is made known 'by the scriptures of the prophets.' The Old Testament prophets — Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Daniel, the Twelve — all bore witness to this mystery. Paul has quoted extensively from the Old Testament throughout Romans, demonstrating that the gospel of Christ is the fulfillment of everything the prophets foretold. The Bible is one book with one message: Christ.

Third, this revelation is 'according to the commandment of the everlasting God.' It is not accidental but intentional. God commanded that the mystery be revealed. He determined the timing, the method, and the scope. And He is the 'everlasting God' — the eternal One whose purposes span all of history and whose faithfulness never fails.

Fourth, it is 'made known to all nations.' The gospel is universal in scope. It is not a tribal religion, not a cultural artifact, not a regional curiosity. It is God's message to every nation, tribe, tongue, and people. The Great Commission (Matthew 28:19-20) flows from this universal scope.

Fifth, the purpose is 'the obedience of faith.' This phrase, identical to Romans 1:5, brackets the entire epistle. The obedience of faith is the proper response to the gospel — not mere intellectual assent, not mere emotional response, but faith that produces obedience, trust that transforms behavior, belief that reshapes the entire life. Faith without obedience is dead (James 2:26). Obedience without faith is legalism. The obedience of faith combines both: we trust God and therefore we obey God. We obey God because we trust God.

Verse 27 offers the final ascription: 'To God only wise, be glory through Jesus Christ for ever. Amen.' 'To God only wise' — God alone possesses the wisdom that conceived the plan of redemption. No human mind, no angelic intelligence, no created being of any kind could have devised a way to satisfy justice and mercy simultaneously, to reconcile sinners and glorify the Savior, to unite Jew and Gentile in one body, to conquer sin and death through apparent defeat. Only the infinitely wise God could do this. And all glory flows to Him 'through Jesus Christ' — the Mediator through whom every blessing descends and every praise ascends.

'For ever.' The glory of God is not a momentary flash but an eternal reality. The plan revealed in Romans will be celebrated for all eternity. The saints in glory will never tire of praising the God who justified the ungodly, who conquered death through death, who made enemies into children, who turned rebels into worshipers.

'Amen.' So be it. It is true. It is settled. It is finished. The gospel has been declared. The mystery has been revealed. The only wise God has spoken. And all that remains is the eternal echo of praise: to Him be glory through Jesus Christ for ever. Amen.


Application is where truth meets life. Let us consider what this passage demands of us.

Romans 16:25-27 is the fitting conclusion to the most profound book in the Bible. Here are final applications for the entire study.

First, be established. The purpose of this doxology — and of the entire epistle — is to establish you in the faith. After thirty days of studying Romans, ask yourself: am I more firmly rooted in the gospel than I was when I began? Can I articulate the doctrine of justification by faith? Do I understand my union with Christ? Am I living in the power of the Spirit? Is my daily life being transformed? If Romans has done its work, you should be more stable, more confident, and more Christ-centered than ever.

Second, embrace the mystery. The gospel is a mystery revealed — not a mystery solved. There are depths in God's plan that we will spend eternity exploring. Do not be frustrated by what you cannot fully understand. Instead, worship the God whose wisdom is unsearchable and whose ways are past finding out. Let mystery lead to worship, not to anxiety.

Third, practice the obedience of faith. This is the purpose for which the mystery was revealed: not mere knowledge but obedience. Review the practical commands of Romans 12-15 and choose three specific applications to implement in the coming month. Present your body as a living sacrifice. Love without hypocrisy. Overcome evil with good. Receive the weak. Put on the Lord Jesus Christ. Let the doctrine of Romans become the practice of your daily life.

Fourth, give God the glory. 'To God only wise, be glory through Jesus Christ for ever.' This is the ultimate application of everything in Romans: that God would receive the glory He deserves. In your work, in your relationships, in your worship, in your suffering, in your joy — let everything point back to Him. You exist for His glory. Romans has shown you how. Now go and live it.

Write down one concrete action you will take this week in response to tonight's study. Do not let this truth remain theoretical. The Puritans measured the success of a sermon not by how much the hearer learned but by how much the hearer changed.


To God only wise, be glory through Jesus Christ for ever. Amen. Father, we have journeyed through the greatest letter ever written, and we stand in awe. You have shown us our sin and Your righteousness, our helplessness and Your power, our unworthiness and Your grace. You have justified the ungodly, united Jew and Gentile, conquered sin and death, and revealed a mystery that angels long to look into. Establish us in this gospel. Make us unshakeable. Transform our minds, our hearts, and our lives by the truth of Romans. And may everything we are and everything we do bring glory to Your name — through Jesus Christ, our Lord, our Savior, our Righteousness, our Hope. To You be glory forever and ever. Amen.

Goodnight. May the truths you have studied tonight take deep root in your heart.
