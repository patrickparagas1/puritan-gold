PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"The Spirit of Wisdom and Revelation"
July 5, 2026

---

"Wherefore I also, after I heard of your faith in the Lord Jesus, and love unto all the saints, cease not to give thanks for you, making mention of you in my prayers; that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give unto you the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him: the eyes of your understanding being enlightened; that ye may know what is the hope of his calling, and what the riches of the glory of his inheritance in the saints, and what is the exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward who believe." -- Ephesians 1:15-19

Having scaled the heights of doctrine in the first fourteen verses, Paul now falls upon his knees in prayer. And what a prayer it is! He does not pray for health, wealth, or earthly prosperity. He prays for something infinitely more valuable: spiritual illumination.

'That the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give unto you the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him.' This is the great need of the church in every age -- not more information, but more illumination. We may have Bibles on our shelves and theology in our heads, yet remain blind to the glory of what we profess to believe. Jonathan Edwards understood this distinction better than most. He taught that there is a difference between having a rational understanding of divine truth and having a spiritual sense of its beauty and excellency. A man may understand the doctrine of God's grace the way he understands a mathematical theorem, without ever being ravished by its sweetness or humbled by its majesty.

Paul prays that three things in particular might be known: the hope of God's calling, the riches of His inheritance, and the exceeding greatness of His power. These three correspond roughly to our past, present, and future in Christ. The hope of His calling points to the certainty of our election and the glory for which we have been set apart. The riches of His inheritance points to the present reality of what we possess in Christ. And the exceeding greatness of His power points to the same resurrection force that raised Christ from the dead, which is at work in us even now.

John Flavel wrote that the power of God toward believers is the same power that conquered death, broke open the tomb, and seated Christ at the right hand of the Majesty on high. And if that power is for us, what can stand against us? If the arm that rolled away the stone from the sepulchre is the arm that upholds our faith, then our faith is as unshakeable as the throne of God itself.

May we never be content with a shallow, secondhand knowledge of God. May the Father of glory open our eyes to see what He has done, what He is doing, and what He shall yet do for those who love Him. Amen.
