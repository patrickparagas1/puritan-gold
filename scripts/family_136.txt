PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 5
"The Hill Difficulty"
Pilgrim Families — Week 1, Day 5


Hello and welcome to our family devotional. Come, young and old alike. God's Word has something for every member of this family tonight. Let us draw near to Him.

Shall we pray? Our Father, we gather tonight hungry for Your truth. We know that man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from Your mouth. Feed us now, Lord. Bless our family time together. In the name of Christ we pray. Amen.

God has preserved His Word for thousands of years so that families like ours could hear it tonight. Let us read from Hebrews 12:1-2; James 1:2-4:

"Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us, Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God. My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing."

That is Hebrews 12:1-2; James 1:2-4. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


After the glorious experience at the cross, Christian's journey did not become easy. He soon came to the Hill Difficulty — a steep, rugged climb that had to be conquered. Two other travelers, Formalist and Hypocrisy, found easier paths around the hill. One took the path called Danger and was lost in a great wood. The other took the path called Destruction and fell on the dark mountains. Only the straight, steep path over the hill was the true way.

Hebrews 12:1-2 paints the picture perfectly: "Let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us, Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame." The Christian life is described as a race, and it is not a sprint on flat ground — it is a long-distance climb. It requires patience, endurance, and a fixed gaze upon Jesus.

James 1:2-4 takes this further: "Count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing." James does not say if you fall into trials — he says when. Trials are guaranteed. Difficulty is part of the journey. But the purpose of difficulty is not to destroy us — it is to develop us.

The Hill Difficulty teaches our families several vital lessons. First, there are no shortcuts in the Christian life. Formalist and Hypocrisy tried to find easier paths, and both were destroyed. In our world, people constantly offer shortcuts to happiness — get rich quick, instant gratification, easier versions of Christianity that require no sacrifice. But the true path goes straight up the hill. Discipleship costs something. Following Jesus means taking up your cross daily.

Second, difficulty comes right after blessing. Christian had just experienced the joy of having his burden removed at the cross — and immediately faced a steep hill. This pattern repeats throughout the Christian life. After a mountain-top experience, expect a valley. After a spiritual breakthrough, expect a challenge. God does not promise us a life without hills; He promises to walk with us up every one of them.

Third, the hill must be climbed, not avoided. There are certain trials in life that you cannot go around. You must go through them. A difficult school year. A family health crisis. A season of financial hardship. A relational conflict that will not resolve easily. The temptation is always to find an easier way, but the easier way leads to Danger or Destruction. The hard way — the hill straight up — leads to the Palace Beautiful on the other side.

Christian also made a mistake on the Hill Difficulty. He stopped in a pleasant arbor halfway up the hill and fell asleep. While sleeping, he dropped a scroll that had been given to him — a scroll that assured him of his salvation. He had to go back down the hill to find it, costing him time and daylight. This teaches us that even in the middle of difficulty, we must not grow lazy or comfortable. Rest is good, but spiritual complacency is dangerous. We must keep pressing forward.

Hebrews tells us the secret to climbing: "Looking unto Jesus." Not looking at the steepness of the hill. Not looking at how far we have to go. Not looking at what others are doing. Looking at Jesus. He is the author and finisher of our faith. He has already climbed the hill. He endured the cross for the joy set before Him. If He made it through, so will we — because He carries us.

Families, you will face hills together. Some seasons will be harder than others. But James tells us to count it all joy — not because the hill feels good, but because the climb is producing something in us. Patience. Character. Maturity. Completeness. The Hill Difficulty is not punishment; it is training. And the view from the top is worth every step.


There is much we can learn from those who walked with God before us. Consider these words.

John Bunyan, in his work "The Pilgrim's Progress," wrote these words:

"The hill, though high, I covet to ascend; the difficulty will not me offend. For I perceive the way to life lies here. Come, pluck up, heart; let's neither faint nor fear."

What John Bunyan is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Hebrews 12:1-2; James 1:2-4 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Family, let us talk about what we have learned. Consider these questions together.

Question 1: What is the hardest 'hill' your family has had to climb together? What did you learn from it?

Question 2: Why did Formalist and Hypocrisy try to go around the hill? What 'shortcuts' do people try to take in their spiritual lives today?

Question 3: How does looking at Jesus help us keep climbing when the way is steep and difficult?

Question 4: James says to 'count it all joy' in trials. How can we find joy even when things are hard?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God." -- Hebrews 12:2

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Learning sticks better when we do something with it. Try this activity as a family:

If possible, take your family on a real hike — even a short one with a steep section. Talk about how it feels to climb, how you keep going even when tired, and how good it feels to reach the top. If you cannot hike, climb stairs together and use the experience as a teaching moment.


Lord, as we close tonight's devotional, we place our family in Your hands. Watch over us as we sleep. Guard our dreams. Prepare our hearts for tomorrow. May the truths from Hebrews 12:1-2; James 1:2-4 follow us into the new day. We love You, Lord. Amen.

Goodnight. Remember your memory verse, and carry it with you tomorrow.
