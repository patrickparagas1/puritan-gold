PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 8
"Choosing Wisdom Over Riches"
Proverbs 8:10-11

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to tonight's couples devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for setting aside this time to draw near to God and to each other. The moments you invest in your marriage before the Lord are among the most important moments of your day.

Let us open God's Word together. Our passage tonight comes from Proverbs 8:10-11:

"Receive my instruction, and not silver; and knowledge rather than choice gold. For wisdom is better than rubies; and all the things that may be desired are not to be compared with it."

That is Proverbs 8:10-11. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Now let us hear from Matthew Henry, one of the great Puritan voices on this subject. The Puritans wrote extensively about marriage because they saw it as one of God's most profound gifts.

In this glorious chapter, Wisdom herself speaks, and here she makes a claim that is breathtaking in its boldness: she is more valuable than silver, gold, and rubies. Matthew Henry comments: 'Wisdom is God's gift, and far more valuable than worldly wealth. Those that have wisdom have that which will stand them in better stead than money.'

Henry notes the comprehensive nature of the comparison. It is not merely that wisdom is better than some material goods; 'all the things that may be desired are not to be compared with it.' Every earthly treasure, every worldly ambition, every material comfort, when weighed against wisdom, is found wanting. This is not because material things are evil in themselves, but because they are finite, temporary, and incapable of providing what the soul truly needs.

Charles Bridges expands on this: wisdom is superior to riches in its permanence (it endures when wealth perishes), its sufficiency (it satisfies when gold cannot), and its fruit (it produces righteousness, peace, and joy). Bridges writes that many marriages have been destroyed by the pursuit of wealth at the expense of wisdom. The couple who sacrifices family worship for overtime hours, who neglects prayer for portfolio management, who measures success by net worth rather than spiritual growth, has exchanged wisdom for rubies and will find the trade disastrous.

Henry further applies this to the ordering of priorities: 'Those that prefer the wealth of the world before the truths and ways of God put a most unreasonable contempt upon religion.' In marriage, this means that the couple must agree on what they truly value. If wisdom holds the highest place, every other decision falls into proper order.

--- Marriage Application ---

Money is one of the most common sources of marital conflict, and this passage goes to the root of the problem. The issue is not usually how much money a couple has, but what place money holds in their hierarchy of values.

Do you and your spouse agree that wisdom is better than rubies? In theory, most Christians would say yes. But in practice, our choices reveal our true values. Do you spend more time planning your budget than planning your spiritual growth? Do financial anxieties dominate your conversations more than prayers of trust?

Choosing wisdom over riches as a couple means making decisions that prioritize spiritual health over financial gain. It means being willing to earn less in order to be more present. It means investing in your church, in your children's spiritual formation, and in one another's souls, even when the world says those investments have no return.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Now let us pause for reflection. Here are some questions for you and your spouse to discuss together. Be honest. Be gentle. Listen more than you speak.

Question 1: Honestly, does the pursuit of financial security sometimes take priority over spiritual growth in our marriage?

Question 2: What is one financial decision we could make that would reflect our belief that wisdom is better than rubies?

Question 3: How can we encourage one another to keep eternal values above temporal ones?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Let us move into a time of prayer together. If you are able, hold hands as you pray. There is something powerful about a husband and wife joining hands before the throne of grace.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray together that God would give you eyes to see the surpassing worth of His wisdom. Confess any areas where material pursuits have crowded out spiritual ones. Ask for the grace to order your finances and priorities according to His values.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord who is the source of all true riches fill your marriage with the treasure of His wisdom, which no moth can corrupt and no thief can steal. Amen.

May the Lord bless your marriage and keep it strong. May He make His face to shine upon your home, and give you peace. Goodnight, dear couple. Walk together in His love.
