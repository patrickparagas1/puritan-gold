PURITAN GOLD -- Study Devotional, Day 12
Series: Romans
"Servants of Righteousness"
From slavery to sin to slavery to God — and the wages each pays

---

Good evening, student of the Word. Every time we open the Scriptures, we stand on holy ground. The God of the universe has spoken, and He invites us to listen, to understand, and to respond. Let us listen well tonight.

We are studying the book of Romans together. Tonight's lesson is titled "Servants of Righteousness." Each session builds on what came before, so if you have been following along, you will see how tonight's passage connects to the larger argument of the book.

--- Scripture Reading ---

Let us read our passage for tonight. It comes from Romans 6:15-23:

"What then? shall we sin, because we are not under the law, but under grace? God forbid. Know ye not, that to whom ye yield yourselves servants to obey, his servants ye are to whom ye obey; whether of sin unto death, or of obedience unto righteousness? But God be thanked, that ye were the servants of sin, but ye have obeyed from the heart that form of doctrine which was delivered you. Being then made free from sin, ye became the servants of righteousness... For when ye were the servants of sin, ye were free from righteousness. What fruit had ye then in those things whereof ye are now ashamed? for the end of those things is death. But now being made free from sin, and become servants to God, ye have your fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life. For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord."

Read that passage again to yourself. Mark any words or phrases that stand out. The Puritans read with a pen in hand, always ready to note what God was showing them.

--- Key Verse ---

Here is the key verse for tonight's study. Let it anchor everything else we discuss.

"For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord." -- Romans 6:23

This verse is the lens through which we will examine the rest of tonight's passage. Keep it in the front of your mind as we proceed.

--- Exposition ---

Having read the text, let us explore its meaning in depth.

John Owen offers valuable commentary on this passage. The Puritans were expositors of the highest order, and their insights remain remarkably fresh.

John Owen, continuing the themes of his writings on sin and sanctification, brought penetrating analysis to Romans 6:15-23. Owen stressed the inescapable reality that Paul presents: every human being is a slave. There is no neutral ground, no autonomous freedom. You are either a slave to sin or a slave to righteousness. The illusion of complete self-determination is itself one of sin's greatest deceptions. Owen argued that true freedom is not the absence of all servitude but servitude to the right master.

Owen gave particular attention to verse 17: 'But God be thanked, that ye were the servants of sin, but ye have obeyed from the heart that form of doctrine which was delivered you.' He noticed that Paul does not say they obeyed the doctrine but that they 'obeyed from the heart that form of doctrine.' The word 'form' (tupos) means a mold or pattern. The gospel is a mold into which the believer is poured, shaping them from the inside out. Owen saw in this the inseparability of doctrine and life. Right belief produces right behavior. The form of sound doctrine reshapes the form of the human heart.

Owen also provided extended meditation on the contrast between 'wages' and 'gift' in verse 23. He observed that sin pays wages — death is what you earn, what you deserve, what you have coming to you. But eternal life is not wages; it is a gift. You cannot earn it, deserve it, or claim it as your due. This asymmetry is the heart of the gospel: justice gives us what we deserve (death), but grace gives us what we do not deserve (life). Owen urged believers to keep this contrast always before their eyes, for it kills both pride and despair — pride because you cannot earn life, and despair because you need not earn it.

Romans 6:15 repeats the objection of verse 1 in slightly different form: 'What then? shall we sin, because we are not under the law, but under grace?' If believers are no longer under law but under grace, does that mean we can sin freely? Paul again responds with his vehement 'God forbid!' and then provides a different angle on the answer — the metaphor of slavery.

Verse 16 presents a universal principle: 'Know ye not, that to whom ye yield yourselves servants to obey, his servants ye are to whom ye obey; whether of sin unto death, or of obedience unto righteousness?' Every person is a slave. The question is not whether you serve but whom you serve. Jesus taught the same truth: 'No man can serve two masters' (Matthew 6:24). There are only two options: sin leading to death, or obedience leading to righteousness. There is no third category of autonomous freedom.

This challenges the modern idol of personal autonomy. Our culture worships individual freedom — the right to do whatever you want, whenever you want, however you want. But Paul says this supposed freedom is actually slavery. The person who thinks they are free because they follow every desire is actually in bondage to those desires. True freedom is not doing whatever you want; it is being empowered to do what you ought. As Augustine said, 'Free will without grace has the power to do nothing but sin.'

Verse 17 is a beautiful expression of thanksgiving: 'But God be thanked, that ye were the servants of sin, but ye have obeyed from the heart that form of doctrine which was delivered you.' Notice the past tense — 'ye were the servants of sin.' That chapter is closed. And notice how the change happened: they 'obeyed from the heart.' Not mere external compliance but heartfelt, willing, joyful obedience. And they obeyed 'that form of doctrine which was delivered you.' The gospel is not merely information to be believed but a pattern to be obeyed. Sound doctrine produces sound living.

Verse 18 states the result: 'Being then made free from sin, ye became the servants of righteousness.' Freedom from sin and servitude to righteousness are not two separate things; they are the same thing viewed from different angles. You cannot be free from sin without becoming a servant of righteousness, and you cannot serve righteousness while remaining in slavery to sin.

In verse 19, Paul acknowledges that the slavery metaphor is imperfect: 'I speak after the manner of men because of the infirmity of your flesh.' Serving God is not really like being a slave to a human master — it is infinitely better, infinitely more dignified. But the metaphor is useful because it communicates the totality of commitment involved.

Verses 20-21 challenge the believer to honest reflection: 'For when ye were the servants of sin, ye were free from righteousness. What fruit had ye then in those things whereof ye are now ashamed? for the end of those things is death.' Look back on your life before Christ. What did sin actually produce? Pleasure? Perhaps temporarily. But what lasting fruit? Only things you are now ashamed of. Sin promises satisfaction and delivers shame. It promises freedom and delivers bondage. It promises life and delivers death. Honest reflection on sin's actual track record is one of the most powerful motivations for holiness.

Verse 22 presents the glorious contrast: 'But now being made free from sin, and become servants to God, ye have your fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life.' The fruit of serving God is holiness — progressive transformation into Christlikeness — and the end is everlasting life. Holiness and eternal life: these are fruits worth living for. They do not bring shame but glory. They do not end in death but in endless life with God.

Verse 23 is one of the most well-known verses in the Bible, and its precision is remarkable: 'For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.' Paul deliberately chooses different words for each side of the equation. Sin pays wages — opsonia, the soldier's pay, what you have earned and are owed. Death is not arbitrary punishment; it is the fair payment for rebellion against the living God. But eternal life is not wages; it is charisma — a free gift, an unearned favor, sheer grace. You earn death; you receive life. You deserve condemnation; you are given salvation. And it comes 'through Jesus Christ our Lord' — not through your efforts, your religion, your moral achievement, but through the Person and work of Jesus Christ.

This verse is the gospel in miniature. It acknowledges the reality of sin, the justice of death, the graciousness of God, the sufficiency of Christ, and the gift of eternal life. It is a verse worth memorizing, meditating on, and sharing with everyone you meet.

--- Application ---

Knowledge puffs up, Paul warns, but love builds up. Let us ensure that what we have studied tonight leads not to pride but to faithful, loving obedience.

Romans 6:15-23 calls us to examine our servitude and choose our master wisely. Here are practical steps.

First, conduct an honest fruit inspection. Verse 21 asks, 'What fruit had ye then in those things whereof ye are now ashamed?' Take time this week to write down the actual fruit of sin in your life — past and present. Not what sin promised, but what it actually delivered. Broken relationships? Financial ruin? Guilt? Shame? Empty pleasure followed by emptier regret? Be honest. Then compare that list with the fruit of righteousness: peace, joy, love, purpose, eternal life. Let the contrast motivate your obedience.

Second, examine what you are yielding your members to. Verse 16 says you are a slave to whatever you obey. What patterns of obedience define your daily life? Do you habitually obey the pull of social media, the urge to criticize, the desire for comfort, the temptation to cut corners at work? Or do you habitually obey God's call to prayer, generosity, kindness, and integrity? Your habits reveal your master. Choose one sinful habit to break this week and one righteous habit to build.

Third, memorize Romans 6:23. This is one of the clearest, most concise statements of the gospel in all of Scripture. Commit it to memory. Use it in evangelism. Let its truth penetrate your heart. When you are tempted to sin, remember: the wages of sin is death. When you are tempted to despair, remember: the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

Fourth, embrace joyful servitude. Being a servant of God is not bondage but the highest freedom. It is the fish in the water, the bird in the air, the creature in its element. You were made for this. Approach your daily obedience not as drudgery but as the most natural, most fulfilling, most glorious life possible. Serve the Lord with gladness (Psalm 100:2).

Write down one concrete action you will take this week in response to tonight's study. Do not let this truth remain theoretical. The Puritans measured the success of a sermon not by how much the hearer learned but by how much the hearer changed.

--- Closing ---

Gracious Master, we thank You that we were once servants of sin but are now servants of righteousness. We confess that sin paid us only shame and death, while Your service yields holiness and eternal life. Help us to yield every member of our bodies as instruments of Your righteousness. Break the power of every remaining enslaving habit. Give us hearts that obey from the inside out. We receive with gratitude the gift of eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord — a gift we could never earn and do not deserve. May we serve You with joy all our days. Amen.

Until our next study, walk in the truth you have received.
