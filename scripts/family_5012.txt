PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 12
"Providence in the Ordinary Moments"
John Flavel — Providence and Family Trust


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Flavel helps us to see God's providence not only in the dramatic events of life but in the quiet, ordinary moments that make up most of our days together. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, open our eyes tonight to see Your hand in the ordinary. You are not only the God of miracles and mountains — You are the God of meals and bedtimes and morning routines. Help us to find You in the everyday rhythms of our family life. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Psalm 139:1-6. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me. Thou knowest my downsitting and mine uprising, thou understandest my thought afar off. Thou compassest my path and my lying down, and art acquainted with all my ways. For there is not a word in my tongue, but, lo, O LORD, thou knowest it altogether. Thou hast beset me behind and before, and laid thine hand upon me. Such knowledge is too wonderful for me; it is high, I cannot attain unto it."

That is Psalm 139:1-6. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


We often look for God in the extraordinary. We expect to find Him in the dramatic answers to prayer, the miraculous deliverances, the mountaintop experiences of worship. And He is certainly present in those moments. But John Flavel taught that one of the most profound expressions of God's providence is His involvement in the ordinary, everyday details of our lives.

The psalmist captures this truth with breathtaking intimacy. God knows your downsitting and your uprising — the simple act of sitting down and standing up. He understands your thoughts before you think them. He is acquainted with all your ways — not just the heroic ones, but the mundane ones. The making of breakfast. The drive to school. The conversation at the dinner table. The quiet moment of reading before sleep. God is present in all of it, governing all of it, caring about all of it.

Flavel believed that this was one of the most transforming truths a family could embrace. When you begin to see God's providence in the ordinary moments, the entire texture of family life changes. The routine becomes sacred. The mundane becomes meaningful. The tasks that seemed like drudgery become acts of worship, because you realize that God is present in them and working through them.

Consider the ordinary rhythms of your family. Every morning, someone in your household wakes up first and begins the work of caring for the family. That act is providential — God ordained it, sustains the energy required for it, and uses it for the good of every family member. Every meal that is prepared is an expression of God's provision — He provided the food, the means to purchase it, the skill to cook it, and the family to share it with. Every conversation — even the casual ones about homework and weekend plans — is a thread in the tapestry of your family's life that God is weaving according to His design.

Flavel warned against the spiritual danger of what we might call "ordinariness blindness" — the tendency to overlook God's hand simply because His works are constant and consistent. We take for granted the things God does every single day: He gives us breath, health, food, shelter, relationships, and purpose. Because these gifts arrive without fanfare, we fail to recognize them as gifts at all. Flavel urged families to fight this blindness by cultivating a habit of daily thanksgiving. When the family gathers for a meal, give thanks not as a routine but as a genuine expression of wonder that God has once again provided. When the children come home safely from school, acknowledge — even if only in your heart — that God's providence brought them home.

Flavel also noticed that God often does His most important work through ordinary means. The salvation of a child's soul does not usually come through a dramatic vision or a miraculous sign. It comes through the steady, faithful witness of godly parents who read the Bible at the breakfast table, who pray before meals, who answer their children's questions with patience and truth, and who model the Christian life day after day, year after year. These ordinary acts of faithfulness are the means God most commonly uses to draw the next generation into His kingdom.

There is a profound comfort in this teaching. If God is present in the ordinary moments, then no moment of your family's life is wasted. The long evening helping with homework is not lost time — it is an investment in your child's life that God will use in ways you cannot yet see. The bedtime routine that feels repetitive and exhausting is actually a sacred ritual of security and love that shapes your child's deepest understanding of God's care. The family dinner that seems unremarkable is, in God's providence, a crucible where relationships are forged and faith is formed.

Tonight, slow down. Look around your home. See the faces of the people God has placed in your life. And thank Him for the extraordinary gift of ordinary family life.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Flavel taught that God is present in the ordinary moments of life. What is one everyday family moment that you might start seeing differently because of this truth?

Question 2: The psalmist says God knows our "downsitting and uprising." How does it feel to know that God cares about the smallest details of your day?

Question 3: How can our family practice daily thanksgiving in a way that helps us notice God's providence in the ordinary?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Thou compassest my path and my lying down, and art acquainted with all my ways." -- Psalm 139:3

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Father, thank You for being the God of the ordinary. Thank You for being present in every meal, every conversation, every bedtime prayer. Forgive us for overlooking Your faithfulness because it comes so steadily. Give our family eyes to see Your hand in every moment of every day. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, known completely and loved perfectly by the God who is acquainted with all your ways.
