PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 10
"Speaking the Truth in Love"
Ephesians 4:15, 25 — Honest Communication in Marriage

---

Welcome, dear couple. One of the most practical applications of mutual submission is the way we speak to one another. Tonight we consider what it means to be truthful and loving in our words.

Our Scripture tonight draws from Ephesians 4:15 and 25:

"But speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ."

"Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another."

If you are one flesh, then you are certainly neighbors — the closest neighbors imaginable.

--- Teaching ---

The Puritans placed enormous weight on the role of speech in marriage. They understood that words have the power to build up or tear down, to heal or to wound, to draw a couple closer or drive them apart.

William Gouge devoted an entire section of his household duties to what he called "the tongue in marriage." He observed that many couples who would never dream of striking each other physically inflict devastating wounds with their words — through sarcasm, criticism, contempt, or silence. Gouge called these "the invisible bruises of marriage" and warned that they can be slower to heal than physical ones.

Paul's phrase "speaking the truth in love" holds two values in perfect tension. Truth without love is brutality. Love without truth is sentimentality. Both are destructive. A husband who tells his wife hard truths without gentleness is not being honest — he is being cruel. A wife who avoids difficult conversations to "keep the peace" is not being loving — she is being fearful. Biblical marriage requires both truth and love, always together, never separated.

Richard Baxter offered practical counsel for difficult conversations in marriage. First, choose your timing wisely — do not bring up sensitive matters when either of you is tired, hungry, or distracted. Second, begin with affirmation — remind your spouse that you are for them, not against them. Third, speak about your own feelings rather than making accusations — say "I feel hurt when..." rather than "You always..." Fourth, listen more than you speak — the Puritans believed that the best communicators are the best listeners. Fifth, seek resolution, not victory — the goal of every marital conversation should be deeper understanding and greater unity, not winning the argument.

Thomas Gataker added that couples should develop the habit of speaking well of each other — not only to each other, but about each other. A husband who speaks admiringly of his wife to his friends builds a fortress of trust. A wife who speaks respectfully of her husband to her family strengthens the bond between them. Conversely, couples who complain about each other to outsiders slowly poison their own marriage.

The phrase "for we are members one of another" is especially powerful in the context of marriage. When you wound your spouse with your words, you wound yourself. When you build up your spouse with encouragement, you strengthen the whole marriage. You are not two separate people having a debate; you are one body trying to function in health.

Tonight, make a commitment to each other: to speak truth, always in love. To be honest, always with gentleness. To listen, always with patience.

--- Reflection Prompts ---

Discuss together:

Question 1: Is there a conversation you have been avoiding because it feels too difficult? How could you approach it with both truth and love?

Question 2: Think about the way you speak to each other in moments of stress or conflict. What patterns have developed? Are they healthy?

Question 3: How do you speak about each other to others — family, friends, coworkers? Would your spouse be encouraged or hurt if they overheard?

--- Prayer Focus ---

Pray together for the gift of truthful, loving speech. Ask God to guard your tongues from cruelty and your hearts from cowardice. Pray for the courage to have honest conversations and the gentleness to have them well.

--- Closing ---

May the God of truth and the God of love govern every word that passes between you. May your marriage be a place where honesty is safe and kindness is constant. The Lord bless your conversations and your silences alike. Goodnight.
