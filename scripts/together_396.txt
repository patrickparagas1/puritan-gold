PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 4
"Nourish and Cherish"
Ephesians 5:28-29 — Loving as Your Own Body


Welcome, dear couple. We are in the heart of Ephesians 5 now, and tonight Paul draws a comparison that is both simple and profound. Listen carefully:

"So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself. For no man ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church."

Ephesians 5:28-29. Two beautiful words stand at the center of tonight's meditation: nourish and cherish.


The Puritans paid careful attention to the specific language of Scripture, and these two words — nourish and cherish — reveal the tender, nurturing character of Christ's love and, by extension, the love a husband is called to give.

To nourish means to feed, to sustain, to provide what is needed for growth. William Gouge noted that this encompasses not merely physical provision — food, shelter, clothing — but emotional and spiritual provision as well. A husband nourishes his wife when he provides a stable home, yes, but also when he provides emotional security, attentive listening, spiritual encouragement, and consistent affirmation. A wife who is well-nourished by her husband's love will flourish like a garden in good soil.

To cherish means to warm, to hold close, to treat with tender care. The Greek word Paul uses here literally refers to a bird warming her eggs — a picture of protective, gentle, life-giving warmth. Gouge wrote that a husband cherishes his wife when he makes her feel safe, valued, and deeply loved. This is not merely an occasional gesture — a birthday gift or an anniversary dinner — but a daily disposition of tenderness.

Paul's logic is striking: love your wife as your own body. No one neglects his own flesh. You feed yourself when you are hungry. You rest when you are tired. You tend to your own wounds. You instinctively care for yourself. Paul says: extend that same instinctive, consistent care to your wife. Her needs should feel as immediate and as natural to you as your own.

Thomas Gataker observed that many marriages suffer not from dramatic failures but from slow neglect. A husband who works long hours and comes home with nothing left to give. A wife who pours herself out for the children and has no energy remaining for her husband. The Puritans counseled against this gradual erosion of care. They urged couples to guard their marriage the way they guard their own health — proactively, consistently, with daily attention.

Richard Baxter put it plainly: "If you would not starve your own body, do not starve your marriage of affection. If you would not expose your own flesh to the cold, do not leave your wife without the warmth of your love."

But this principle works both ways. While Paul addresses husbands specifically, the Puritans recognized that wives also nourish and cherish their husbands — through respect, through encouragement, through physical affection, through partnership in the work of the home and the raising of children. A healthy marriage is one where both partners are actively investing in each other's wellbeing.

Tonight, ask yourselves honestly: am I nourishing my spouse? Am I cherishing them? Not in theory, but in practice. Not in intention, but in action. The Puritans believed that love is not primarily a feeling — it is a commitment expressed through daily deeds. Feelings follow faithfulness.


Turn to each other and discuss:

Question 1: On a scale of one to ten, how well-nourished do you feel in this marriage — emotionally, spiritually, physically? Share honestly, without defensiveness.

Question 2: What specific act of cherishing from your spouse has meant the most to you recently? What would mean the most to you this week?

Question 3: Are there areas of neglect that have crept into your marriage — not from malice, but from busyness or distraction? What would it look like to address them?


Pray together that God would help you nourish and cherish each other with the same instinctive, consistent care that Christ gives the Church. Ask Him to open your eyes to your spouse's unspoken needs and to give you the energy and creativity to meet them.


May the Lord who nourishes and cherishes His Church fill your marriage with the same tender, life-giving love. May you never cease to care for one another with warmth, with attention, and with joy. Grace and peace be with you both. Goodnight.
