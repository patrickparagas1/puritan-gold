PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 6
"United in Teaching Your Children"
Proverbs 6:20-23

---

Good evening, dear couple. The Puritans believed that marriage was one of the greatest means of grace God provides. Tonight we enter into that grace together. Let us open our hearts and our ears to what the Lord has for us.

As we settle in together, let us hear from Proverbs 6:20-23:

"My son, keep thy father's commandment, and forsake not the law of thy mother: Bind them continually upon thine heart, and tie them about thy neck. When thou goest, it shall lead thee; when thou sleepest, it shall keep thee; and when thou awakest, it shall talk with thee. For the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light; and reproofs of instruction are the way of life."

That is Proverbs 6:20-23. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

One of the gifts of the Christian tradition is that we do not study alone. We have the wisdom of the saints who came before us. Tonight we hear from Matthew Henry.

Solomon here addresses the child, but in doing so he reveals the calling of the parents. Matthew Henry notes that both father and mother are mentioned: 'The father's commandment and the mother's law.' This is not accidental. God has ordained that both parents share the responsibility of instructing their children in the way of righteousness.

Henry observes that the father's role is described as 'commandment,' suggesting authority and direction, while the mother's is 'law' (Hebrew: torah, instruction), suggesting nurture and teaching. Together, they provide the full-orbed instruction that a child needs. Henry writes: 'Good parents will give good instructions, and good children will attend to them and be governed by them.' The tragedy is when either parent fails in this duty, or when parents contradict one another, leaving the child confused.

The imagery of binding and tying speaks of constant, intimate connection with parental instruction. Henry notes the threefold promise: the instruction will lead the child when walking (in active life), keep the child when sleeping (in rest and vulnerability), and talk with the child upon waking (in reflection and new beginnings). This comprehensive guidance is the fruit of faithful, united parental teaching.

Charles Bridges adds that the parents' instruction is likened to a lamp and a light because it illuminates the path through a dark world. But this light must shine from both parents in harmony. Bridges warns that 'where parents are divided in their counsel, the child walks in confusion.' The greatest gift parents can give their children is not merely good teaching, but united teaching flowing from a marriage that is itself submitted to the Word of God.

--- Marriage Application ---

If you have children, or hope to, this passage speaks directly to your partnership. You are called to be united instructors of the next generation. This means that you must first agree on what you will teach, and that agreement must be grounded in Scripture, not in personal preference.

Discuss your approach to discipline, to moral instruction, to spiritual formation. Are you on the same page? Do your children see a unified front, or do they see contradictions between their father's commandment and their mother's law? Children are remarkably perceptive; they quickly learn to exploit divisions between parents.

United instruction also means supporting one another's authority. When one parent has made a decision, the other should uphold it (discussing disagreements privately, not before the children). This kind of unity requires ongoing communication, mutual respect, and a shared commitment to the authority of God's Word over both your opinions.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Discussion time. Husband, listen to your wife. Wife, listen to your husband. Let this be a safe and sacred space.

Question 1: Are we truly united in how we instruct and discipline our children, or are there areas of significant disagreement?

Question 2: What are we doing to ensure that God's Word is the foundation of our parental instruction?

Question 3: How can we better support one another's authority and teaching in the eyes of our children?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Let us close our eyes and open our hearts to the Lord in prayer. Husband, pray for your wife. Wife, pray for your husband. There is no greater gift you can give each other.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray for unity in your parenting and for the grace to be faithful teachers of God's truth. Ask the Lord to make His commandments a lamp and a light to your children through your consistent, loving instruction.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Lord who entrusted children to your care grant you wisdom and unity in teaching them, that His truth may be a lamp to their feet for all their days. Amen.

Go in peace, husband and wife. The God who ordained your marriage is faithful to sustain it. Trust Him. Love each other. And come back tomorrow for more. Goodnight.
