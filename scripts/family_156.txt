PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 25
"Waiting on the Lord"
Pilgrim Families — Week 4, Day 4


Good evening, beloved family. The Puritans believed that the family altar was the most important altar in all the land. Tonight we gather at that altar together. Welcome to Puritan Gold.

Let us come before the Lord. Gracious God, thank You for the gift of family. Thank You that we can come to You together. We pray that You would teach us from Your Word this evening. Let every word sink deep into our hearts. We love You, Lord. In Jesus' name, Amen.

The Word of God is a lamp to our feet and a light to our path. Tonight that light shines from Isaiah 40:31; Psalm 27:14:

"But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint. Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD."

That is Isaiah 40:31; Psalm 27:14. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Few spiritual disciplines are harder than waiting. We live in a world of instant everything — instant food, instant information, instant entertainment. But God frequently calls His children to wait. And the waiting is not wasted time — it is sacred time, time when God does His deepest work in our hearts.

Isaiah 40:31 contains one of the most majestic promises in all of Scripture: "They that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint." The promise is for those who wait. Not for those who rush ahead. Not for those who give up. Not for those who try to solve everything themselves. But for those who wait upon the Lord.

The progression is interesting: mounting up, then running, then walking. You might expect it to go the other way — starting slow and building to a climax. But Isaiah knows that the hardest thing is not the dramatic moments of soaring. The hardest thing is the daily plodding — the walking without fainting. And God promises strength even for that.

David echoes this in Psalm 27:14: "Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD." David says it twice — wait, then wait again. As if he knows we need to hear it more than once. As if he knows that our natural impulse is to stop waiting and start doing. But God says wait. Be of good courage. I will strengthen your heart.

Waiting on the Lord does not mean doing nothing. It means actively trusting God while He works according to His timeline rather than ours. It means continuing to pray, continuing to obey, continuing to hope — even when we cannot see any progress. It means refusing to take matters into our own hands because we believe God's timing is better than ours.

Rutherford spent years in exile, waiting for God to restore him to his congregation. He could have grown bitter. He could have given up. He could have compromised his convictions to end the exile sooner. But instead, he waited. And while he waited, he wrote letters that have blessed the church for four hundred years. The waiting was not wasted — it was the most productive season of his entire life.

The Bible is full of people who had to wait. Abraham waited 25 years for Isaac. Joseph waited 13 years in slavery and prison before becoming ruler of Egypt. David waited years between his anointing and his crowning. The Israelites waited 400 years in Egypt before the Exodus. In every case, the waiting was purposeful. God was preparing both the circumstances and the person for what was coming.

For families, waiting is often the hardest test. Waiting for a prayer to be answered. Waiting for a prodigal child to come home. Waiting for a financial breakthrough. Waiting for healing. Waiting for direction. The temptation during the wait is to give up on God or to take shortcuts. But the promise is clear: those who wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength.

How do we wait well as a family? First, by remembering God's past faithfulness. If He has come through before, He will come through again. Second, by staying active in obedience. Wait does not mean sit idle — it means keep doing what God has already told you to do while trusting Him for what comes next. Third, by encouraging each other. When one family member is struggling to wait, others can hold them up with reminders of God's promises.

Rutherford wrote: "It is faith's work to claim and challenge loving-kindnesses out of all the roughest providences." Even in the roughest seasons, faith reaches up and claims God's loving-kindness. Even in the dark, faith trusts. Even in the waiting, faith hopes. This is the work of faith — not to understand everything, but to trust the One who does.


The wisdom of the Puritans remains as fresh today as when it was first written. Listen to this insight.

Samuel Rutherford, in his work "Letters of Samuel Rutherford," wrote these words:

"It is faith's work to claim and challenge loving-kindnesses out of all the roughest providences that befall us, and to trust in the dark and wait for the Lord."

What Samuel Rutherford is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Isaiah 40:31; Psalm 27:14 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Here are some questions for us to think about and discuss together. There are no wrong answers here, just honest hearts.

Question 1: Why is waiting so hard? What does our difficulty with waiting reveal about our hearts?

Question 2: Isaiah mentions mounting up, running, and walking. Which one do you think is hardest? Why does walking without fainting require the most strength?

Question 3: Can you think of a time our family had to wait for something and God came through?

Question 4: What is our family currently waiting for? How can we wait well together?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint." -- Isaiah 40:31

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a hands-on way to remember what we have learned tonight:

Set a timer for five minutes and sit in complete silence together, doing nothing but waiting. Afterward, discuss how it felt. Was it uncomfortable? Did your mind wander? Talk about how waiting on God requires the same patience and discipline — and how God meets us in the stillness.


Father, we have heard Your Word tonight, and we are grateful. We confess that we cannot live it out in our own strength. Fill us with Your Spirit. Give us wisdom for tomorrow, patience with one another, and a deep love for You. Keep our family close to You always. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Rest well tonight. God is faithful, and His wisdom is sure.
