PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 15
"No Condemnation"
Romans for Marriage — Day 15

---

Good evening, dear husband and wife. Welcome to tonight's couples devotional from Puritan Gold. Thank you for setting aside this time to draw near to God and to each other. The moments you invest in your marriage before the Lord are among the most important moments of your day.

Let us open God's Word together. Our passage tonight comes from Romans 8:1:

"There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit."

That is Romans 8:1. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

Now let us hear from Richard Sibbes, one of the great Puritan voices on this subject. The Puritans wrote extensively about marriage because they saw it as one of God's most profound gifts.

Romans 8:1 may be the single most liberating verse in the entire Bible: 'There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus.' No condemnation. Not reduced condemnation. Not occasional condemnation. No condemnation whatsoever. The verdict has been rendered, and it is permanent acquittal.

Richard Sibbes, whose ministry was characterized by extraordinary tenderness toward struggling souls, built much of his pastoral care on this verse. Sibbes understood that many believers live under a constant cloud of self-condemnation — and they extend that same condemning spirit to everyone around them, especially their spouses.

Sibbes taught that condemnation is the devil's primary weapon against Christian marriages. When a husband feels constantly condemned — by his wife's disappointment, by his own failures, by an internal voice that says he will never be good enough — he either shuts down emotionally or lashes out in anger. When a wife feels condemned — by her husband's criticism, by her sense of inadequacy, by the relentless pressure to be perfect — she either withdraws into depression or builds walls of self-protection. Condemnation kills intimacy.

But Romans 8:1 shatters the power of condemnation. If God Himself — the righteous Judge of all the earth — declares 'no condemnation,' then who are we to condemn our spouses? Who are we to condemn ourselves? The highest court in the universe has rendered its verdict. Case dismissed. Forever.

Sibbes urged believers to apply this truth to every relationship, beginning with marriage. Stop condemning your spouse for their failures. Stop condemning yourself for yours. The blood of Christ has settled the matter. You stand before God without a single charge against you. Live in that freedom, and extend that freedom to the person you sleep next to every night.

--- Marriage Application ---

Condemnation is subtle. It shows up as a sigh of disappointment, a pointed comment, a withering look, a withdrawn affection. It says without words: 'You are not enough. You have failed again. You will never change.'

Tonight, make a deliberate decision to banish condemnation from your marriage. This does not mean you never address problems — it means you address them without condemning the person. There is a vast difference between saying 'What you did hurt me' and 'You always ruin everything.' The first addresses behavior; the second condemns the person.

Say to each other tonight: 'There is no condemnation between us, because there is no condemnation in Christ.' Then ask: 'Is there any way I have been making you feel condemned? How can I change that?'

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

Now let us pause for reflection. Here are some questions for you and your spouse to discuss together. Be honest. Be gentle. Listen more than you speak.

Question 1: In what ways — even subtle ones — do you make your spouse feel condemned?

Question 2: Do you live under self-condemnation? How does that affect your marriage?

Question 3: What would your marriage look like if both of you truly believed and practiced 'no condemnation'?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Let us move into a time of prayer together. If you are able, hold hands as you pray. There is something powerful about a husband and wife joining hands before the throne of grace.

Tonight's prayer focus: Thank God for the glorious truth of Romans 8:1 — no condemnation in Christ. Ask Him to root out every condemning word, look, and attitude from your marriage and replace it with the freedom of the Gospel.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May your home be a condemnation-free zone — a place where grace reigns, where failure is met with compassion, and where the verdict of Christ silences every accusing voice.

May the Lord bless your marriage and keep it strong. May He make His face to shine upon your home, and give you peace. Goodnight, dear couple. Walk together in His love.
