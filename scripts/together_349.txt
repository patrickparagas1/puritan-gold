PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 18
"Unbreakable Covenant Love"
Romans for Marriage — Day 18


Good evening. What a blessing it is to see a husband and wife seeking the Lord together. The Puritans believed that a praying couple is an unstoppable force for the kingdom of God. Let us pray together tonight.

The Word of God has sustained marriages for thousands of years. Tonight it speaks to yours from Romans 8:35-39:

"Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword? Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us. For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

That is Romans 8:35-39. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.


Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher. Samuel Rutherford reflected deeply on the truths of Scripture and how they apply to the covenant of marriage.

Paul's triumphant declaration in Romans 8:35-39 is the crescendo of the greatest theological argument ever written. After systematically building the case for God's unshakable love, Paul throws down the ultimate challenge: 'Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?' And then he lists every conceivable adversary — tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword, death, life, angels, principalities, powers, things present, things to come, height, depth, and any other creature — and declares that none of them can succeed.

Samuel Rutherford, who experienced many of these adversities personally, wrote with burning conviction about the inseparable love of Christ. He knew tribulation — he was exiled for his faith. He knew distress — he was separated from his congregation and his family. He knew persecution — he was silenced by the authorities. Yet from the depths of his suffering, he declared that nothing could separate him from the love of Christ.

For marriage, this passage provides the theological foundation for covenant faithfulness. Your marriage vow is a reflection of God's covenant love — a love that does not break under pressure. Just as nothing can separate the believer from Christ's love, nothing should separate husband from wife. The same list Paul gives — tribulation, distress, financial hardship, illness, danger — these are the very things that test marriages most severely. But they are not meant to break the covenant; they are meant to prove its strength.

Rutherford understood that covenant love is not the same as romantic feeling. Feelings fluctuate with circumstances. Covenant love endures through all circumstances. The husband who loves his wife in health and in sickness, in wealth and in poverty, in joy and in sorrow — that husband is reflecting the love of Christ, who loved us through the worst circumstances imaginable.

'We are more than conquerors through him that loved us.' Not merely survivors. More than conquerors. The love of Christ does not just help us endure — it empowers us to triumph. A marriage anchored in Christ's inseparable love is not merely holding on by a thread; it is conquering through the power of divine love.


Read Romans 8:35-39 aloud to each other tonight, replacing 'us' with your names. 'Who shall separate [husband's name] and [wife's name] from the love of Christ?' Let the list wash over you: nothing present, nothing future, nothing above, nothing below — nothing in all creation.

Then make your own declaration: As Christ's love for us is unbreakable, so our covenant love for each other will be unbreakable. Not because we are strong, but because He who loved us is strong.

If your marriage has been under attack — from stress, conflict, weariness, or any other force — stand together tonight and declare: we are more than conquerors. The enemy will not win. Our covenant will hold. Because Christ holds us.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.


Here are some questions to guide your conversation tonight. Take turns sharing, and give each other the gift of your full attention.

Question 1: What forces have been trying to separate you from each other? How can you face them together with Romans 8:35-39?

Question 2: What does it mean to be 'more than conquerors' in your marriage? How is that different from barely surviving?

Question 3: How does Christ's unbreakable love for you empower you to love each other with unbreakable commitment?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.


Prayer is the heartbeat of a Christian marriage. Let us pray together now. You do not need eloquent words, just honest ones.

Tonight's prayer focus: Pray Romans 8:35-39 over your marriage. Declare that nothing will separate you from each other or from the love of Christ. Ask God to make your covenant love as firm and unshakable as His.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.


May the love that holds the universe together hold your marriage together. May nothing in all creation — no trial, no enemy, no fear — separate what God has joined. You are more than conquerors.

May your love for each other grow deeper with every passing day, and may your love for God be the source and the strength of it all. Goodnight, dear ones.
