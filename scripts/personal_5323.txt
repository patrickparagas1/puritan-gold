PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Doctrine Deep-Dive, Day 23
"The Last Judgment"
The Doctrine of Final Judgment — When Every Secret Shall Be Revealed


Good evening, friend. Tonight we take up one of the most solemn and awe-inspiring doctrines in all of Scripture: the final judgment. This is a truth that the Puritans preached with both gravity and hope, for they understood that the last judgment is not merely a terrifying prospect for the wicked but a glorious vindication for the righteous. Let us examine it together with reverent hearts.


Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is 2 Corinthians 5:10:

"For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ; that every one may receive the things done in his body, according to that he hath done, whether it be good or bad."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.


The doctrine of the last judgment teaches that at the end of this present age, the Lord Jesus Christ will return in glory and all people — the living and the dead — will stand before His throne to give an account of their lives. The Westminster Confession of Faith declares that God hath appointed a day wherein He will judge the world in righteousness by Jesus Christ, to whom all power and judgment is given of the Father. This is not a metaphor. It is not a symbol. It is a promise — and it will come to pass.

The Puritans treated this doctrine with the utmost seriousness because they believed it had immediate practical consequences for how believers live today. Thomas Watson wrote that the thought of the last judgment should be a bridle to restrain us from sin and a spur to quicken us to holiness. If every idle word, every secret thought, every hidden deed will be brought into the light, then how carefully ought we to walk before God?

The judge at this final tribunal is none other than Jesus Christ Himself. This is a truth of tremendous significance. The One who judges is the same One who died for sinners. John Owen reflected that Christ's appointment as judge is both a terror to the impenitent and a comfort to the believer. For the unrepentant sinner, it means that the very Savior he rejected will be the One who pronounces his sentence. For the believer, it means that his judge is also his advocate, his redeemer, and his friend. The believer approaches the judgment seat not with servile fear but with reverent confidence, knowing that the One who sits upon the throne has already borne his condemnation.

The scope of the judgment is comprehensive. Nothing will be hidden. Jesus said, "For there is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed; neither hid, that shall not be known" (Luke 12:2). The Puritans took this with utter seriousness. Stephen Charnock wrote that God's omniscience guarantees that the judgment will be perfectly just. No crime will go unpunished. No good deed will go unrewarded. No secret sin will escape notice. No hidden act of faithfulness will be overlooked. The judgment of God is infallible because the knowledge of God is infinite.

For the unbeliever, the last judgment is a day of unimaginable dread. The books will be opened, and every transgression will be revealed. There will be no excuse, no defense, no appeal. The righteousness of God will be vindicated, and the wages of sin will be paid in full. The Puritans did not shy away from this truth. They preached it plainly because they believed that an honest reckoning with the reality of judgment is one of the means God uses to awaken sinners to their need for Christ.

But for the believer, the last judgment is a day of vindication and joy. The believer's sins have already been judged at the cross. Christ has already borne the penalty. When the believer stands before the judgment seat, he stands clothed in the righteousness of Christ, and the verdict has already been pronounced: "No condemnation." Richard Baxter wrote that the saints will hear on that day the most welcome words ever spoken: "Come, ye blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world" (Matthew 25:34).

The Puritans also taught that the last judgment will vindicate the ways of God before the watching universe. Every question about God's justice, every complaint about the prosperity of the wicked and the suffering of the righteous, every mystery of providence will be resolved on that day. Watson wrote that the last judgment is the great answer to the problem of evil. What seems unjust in time will be revealed as perfectly just in eternity.


First, let the reality of the last judgment sober your daily walk. Live today as one who will give an account tomorrow. Ask yourself each evening: if Christ returned tonight, would I be ashamed of how I spent this day?

Second, let the gospel transform your fear of judgment into confident hope. If you are in Christ, the verdict is already settled. Rest in the finished work of the cross and let your assurance produce not complacency but gratitude.

Third, let the doctrine of judgment fuel your compassion for the lost. If the unrepentant will face the wrath of God, then the most loving thing you can do for your unbelieving neighbor is to point him to the Savior before that day arrives.


Righteous Judge, we tremble at the thought of the great day when all shall stand before Thy throne. Yet we rejoice that our Judge is also our Redeemer. Keep us faithful until that day. Keep us sober in our walk and fervent in our witness. And when Thou dost appear in glory, may we hear those blessed words: "Well done, good and faithful servant." Through Christ our Lord, Amen.

Rest tonight in the knowledge that your Judge is your Savior, and His verdict over you is grace.
