PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 16
"Walking the Talk"
William Secker — The Nonsuch Professor

---

Hello, dear ones. How wonderful it is to sit together as a family and open the Scriptures. Welcome to Puritan Gold's family devotional. Let us begin with thankful hearts.

Let us pray together. Lord God, we come before You as a family, grateful for Your love and Your provision. We ask that You would quiet our minds and soften our hearts as we hear from You tonight. Help us to understand, to remember, and to obey what You teach us. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from James 1:22-25. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves. For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass: For he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was. But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed."

That is James 1:22-25. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Yesterday we learned that true faith is more than words. Today we go deeper into what it means to be a 'doer' of God's Word, not merely a 'hearer.' James writes with vivid clarity in chapter 1, verses 22-25.

'But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.' James uses a strong word — 'deceiving.' A person who hears God's Word but does not obey it is not just falling short. That person is self-deceived. They think they are doing fine because they hear sermons, read the Bible, and go to church. But hearing without doing is a dangerous illusion.

Then James gives a brilliant illustration. He says that the person who hears but does not do is like someone who looks at his face in a mirror, sees that it is dirty, and then walks away and immediately forgets what he looked like. How foolish! The whole purpose of a mirror is to show you what needs to be fixed. If you see a smudge on your face in the mirror and then walk away without wiping it off, what was the point of looking?

The Bible is our spiritual mirror. It shows us who we really are — our sins, our weaknesses, our need for grace. But the mirror is not meant merely to inform us. It is meant to transform us. When we see something in God's Word that needs to change in our lives, we should change it — not walk away and forget about it.

But James does not end with the negative example. He gives us the positive: 'But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed.' Notice two things: first, God's law is called 'the perfect law of liberty.' It is not a prison — it is freedom. Obeying God does not restrict us; it liberates us from sin and its consequences. Second, the person who continues — who perseveres in obeying — will be blessed.

Jesus told a parable that illustrates this perfectly. At the end of the Sermon on the Mount, in Matthew 7:24-27, He said that the person who hears His words and does them is like a wise man who built his house upon a rock. When the storms came, the house stood firm. But the person who hears and does not do is like a foolish man who built his house upon sand. When the storms came, the house collapsed. The difference between the wise and the foolish was not what they heard — they both heard the same teaching. The difference was what they did with it.

William Secker drove this point home with his characteristic wit. He wrote that it is not the one who makes the highest profession but the one who makes the most progress who proves the best Christian. Leaves without fruit and words without works, he said, are but shadows without substance.

Secker loved contrasts. He wrote that it is better to walk with God in a plain way than to talk of God in a fine way. A Christian whose daily life reflects the gospel — who is honest, kind, patient, generous, and humble — preaches a more powerful sermon than the most eloquent speaker who lives selfishly.

Secker also observed that many people are experts at identifying sin in others while blind to sin in themselves. He wrote that it is easier to preach a good sermon than to live a good life, and that the world will judge Christianity not by our creeds but by our conduct.

This is an important lesson for families. Children are watching. They notice when there is a gap between what we say and what we do. They notice when Mom and Dad talk about kindness on Sunday but are harsh on Monday. They notice when we tell them to be honest but then stretch the truth ourselves. Consistency between our words and our actions is one of the most powerful testimonies we can give.

For our little ones: it is not enough to just listen to God — we need to do what He says! If God says be kind, we should be kind. If God says share, we should share. For older children: think of one thing you have learned from the Bible recently. Are you actually doing it? Or did you look in the mirror and walk away? For the whole family: let us choose one thing from God's Word this week and intentionally put it into practice. Not just hear it — do it.

--- Puritan Wisdom ---

The Puritans were men and women who treasured God's Word above all else. Let us hear from one of them now.

William Secker, in his work "The Nonsuch Professor in His Meridian Splendour," wrote these words:

"It is not he who makes the highest profession, but he who makes the most progress, that proves the best Christian. Leaves without fruit and words without works are but shadows without substance."

What William Secker is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in James 1:22-25 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.

--- Family Discussion ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: What happens when you look in a mirror and see something that needs fixing? Do you just walk away?

Question 2: Why does James say hearing the Bible without obeying it is like forgetting what you saw in a mirror?

Question 3: What does Secker mean by 'leaves without fruit'? Can you think of an example?

Question 4: What is one thing we have learned from God's Word recently that we can put into action this week?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves." -- James 1:22

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.

--- Family Activity ---

To make tonight's lesson come alive, try this activity together as a family:

Do a mirror exercise. Stand in front of a mirror as a family. Put a small sticker (or dab of washable marker) on each person's nose before they look. When they see it in the mirror, they should wipe it off right away. Then discuss: how silly would it be to see it and walk away? That is what we do when we hear God's Word but do not obey it.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for this time as a family. Thank You for speaking to us through Your Word. Plant these truths deep in our hearts, especially in the hearts of our children. Help us to walk in wisdom tomorrow and every day. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
