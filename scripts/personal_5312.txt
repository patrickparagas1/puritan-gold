PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Reading, Day 12
"Precious Remedies, Part 2"
Continuing Brooks' Practical Counsel Against the Accuser

---

Good evening, friend. We return tonight to Thomas Brooks and his "Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices." Last time we examined the devil's tactics for luring the soul into sin. Tonight we turn to an equally vital subject: Satan's devices for keeping the believer in a state of discouragement, doubt, and spiritual darkness. If you have ever felt crushed under the weight of your own failures, this reading is for you.

--- Scripture ---

Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is Romans 8:1:

"There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit."

And Revelation 12:10:

"For the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.

--- Teaching ---

Having exposed how Satan draws the soul into sin, Brooks now turns his attention to a different category of assault: the devices the devil uses against discouraged and doubting believers. This portion of "Precious Remedies" is among the most pastorally sensitive writing in all of Puritan literature. Brooks understood that the enemy is not only a tempter but also an accuser, and that many genuine Christians live under a cloud of condemnation that the gospel has already lifted.

The first device in this section is that Satan uses the believer's sins to undermine his assurance. The devil takes your genuine failures and magnifies them beyond all proportion. He whispers, "Look at what you have done. A true Christian would never sin like this. Your faith must be false." Brooks responds with a crucial distinction: there is a difference between having sin and being ruled by sin. The believer sins, but sin is no longer his master. The presence of sin in the Christian life is not evidence that you are unregenerate; it is evidence that you are still in the body, still fighting the fight of faith. The question is not whether you have sinned but whether you grieve over your sin and flee to Christ for pardon.

The second device is that Satan persuades the believer to compare himself with the strongest Christians and despair. He points to the great heroes of the faith and says, "Your devotion is nothing compared to theirs. You are not fit to call yourself a Christian." Brooks replies that God does not measure faith by its size but by its sincerity. A mustard seed of genuine faith is worth infinitely more than a mountain of false profession. Thomas Watson echoes this truth: the least drop of faith is as precious to God as the ocean of faith possessed by the greatest saint.

The third device is that Satan interprets spiritual dryness as evidence of God's rejection. When the believer feels nothing in prayer, when the Scriptures seem lifeless, when worship is a duty rather than a delight, the devil steps in to explain: "God has abandoned you. He has withdrawn His presence because He is displeased with you." Brooks answers that feelings are not the measure of grace. The sun shines even when clouds obscure it. The love of God for His children does not rise and fall with their emotions. The remedy for spiritual dryness is not despairing self-examination but patient perseverance in the means of grace — prayer, Scripture, fellowship, the Lord's Table — trusting that the season will change because God is faithful.

The fourth device is isolation. Satan knows that a Christian separated from fellowship is a vulnerable Christian. He convinces the discouraged soul that no one will understand, that opening up will bring judgment rather than comfort, that it is better to suffer alone. Brooks counters this by pointing to the purpose of the body of Christ. "Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ" (Galatians 6:2). The discouraged believer who finds a faithful friend and opens his heart will discover that his struggles are not unique and that the prayers of the saints are a powerful weapon against the accusations of the enemy.

--- Application ---

First, learn to distinguish between the conviction of the Holy Spirit and the accusation of the devil. The Spirit convicts to lead you to repentance and restoration. The devil accuses to lead you to despair and paralysis. If your sense of guilt is driving you toward Christ, it is from the Spirit. If it is driving you away from Christ, it is from the enemy.

Second, stop measuring your faith by comparison with others. God has given you a measure of faith suited to your calling and your season. Nurture what you have been given rather than lamenting what you have not.

Third, resist the temptation to isolate. Find a trusted brother or sister in Christ and open your heart. The burdens you carry alone will crush you; the burdens you share will become bearable.

--- Closing ---

Lord Jesus, Thou art our Advocate before the Father. When the accuser brings his charges against us, Thou dost silence him with Thy blood. Teach us to rest not in our own righteousness but in Thine alone. Lift the cloud of false condemnation from every discouraged heart tonight. Amen.

Go in peace, friend. Greater is He that is in you than he that is in the world.
