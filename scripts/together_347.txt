PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 16
"The Spirit Prays for Your Marriage"
Romans for Marriage — Day 16

---

Hello, beloved couple. It is a beautiful thing when husband and wife sit down together in God's Word. The Puritans called marriage a little church within the church. Tonight, your little church is in session. Welcome.

Tonight we turn to Romans 8:26-28. Listen with your hearts, not just your ears:

"Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered. And he that searcheth the hearts knoweth what is the mind of the Spirit, because he maketh intercession for the saints according to the will of God. And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose."

That is Romans 8:26-28. Take a breath. Let those words settle between the two of you. God is speaking to your marriage through this passage.

--- Puritan Commentary ---

John Owen had deep insight into the truths we have just read. The Puritans did not separate theology from daily life, and their reflections on marriage are remarkably practical.

Paul reveals a stunning truth about the Holy Spirit's ministry: He intercedes for us when we do not know how to pray. There are burdens in marriage so heavy that words cannot carry them. There are sorrows so deep that no prayer seems adequate. There are situations so complex that we genuinely do not know what to ask God for. In those moments, the Spirit Himself takes over our prayer life, interceding with groanings too deep for words.

John Owen devoted an entire treatise to the work of the Holy Spirit in prayer. Owen taught that the Spirit's intercession is not a backup plan for when our prayers fail — it is the hidden engine behind every true prayer. Every genuine cry from the heart of a believer is prompted, shaped, and carried to the Father by the Holy Spirit. We never pray alone.

For marriage, this truth is immensely comforting. There are seasons when you do not know what to pray for your marriage. The problems feel too tangled. The pain feels too deep. The future feels too uncertain. You open your mouth and nothing adequate comes out. Romans 8:26 says: that is exactly when the Spirit steps in.

Owen observed that the Spirit knows the will of God perfectly. When we do not know what is best for our marriage, the Spirit does. He intercedes 'according to the will of God.' This means your marriage is being prayed for perfectly at all times by the third Person of the Trinity. Even when your prayers are confused, the Spirit's prayers are crystal clear.

And then comes the magnificent promise of verse 28: all things work together for good. All things — including the prayers you cannot articulate, the problems you cannot solve, the pain you cannot explain. God is weaving every thread into a tapestry of good for those who love Him. Your marriage is in His hands, interceded for by His Spirit, and governed by His purpose.

--- Marriage Application ---

If you have felt unable to pray for your marriage — because the situation is too complex, too painful, or too confusing — take comfort tonight. The Holy Spirit is praying for you right now. He knows exactly what your marriage needs, and He is interceding with the Father on your behalf.

This should release you from the burden of having to have all the answers. You do not need to know exactly what your marriage needs. You do not need a perfect prayer. You just need to come before God honestly — even with groans and tears — and let the Spirit do His work.

Tonight, instead of trying to formulate the perfect prayer, simply sit before God together in silence. Let the Spirit intercede. Trust that He is working all things — even the things you cannot understand — for your good.

The Puritans understood that marriage is not a destination but a journey. Every day presents new opportunities to love, to serve, to forgive, and to grow together. The truths we have studied tonight are not just for tonight. They are for tomorrow morning when the alarm goes off, for the difficult conversation that has been waiting, for the quiet moments when only God sees how you treat each other.

--- Reflection ---

It is time for reflection. Turn to each other and consider these questions together. There is no rush. Let the conversation go wherever the Spirit leads.

Question 1: Have you ever felt so burdened that you did not know how to pray for your marriage? How does this passage comfort you?

Question 2: How does knowing the Spirit intercedes 'according to the will of God' change your anxiety about the future?

Question 3: Do you truly believe that 'all things work together for good' for your marriage? Where is that belief tested most?

Take a few minutes with each question. Do not rush. The best conversations in marriage happen when both people feel truly heard.

--- Prayer ---

Now let us pray together. The Puritans said that a couple who prays together is bound by cords that cannot be broken. Let us strengthen those cords tonight.

Tonight's prayer focus: Spend time in quiet prayer tonight, trusting the Spirit to intercede on your behalf. You do not need to have the right words. Simply come before God and let the Spirit pray through you with groanings that go deeper than language.

If one of you finds it difficult to pray out loud, that is perfectly fine. Simply holding hands in silence before the Lord is itself a powerful act of worship. Let the Spirit intercede where words fail.

--- Blessing ---

May the Holy Spirit, who intercedes for you with perfect knowledge and perfect love, carry your marriage before the throne of God tonight. May all things — seen and unseen — work together for your good.

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you both, tonight and always. Goodnight.
