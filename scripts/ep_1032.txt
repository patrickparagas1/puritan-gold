PURITAN GOLD -- Study Topic: The Book of Melchizedek, Part 2 of 8
"A Priest Forever"
The Book of Melchizedek — Psalm 110:4 — David's prophetic declaration

---

Dear friends, welcome back to our study of Melchizedek. In our first episode, we encountered this mysterious king-priest in Genesis 14, where he appeared without warning to bless Abraham after battle. Today we leap forward roughly a thousand years in biblical history — from the age of the patriarchs to the age of the monarchy — to find Melchizedek's name invoked once more, this time in one of the most important messianic psalms in all of Scripture.

Let us read Psalm 110 in its entirety:

"The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool. The LORD shall send the rod of thy strength out of Zion: rule thou in the midst of thine enemies. Thy people shall be willing in the day of thy power, in the beauties of holiness from the womb of the morning: thou hast the dew of thy youth. The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek. The LORD at thy right hand shall strike through kings in the day of his wrath. He shall judge among the heathen, he shall fill the places with the dead bodies; he shall wound the heads over many countries. He shall drink of the brook in the way: therefore shall he lift up the head."

--- Teaching ---

Psalm 110 is the most frequently quoted psalm in the New Testament. Jesus Himself cited it to confound the Pharisees (Matthew 22:41-46). Peter quoted it in his Pentecost sermon (Acts 2:34-35). Paul referred to it in his teaching on the exaltation of Christ (1 Corinthians 15:25). And the writer of Hebrews built his entire argument about Christ's priesthood upon its fourth verse. If we would understand the significance of Melchizedek, we must understand Psalm 110.

The psalm opens with an extraordinary declaration: "The LORD said unto my Lord." In Hebrew, this reads: "Yahweh said unto Adonai." David, the king of Israel, describes a conversation within the Godhead — Yahweh, the covenant God of Israel, addressing another divine figure whom David calls "my Lord." This is one of the clearest Old Testament intimations of the Trinity. David, the greatest king in Israel's history, acknowledges a Lord who is greater than himself — and this Lord is invited to sit at God's right hand, the position of supreme honor and authority.

Jesus used this very verse to demonstrate that the Messiah must be more than a mere human descendant of David. "If David then call him Lord, how is he his son?" (Matthew 22:45). The Pharisees could not answer, for they had not reckoned with the possibility that the Messiah would be both David's son according to the flesh and David's Lord according to His divine nature. Psalm 110 demands a Messiah who is both human and divine — and that Messiah is Jesus Christ.

But it is verse 4 that concerns us most directly in this series: "The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek."

Consider the weight of this declaration. It comes with a divine oath — "The LORD hath sworn." Throughout Scripture, when God swears by Himself, He is giving the highest possible assurance that His word will stand. As the writer of Hebrews explains, "For when God made promise to Abraham, because he could swear by no greater, he sware by himself" (Hebrews 6:13). God does not need to swear — His bare word is sufficient — but He condescends to the weakness of human faith by adding an oath. When God swears, the matter is settled beyond all doubt or revision.

And what has God sworn? That the Messiah — David's Lord, the one seated at God's right hand — is "a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek." This is a stunning declaration, and its implications are immense.

First, it establishes that the Messiah will be a priest. The kings of Israel were not priests. David, for all his greatness, could not enter the holy place and offer sacrifice. That was the exclusive domain of the Levitical priests, the sons of Aaron. Yet Psalm 110 declares that the Messiah — who is explicitly identified as a king in verse 1 (sitting at God's right hand, ruling over his enemies) — will also be a priest. He will hold both offices simultaneously, just as Melchizedek did.

Second, this priesthood is "for ever." The Levitical priests served for a limited time. They grew old. They died. They were replaced by their sons. The high priest served until his death, and then another took his place. But the priesthood of the Messiah has no expiration date, no sunset clause, no successor. It is eternal.

Third, this priesthood is "after the order of Melchizedek." It is not after the order of Aaron. It is not Levitical. It belongs to a different category altogether — a category established a thousand years before Moses, before the law was given, before the tabernacle was built, before the first Levitical sacrifice was offered. The order of Melchizedek is older, greater, and more enduring than the order of Aaron.

John Owen, in his commentary on Hebrews, devoted dozens of pages to unpacking the significance of this verse. Owen argued that the divine oath in Psalm 110:4 was "the great foundation of the apostle's argument" in Hebrews 7. If God swore with an oath that the Messiah would be a priest after the order of Melchizedek, then the Levitical priesthood was never intended to be permanent. It was a provisional arrangement — a temporary system that pointed forward to something better. The oath of Psalm 110:4, sworn centuries after the establishment of the Levitical priesthood, announced that a new and superior priesthood was coming.

Owen further observed that the oath was not merely a prediction but an appointment. When God said "Thou art a priest for ever," He was not describing a future event in passive terms — He was actively installing the Messiah in His eternal office. The priesthood of Christ is not something He earned or achieved; it is something the Father bestowed upon Him by sovereign decree and irrevocable oath.

Thomas Goodwin, in his treatise on the person of Christ, meditated on the significance of the word "forever." The Levitical priests ministered for a season and then passed away. But Christ's priesthood is coextensive with eternity. He is a priest today — at this very moment, seated at the right hand of the Father — and He will be a priest forever. He will never retire. He will never be replaced. He will never grow weary of interceding for His people. "Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them" (Hebrews 7:25).

Stephen Charnock, in his great work on the attributes of God, connected the immutability of God with the immutability of Christ's priesthood. God does not change; therefore His oath does not change; therefore Christ's priesthood does not change. The security of the believer rests not upon the shifting sands of human performance but upon the unchangeable purpose and oath of Almighty God.

What a comfort this is to the troubled conscience! Your High Priest is not a fallible man who might fail in his office. He is the eternal Son of God, appointed by an irrevocable divine oath, who holds His priesthood by virtue of an indestructible life. He cannot fail. He will not fail. And because He lives and intercedes, all who come to God through Him will be saved — completely, perfectly, and forever.

--- Application ---

1. Ground your assurance in the oath of God. Your salvation does not depend upon the strength of your faith but upon the faithfulness of God's promise. He has sworn with an oath that Christ is your eternal priest — and God cannot lie.

2. Worship Christ as both King and Priest. He rules your life with sovereign authority, and He intercedes for your soul with tender mercy. Both offices are exercised simultaneously and eternally on your behalf.

3. Understand that the Old Testament law was preparatory, not final. The Levitical system was glorious in its time, but it was always pointing forward to something greater. Do not cling to shadows when the substance has come.

4. Take comfort in the permanence of Christ's intercession. He ever liveth to make intercession for you. When you fall into sin, when your prayers feel cold, when your faith wavers — your High Priest does not waver. He continues to plead your cause before the Father.

--- Closing ---

O Lord, who hast sworn and wilt not repent — we praise Thee for the eternal priesthood of Thy Son. What comfort it brings to know that our great High Priest is not appointed by human decree but by divine oath, not for a season but forever, not after the order of Aaron but after the order of Melchizedek. Strengthen our faith in this unshakeable promise, and draw us ever nearer to the throne of grace where Christ our Priest continually intercedes. In His name we pray. Amen.

Go in peace, beloved. Your High Priest stands before the Father on your behalf, this day and forevermore. Grace be with you all.
