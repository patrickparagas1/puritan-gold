PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 16
"The Beauty of God and the Joy of the Family"
Jonathan Edwards — Religious Affections Applied to the Family

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Jonathan Edwards teaches us that the foundation of all holy affections is the beauty of God — and that a family captivated by God's beauty is a family overflowing with joy. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Beautiful Savior, open the eyes of our hearts tonight to see Your glory. Let Your beauty captivate our family and fill us with a joy that the world cannot give and cannot take away. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Psalm 27:4 and Psalm 34:8. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"One thing have I desired of the LORD, that will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD, and to enquire in his temple. O taste and see that the LORD is good: blessed is the man that trusteth in him."

That is Psalm 27:4 and Psalm 34:8. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

At the heart of Jonathan Edwards's theology is a breathtaking vision of God's beauty. Edwards believed that God is infinitely, perfectly, and overwhelmingly beautiful — not merely in an aesthetic sense, but in a moral and spiritual sense. The beauty of God is the harmony of all His attributes: His holiness and His love, His justice and His mercy, His power and His tenderness, His transcendence and His intimacy. When these attributes are seen together, they form a picture of such stunning beauty that the soul cannot help but be moved to wonder, worship, and delight.

Edwards taught that the true source of all genuine spiritual joy is the perception of divine beauty. When a person truly sees — through the eyes of faith, opened by the Holy Spirit — how glorious and good God is, the response is not dutiful obedience but delighted worship. The heart is drawn to God as naturally as the eye is drawn to a sunset or the ear to a symphony. This is what Edwards meant by "holy affections" — they are the heart's response to the beauty of God.

For families, this teaching has revolutionary implications. If the deepest source of joy is God Himself, then the most joyful family is not the wealthiest, the most successful, or the most entertained. It is the family most captivated by the beauty of God. Edwards would challenge every family to ask: What is the chief source of our joy? Is it our possessions? Our achievements? Our entertainment? Or is it God Himself?

Edwards observed that families often seek joy in the wrong places. They pile up activities, vacations, purchases, and experiences, hoping that the next one will finally satisfy. But worldly pleasures, however legitimate, are like salt water: the more you drink, the thirstier you become. Only God can satisfy the deepest longings of the human heart, because we were made for Him. As Augustine famously prayed, "Thou hast made us for Thyself, and our hearts are restless until they rest in Thee."

How does a family cultivate a sense of God's beauty? Edwards would say it begins with attention. We must slow down enough to look at what God reveals about Himself. In Scripture, we see His character unveiled page by page — His patience with Israel, His tenderness toward the broken, His fury against injustice, His delight in mercy. In creation, we see His artistry — the intricacy of a flower, the vastness of the night sky, the power of a thunderstorm. In the gospel, we see the supreme display of His beauty — the God of infinite glory laying aside His glory to become a servant, suffering the agony of the cross for the joy of redeeming His people.

Parents can cultivate this sense of beauty in their children by pointing out the glory of God wherever they see it. On a walk in the park: "Look at this — God made that." In a moment of forgiveness: "This is what God's mercy looks like." In a season of difficulty: "God is showing us how strong His love is." Over time, children who are taught to see God's beauty in all things develop a deep, abiding joy that does not depend on circumstances.

Edwards also connected beauty and joy to family worship. He believed that worship at its best is a response to perceived beauty. When a family gathers for devotions and truly encounters the beauty of God in His Word, the experience is not drudgery but delight. The key is not to rush through the motions but to linger in God's presence, to meditate on His truth, and to give the Holy Spirit time to open the eyes of the heart.

Tonight, taste and see that the Lord is good. Let your family dwell on the beauty of God, and let that beauty fill your home with an unspeakable joy.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Edwards said that the deepest source of joy is the beauty of God. What is one attribute of God that you find especially beautiful or wonderful?

Question 2: How can our family learn to look for God's beauty in everyday life — in nature, in Scripture, in our relationships with one another?

Question 3: The psalmist said, "One thing have I desired — to behold the beauty of the Lord." If this became our family's one desire, how would our daily life change?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"O taste and see that the LORD is good: blessed is the man that trusteth in him." -- Psalm 34:8

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Glorious God, You are more beautiful than anything we have ever seen or imagined. Open the eyes of our hearts to see Your beauty more clearly. Fill our family with the joy that comes from beholding You. Let our home be a place where Your beauty is known, celebrated, and treasured. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, resting in the beauty and goodness of the God who loves you.
