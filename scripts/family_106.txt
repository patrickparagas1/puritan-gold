PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 6
"The Father's Sacred Calling"
John Angell James — The Family Monitor


Good evening, family. There is no place God loves to meet His people more than when families gather in His name. Welcome to Puritan Gold. Let us open our Bibles and our hearts.

Let us begin in prayer. Lord Jesus, You said that where two or three are gathered in Your name, there You are in the midst of them. We claim that promise tonight as a family. Be here with us. Teach us. Shape us. We pray in Your holy name. Amen.

Every word of Scripture is breathed out by God. Tonight we receive His breath from Genesis 18:19:

"For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do justice and judgment; that the LORD may bring upon Abraham that which he hath spoken of him."

That is Genesis 18:19. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Today we look at the sacred calling of fathers. In Genesis 18:19, God speaks about Abraham and gives us one of the most remarkable statements about fatherhood in the entire Bible: 'For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do justice and judgment.'

Let us consider the context. God is about to judge Sodom and Gomorrah for their wickedness. But before He does, He pauses and says something extraordinary — He decides to share His plans with Abraham. Why? Because God knows Abraham. He knows Abraham's character. And specifically, God knows that Abraham will command his children and his household to keep the way of the Lord.

The word 'command' here is significant. It does not mean a harsh, dictatorial barking of orders. In Hebrew, the word 'tsavah' means to charge, to commission, to give a solemn instruction. Abraham would solemnly charge his household to walk in God's ways. He would take responsibility for the spiritual direction of his family.

Notice what God says this will produce: 'they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do justice and judgment.' When a father faithfully leads his household in God's truth, the fruit is righteousness — children who do what is just and right. And notice the purpose: 'that the LORD may bring upon Abraham that which he hath spoken of him.' God's covenant promises are connected to faithful family leadership.

This is not the only place Scripture speaks of a father's spiritual role. In Psalm 78:5-7, we read, 'For he established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children: That the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to their children: That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments.'

Do you see the chain? Fathers teach their children, who teach their children, who teach their children. Godly fatherhood creates a multi-generational legacy of faith. When one father faithfully teaches his children about God, the impact ripples forward through centuries.

John Angell James wrote with great earnestness about the father's role. He called the father the priest of the household — the one who goes before his family in the worship of God. James said that a house without a family altar, meaning a regular time of family worship led by the father, is like a house without a roof — open to every storm of temptation.

Now James was careful to point out that this does not mean the mother's role is unimportant — far from it, as we will see tomorrow. But he believed that God has given fathers a special charge to lead spiritually. When fathers abdicate this role, the whole family suffers.

James observed that many fathers are diligent in providing for their families materially but neglect their spiritual responsibilities. A father who works hard to put food on the table but never opens the Bible with his children is feeding their bodies while starving their souls. Both kinds of provision are necessary, but the spiritual is more important because it has eternal consequences.

James urged fathers to be regular, consistent, and warm in family worship. He counseled against cold formality — God does not want mechanical reading and rote prayers. He wants a father who genuinely loves God and allows that love to overflow into his leadership of the household.

For fathers hearing this: take courage. You do not have to be a seminary graduate or a gifted speaker. Abraham was a simple herdsman, not a professional clergyman. God simply asks you to be faithful — to open His Word, to pray with your family, and to walk in integrity before your children. For our youngest ones: God gave you a father (or father figure) to help teach you about Him. That is a special gift. For older children: watch for ways you can encourage and support your father in leading your family spiritually.


One of the great blessings of the Christian tradition is that we stand on the shoulders of faithful saints who came before us.

John Angell James, in his work "The Family Monitor," wrote these words:

"The father is the priest of the household. He is to go before his family in the worship of God. The house that has no family altar is a house without a roof — open to every storm of temptation."

What John Angell James is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Genesis 18:19 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Now we get to the best part: talking about God's Word together as a family. Here are tonight's questions.

Question 1: Who did God say would teach his children about the Lord? (Abraham!)

Question 2: Why do you think God chose to tell Abraham His plans? What was special about Abraham as a father?

Question 3: What does it mean to 'command' your household — is it about being bossy, or something deeper?

Question 4: How can we encourage fathers (or father figures) in our lives to lead our families in knowing God?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD." -- Genesis 18:19a

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Here is a practical way to live out tonight's devotional:

Write a thank-you note to your father or a father figure in your life. Thank him for the ways he leads and cares for your family. If the father is present, each family member can share one thing they appreciate about his leadership.


Gracious God, thank You for meeting with our family tonight. Your Word never returns void, and we trust that what we have heard will bear fruit in each of our lives. Strengthen our family bonds, deepen our faith, and make us a light in this world. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

May the peace of God guard your hearts and minds tonight. Goodnight, family.
