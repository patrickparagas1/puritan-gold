PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 3
"The Wicket Gate"
Pilgrim Families — Week 1, Day 3


Welcome, family. Whether you are gathered on the couch, around the dinner table, or riding in the car, God is here with you. Let us turn our attention to Him and see what treasure He has stored up for us tonight.

Let us bow our heads. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family in Your presence. We need Your wisdom tonight. We need Your guidance. Open our ears to hear, our minds to understand, and our hearts to receive. We pray in the precious name of Jesus. Amen.

Let us turn together to John 10:9; Matthew 7:7-8. Follow along in your Bible if you have one:

"I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture. Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you: For every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and to him that knocketh it shall be opened."

That is John 10:9; Matthew 7:7-8. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


After escaping the Slough of Despond, Christian finally arrives at the Wicket Gate. This is a small, narrow gate — and above it are written the words, "Knock, and it shall be opened unto you." Christian knocks, and a man named Good-will opens the gate and pulls Christian inside with great urgency. Why the urgency? Because Beelzebub's castle is nearby, and arrows are being shot at all who approach the gate.

Our Scripture today gives us the key to understanding this scene. Jesus declares in John 10:9: "I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture." Jesus is the Wicket Gate. He is the only door to salvation. There is no other way to God except through His Son. Not through good works, not through religion, not through philosophy — only through Christ.

And in Matthew 7:7-8, Jesus invites us: "Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you: For every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and to him that knocketh it shall be opened." These are three levels of intensity — asking, seeking, knocking. Each one requires more effort than the last. Knocking means standing at the door, pounding with your fist, refusing to leave until it opens. This is earnest, persistent faith.

Christian knocked again and again. He did not knock once and walk away. He did not give up when the gate did not open immediately. He kept knocking. And when it opened, Good-will did not merely invite him in — he grabbed Christian and pulled him through. Bunyan is showing us that coming to Christ requires both our earnest seeking and God's powerful grace. We knock; He pulls us through.

Notice that arrows were flying at Christian as he approached the gate. The enemy does not want anyone to come to Christ. When your family decides to get serious about faith — to read the Bible together, to pray, to turn away from worldly distractions — do not be surprised if opposition comes. The devil fires arrows of doubt, distraction, and discouragement at everyone who approaches the gate.

But the gate is open to all who knock. Jesus said, "Every one that asketh receiveth." Not some. Not the worthy ones. Not the ones who have their lives together. Everyone. This is the glorious invitation of the Gospel. It does not matter how far you have fallen, how long you have wandered, how deep your sin — if you come to Jesus, He will not turn you away. John 6:37 confirms this: "Him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out."

The Wicket Gate also teaches us that salvation is personal. No one can go through the gate for you. Christian's wife and children stayed in the City of Destruction. His neighbor Pliable turned back at the Slough. Each person must enter through the gate for themselves. Parents, you cannot save your children by your own faith alone. You can bring them to the gate, you can teach them about the gate, you can show them the way — but each one must knock for themselves.

This does not mean we are passive. God commands parents to train up their children in the way they should go (Proverbs 22:6). We bring them to the gate every day through family worship, through teaching Scripture, through living out our faith in front of them. And we pray earnestly that the Holy Spirit will open their eyes and draw them through.

The image of the narrow gate also reminds us that the Christian life begins with a definite decision. There is a moment when you step through. You leave the outside and enter in. Salvation is not a vague feeling or a gradual process of becoming generally nicer. It is a crossing over from death to life, from darkness to light, from the broad way to the narrow way.

Tonight, talk together about what it means that Jesus is the only door. In a world that says all paths lead to God, the Bible is uncompromising: "Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved" (Acts 4:12). This is not narrow-mindedness — it is the narrow gate. And it leads to life everlasting.


For centuries, faithful Christians have reflected on passages like this one. Let us hear from a trusted voice of the past.

John Bunyan, in his work "The Pilgrim's Progress," wrote these words:

"I am willing with all my heart, said Christian, and knocked, and knocked again and again."

What John Bunyan is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in John 10:9; Matthew 7:7-8 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Discussion time. These questions are for every member of the family, young and old alike.

Question 1: What does it mean that Jesus is the only door? Why can we not be saved any other way?

Question 2: Christian knocked again and again at the gate. What does persistent knocking look like in our prayer life?

Question 3: Arrows were being shot at Christian as he approached the gate. What kinds of 'arrows' does the enemy shoot at people who are seeking God?

Question 4: Why must each person go through the gate for themselves? How can we help each other in our family come to know Jesus personally?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture." -- John 10:9

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


The Puritans believed that truth must be practiced, not just heard. Here is something you can do together:

Set up a doorway in your home as the 'Wicket Gate.' Have each family member take turns knocking and stepping through. As they step through, have them say one reason they are thankful that Jesus is the door. Talk about what it means to enter through Christ by faith.


Let us bow our heads and pray. Dear Father in heaven, what a privilege it is to gather as a family around Your Word. We ask You to write these truths on our hearts. Bless our family, protect our home, and lead us in the path of righteousness for Your name's sake. In Christ we pray. Amen.

Until tomorrow, family. May the Lord bless you and keep you.
