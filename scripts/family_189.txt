PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 28
"The Eternal Perspective of the Home"
Richard Baxter — The Christian Directory


Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. We close our week with Richard Baxter by stepping back and viewing the Christian home from the perspective of eternity. Baxter was a man who lived with one eye on this world and one eye on the next, and he calls every family to do the same. Let us draw near to God.

Let us pray. Eternal God, we live in time, but You call us to live for eternity. Help us tonight to see our home, our relationships, and our daily duties in the light of Your everlasting kingdom. Give us the long view — the view that transforms everything. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from 2 Corinthians 5:1-5, 10. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"For we know that if our earthly house of this tabernacle were dissolved, we have a building of God, an house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens. For in this we groan, earnestly desiring to be clothed upon with our house which is from heaven: If so be that being clothed we shall not be found naked. For we that are in this tabernacle do groan, being burdened: not for that we would be unclothed, but clothed upon, that mortality might be swallowed up of life. Now he that hath wrought us for the selfsame thing is God, who also hath given unto us the earnest of the Spirit. For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ; that every one may receive the things done in his body, according to that he hath done, whether it be good or bad."

That is 2 Corinthians 5:1-5, 10. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Richard Baxter wrote a book called "The Saints' Everlasting Rest" that became one of the most influential devotional works of the seventeenth century. In it, Baxter meditates at length on the heavenly rest that awaits every believer — and he insists that meditation on heaven is not escapism but the most practical thing a Christian can do. For the person who lives with eternity in view will live differently in the present. Every choice, every relationship, every use of time and energy will be weighed against the standard of eternity.

Baxter brings this eternal perspective directly into his counsel on family life. He writes that parents must raise their children not merely for success in this world but for the world to come. The most important question a parent can ask is not "Will my child be prosperous?" or "Will my child be respected?" but "Will my child be saved?" All other concerns, however legitimate, are subordinate to this one overwhelming question. For what does it profit a family to achieve every earthly ambition and lose its own soul?

Paul reminds us that "we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ." This includes parents. One day, every father and mother will stand before the Lord and give an account of how they stewarded the children God entrusted to them. Did they teach them the truth? Did they model the faith? Did they pray for their souls? Did they make the salvation of their children their highest priority? Baxter writes that this coming accountability should sober every parent and motivate every household to take spiritual responsibilities with the utmost seriousness.

But Baxter's eternal perspective is not merely sobering — it is comforting. He writes that the godly home is a foretaste of heaven. When a family worships together, they are doing on earth what they will do forever in heaven. When a family loves one another with the love of Christ, they are experiencing a preview of the love that will fill eternity. When a family forgives, serves, encourages, and prays for one another, they are practicing the communion of saints that will be perfected in glory.

Baxter also speaks about the reunions that await believing families. One of the sweetest comforts of the faith is the knowledge that death does not separate believers — it only interrupts their fellowship for a time. The family that is united in Christ on earth will be reunited in Christ in heaven. The parent who buries a believing child will see that child again. The child who loses a godly parent will be embraced by that parent once more. And the reunion will be without sorrow, without separation, without end.

This eternal perspective transforms the daily life of the household. The mundane becomes meaningful when it is done for eternity. The difficult becomes bearable when it is seen against the backdrop of everlasting joy. The sacrifices of parenting — the sleepless nights, the patient repetitions, the daily dying to self — become investments in an inheritance that will never fade. Every family devotion, every prayer, every conversation about God is a brick laid in the eternal house that God is building.

As we close this week with Baxter, let us hear his great exhortation: live for heaven. Let eternity shape your priorities, your conversations, your ambitions, and your love. Build a home that is oriented not toward the passing pleasures of this world but toward the everlasting joy of the world to come. For the home that is built on eternal foundations will stand when everything else has passed away.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Baxter says the most important question for a family is not "Will we be successful?" but "Will we be saved?" How does this eternal perspective change our family's priorities?

Question 2: Baxter describes the godly home as a foretaste of heaven. In what ways does our family's life together give us a glimpse of what heaven will be like?

Question 3: If we knew that Christ was returning tomorrow, what would we do differently as a family today? What would stay exactly the same?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ; that every one may receive the things done in his body, according to that he hath done, whether it be good or bad." -- 2 Corinthians 5:10

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord God, we want to be a family that lives for eternity. Help us to invest our time, our love, and our energy in things that will last forever. Keep the hope of heaven burning bright in our hearts, and use that hope to transform the way we live together each day. We long for the day when we will worship You as a family without interruption, without sin, and without end. Until that day, keep us faithful. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
