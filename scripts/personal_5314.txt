PURITAN GOLD -- Personal Growth: Doctrine Deep-Dive, Day 14
"The Perseverance of the Saints"
The Doctrine of Eternal Security

---

Good evening, friend. Tonight we come to one of the most comforting doctrines in all of Christian theology: the perseverance of the saints. Can a true believer lose his salvation? The Puritans answered with one resounding voice: No. And they answered not from wishful thinking but from the deep wells of Scripture and the settled purposes of God. Let us consider this glorious truth together.

--- Scripture ---

Let us ground ourselves in God's Word before we go further. Our passage tonight is John 10:27-29:

"My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me: And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father's hand."

Hold these words in your heart as we continue. They are the foundation upon which everything else tonight is built.

--- Teaching ---

The doctrine of the perseverance of the saints teaches that all whom God has truly saved He will keep saved to the end. Not one of His elect will be lost. Not one of His sheep will finally perish. The Westminster Confession of Faith puts it with precise beauty: "They whom God hath accepted in His Beloved, effectually called and sanctified by His Spirit, can neither totally nor finally fall away from the state of grace; but shall certainly persevere therein to the end, and be eternally saved."

It is important to understand what this doctrine does not teach. It does not teach that everyone who professes faith is secure. Many will say "Lord, Lord" on the last day and be turned away. It does not teach that true believers will never stumble or fall into serious sin. David fell grievously. Peter denied Christ three times. But the doctrine does teach that those whom God has regenerated will never fall away completely or finally. They may stumble, but they will not be utterly cast down, for the Lord upholdeth them with His hand.

John Owen grounded the doctrine of perseverance in the nature of God's covenant. Owen argued that our security rests not on the strength of our grip on God but on the strength of His grip on us. The covenant of grace is not a conditional contract that depends on our performance. It is a sovereign promise secured by the blood of Christ and sealed by the Holy Spirit. Owen writes that if our perseverance depended upon our own faithfulness, every one of us would be lost before the week was out. But it depends upon the faithfulness of God — and God cannot fail.

Thomas Watson illustrated this truth with characteristic warmth. Watson taught that the believer is kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation. He compared the Christian to a child held in his father's arms. The child may squirm and struggle, but the father does not let go. Watson further observed that the security of the believer is woven into the very prayers of Christ. On the night before His death, Jesus prayed: "Holy Father, keep through thine own name those whom thou hast given me" (John 17:11). Are we to suppose that the prayers of the Son of God go unanswered?

The grounds of perseverance are manifold. First, the unchangeable purpose of God in election. Whom He foreknew, He predestinated. Whom He predestinated, He called. Whom He called, He justified. Whom He justified, He glorified. The golden chain of Romans 8 has no broken links. Second, the continuing intercession of Christ. He ever liveth to make intercession for us (Hebrews 7:25). Third, the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, who is given as a deposit, a seal, a guarantee of the inheritance to come (Ephesians 1:13-14). Fourth, the nature of the life that is given in regeneration. It is eternal life — not temporary life, not probationary life, but eternal.

Stephen Charnock reminds us that this doctrine, rightly understood, does not produce carelessness but confidence. The man who knows he is held by the hand of God does not run toward the cliff. He runs toward holiness with renewed strength, knowing that his labor is not in vain. Security produces not complacency but gratitude, and gratitude is the strongest motive for obedience.

--- Application ---

First, examine whether your assurance rests on the right foundation. Do you look to your own performance, your own feelings, your own consistency? Or do you rest upon the unchangeable promises of God and the finished work of Christ? True assurance is anchored not in you but in Him.

Second, if you are a genuine believer struggling with doubt, take heart. Your struggle itself may be evidence of your salvation. The hypocrite does not agonize over whether his faith is real. The fact that you care so deeply about your standing with God suggests that the Spirit is at work in you.

Third, let this doctrine move you to worship. You are held by two hands — the hand of the Son and the hand of the Father — and no power in heaven or earth or hell can pry you free.

--- Closing ---

Almighty Father, we praise Thee that our salvation depends not on our strength but on Thine. Thou hast begun a good work in us, and Thou wilt complete it. Hold us fast when we stumble. Restore us when we fall. And bring us safely home to glory, through Jesus Christ, who holds us in His hand and will never let us go. Amen.

Rest tonight, friend. You are held by an everlasting love.
