PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 15
"Holy Affections in the Home"
Jonathan Edwards — Religious Affections Applied to the Family


Good evening, dear family. Welcome to a new week of Puritan Gold. This week we turn to one of the greatest minds in the history of the church, Jonathan Edwards, and his masterwork on the affections of the heart. We will explore how genuine spiritual emotions should shape our family life. Let us begin with prayer.

Let us pray together. Lord, we do not want a cold, lifeless faith. Nor do we want a faith that is all emotion and no substance. Teach us this week, through the wisdom of Jonathan Edwards, what it means to have hearts that are genuinely moved by the truths of Your Word. Kindle holy affections in our family. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from 1 Peter 1:8. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"Whom having not seen, ye love; in whom, though now ye see him not, yet believing, ye rejoice with joy unspeakable and full of glory."

That is 1 Peter 1:8. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


Jonathan Edwards was the pastor of Northampton, Massachusetts, during the Great Awakening of the 1730s and 1740s — a period of extraordinary spiritual revival that swept through the American colonies. Edwards witnessed remarkable conversions, passionate worship, and powerful displays of spiritual emotion. But he also witnessed excesses, counterfeits, and emotional outbursts that had nothing to do with genuine faith. This experience led him to write one of the most important works in the history of Christian thought: A Treatise Concerning Religious Affections.

Edwards's central argument was this: true religion, in great part, consists in holy affections. By "affections," Edwards meant something more than mere feelings. He meant the deep, settled dispositions of the heart that move the whole person — mind, will, and emotions together — toward God or away from Him. The affections are not superficial sentiments that come and go with the weather. They are the driving forces of the human soul, the springs from which all our actions flow.

For families, this teaching is transformative. Edwards would say that the most important question you can ask about your family's spiritual health is not "Do we attend church regularly?" or "Do we read the Bible?" — though those things matter deeply. The most important question is: "Do we love God? Are our hearts genuinely moved by His beauty, His goodness, His grace?" A family can go through all the motions of religion without ever being truly touched in the depths of the heart. And Edwards would say that such a family, for all its external piety, is spiritually impoverished.

But Edwards was equally clear that not all spiritual emotions are genuine. Some emotions are produced by the flesh rather than the Spirit. Some are the result of excitement, social pressure, or emotional manipulation rather than a genuine encounter with God. Edwards devoted considerable attention to distinguishing true affections from false ones, and his analysis is remarkably practical for families.

One of the marks of true affections, Edwards taught, is that they arise from spiritual understanding. Genuine love for God is not a blind feeling. It is a response to knowing God — understanding His character, His works, and His ways. This means that family devotions are not just about creating warm feelings. They are about deepening understanding. When your family reads the Bible together and discusses what it means, you are cultivating the soil in which holy affections grow. When parents explain the attributes of God to their children — His holiness, His justice, His mercy, His faithfulness — they are planting seeds that will blossom into genuine love for God.

Another mark of true affections is that they produce fruit. Genuine love for God does not remain locked in the heart. It expresses itself in action — in obedience, service, generosity, kindness, and self-sacrifice. A family with true affections is not merely a family that feels warmly about God during devotions. It is a family that lives differently because of their love for God. They serve their neighbors. They show hospitality. They forgive quickly. They speak with grace. Their love for God overflows into love for others.

Tonight, Edwards invites your family to examine the temperature of your hearts. Not to induce guilt, but to kindle fresh desire. Do you love God? Not merely with your lips, but with your lives? Not merely with your heads, but with your hearts? If the fire has grown dim, ask God to rekindle it. And if the fire is burning, ask Him to make it burn brighter still.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Edwards said that true religion consists in "holy affections." What is the difference between genuine spiritual affections and mere emotions?

Question 2: How can our family devotional time cultivate genuine love for God rather than just going through the motions?

Question 3: Edwards taught that true affections produce visible fruit. What fruits of love for God are visible in our family's life? Where would we like to see more?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Whom having not seen, ye love; in whom, though now ye see him not, yet believing, ye rejoice with joy unspeakable and full of glory." -- 1 Peter 1:8

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.


Let us pray together. Lord, kindle holy affections in our family. Do not let our faith become cold or routine. Move our hearts with Your beauty. Fill us with genuine love for You that overflows into every area of our lives. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, loved by the God who created your heart and longs to fill it with Himself.
