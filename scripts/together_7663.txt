From "Religious Affections" by Jonathan Edwards:

PURITAN GOLD -- Religious Affections: Complete Summary A Comprehensive Overview of Jonathan Edwards's Classic Work on True Religion A Treatise Concerning Religious Affections. By Jonathan Edwards. A Complete Summary. Welcome to this summary of Jonathan Edwards's Religious Affections, widely regarded as the greatest work ever written on the nature of true Christian experience. Published in 1746, it was born out of the Great Awakening and addresses the crucial question: how can we distinguish genuine religious experience from mere counterfeits? THE AUTHOR AND HIS PURPOSE. Jonathan Edwards was the greatest theologian and philosopher America has ever produced. He served as pastor in Northampton, Massachusetts, and later as president of the College of New Jersey. The Religious Affections was written to provide a careful, biblical standard for discerning true from false religion — a need made urgent by the excesses and controversies of the Great Awakening. PART ONE: THE NATURE AND IMPORTANCE OF THE AFFECTIONS. Edwards begins by establishing that true religion, in great part, consists in holy affections. The affections are not mere emotions or passing feelings but the vigorous and active exercises of the will — the inclinations and aversions of the soul. Edwards argues from Scripture that love, joy, hope, fear, hatred of sin, and desire for God are central to genuine Christianity. A religion that is merely intellectual, without affecting the heart, is no true religion at all. PART TWO: TWELVE SIGNS THAT ARE NO CERTAIN EVIDENCE. In the longest and most nuanced section, Edwards systematically examines twelve common signs that people take as proof of true grace — and shows that none of them, by themselves, are reliable. These include: intense religious feelings, great religious talk, bodily effects, fluency in speaking of religion, being assured of one's own salvation, quoting Scripture from memory, experiencing great comfort, spending much time in religious activity, and praising God with the mouth. Edwards demonstrates that all of these can be produced by the devil, by natural temperament, or by self-deception, and therefore prove nothing by themselves. PART THREE: TWELVE DISTINGUISHING SIGNS OF TRULY GRACIOUS AFFECTIONS. This is the heart of the book. Edwards provides twelve positive marks that together distinguish genuine grace from all counterfeits. True spiritual affections arise from the supernatural work of the Holy Spirit. They are founded on the loveliness of God's moral perfections, not on self-interest. They are accompanied by spiritual understanding, evangelical humiliation, a changed nature, Christian tenderness, spiritual symmetry and proportion, a longing for more holiness, and above all, they issue in the practice of Christianity in everyday life. LASTING SIGNIFICANCE. Religious Affections remains the most penetrating analysis of Christian experience ever written. Edwards refuses to make the test either too easy or too hard. He honors genuine emotion while ruthlessly exposing counterfeits. The book is indispensable for pastors, counselors, and anyone who wants to know whether their faith is real.
