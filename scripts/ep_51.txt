PURITAN GOLD -- Study Devotional, Day 20
Series: Romans
"How Shall They Hear?"
The necessity of preaching and Israel's rejection of the message

---

Welcome. The practice of sustained Bible study is one of the greatest gifts a Christian can give themselves. Tonight we continue to build our understanding, one passage at a time, guided by the Holy Spirit and the wisdom of the Puritans.

We are studying the book of Romans together. Tonight's lesson is titled "How Shall They Hear?." Each session builds on what came before, so if you have been following along, you will see how tonight's passage connects to the larger argument of the book.

--- Scripture Reading ---

Let us read our passage for tonight. It comes from Romans 10:14-21:

"How then shall they call on him in whom they have not believed? and how shall they believe in him of whom they have not heard? and how shall they hear without a preacher? And how shall they preach, except they be sent? as it is written, How beautiful are the feet of them that preach the gospel of peace, and bring glad tidings of good things! But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Esaias saith, Lord, who hath believed our report? So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God... But I say, Did not Israel know? First Moses saith, I will provoke you to jealousy by them that are no people, and by a foolish nation I will anger you. But Esaias is very bold, and saith, I was found of them that sought me not; I was made manifest unto them that asked not after me. But to Israel he saith, All day long I have stretched forth my hands unto a disobedient and gainsaying people."

Read that passage again to yourself. Mark any words or phrases that stand out. The Puritans read with a pen in hand, always ready to note what God was showing them.

--- Key Verse ---

One verse in particular demands our attention tonight. Let us read it carefully.

"So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God." -- Romans 10:17

This verse is the lens through which we will examine the rest of tonight's passage. Keep it in the front of your mind as we proceed.

--- Exposition ---

Let us now exposit this passage carefully, as the Puritans would have done.

Robert Haldane offers valuable commentary on this passage. The Puritans were expositors of the highest order, and their insights remain remarkably fresh.

Robert Haldane regarded Romans 10:14-21 as the great missionary text of the Bible and argued that it establishes the divine order of gospel proclamation with perfect logical precision. The chain runs backward from the goal to the source: calling upon God requires believing; believing requires hearing; hearing requires preaching; preaching requires sending. At every link, God is the ultimate agent. He sends preachers, provides the word, enables hearing, creates faith, and receives the prayer of the believing sinner.

Haldane gave special emphasis to verse 17: 'So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.' He argued that this verse demolishes every mystical approach to faith that bypasses the Scriptures. Faith does not come by inner light, emotional experience, or unmediated revelation. It comes by hearing — specifically, hearing the word of God. This is why preaching is not optional but essential to God's plan. The Spirit works through the word; the word is proclaimed through preaching; preaching requires preachers; preachers must be sent. The entire missionary enterprise of the church rests on this chain.

Haldane was deeply moved by verse 21, which quotes Isaiah 65:2: 'All day long I have stretched forth my hands unto a disobedient and gainsaying people.' He saw in this image the patient, persistent, grief-filled love of God toward rebellious Israel. God did not reject Israel in haste. He stretched out His hands all day long — pleading, inviting, beseeching. Israel's rejection was not God's failure but Israel's stubbornness. Haldane urged missionaries and evangelists to take heart from this: even when the gospel is rejected, the preacher has done his duty, and God is glorified in the faithful proclamation of truth whether men receive it or refuse it.

Romans 10:14-21 establishes the theological foundation for Christian missions and addresses Israel's culpability for rejecting the gospel. It contains one of the most logical chains of reasoning in all of Paul's letters.

Verses 14-15 present a chain of four questions, each building on the previous one, working backward from the desired result to the necessary starting point. 'How then shall they call on him in whom they have not believed?' You cannot genuinely call upon a God you do not believe in. 'And how shall they believe in him of whom they have not heard?' Belief requires information — you must hear the message before you can respond to it. 'And how shall they hear without a preacher?' The message must be proclaimed by a human messenger. 'And how shall they preach, except they be sent?' Preachers do not arise spontaneously; they are commissioned and sent.

This chain establishes several critical truths. First, salvation is not automatic or universal. People must hear the gospel in order to believe. Those who have never heard cannot believe, and those who do not believe cannot be saved. This creates an urgency for missions that no doctrine of election can diminish. The means God has ordained for bringing His elect to faith is the proclamation of the gospel through human preachers.

Second, preaching is God's ordained method of bringing people to faith. In an age of social media, podcasts, books, and digital communication, the fundamental principle remains: the gospel must be verbally proclaimed. The Greek word for 'preacher' is kerux — a herald, one who announces the king's decree. The preacher does not offer opinions; he announces realities. He declares what God has done in Christ.

Third, preachers must be sent. This implies both divine calling and church commissioning. The beautiful feet of verse 15 (quoting Isaiah 52:7) belong to those who bring good news. In the ancient world, a messenger running toward a besieged city with news of victory was the most welcome sight imaginable. His feet were beautiful because of the message they carried. The gospel preacher carries the best news the world has ever heard: peace with God through Jesus Christ.

Verse 16 introduces a sobering reality: 'But they have not all obeyed the gospel.' Not everyone who hears responds with faith. Isaiah anticipated this: 'Lord, who hath believed our report?' (Isaiah 53:1). The gospel demands obedience — not the obedience of works-righteousness but the obedience of faith (Romans 1:5; 16:26). To reject the gospel is an act of disobedience against God.

Verse 17 draws the theological conclusion: 'So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.' This is one of the most foundational verses in all of theology. Faith is not self-generated. It does not arise from within the human heart spontaneously. It comes from the outside — through the word of God entering the ear and, by the Spirit's power, penetrating the heart. This is why the church must preach. This is why missionaries must be sent. This is why Bibles must be translated. This is why Sunday sermons matter. Faith comes by hearing the word of God.

Verses 18-21 address Israel's specific situation. Verse 18 asks whether Israel's failure was due to not hearing. 'But I say, Have they not heard? Yes verily, their sound went into all the earth, and their words unto the ends of the world.' Paul quotes Psalm 19:4, applying the universal reach of general revelation to the universal reach of the gospel proclamation. Israel heard.

Verse 19 asks whether Israel's failure was due to not understanding. 'But I say, Did not Israel know?' The answer, quoting Moses in Deuteronomy 32:21, is that God will provoke Israel to jealousy by saving Gentiles — 'them that are no people,' 'a foolish nation.' If a foolish pagan nation can understand and receive the gospel, Israel's failure to understand is inexcusable.

Verses 20-21 present the stunning contrast using Isaiah. God was 'found of them that sought me not' and 'made manifest unto them that asked not after me' — this is the Gentile experience. The Gentiles were not looking for the God of Israel, yet God revealed Himself to them. But to Israel, God says, 'All day long I have stretched forth my hands unto a disobedient and gainsaying people.' The imagery is heartbreaking: God with outstretched arms, all day long, pleading with His people to return — and they stubbornly, rebelliously refuse.

This passage holds together two truths that some find difficult to reconcile. God is sovereign in salvation (chapter 9), yet human beings are genuinely responsible for their response to the gospel (chapter 10). Israel was not rejected because God arbitrarily chose to exclude them. Israel was rejected because they rejected the gospel — they were 'disobedient and gainsaying.' Both God's sovereignty and human responsibility are fully real, and both must be affirmed.

--- Application ---

What does this truth look like in the ordinary moments of your life? At work, at home, in your relationships? Let us make it concrete.

Romans 10:14-21 has direct implications for how you live as a follower of Christ.

First, take the Great Commission personally. The chain of verses 14-15 means that people around you cannot believe unless they hear, and they cannot hear unless someone tells them. You are that someone. Not just pastors, not just missionaries, but every Christian is called to proclaim the gospel. Who in your circle of influence has never clearly heard the gospel? Make a plan to share it with them this month.

Second, support the sending of preachers. 'How shall they preach, except they be sent?' Sending involves praying, giving, and going. Pray for missionaries and church planters by name. Give sacrificially to missions. Consider whether God might be calling you to go. The unreached peoples of the world are still waiting for someone with beautiful feet to bring them good news.

Third, saturate yourself in the word of God. If faith comes by hearing and hearing by the word of God, then the word must occupy the central place in your life. Read it daily. Listen to it preached. Meditate on it throughout the day. Memorize it. Discuss it with other believers. The word is the seed from which faith grows. Starve yourself of the word and your faith will wither.

Fourth, do not be discouraged by rejection. 'They have not all obeyed the gospel.' Even Isaiah experienced rejection. Even God Himself stretches out His hands to a disobedient people all day long. If the gospel is sometimes rejected, that does not mean the messenger has failed. Your job is to proclaim; God's job is to save. Be faithful to speak and leave the results to the sovereign Lord.

Write down one concrete action you will take this week in response to tonight's study. Do not let this truth remain theoretical. The Puritans measured the success of a sermon not by how much the hearer learned but by how much the hearer changed.

--- Closing ---

Lord of the harvest, we hear Your call to go, to preach, to send. We thank You that faith comes by hearing and hearing by Your word. Raise up preachers and send them to every nation, tribe, and tongue. Give us beautiful feet — eager to carry the gospel of peace. Burden us for those who have never heard. And give us courage in the face of rejection, knowing that You stretched out Your hands all day long to a disobedient people and did not grow weary. May Your word run and be glorified throughout all the earth. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Goodnight. Remember, the goal of study is not information but transformation.
