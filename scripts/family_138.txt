PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 7
"The Valley of the Shadow of Death"
Pilgrim Families — Week 1, Day 7


Welcome, dear family. Another day has passed, and God has been faithful through it all. Now let us close this day together by sitting at the feet of Jesus and hearing His Word.

Let us pray. Almighty God, You are the giver of every good gift, and one of the greatest gifts You have given us is this family. We dedicate this time to You. Help us to listen well and to grow in wisdom together. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

Our passage tonight is Psalm 23:4; Isaiah 41:10. Listen with expectant hearts:

"Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me. Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness."

That is Psalm 23:4; Isaiah 41:10. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


The Valley of the Shadow of Death is perhaps the most terrifying passage in The Pilgrim's Progress. Christian must walk through utter darkness. On one side is a deep ditch — the ditch into which the blind have led the blind in all ages. On the other side is a dangerous quagmire — a swamp with no bottom. The path between them is extremely narrow. Fiends whisper blasphemous thoughts into Christian's ears, and he cannot tell whether they are his own thoughts or the enemy's. It is the darkest, loneliest stretch of the entire pilgrimage.

But our Scripture tonight transforms the darkness with light. Psalm 23:4 declares: "Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me." And God Himself speaks in Isaiah 41:10: "Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness."

Notice three things David says in Psalm 23:4. First, "I walk through." The valley is not a permanent address. It is a passage. You walk through it. Whatever darkness your family is going through right now — grief, illness, confusion, spiritual attack — it is a valley you are walking through, not a place where you will stay forever.

Second, "I will fear no evil." This does not mean there is no evil present. The valley is full of evil — ditches, quagmires, fiends. But David refuses to fear because of the third thing: "for thou art with me." God's presence changes everything. The darkness does not disappear, but the one who walks beside us is greater than anything in the darkness.

Isaiah 41:10 is one of the most comforting verses in all of Scripture. God gives us four promises in rapid succession: I am with thee. I will strengthen thee. I will help thee. I will uphold thee. Each promise builds upon the last. God is not distant when we suffer. He is present, He is strengthening, He is helping, He is holding us up with His own righteous hand.

In Bunyan's story, Christian discovers something crucial in the Valley of the Shadow. His sword — the Word of God — is not enough by itself for this particular battle. The darkness is so thick that he cannot see to use it. So he takes up another weapon: All-prayer. When you cannot see the way forward, when the darkness presses in from every side, when the enemy whispers lies and doubts into your mind — pray. Just pray.

Christian also heard a voice ahead of him in the darkness quoting Psalm 23: "Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil; for thou art with me." It was another pilgrim, Faithful, walking the same dark path. Christian could not see him, but he could hear him clinging to God's Word. How encouraging it was to know he was not alone! In our darkest hours, the testimony of other believers who have walked this path before us is a lifeline.

Families, every family will walk through dark valleys. Some of you may be in one right now. A parent battling illness. A child struggling with anxiety or fear. A family facing an uncertain future. The valley is real, and it is dark. But God is with you in it.

The Puritans knew suffering intimately. Bunyan himself spent twelve years in prison, separated from his wife and children — including a blind daughter he loved dearly. He knew the Valley of the Shadow from personal experience. Yet he wrote with unshakable confidence that the pilgrim makes it through. The valley does not end in death — it ends in daylight. Christian emerged from the darkness to find the sun rising and a new companion, Faithful, walking beside him.

Your family's dark valley will end in daylight too. Not because the darkness is not real, but because the God who walks with you is stronger than any shadow. His rod and His staff comfort you. He will strengthen you. He will help you. He will uphold you. Fear thou not.


Let us sit at the feet of a Puritan teacher and learn something that will deepen our understanding.

John Bunyan, in his work "The Pilgrim's Progress," wrote these words:

"About the midst of this valley, I perceived the mouth of hell to be, and it stood also hard by the wayside. Now, thought Christian, what shall I do? And ever and anon the flame and smoke would come out in such abundance, with sparks and hideous noises, that he was forced to put up his sword, and betake himself to another weapon, called All-prayer."

What John Bunyan is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Psalm 23:4; Isaiah 41:10 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Let us discuss what we have heard. Parents, encourage even the youngest to share what they are thinking.

Question 1: What is the difference between a shadow and the real thing? Why is death called a 'shadow' for Christians?

Question 2: Christian used 'All-prayer' when he could not use his sword. Why is prayer so important in our darkest moments?

Question 3: Has our family ever gone through a 'dark valley'? How did God help us through it?

Question 4: Isaiah 41:10 gives four promises. Which one do you need most right now?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me." -- Psalm 23:4

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


Before we close, here is a family challenge for the days ahead:

Turn off all the lights in a room. Sit together in the darkness for one full minute in silence. Then have one person turn on a flashlight and read Psalm 23:4 aloud. Talk about how even a small light changes everything in the darkness — and God's presence is infinitely brighter than any flashlight.


Heavenly Father, we end where we began, in gratitude. Thank You for Your Word, for this family, and for the hope we have in Christ. May tonight's lesson from Psalm 23:4; Isaiah 41:10 shape the way we live, the way we speak, and the way we love one another. Amen and amen.

Until next time, dear family. The Lord goes before you. Walk with Him.
