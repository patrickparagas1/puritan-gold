PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 2
"The Slough of Despond"
Pilgrim Families — Week 1, Day 2


Hello, dear ones. How wonderful it is to sit together as a family and open the Scriptures. Welcome to Puritan Gold's family devotional. Let us begin with thankful hearts.

Let us pray together. Lord God, we come before You as a family, grateful for Your love and Your provision. We ask that You would quiet our minds and soften our hearts as we hear from You tonight. Help us to understand, to remember, and to obey what You teach us. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Psalm 40:1-3; Psalm 130:1-4. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry. He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings. And he hath put a new song in my mouth, even praise unto our God: many shall see it, and fear, and shall trust in the LORD. Out of the depths have I cried unto thee, O LORD. Lord, hear my voice: let thine ears be attentive to the voice of my supplications. If thou, LORD, shouldest mark iniquities, O Lord, who shall stand? But there is forgiveness with thee, that thou mayest be feared."

That is Psalm 40:1-3; Psalm 130:1-4. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.


In The Pilgrim's Progress, shortly after Christian begins his journey toward the Celestial City, he falls into a terrible swamp called the Slough of Despond. He sinks into the muck under the weight of his burden and cannot pull himself out. His companion, a man called Pliable, manages to scramble out on the side closest to the City of Destruction — and goes back home. But Christian, despite his despair, keeps struggling toward the far side, the side that leads to the Wicket Gate.

Our Scripture today speaks directly to this experience. In Psalm 40:1-3, David writes: "I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry. He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings." What a beautiful picture! David knew what it was like to be stuck in the mire — overwhelmed by sin, by fear, by despair. But he also knew that God is the one who lifts us out.

The Psalmist in Psalm 130 cries from the same depths: "Out of the depths have I cried unto thee, O LORD." This is the cry of a soul that feels utterly stuck. Perhaps you have felt this way. Perhaps your family has gone through a season where everything seemed dark, where problems piled up, where you felt like you were sinking. The Slough of Despond is not just a fictional swamp — it is a real experience that every Christian faces at some point.

Bunyan explains that the Slough of Despond is made up of all the fears, doubts, and discouragements that come when a person first begins to see their sin. When the Holy Spirit convicts us, we do not immediately feel joyful. We often feel terrible. We see how far we have fallen. We see how great our sin is. And like quicksand, these feelings of guilt and shame can drag us down.

But notice something crucial: there are steps through the Slough. Bunyan tells us that by the King's command, solid steps have been placed through the mire — they are the promises of God's Word. The problem is that people do not see them because they are so focused on their despair. A character named Help comes alongside Christian, reaches down his hand, and pulls him out.

This is what the Psalm teaches us. God hears our cry from the depths. He reaches down. He pulls us from the miry clay. He sets our feet upon a rock. And then — He puts a new song in our mouth. The movement is from despair to praise, from sinking to standing, from crying to singing.

Psalm 130:3-4 gives us the theological foundation for this rescue: "If thou, LORD, shouldest mark iniquities, O Lord, who shall stand? But there is forgiveness with thee, that thou mayest be feared." If God kept a record of all our sins, none of us could stand before Him. Not one. But there is forgiveness with God. This forgiveness is not cheap — it cost the blood of His Son. But it is real, it is free, and it is offered to every sinking soul who cries out.

Families, we all go through seasons of discouragement. Children feel it when they fail at school, when friendships fall apart, when they struggle with sin they cannot seem to overcome. Parents feel it when finances are tight, when health fails, when marriage is hard. The Slough of Despond is a familiar place.

But we do not have to stay there. God has placed stepping stones through every swamp — His promises. "I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee" (Hebrews 13:5). "The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart" (Psalm 34:18). "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28). These promises are solid ground beneath our feet.

And when we cannot find the steps ourselves, God sends help. He sends His Word, His Spirit, and His people. Like the character Help who pulled Christian out, God places people in our lives to lift us up when we are sinking. Be that person for someone else. And do not be ashamed to ask for help when you are the one who is stuck.


The Puritans were men and women who treasured God's Word above all else. Let us hear from one of them now.

John Bunyan, in his work "The Pilgrim's Progress," wrote these words:

"This miry slough is such a place as cannot be mended; it is the descent whither the scum and filth that attends conviction for sin doth continually run, and therefore it is called the Slough of Despond."

What John Bunyan is reminding us is that the truths we have read tonight in Psalm 40:1-3; Psalm 130:1-4 are not new. They have been treasured by faithful Christians for centuries. And they are just as true and just as needed in our family today as they were hundreds of years ago. Let us take these words to heart and let them shape the way we live together as a family.


Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Have you ever felt stuck in sadness or discouragement — like you were sinking in a swamp? What happened?

Question 2: Why do you think Pliable went back to the City of Destruction instead of pushing through?

Question 3: What are some of God's promises that can be 'stepping stones' when we feel discouraged?

Question 4: How can our family be like the character Help — lifting each other up when someone is struggling?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.


Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings." -- Psalm 40:2

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory. Tomorrow morning, try again. The more we hide God's Word in our hearts, the more it will guide us when we need it most.


To make tonight's lesson come alive, try this activity together as a family:

Write three of God's promises on separate stones (or pieces of paper shaped like stepping stones). Place them on the floor and have family members step from one to the next while reading each promise aloud. Talk about how God's promises are solid ground even when everything else feels like sinking mud.


Let us pray together. Lord God, we thank You for this time as a family. Thank You for speaking to us through Your Word. Plant these truths deep in our hearts, especially in the hearts of our children. Help us to walk in wisdom tomorrow and every day. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
