PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 18
"The Perseverance of the Saints"
Theological Day — The Security of the Believer

---

Welcome. Today is a Theological day. We examine the doctrine of the perseverance of the saints — the glorious truth that those whom God has truly saved will be kept by His power unto final salvation.

Let us read John 10:27-29 (KJV):

"My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me: And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father's hand."

--- Teaching ---

The doctrine of perseverance is the final link in the golden chain of salvation described in Romans 8:29-30: foreknowledge, predestination, calling, justification, glorification. Notice that the chain has no broken links. Everyone who is foreknown is predestined. Everyone who is predestined is called. Everyone who is called is justified. And everyone who is justified is glorified. There is no dropout rate. None whom God has chosen will be lost.

The Puritans held this doctrine with both theological rigor and pastoral tenderness. John Owen argued that perseverance is grounded not in the strength of the believer's faith but in the faithfulness of God's covenant. If perseverance depended on us — on the quality of our faith, the consistency of our obedience, the depth of our repentance — then no one could be sure of salvation. But because perseverance depends on God — on His eternal purpose, His covenant promise, His sustaining grace — it is as certain as God Himself.

The Westminster Confession states: "They whom God hath accepted in His Beloved, effectually called and sanctified by His Spirit, can neither totally nor finally fall away from the state of grace; but shall certainly persevere therein to the end, and be eternally saved." Notice the careful language: they can neither totally nor finally fall away. The Puritans acknowledged that believers may fall into serious sin. They may go through seasons of backsliding, doubt, and spiritual coldness. David fell into adultery. Peter denied Christ. But they did not fall away totally or finally. They were restored. They returned. The same grace that saved them preserved them.

Thomas Watson distinguished between the perseverance of the saints and the presumption of the careless. Some people claim the doctrine of perseverance as a license for sin: "I am saved, so it does not matter how I live." Watson responded that such persons give no evidence of being among the elect at all. The genuinely elect person is marked by a pattern of growth, repentance, and obedience — not perfection, but direction. The one who claims security while living in unrepentant sin has no biblical basis for that claim.

The grounds of perseverance, as the Puritans identified them, are multiple. First, the eternal purpose of God. God chose His people before the foundation of the world, and His purposes cannot be thwarted. Second, the covenant of grace. God has bound Himself by promise and oath to save His people, and He does not break His word. Third, the intercession of Christ. Jesus lives forever to make intercession for those who come to God through Him. He prayed for Peter that his faith would not fail, and that prayer was answered. He prays the same prayer for every believer. Fourth, the indwelling of the Holy Spirit. The Spirit who regenerated you continues to sustain, convict, guide, and preserve you. He is the seal and guarantee of your inheritance. Fifth, the nature of saving faith itself. True faith — implanted by the Spirit, rooted in Christ, nourished by the Word — is an enduring faith. It may waver. It may weaken. But it will not die.

The pastoral effect of this doctrine is immense comfort. If you are in Christ — if you have been born again by the Spirit, if you trust in Jesus alone for salvation, if you are walking, however imperfectly, in the direction of holiness — then you are secure. Not because of the quality of your faith but because of the character of your God. You shall never perish. No one can pluck you from His hand.

--- Closing ---

You are held. By a God who does not let go. By a Savior who intercedes for you. By a Spirit who seals you. Rest in that security today. Lord, I thank You that my salvation does not depend on my grip on You but on Your grip on me. When I stumble, hold me. When I wander, bring me back. And bring me safely home at last. Amen.
