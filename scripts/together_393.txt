PURITAN GOLD -- Together: Couples Devotional, Day 1
"The Great Mystery Unveiled"
Ephesians 5:22-24 — Wives and the Church


Welcome, dear couple. We begin a new month together in the Word of God, and what a month it will be. For the next thirty days, we will dwell in one of the richest, most beautiful, and most challenging passages in all of Scripture: Ephesians chapter 5 and its vision of marriage as a reflection of Christ and the Church. The Puritans treasured this passage above almost any other when it came to marriage, and we will draw deeply from their wells.

Let us begin with our Scripture for tonight. Listen carefully — both of you — with open hearts:

"Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord. For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body. Therefore as the church is subject unto Christ, so let the wives be to their own husbands in every thing."

That is Ephesians 5:22-24. Before we go any further, let us take a breath and acknowledge that this is a passage that has been greatly misunderstood and greatly misused. The Puritans were far more careful and far more tender with these words than many who have quoted them since.


The Puritan minister William Gouge, in his masterwork on household duties, insisted that this passage must never be read apart from the verse that precedes it: "Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God" (Ephesians 5:21). The context is mutual submission. The entire passage is addressed to a community of believers who are filled with the Spirit and who relate to one another in humility and love.

Paul's instruction to wives is not a command to become doormats or to abandon their God-given wisdom and conscience. The Puritans were emphatic on this point. Gouge wrote that a wife's submission is voluntary, intelligent, and offered "as unto the Lord" — meaning it is an act of worship, not an act of servitude. A wife who submits to her husband does so because she recognizes in the marriage relationship a picture of something far greater: the Church's willing, joyful devotion to Christ.

Notice the comparison Paul makes: "as Christ is the head of the church." This is not a comparison to a tyrant and his subjects, or a master and his slave. Christ is the head of the Church as its Saviour, its Protector, its Provider, its Lover. The headship of Christ is defined by sacrifice, not domination. And therefore the submission Paul describes is a response to love, not a capitulation to force.

Richard Baxter, the great Puritan pastor, wrote that a wife's respect for her husband should flow from the same spring as her love for Christ — freely, gladly, and with deep trust that God's design for marriage is good. When a wife sees her husband striving to love her as Christ loves the Church, her respect and willing partnership become natural, even joyful. But Baxter was also honest: when a husband fails to love sacrificially, the wife's calling remains, though it becomes harder — and in those moments, her submission is offered directly to the Lord, who sees and who will vindicate.

The Puritans understood that this passage is not primarily about authority structures. It is about the Gospel. Marriage is meant to be a living sermon, a visible parable of the invisible relationship between Christ and His people. When a wife willingly honors her husband, and when a husband sacrificially loves his wife, the watching world catches a glimpse of the greatest love story ever told.

Tonight, as you sit together, let this truth settle deep: your marriage is not merely a human arrangement. It is a sacred picture of divine love. Every act of kindness, every word of encouragement, every choice to honor one another — these are brushstrokes on a canvas that the whole world needs to see.

The Puritans would remind you that this vision of marriage is both a high calling and a daily practice. It is not achieved in a single dramatic gesture but in a thousand small acts of faithfulness. The wife who speaks well of her husband when he is not present, the husband who notices his wife's weariness and serves without being asked — these are the daily rhythms that paint the Gospel on the canvas of ordinary life.


Take a few moments now to turn to each other and consider these questions together:

Question 1: How do you understand the word "submission" in light of what we have read tonight? Has your understanding changed or deepened?

Question 2: In what ways does your marriage currently reflect the relationship between Christ and the Church? In what ways would you like it to grow?

Question 3: What is one practical step each of you can take this week to honor each other in the spirit of Ephesians 5?


Tonight, pray together that God would give you both a fresh vision of your marriage as a picture of Christ and the Church. Ask Him to remove any distortions or wounds that have been attached to this passage, and to replace them with the beauty of His original design. Pray for grace to live out your respective callings with joy, humility, and deep love.


May the Lord who designed marriage fill your hearts with wonder at the mystery He has entrusted to you. May your love for one another grow deeper with every passing day, and may the world see Christ in your home. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you both. Goodnight.
