From "The Attributes of God" by Arthur Pink:

PURITAN GOLD -- The Attributes of God: Complete Summary A Comprehensive Overview of Arthur W. Pink's Classic Work on the Divine Character The Attributes of God. By Arthur W. Pink. A Complete Summary. Welcome to this summary of Arthur W. Pink's The Attributes of God, one of the most beloved works of twentieth-century Reformed theology. In nineteen chapters, Pink explores the perfections of the divine character as revealed in Holy Scripture, calling readers to a deeper knowledge of God that transforms the mind and inflames the heart. Pink begins with The Solitariness of God, showing that before creation existed, God was alone, self-sufficient, needing nothing outside Himself. In The Decrees of God, he establishes that everything that comes to pass was foreordained by God from all eternity. The Knowledge of God and The Foreknowledge of God distinguish between God's comprehensive knowledge of all things and His particular foreknowledge that is grounded in His sovereign purpose. The Supremacy of God demonstrates that God occupies the highest place in the universe, and The Sovereignty of God shows that He exercises His supremacy over all His creatures without exception. In The Immutability of God, Pink reveals that God is unchanging in His being, His attributes, His counsels, and His promises. The Holiness of God sets forth the absolute purity and moral perfection of the divine nature. The Power of God declares that nothing is impossible with God, and that He accomplishes all His will. The Faithfulness of God assures believers that God always keeps His promises and never fails those who trust Him. In The Goodness of God, Pink unfolds the benevolence of God toward all His creatures. The Patience of God explores God's longsuffering with sinners, while The Grace of God reveals the free and unmerited favor bestowed upon the undeserving. The Mercy of God distinguishes mercy from grace: grace provides what we do not deserve, while mercy withholds what we do deserve. The Lovingkindness of God, The Love of God, and The Love of God to Us form a trilogy on divine love, tracing it from eternity, through the gift of Christ, to its particular manifestation toward believers. The Wrath of God soberly addresses God's holy hatred of sin and His righteous judgment upon the impenitent. Pink concludes with The Contemplation of God, urging every reader to make the study of God's attributes not merely an intellectual exercise but a lifelong devotion that produces worship, humility, and practical holiness. This book remains essential reading because it grounds every attribute of God firmly in Scripture, refuses to soften or sentimentalize the divine character, and calls every reader to approach the Almighty with reverence, gratitude, and holy fear. May this summary draw you deeper into the knowledge of the God who is infinitely worthy of our worship.
