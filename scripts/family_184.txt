PURITAN GOLD -- Family Devotional, Day 23
"Winning the Hearts of Children"
Richard Baxter — The Christian Directory

---

Good evening, dear family. Welcome back to Puritan Gold. Tonight Richard Baxter gives us some of his most tender and searching counsel — on how parents can win the hearts of their children for Christ. Let us listen together.

Let us pray. Father, You know the hearts of our children better than we do. You love them more than we ever could. Give us wisdom tonight to learn from Your servant Baxter how to reach their hearts — not merely to command their behavior, but to win their affection for You. In Christ's name, Amen.

Our Scripture reading this evening comes from Ephesians 6:4 and Proverbs 23:26. Let us hear the Word of the Lord:

"And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord. My son, give me thine heart, and let thine eyes observe my ways."

That is Ephesians 6:4 and Proverbs 23:26. Let those words settle in your heart for a moment before we continue.

--- Teaching ---

Richard Baxter understood something that many parents miss: the goal of parenting is not merely to control children's behavior but to win their hearts. A child who obeys out of fear alone will abandon that obedience the moment the fear is removed. But a child who obeys out of love — love for God and love for parents — will carry that obedience into adulthood and transmit it to the next generation.

Paul's command in Ephesians 6:4 contains both a warning and a commission. The warning: "Provoke not your children to wrath." The commission: "Bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord." Baxter devotes extensive attention to both.

First, the warning. Baxter identifies several ways parents provoke their children to anger. The first is excessive severity — punishing too harshly, correcting too frequently, and creating an atmosphere of fear rather than love. Baxter observes that some parents, in their zeal for obedience, crush the spirits of their children and produce not godliness but resentment. A child who is constantly criticized, harshly punished, and never praised will eventually shut down emotionally — and may shut down spiritually as well.

The second way parents provoke children is through inconsistency — enforcing rules one day and ignoring them the next, punishing one child for behavior that is tolerated in another, making promises that are never kept. Inconsistency breeds confusion, and confusion breeds frustration, and frustration breeds anger. Children need to know that the rules are clear, fair, and consistently applied.

The third way is through hypocrisy — demanding standards of behavior that the parents themselves do not meet. Children are extraordinarily perceptive. They can detect the gap between what their parents say and what their parents do. A father who preaches honesty but lies about his taxes, or a mother who demands kindness but speaks cruelly about her neighbors, will lose the moral authority to instruct their children. Baxter insists that the most powerful teaching tool a parent possesses is personal example.

Now, the commission: "Bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord." Baxter identifies several keys to winning children's hearts. The first is affection. Children need to know — not just theoretically, but experientially — that they are loved. This means expressing affection in words, in touch, in time, and in attention. Baxter counsels parents to play with their children, to laugh with them, to listen to them, and to take a genuine interest in their lives. A parent who is only present for instruction and correction but absent for companionship and delight will struggle to win a child's heart.

The second key is patience. Children learn slowly. They forget quickly. They repeat the same mistakes. They test boundaries. They make messes. This is not defiance — it is childhood. Baxter urges parents to be patient with the process of growth, to correct without exasperation, and to remember that they themselves are still growing and still in need of God's patience.

The third key is encouragement. Baxter writes that parents should be at least as diligent in praising what is good as in correcting what is wrong. When a child tells the truth, praise the truth-telling. When a child shows kindness, celebrate the kindness. When a child makes an effort — even if the result is imperfect — acknowledge the effort. Encouragement waters the seeds of virtue. Constant criticism pulls them up by the roots.

The fourth key is spiritual instruction. Baxter does not separate heart-winning from teaching. Indeed, he sees them as inseparable. The parent who wins a child's affection earns the right to shape a child's beliefs. Instruction given in the context of a loving relationship is instruction that takes root. Baxter counsels parents to teach the faith in small, frequent, natural moments — at mealtimes, at bedtimes, during walks, during work — rather than in formal, stiff, intimidating sessions.

God Himself models this approach. He does not merely command our obedience — He wins our hearts through love. "We love him, because he first loved us" (1 John 4:19). Parents who follow this divine pattern will find that obedience flows not from fear but from love — and love is a far more powerful and lasting motivation than fear will ever be.

--- Discussion Questions ---

Let us pause and talk as a family. Here are tonight's discussion questions. Everyone gets a turn to share.

Question 1: Baxter says parents can provoke children through severity, inconsistency, and hypocrisy. Which of these dangers do we need to be most watchful against in our family?

Question 2: Proverbs 23:26 says, "My son, give me thine heart." How is winning a child's heart different from merely commanding a child's obedience?

Question 3: Baxter says encouragement "waters the seeds of virtue." What is one thing each family member could be encouraged about tonight?

Take a few minutes to talk about these questions together. Let everyone share, and listen to one another with patience and love.

--- Memory Verse ---

Now, here is our memory verse for today. Let us say it together:

"And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord." -- Ephesians 6:4

Try to say it out loud three times right now. Then, before bed tonight, see if you can say it from memory.

--- Closing ---

Let us pray together. Lord, help us to parent the way You parent us — with love, with patience, with encouragement, and with faithful instruction. Guard us from provoking our children. Help us to win their hearts for You. Give us grace to model the faith we teach and to build a home where obedience flows from love. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Sleep well tonight, knowing that God watches over your family.
