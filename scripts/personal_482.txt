PURITAN GOLD -- My Growth, Day 21
"The Attributes of God — Stephen Charnock"
Theological Day — The Existence and Attributes of God


Welcome. Today is a Theological day. We draw from Stephen Charnock's monumental work The Existence and Attributes of God — perhaps the most comprehensive treatment of the divine nature in the English language.

Let us read Exodus 3:14 and Psalm 145:3 (KJV):

"And God said unto Moses, I AM THAT I AM: and he said, Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, I AM hath sent me unto you." "Great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised; and his greatness is unsearchable."


Stephen Charnock (1628-1680) produced a work of staggering scope and depth in The Existence and Attributes of God. Running to over a thousand pages, it examines the divine nature with a combination of philosophical rigor, exegetical precision, and devotional warmth that is unmatched in Christian literature.

Charnock begins with the existence of God — establishing through reason, Scripture, and the testimony of creation that God is real, personal, and sovereign. But his greatest contribution lies in his treatment of the divine attributes — the perfections that characterize God's nature.

God's self-existence. God is the I AM — the One who depends on nothing outside Himself for His existence. Every created thing depends on something else. You depend on oxygen, food, water, and the sustaining will of God. But God depends on nothing. He is the uncaused cause, the self-sufficient One, the fountain of all being. Charnock writes that God's self-existence is the foundation of all His other attributes, for a God who depended on anything else would not truly be God.

God's immutability. God does not change. Malachi 3:6: "I am the Lord, I change not." In a world of constant change — shifting relationships, fluctuating health, uncertain economies — the immutability of God is an anchor. Charnock argues that God's immutability is the guarantee of all His promises. If God could change His mind, no promise would be secure. But because He is unchangeable, His covenant, His love, His purposes, and His character are all absolutely reliable.

God's eternity. God exists outside of time. He has no beginning and no end. He does not experience the passage of time as we do — moving from past through present to future. He sees all of time simultaneously, as a man on a mountaintop sees the entire valley below. Charnock writes that God's eternity gives Him a perspective on our lives that we cannot share. What seems unbearable to us — because we are trapped in the moment — is, from God's eternal perspective, a brief chapter in an infinite story.

God's omniscience. God knows all things — past, present, and future, actual and possible. He knows every thought you have ever had, every word you will ever speak, every decision you will ever make. This is simultaneously terrifying and comforting. Terrifying, because nothing is hidden. Comforting, because nothing is overlooked. Charnock writes that God's omniscience means that you are never forgotten, never misunderstood, and never lost from His sight.

God's omnipotence. God can do all things that are consistent with His nature. There is no limit to His power except His own character — He cannot lie, He cannot deny Himself, He cannot sin. But within the scope of His perfect nature, His power is infinite. Charnock applies this to the discouraged believer: the God who created the universe from nothing can certainly handle your problems. The question is never whether God is able but whether you are willing to trust His power.

God's holiness. Charnock considers holiness to be the most prominent of all divine attributes — the one that most comprehensively describes who God is. Holiness is not merely one attribute among many; it is the character of all God's attributes. His love is a holy love. His justice is a holy justice. His mercy is a holy mercy. His power is a holy power. Everything God is and everything God does is characterized by absolute moral perfection.

God's goodness. God is not only powerful and holy; He is good. His goodness overflows in creation, in providence, in redemption, and in the daily mercies that sustain every creature. Charnock writes that the goodness of God is the attribute that makes all His other attributes comforting. Power without goodness would be terrifying. Holiness without goodness would be crushing. But a God who is powerful, holy, and good is a God in whom we can rest completely.

The purpose of studying the attributes of God is not merely intellectual. It is transformational. The more you know God, the more you trust Him. The more you trust Him, the more you worship Him. And the more you worship Him, the more you become like Him.


God is greater than you have ever imagined. Study Him. Know Him. And let the knowledge transform you. Lord, I have barely begun to know You. Your greatness is unsearchable. But I ask for deeper knowledge — not to satisfy curiosity but to fuel worship. Show me more of who You are, and let what I see change who I am. Amen.
