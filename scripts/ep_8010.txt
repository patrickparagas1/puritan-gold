PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"Made Nigh by the Blood of Christ"
July 10, 2026

---

"Wherefore remember, that ye being in time past Gentiles in the flesh, who are called Uncircumcision by that which is called the Circumcision in the flesh made by hands; that at that time ye were without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope, and without God in the world: but now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ. For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us." -- Ephesians 2:11-16

Paul now turns from the individual experience of salvation to its corporate dimensions. The Gentiles who were once strangers and aliens, far off from God and His covenant people, have been brought near by the blood of Christ. And not only brought near, but united with Jewish believers into one new body, one new humanity, one new temple.

'He is our peace.' Not merely that He gives peace or teaches peace, but that He Himself is our peace. In His own body on the cross, He broke down the wall of hostility that separated Jew from Gentile, and created in Himself one new man in place of the two. The ceremonial law with its regulations and ordinances, which had served as a dividing wall, was abolished -- not because it was evil, but because it had served its purpose as a guardian until Christ came.

Samuel Bolton, in his excellent treatise 'The True Bounds of Christian Freedom,' taught that Christ fulfilled the law in every respect -- its moral demands by His perfect obedience, its ceremonial shadows by His substance, and its penal curse by His suffering. Therefore the believer, whether Jew or Gentile, stands before God not on the ground of legal obedience but on the ground of Christ's finished work. The middle wall is down. The distinction is abolished. There is neither Jew nor Greek in Christ Jesus.

What a powerful word this is for our divided age! The gospel does not merely tolerate diversity; it creates unity out of the deepest divisions. If the blood of Christ could reconcile Jew and Gentile -- two peoples separated by centuries of hatred, by divine law, by cultural incompatibility -- then there is no human division that the cross cannot heal. The gospel is the only true foundation for unity, because it brings every soul to the same level at the foot of the cross, where all are equally sinful, equally helpless, and equally loved. Amen.
