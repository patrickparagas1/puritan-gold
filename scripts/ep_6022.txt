PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"My Heart Is Inditing a Good Matter"
June 22, 2026

---

"My heart is inditing a good matter: I speak of the things which I have made touching the king: my tongue is the pen of a ready writer. Thou art fairer than the children of men: grace is poured into thy lips: therefore God hath blessed thee for ever. Gird thy sword upon thy thigh, O most mighty, with thy glory and thy majesty." -- Psalm 45:1-3

This psalm stands apart from all others in the Psalter, for it is a wedding song, a royal epithalamium composed for the marriage of the king. And yet it rises so far above any earthly marriage, ascribes to the bridegroom such divine attributes, and breathes such an atmosphere of heavenly glory that the church has always understood it as a prophetic portrait of the Lord Jesus Christ and His bride. The author of the epistle to the Hebrews confirms this interpretation, quoting from this psalm in direct reference to the Son of God. It is Christ who is fairer than the children of men. It is Christ into whose lips grace has been poured. It is Christ whom God has blessed forever.

"My heart is inditing a good matter." The word "inditing" means to bubble up, to overflow, like a spring that cannot contain its waters. The psalmist is not composing by cold calculation or literary craftsmanship alone. His heart is overflowing with a subject so glorious that the words pour forth almost faster than his tongue can utter them. "My tongue is the pen of a ready writer." The pen moves swiftly because the inspiration is strong, and the inspiration is strong because the subject is Christ.

Spurgeon confessed that he never approached this psalm without a trembling sense of inadequacy, for who can worthily describe the beauty of Christ? All the poets of every age, all the preachers of every nation, all the saints of every generation have attempted it, and all have confessed that their best efforts fall infinitely short of the reality. Christ is fairer than the children of men, but the word "fairer" is hopelessly inadequate to express the surpassing excellence of His person. He is not merely better-looking than other men, as one flower might be prettier than another. He is beauty itself, the fountain from which all created beauty flows, the original of which all earthly loveliness is but a faint and broken reflection.

"Grace is poured into thy lips." When Christ speaks, grace flows. His words are not merely wise or true or eloquent, though they are all of these. They are grace-bearing words, words that carry in them the power to transform, to heal, to deliver, to save. When He said to the paralytic, "Rise, take up thy bed, and walk," grace was poured into those words, and the paralytic walked. When He said to the storm, "Peace, be still," grace was poured into those words, and the winds and the waves obeyed. When He said to the thief upon the cross, "Today shalt thou be with me in paradise," grace was poured into those words, and a dying criminal was transported from the brink of hell to the gate of heaven.

Thomas Goodwin wrote with rapturous devotion upon the grace of Christ, declaring that all the grace that has ever been bestowed upon all the saints of all the ages is but a single drop from the infinite ocean of grace that dwells in the person of the Redeemer. He is full of grace, as the sun is full of light, as the ocean is full of water, as space is full of stars. And from His fulness we have all received, and grace upon grace.

"Gird thy sword upon thy thigh, O most mighty, with thy glory and thy majesty." The psalmist now turns from the beauty of the king to his power. Christ is not only beautiful; He is mighty. He is not only gracious; He is glorious. The sword upon His thigh is the Word of God, which is sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart. With this sword He conquers His enemies, subdues the nations, and brings all things into subjection to Himself.

Richard Sibbes delighted in preaching upon the majesty of Christ, and he taught that the church must never separate the gentleness of Christ from His greatness, nor His beauty from His power. He is the Lamb who is also the Lion. He is the suffering servant who is also the King of kings. He is the meek and lowly Jesus who rode into Jerusalem on a donkey, and He is the mighty conqueror who shall ride forth on a white horse to judge the nations and to rule them with a rod of iron.

Let us therefore fix our hearts upon this glorious King, and let them overflow with praise as the psalmist's heart overflowed. For Christ is worthy of all our worship, all our love, all our devotion. He is fairer than the children of men, and grace is poured into His lips, and God has blessed Him forever. To Him be glory and majesty, dominion and power, now and forevermore. Amen.
