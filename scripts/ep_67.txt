PURITAN GOLD -- Daily Study
"The Lord Is My Light"
May 6, 2026

---

"The Lord is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? the Lord is the strength of my life; of whom shall I be afraid? When the wicked, even mine enemies and my foes, came upon me to eat up my flesh, they stumbled and fell. Though an host should encamp against me, my heart shall not fear: though war should rise against me, in this will I be confident." -- Psalm 27:1-3

There are seasons in the life of every believer when the darkness seems to close in from every side. Enemies multiply. Troubles deepen. The way ahead is hidden, and the heart is tempted to give way to fear and despair. It is in such seasons that this psalm speaks with a power that no human eloquence can match. For it does not merely offer encouragement from one man to another. It offers the believer a solid, immovable foundation upon which to stand when all else is shaking: "The Lord is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?"

David does not say that the Lord gives light. He says the Lord is light. There is an immeasurable difference between a man who carries a lantern and a man who is himself the sun. The Lord does not merely illuminate the path before us; He is the light itself. In Him there is no darkness at all. And when He shines upon the soul, every shadow flees, every doubt dissolves, every fear is consumed as mist is consumed by the morning sun. The believer who walks in the light of God's countenance walks in a brightness that no earthly power can extinguish.

"And my salvation." Not merely my helper, or my advisor, or my companion in trouble, but my salvation. The Lord Himself is the whole of my deliverance. He does not merely point the way to safety; He is the way. He does not merely rescue from danger; He is the rescue. All that the soul needs in its most desperate extremity is found in Him alone. And if God is both our light and our salvation, then the question follows with irresistible logic: "Whom shall I fear?" The answer, of course, is no one. Absolutely no one.

Jeremiah Burroughs, that gentle and gracious Puritan who wrote so profoundly on the subject of Christian contentment, once observed that fear is the natural consequence of looking at our circumstances instead of looking at our God. When Peter walked upon the water, he was safe so long as his eyes were fixed upon Christ. But the moment he looked at the boisterous waves, he began to sink. So it is with us. When we look at the host encamped against us, when we count the numbers of our enemies, when we measure the strength of the opposition, our hearts melt within us. But when we lift our eyes above the host and behold the Lord of hosts, when we see Him who is invisible, when we reckon not by sight but by faith, then our hearts are strengthened, and we can say with David, "In this will I be confident."

"One thing have I desired of the Lord, that will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the Lord, and to enquire in his temple." Here is the single-hearted devotion that the Lord looks for in His people. David had many desires. He desired deliverance from his enemies. He desired the restoration of his kingdom. He desired peace and prosperity for his people. But above all these earthly desires, there was one supreme longing that dominated his soul: to dwell in the presence of God. To behold the beauty of the Lord. To enquire in His temple.

What is the beauty of the Lord? It is the perfection of His character displayed before the eyes of faith. It is the harmony of all His attributes working together in infinite wisdom and love. It is His holiness, which fills the seraphim with awe. It is His justice, which gives confidence to every oppressed soul. It is His mercy, which stoops to lift the fallen and to cleanse the defiled. It is His faithfulness, which stands firm when every human promise has crumbled to dust. To behold this beauty is the highest occupation of the human soul, and it is the one occupation that shall never grow weary or stale, for the beauty of the Lord is infinite, and eternity itself shall not exhaust its depths.

David understood something that many professing Christians have yet to learn: that the presence of God is not merely a pleasant addition to an otherwise satisfactory life, but is itself the one thing needful, the pearl of great price, the treasure hidden in the field for which a wise man sells all that he has. Without the presence of God, all the pleasures of this world are ashes in the mouth. With the presence of God, even the darkest valley becomes a place of singing.

"For in the time of trouble he shall hide me in his pavilion: in the secret of his tabernacle shall he hide me; he shall set me up upon a rock." The pavilion of God is His secret presence. It is the place where the believer is hidden from the strife of tongues, from the plots of the wicked, from the fury of the storm. The world cannot find him there. The enemy cannot reach him there. He is set upon a rock so high that the floodwaters of trouble cannot touch him.

John Owen taught that the believer's communion with God is the great antidote to every form of spiritual distress. When the soul is in sweet communion with the Father, through the mediation of the Son, and by the power of the Holy Spirit, it is lifted above the reach of its enemies. Not that the troubles disappear. The host may still be encamped around. The war may still be raging. But the soul is at peace, because it is hidden in the pavilion of the Almighty.

"Wait on the Lord: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the Lord." The psalm closes with this double exhortation to wait. Why does the Spirit repeat it? Because our hearts are so prone to impatience. We want deliverance now. We want answers now. We want the darkness to lift now. But the ways of God are not the ways of the microwave. They are the ways of the oak tree, slow and deep and enduring. And the soul that learns to wait upon the Lord shall find its heart strengthened with a strength that does not come from human resources but from the inexhaustible might of God Himself. Wait, beloved. Be of good courage. The light has not failed. The salvation has not been revoked. The Lord who is your light and your salvation is the same yesterday, today, and forever. Amen.
